    Guide to Further Reading¶

    The following works, all listed in the bibliography, are recommended starting points. For the technical vocabulary of the Hindu Tantras, the Tāntrikābhidhānakośa (Brunner et al. 2000), a dictionary of terms with references to the sources, is the standard reference work.

    Editions and translations of primary texts. Among the primary sources discussed in this volume, the following are available in editions or translations listed in the bibliography: the Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā (Goodall et al. 2015); the Brahmayāmala (Hatley 2018); the Kiraṇavṛtti of Rāmakaṇṭha (Goodall 1998); the Rauravāgama (Bhatt 1961–1988); Somaśambhu’s manual (Brunner-Lachaux 1963–1998); the yogic chapters of the Mālinīvijayottara (Vasudeva 2004); the Vīṇāśikhatantra (Goudriaan 1985); the Kubjikāmata (Goudriaan and Schoterman 1988) and the opening chapters of the Ṣaṭsāhasrasaṃhitā (Schoterman 1982); the early Pāñcarātra works edited by Acharya (Acharya 2015); Yāmuna’s Āgamaprāmāṇya (van Buitenen 1971); the Haribhaktivilāsa (Broo 2023); the Kulārṇava Tantra (Vidyāratna 1917); the Mahānirvāṇa Tantra in translation (Avalon 1913); the Saundaryalaharī with Lakṣmīdhara’s commentary (Sastri and Ayyangar 1937); the Tantrāloka with Jayaratha’s commentary (Abhinavagupta 1918–1938); Utpaladeva’s Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā (Torella 2002); Somānanda’s Śivadṛṣṭi, chapters 1–3 (Nemec 2011); the Śivasūtra, Spandakārikā and Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya in translation (Singh 1979, 1980, 1963); and the Hevajra Tantra (Snellgrove 1959).

    General introductions. André Padoux’s The Hindu Tantric World (Padoux 2017) and Gavin Flood’s The Tantric Body (Flood 2006) are reliable overviews. Teun Goudriaan and Sanjukta Gupta’s Hindu Tantric and Śākta Literature (Goudriaan and Gupta 1981) and Hindu Tantrism (Gupta et al. 1979) survey the texts. David Gordon White’s anthology Tantra in Practice (White 2000) offers translations with introductions.

    History. Alexis Sanderson’s “Śaivism and the Tantric Traditions” (Sanderson 1988) is the classic short history; his “The Śaiva Age” (Sanderson 2009) presents the evidence for royal patronage in detail. Geoffrey Samuel’s The Origins of Yoga and Tantra (Samuel 2008) sets Tantra in a long history of Indic religion; Ronald Davidson’s Indian Esoteric Buddhism (Davidson 2002) gives a social history of Buddhist Tantra.

    Śaiva Siddhānta and temple ritual. Richard Davis’s Ritual in an Oscillating Universe (Davis 1991) and A Priest’s Guide for the Great Festival (Davis 2010); Hélène Brunner’s edition of Somaśambhu’s manual (Brunner-Lachaux 1963–1998); C.J. Fuller’s Servants of the Goddess (Fuller 1984) and The Renewal of the Priesthood (Fuller 2003); Stella Kramrisch’s The Hindu Temple (Kramrisch 1946). For the early scriptures, Goodall’s editions of the Kiraṇa commentary and the Niśvāsa (Goodall 1998; Goodall et al. 2015).

    Vaiṣṇava traditions. Schrader’s Introduction to the Pāñcarātra (Schrader 1916), Matsubara’s Pāñcarātra Saṃhitās and Early Vaiṣṇava Theology (Matsubara 1994) and van Buitenen’s translation of Yāmuna (van Buitenen 1971). Acharya’s edition of the earliest Pāñcarātra texts (Acharya 2015), Rastelli on the Pārameśvara (Rastelli 2006), Colas on Vaikhānasa images and fires (Colas 1996) and Broo’s edition of the Haribhaktivilāsa (Broo 2023) take the subject further. On the Tantric Vaiṣṇavism of Bengal, Dimock’s The Place of the Hidden Moon (Dimock 1966), Openshaw’s Seeking Bāuls of Bengal (Openshaw 2002) and Urban’s The Economics of Ecstasy (Urban 2001).

    Śākta traditions. Thomas Coburn on the Devīmāhātmya (Coburn 1984); David Kinsley on the Mahāvidyās (Kinsley 1997); Douglas Brooks on Śrīvidyā (Brooks 1990, 1992); June McDaniel on Bengal (McDaniel 2004); Vidya Dehejia on yoginī temples (Dehejia 1986); and, on the Kaula traditions and their interpretation, David Gordon White’s Kiss of the Yoginī (White 2003) and Hugh Urban’s Tantra (Urban 2003). On women in the Tantric traditions, Törzsök on the early Śākta scriptures (Törzsök 2014), Biernacki on the late Kaula texts (Biernacki 2007), Gupta’s survey (Gupta 1992) and McDaniel on women tāntrikās in Bengal (McDaniel 2007).

    Healing, possession and the lesser streams. Slouber’s Early Tantric Medicine (Slouber 2017); Goudriaan’s edition of the Vīṇāśikhatantra (Goudriaan 1985); Frederick Smith’s The Self Possessed (Smith 2006).

    Kingship and the Śaiva Age. Sanderson’s “The Śaiva Age” (Sanderson 2009) with his studies of the Khmers and of inscriptions (Sanderson 2003–2004, 2013); Hermann Kulke’s Kings and Cults (Kulke 1993); B.D. Chattopadhyaya’s The Making of Early Medieval India (Chattopadhyaya 1994); Hans Bakker’s The World of the Skandapurāṇa (Bakker 2014); Florinda De Simini’s Of Gods and Books (De Simini 2016); and, for wider perspectives, Davis’s Lives of Indian Images (Davis 1997), Pollock’s The Language of the Gods in the World of Men (Pollock 2006), Talbot on medieval Andhra (Talbot 2001), Fuller on Navarātri and royal ritual (Fuller 1992) and Sarkar on the royal cult of Durgā (Sarkar 2017). On ascetic orders, Lorenzen’s The Kāpālikas and Kālāmukhas (Lorenzen 1972); on the yoginīs, Hatley’s study and edition of the Brahmayāmala (Hatley 2007, 2018) and the essays edited by Keul (Keul 2013).

    Regional traditions. For Kerala, Tarabout (Tarabout 1986) and Caldwell (Caldwell 1999); for Bengal, McDermott (McDermott 2001) and Sircar on the pīṭhas (Sircar 1973); for Assam, Urban (Urban 2010); for Odisha, Eschmann, Kulke and Tripathi (Eschmann et al. 1978); for Nepal, Levy’s Mesocosm (Levy 1990) and Dyczkowski on Kubjikā (Dyczkowski 1988); for the goddesses of the north-western hills, Erndl (Erndl 1993); for Rajasthan, Harlan (Harlan 1992) and the Jodhpur catalogue Garden and Cosmos (Diamond et al. 2008); for the Tamil Tirumantiram, Zvelebil (Zvelebil 1975); for Bali, Hooykaas (Hooykaas 1966).

    Jain, Saura and Gāṇapatya traditions. Dundas’s The Jains (Dundas 2002) and his essay on the sūrimantra (Dundas 1998); Gough’s Making a Mantra (Gough 2021); Cort’s Jains in the World (Cort 2001); Laidlaw’s Riches and Renunciation (Laidlaw 1995); von Stietencron on the Sun priests (Stietencron 1966); Courtright on Gaṇeśa (Courtright 1985).

    Kashmiri Śaivism. Mark Dyczkowski’s The Doctrine of Vibration (Dyczkowski 1987); Paul Muller-Ortega’s The Triadic Heart of Śiva (Muller-Ortega 1989); Jaideva Singh’s translations of the Śivasūtra and Spandakārikā (Singh 1979, 1980). For the Pratyabhijñā, Torella’s edition of Utpaladeva (Torella 2002), Nemec on Somānanda (Nemec 2011) and Ratié on the self (Ratié 2011); for the older scholarship, Pandey’s Abhinavagupta (Pandey 1963) and Silburn (Silburn 1988). For the Krama, Rastogi’s The Krama Tantricism of Kashmir (Rastogi 1979), and for the history of Kashmiri exegesis, Sanderson’s “The Śaiva Exegesis of Kashmir” (Sanderson 2007). On the yoga of the Śaiva scriptures, Vasudeva’s study of the Mālinīvijayottara (Vasudeva 2004).

    Reception. Taylor on Woodroffe and his collaborators (Taylor 2001); Urban on colonial and Western constructions of Tantra and on modern sexual magic (Urban 2003, 2006); Love on Pierre Bernard (Love 2010); Padoux on the category “Tantrism” (Padoux 2002); and, for the Kālī’s Child debate, Kripal and his critics (Kripal 1995; Swami Tyagananda and Pravrajika Vrajaprana 2010).

    Mantra, yantra and the Word. Padoux’s Vāc (Padoux 1990); Alper’s Understanding Mantras (Alper 1989); Bühnemann’s Maṇḍalas and Yantras (Bühnemann 2003) and Pūjā (Bühnemann 1988). On Tantric art and its modern reception, Rawson’s The Art of Tantra (Rawson 1973), Mookerjee’s Tantra Art (Mookerjee 1966) and the British Museum catalogue Tantra: Enlightenment to Revolution (Ramos 2020).

    Buddhist Tantra. Snellgrove’s Indo-Tibetan Buddhism (Snellgrove 1987) and his edition of the Hevajra (Snellgrove 1959); Gray’s Cakrasaṃvara (Gray 2007); Wedemeyer’s Making Sense of Tantric Buddhism (Wedemeyer 2013); Miranda Shaw’s Buddhist Goddesses of India (Shaw 2006); English on Vajrayoginī (English 2002); Wallace on the “inner” Kālacakra (Wallace 2001); and Dalton on violence and liberation in Tibetan Buddhism (Dalton 2011).

    Yoga and alchemy. Mallinson and Singleton’s Roots of Yoga (Mallinson and Singleton 2017); White’s The Alchemical Body (White 1996); Zvelebil’s The Poets of the Powers on the Tamil Siddhas (Zvelebil 1973); Ernst on the Persian and Arabic translations of yoga (Ernst 2003); and McLeod on Guru Nānak and the Nāths (McLeod 1968).
