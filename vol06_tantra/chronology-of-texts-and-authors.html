    Chronology of Texts and Authors¶

    Dates are approximate and follow mainstream scholarship; traditional dates, where they differ, are noted. Many Āgamic and Tantric texts cannot be dated more closely than to a range of centuries.

    Date Text, author or event
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Ṛgveda, including the hymn of Vāc (10.125)
    c. 1st millennium BCE Śatarudrīya; Taittirīya Āraṇyaka with the five Brahma mantras; Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad
    c. 2nd century CE Lakulīśa, founder of the Pāśupatas (date inferred from the Mathurā inscription of 380)
    380 CE Mathurā pillar inscription naming Pāśupata teachers
    5th century Bhartṛhari, Vākyapadīya
    c. 450–550 Oldest layer of the Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā
    c. 5th–6th century Devīmāhātmya
    c. 6th–9th century Early Siddhānta scriptures (Svāyambhuvasūtrasaṅgraha, Kiraṇa, Mataṅgapārameśvara and others)
    7th century Kumārila Bhaṭṭa; Mahāvairocana; Mattavilāsa of Mahendravarman I; Bāṇa’s Harṣacarita
    c. 6th–9th century Tamil Nāyaṉmārs (Campantar, Appar, Cuntarar) and Āḻvārs
    c. 7th–8th century Brahmayāmala; Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṅgraha
    8th century Guhyasamāja (probably later in the century)
    c. 675–725 Sadyojyotis, first great exegete of the Siddhānta (Sanderson’s dating)
    c. 700 Pallava Rājasiṃha’s Kailāsanātha temple, Kāñcīpuram
    8th century Śubhakarasiṃha, Vajrabodhi and Amoghavajra in China; Padmasambhava and Śāntarakṣita in Tibet (traditional)
    c. 8th–10th century Earliest surviving Pāñcarātra scriptures (Svāyambhuvapañcarātra and others), followed by the “three gems” (Jayākhya, Sātvata, Pauṣkara); Vaikuṇṭha images in Kashmir; Jayadrathayāmala (before Abhinavagupta)
    c. 800 Borobudur; Jayavarman II’s Śaiva royal rites in Cambodia (recorded 1052)
    9th century Vasugupta and the Śivasūtra; Spandakārikā; Kūkai (774–835) founds Shingon
    9th century (probably) Jñānanetra (Śivānanda) and his three women disciples at the head of the Krama lineage
    856 Prambanan Śivagṛha inscription
    881 Indravarman I consecrates the liṅga Indreśvara at Bakong, Cambodia
    c. 900–975 Somānanda; Utpaladeva
    10th century Nāthamuni; Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇakaṇṭha and Rāmakaṇṭha; Kubjikāmata; Hirapur and other yoginī temples
    939 Indranandi, Jvālāmālinīkalpa
    c. 950–1016 Abhinavagupta (conventional dates; see below)
    990/91–1014/15 Abhinavagupta’s dated works
    early 11th century Kṣemarāja; Kālacakra Tantra and Vimalaprabhā
    c. 1010 Bṛhadīśvara temple, Thanjavur, under Rājarāja I
    10th–11th century Yāmuna, Āgamaprāmāṇya
    by c. 11th century Kriyākālaguṇottara, digest of the Gāruḍa and Bhūta Tantras
    982–1054 Atīśa
    1027 Palm-leaf manuscript of the Svāyambhuvapañcarātra copied in Nepal
    1095 Somaśambhu’s ritual manual
    1017–1137 (traditional) Rāmānuja; some scholars prefer c. 1077–1157
    12th century Aghoraśiva; Cēkkiḻār, Periyapurāṇam; Basava and the Vīraśaiva poets
    12th–13th century Pārameśvara Saṃhitā (Śrīraṅgam); Mount Ābu inscription naming the woman Pāśupata Yogeśvarī
    c. 1193–1205 Sack of Nālandā, Vikramaśīla and Odantapurī
    13th century Jayaratha’s commentaries on the Tantrāloka and the Vāmakeśvarīmata
    13th century Meykaṇṭār, Civañāṉapōtam; Aruḷnanti
    c. 11th–13th century Gorakṣanātha and the early Nāthas
    c. 12th–14th century Kulārṇava Tantra; Tantrarāja
    1261 Malkāpuram inscription of the Golakī preceptor Viśveśvaraśambhu
    1306 Jinaprabhasūri, Vidhimārgaprapā (Jain ritual manual)
    14th century Vedānta Deśika (traditional dates 1268–1369); Piḷḷai Lokācārya; Umāpati Śivācārya; Tsongkhapa (1357–1419)
    15th century Haṭhapradīpikā; Tantrasamuccaya (Kerala); Maṇavāḷa Māmuṉi (1370–1443)
    15th century (probably) Keśava, Kramadīpikā
    1449–1568 (traditional) Śaṅkaradeva, founder of Assamese Vaiṣṇava devotion
    1469–1539 Guru Nānak; his Siddh Goṣṭ records a dialogue with Nāth yogis
    1486–1533 Caitanya; Bengali Vaiṣṇava devotion, the matrix of the later Sahajiyā tradition
    c. 1520 Domingo Paes describes the Mahānavamī festival at Vijayanagara
    c. 1540 Haribhaktivilāsa, ritual code of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas
    c. 16th century Lakṣmīdhara’s commentary on the Saundaryalaharī
    1577 Pūrṇānanda, Śrītattvacintāmaṇi, including the Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa
    1589 Mahīdhara, Mantramahodadhi
    late 16th–17th century Kṛṣṇānanda Āgamavāgīśa, Tantrasāra
    1696–1744 Ahom kings Rudra Siṃha and Śiva Siṃha patronise Kāmākhyā
    18th century Bhāskararāya, Saubhāgyabhāskara (1728); Rāmprasād Sen
    1752–53 Bhāratcandra Rāy, Annadāmaṅgal
    late 18th century Composition of the Mahānirvāṇa Tantra (Derrett)
    1803–1843 Reign of Mān Siṃh of Jodhpur, patron of the Nāths
    1811 First edition of William Ward’s account of the Hindus (Serampore)
    1836–1886 Rāmakṛṣṇa
    1837–1911 Vāmākṣepā of Tārāpīṭh
    1861; 1872 The Bhairavī Brāhmaṇī arrives at Dakṣiṇeśvar; Rāmakṛṣṇa worships Śāradā Devī as Ṣoḍaśī
    1882 Bankimcandra Chattopadhyay, Ānandamaṭh
    1888 Nārāyaṇa Guru consecrates a Śiva image at Aruvippuram
    d. 1890 Lālan Fakir, Bāul poet of Bengal
    1905 The pamphlet Bhavānī Mandir
    1911 onwards Kashmir Series of Texts and Studies
    1913–1919 Arthur Avalon (Woodroffe): Tantra of the Great Liberation, The Serpent Power
    1924–25; 1936 Vaikom Satyagraha; Travancore Temple Entry Proclamation
    1947 Madras Devadasis (Prevention of Dedication) Act
    1955 Foundation of the Institut Français de Pondichéry
    1961–1988 N.R. Bhatt’s edition of the Rauravāgama
    1971 Exhibition “Tantra” at the Hayward Gallery, London (Philip Rawson)
    1995 Jeffrey Kripal’s Kālī’s Child and the ensuing controversy
    2000 First volume of the Tāntrikābhidhānakośa, the dictionary of Tantric technical terms
    2010 Muṭiyēṟṟu of Kerala inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List
    2015 First volume of the critical edition of the Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā; Acharya’s edition of three early Pāñcarātra works
