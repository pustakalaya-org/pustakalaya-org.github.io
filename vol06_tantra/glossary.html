    Glossary¶

    Term Meaning
    Abhiṣeka Consecration by sprinkling or bathing; in Buddhism, Tantric initiation
    Ācāra “Conduct”; in Śākta texts, a mode of practice (Vedic, Dakṣiṇa, Vāma, Kaula etc.)
    Ācārya Preceptor; in Śaivism, an initiate consecrated to initiate others and perform consecrations
    Adhikāra Entitlement or qualification for a practice
    Adhvan “Path”; the six levels of the cosmos purified in Śaiva initiation
    Ādiśaiva Hereditary priest of Tamil Śaiva Āgamic temples
    Āgaṃ Newar secret shrine of a lineage’s Tantric deity, open only to initiates
    Āgama “That which has come down”; revealed scripture, especially of the Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava traditions
    Ajapā The “unrecited” mantra haṃsa, identified with the breath
    Āmnāya “Transmission”; one of the directional families (eastern, northern, western, southern and upper) into which later Kaula and Śākta traditions group their cults
    Āṇavamala Innate impurity that limits the soul (Śaiva Siddhānta)
    Anugraha Grace; the fifth of Śiva’s five acts
    Arcā The consecrated image as a mode of the deity’s presence
    Atimārga The early ascetic Śaiva orders (Pāśupata, Lākula, Kāpālika)
    Āvaraṇa Enclosure of a maṇḍala or yantra, with its deities
    Bhūtaśuddhi Ritual purification of the elements of the worshipper’s body
    Bhūta Tantra Scripture of the western stream of the Mantramārga, on possessing spirits and their exorcism
    Bīja “Seed”; a monosyllabic mantra
    Bindu “Point”; the centre of a yantra; a drop of vital essence
    Cakra “Wheel”; a centre in the subtle body; a ritual circle; a diagram
    Caryāpāda Section of an Āgama on conduct
    Ḍākinī In Buddhist Tantra, a female deity or human practitioner who embodies wisdom and grants or guards the teaching; in Hindu sources, a class of dangerous female spirits
    Dakṣiṇācāra “Right-hand” practice conforming to purity rules
    Dhāraṇā “Fixation”; a limb of yoga; in the Śaiva scriptures, especially the fixation of the mind on the elements and the levels of the cosmos
    Dhāraṇī Buddhist protective or mnemonic formula
    Dhruva The fixed principal image in the sanctum of a Vaikhānasa temple
    Dīkṣā Initiation
    Dūtī Female ritual partner in Kaula rites
    Gāruḍa Tantra Scripture of the eastern stream of the Mantramārga, on the treatment of snakebite and poison
    Heruka Fierce male deity of the Buddhist Yoginītantras, such as Cakrasaṃvara or Hevajra
    Homa Fire offering
    Japa Repeated recitation of a mantra
    Jñānapāda Doctrinal section of an Āgama (also vidyāpāda)
    Kaḷameḻuttu Kerala floor drawing of a deity, especially Bhadrakālī, in coloured powders
    Kālasaṁkarṣaṇī “She who draws in time”; the supreme Kālī of the Jayadrathayāmala and the Krama
    Kālīkula The Kālī-centred scriptures and lineages of the Vidyāpīṭha, including the Krama
    Kañcuka The five “coverings” that limit the soul’s powers
    Kapāla Skull; the skull-bowl of Kāpālika ascetics and fierce deities
    Kaula Tantric traditions of the kula, the clan of goddesses; associated with transgressive ritual
    Kautuka The small “power” image of a Vaikhānasa temple, used for daily worship
    Khaṭvāṅga Skull-topped staff carried by skull-bearing ascetics and fierce deities
    Krama “Sequence”; the Kālī-centred non-dual tradition of Kashmir
    Kriyāpāda Ritual section of an Āgama
    Kubjikā “The hunchbacked one”; goddess of the western transmission of the Kaula traditions, whose cult survives among the Newars
    Kuladevī Lineage goddess of a clan or royal house
    Kumārīpūjā Worship of a young girl as the Goddess
    Kumbhābhiṣeka Consecration of a temple with water from consecrated pots
    Kuṇḍa Fire-pit for homa
    Kuṇḍalinī The coiled power in the subtle body
    Mahāsiddha “Great adept”; Buddhist (and Śaiva) Tantric master
    Mahāvidyā One of ten goddesses forming a late Śākta group
    Mahāvrata “Great observance”; the skull observance of the Lākula and Kāpālika ascetics
    Mala Impurity or bond of the soul
    Maṇḍala Circle; diagram in which a deity and retinue are installed
    Mantra Formula of sacred speech; the deity’s form in sound
    Mantramārga The Tantric Śaivism of initiation and mantra
    Mantrapīṭha “Seat of mantras”; the branch of the Bhairava Tantras centred on Svacchandabhairava
    Maṭha Monastery, often endowed with land and attached to a temple
    Mātṛkā The phonemes of the alphabet as the matrix of mantras and cosmos
    Māyā In Śaivism, the real material cause of the impure worlds; a tattva
    Melaka “Meeting”; the adept’s encounter with the yoginīs that confers power
    Mudrā Ritual gesture; also parched grain or the female partner in Kaula usage
    Nāḍī Channel of the subtle body
    Nātha Order of Śaiva yogis associated with haṭha yoga
    Nirbījadīkṣā “Seedless” initiation that frees the initiate (e.g. a king) from post-initiatory rules
    Nirvāṇadīkṣā Liberating initiation
    Nyāsa Ritual placing of mantras on the body or an image
    Paddhati Ritual manual setting out the procedure of worship, initiation or consecration step by step
    Pañcabera The set of five images (fixed, power, bathing, festival and offering) of a Vaikhānasa temple
    Pañcamakāra The “five Ms” of Kaula ritual: wine, meat, fish, parched grain, sexual union
    Pāñcarātra Tantric Vaiṣṇava tradition and its scriptures (saṃhitās)
    Paśu, Pati, Pāśa Soul, Lord and bond: the three categories of the Śaiva Siddhānta
    Pīṭha Seat; a sacred site of the Goddess
    Prakāśa, Vimarśa The light of consciousness and its reflexive self-awareness
    Prapatti Surrender to the Lord (Śrīvaiṣṇava)
    Pratiṣṭhā Consecration of an image or temple
    Pratyabhijñā “Recognition”; the Kashmiri non-dual philosophy
    Pūjā Worship
    Rājaguru Royal preceptor; in the Śaiva Age often the king’s initiating guru
    Rasa Aesthetic savour
    Sādhaka Initiate who propitiates a mantra to gain powers
    Sādhakī Female practitioner; in early Śākta scriptures, a woman fully initiated to practise for powers
    Sahaja “Innate”; the natural state of awakened mind (Buddhist) or of divine love (Vaiṣṇava Sahajiyā)
    Śakti Power; the Goddess
    Śaktipāta “Descent of power”; grace
    Samaya Rule or vow of initiates
    Siddhi Supernatural power or accomplishment
    Spanda “Vibration”; the dynamism of consciousness
    Śrīcakra The diagram of the goddess Tripurasundarī
    Śrīvidyā The tradition and mantra of Tripurasundarī
    Srotas “Stream”; one of the five classes of Śaiva scripture said to issue from the five faces of Sadāśiva
    Sūrimantra Secret mantra conferred on a Śvetāmbara Jain monk raised to the rank of ācārya
    Svātantrya The absolute freedom of consciousness
    Tantra “Loom”; system; a class of revealed scripture
    Tantrī In Kerala, the hereditary Nampūtiri ritual authority of a temple
    Tattva Principle or level of reality (thirty-six in Śaivism)
    Trika “Triad”; the Kashmiri Śaiva tradition of three goddesses
    Upāya “Means”; in Kashmiri Śaivism, one of the graded ways to liberation (of the individual, of Śakti, of Śiva, and “no means”); in Buddhism, compassionate skill in means
    Upacāra An offering or service in worship
    Utsava image Movable festival image carried in procession
    Vāc Speech; the Word
    Vaikhānasa Hereditary Vaiṣṇava priestly tradition claiming Vedic authority
    Vaikuṇṭha Four-faced image of Viṣṇu with human, lion, boar and fierce faces, prominent in Kashmir
    Vajrayāna “Vehicle of the vajra”; Buddhist Tantra
    Vāmācāra “Left-hand” practice involving the five Ms
    Vāma Tantra Scripture of the northern stream of the Mantramārga, centred on Tumburu and his four sisters
    Vāstupuruṣamaṇḍala The grid diagram underlying temple plans
    Vidyāpīṭha “Seat of female mantras”; the goddess-centred branch of the Bhairava Tantras, including the Trika and Kālī-centred scriptures
    Vimāna Sanctum with its superstructure (South Indian temples)
    Vyūha “Emanation”; one of the four forms (Vāsudeva, Saṁkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha) of the Lord in Pāñcarātra theology
    Yantra Diagram, often metal, serving as the seat of a deity
    Yoginī Powerful female deity of the Kaula and Vidyāpīṭha cults
