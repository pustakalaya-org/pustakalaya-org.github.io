    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Pa. = Pali, Pk. = Prakrit, Ar. = Arabic, Pe. = Persian, Pu. = Punjabi, Ta. = Tamil, Be. = Bengali, Gu. = Gujarati, Hi. = Hindi, Ur. = Urdu).

    Term Meaning
    adhyāsa Superimposition; in Advaita, the mistaking of the non-self for the self
    Advaita “Non-duality”; the Vedānta of Śaṅkara, holding that Brahman alone is ultimately real
    ahiṃsā Non-injury, non-violence
    ajātivāda “Non-origination”; Gauḍapāda’s doctrine that nothing is ever really born
    aloka In Jain cosmology, the infinite empty space beyond the occupied universe
    anātman “Not-self”; the Buddhist teaching that no permanent self is found in the constituents of a person (Pali anattā)
    anattā (Pa.) See anātman
    anekāntavāda The Jain doctrine of the many-sidedness of reality
    ānvīkṣikī “Investigation”; the science of reasoning, in the Arthaśāstra and later
    āpaddharma The rules permitted in times of distress
    aṟam (Ta.) Virtue, dharma; the first book of the Tirukkuṟaḷ
    artha Wealth, power, material well-being; one of the aims of life
    āryikā A Digambara Jain nun
    asaṃkhyāta “Innumerable”; in Jain mathematics, a class of numbers between the countable and the infinite
    āsana A posture; in the Yogasūtra, a steady seat for meditation
    āśrama A stage or mode of life: student, householder, forest-dweller, renouncer
    Ati-Śūdra “Below (or beyond) the Śūdras”; an older term, made central by Phule, for the castes treated as untouchable
    ātman The self
    avadhāna “Attention”; public feats of memory and simultaneous attention, such as the young Śrīmad Rājacandra performed
    avatāra “Descent”; an incarnation of a deity, especially of Viṣṇu
    avidyā Ignorance; in Advaita, the cause of the appearance of multiplicity
    Āyurveda “Knowledge of life”; the classical Indian system of medicine
    Bahujan (Hi.) “The many”; in modern anti-caste usage, the non-elite majority of lower castes
    Bāul (Be.) A wandering devotional singer of Bengal
    bhakti Devotion to a personal deity
    bhavacakra The Buddhist “wheel of becoming” depicting the realms of rebirth
    bhikkhunī (Pa.) A fully ordained Buddhist nun
    bidʿa (Ar.) “Innovation”; a practice without warrant in the Qurʾān and the Prophet’s example, condemned by reformers
    bodhisattva One who seeks awakening for the sake of all beings
    Brahman In the Upaniṣads and Vedānta, the ultimate reality
    brahmāṇḍa “Egg of Brahmā”; a world-system in Purāṇic cosmology
    cakra “Wheel”; a centre in the subtle body in yogic and tantric texts
    cakravartin “Wheel-turner”; the ideal universal monarch
    cetanā (Pa.) Intention; identified by the Buddha with karma
    cuyamariyātai (Ta.) Self-respect; the ideal and the name of Periyar’s movement
    daṇḍa Punishment; the coercive power of the king
    dargāh (Pe.) The shrine and tomb of a Sufi saint
    darśana “View”; a philosophical system; also the seeing of a deity or holy person
    dhammasattha (Pa.) A law book of the Theravāda kingdoms of Southeast Asia
    dharma Law, duty, right order; the Buddha’s teaching; in Jainism also the medium of motion
    dharmasaṅkaṭa A conflict of duties
    dhvani Suggestion; in poetics, meaning conveyed without being stated
    dhyāna Meditation; meditative absorption (Pa. jhāna)
    Digambara “Sky-clad”; the Jain tradition whose monks go naked
    doṣa “Fault”; in Āyurveda, one of the three bodily principles, vāta, pitta and kapha
    dravya Substance; in Jainism, the six constituents of the universe
    Dvaita “Dualism”; the Vedānta of Madhva
    ekaśaraṇa “Refuge in the One”; the devotional order of Śaṅkaradeva in Assam
    fatwā (Ar.) A legal opinion given by a qualified scholar in answer to a question
    gharānā (Hi.) A lineage or school of Hindustani musicians
    guṇa In Sāṃkhya, the three constituents of matter: sattva, rajas, tamas
    guṇasthāna In Jainism, one of the fourteen stages of spiritual progress
    ḥākimiyya (Ar.) Sovereignty; in Mawdūdī’s thought, the exclusive sovereignty of God over law and the state
    haṭhayoga The “yoga of force”, using postures, breath control and seals
    hukam (Pu.) The divine order or command, in Sikh teaching
    ijtihād (Ar.) Independent reasoning from the sources of Islamic law
    jāti Birth-group; caste as an endogamous, often occupational community
    jīva A living being or soul
    jyotiṣa The science of the luminaries: astronomy, calendrics and astrology
    kaivalya “Isolation”; the goal of Sāṃkhya and of Patañjali’s yoga
    kalpa An aeon; in the Purāṇas, a day of Brahmā
    kāma Desire, pleasure, love; one of the aims of life
    karma Action, and its consequences for the agent
    khālsā (Pu.) The community of initiated Sikhs founded by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699
    khudī (Pe.) Selfhood; the central concept of Iqbal’s philosophy
    kṣaṇikavāda The Buddhist doctrine of momentariness
    laṅgar (Pu.) The Sikh community kitchen open to all
    loka A world or realm of existence
    lokapuruṣa The Jain universe imagined as a giant human figure
    madrasa (Ar.) A school of Islamic learning
    mahāvākya “Great sentence”; an Upaniṣadic statement of the identity of self and Brahman
    mahāyuga The great cycle of four ages (4,320,000 years in the Purāṇic reckoning)
    manvantara The period of one Manu; 71 mahāyugas
    maʿqūlāt (Ar.) The “rational” sciences of the madrasa curriculum, as against the transmitted sciences (manqūlāt)
    maṭha A monastery, especially of Hindu renouncers
    māyā Illusion, or the creative power that makes the world appear
    Meru The cosmic mountain at the centre of the world in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain cosmology
    mettā (Pa.) Loving-kindness
    millat (Ar.) A community of faith; distinguished by Ḥusain Aḥmad Madanī from the territorial nation (qaum)
    mīrī–pīrī (Pu.) Temporal and spiritual authority, symbolised by Guru Hargobind’s two swords
    mokṣa Liberation from the round of rebirth
    nafs (Ar.) The self or soul; in Sufism, the lower self to be disciplined
    nām (Pu.) The divine name, on which Sikhs meditate
    Navya-Nyāya The “new reason”; the logic and epistemology founded by Gaṅgeśa
    nēcharī (Ur.) “Naturalist”; a nickname for the followers of Sayyid Ahmad Khan
    nidāna A link in the Buddhist chain of dependent origination
    nirguṇa “Without qualities”; of the divine, beyond all attributes
    nirvāṇa “Blowing out”; the Buddhist goal, the cessation of craving and rebirth
    niścaya-naya In Jain thought, the ultimate standpoint, from which the pure soul is untouched by karma
    pañcāṅga The traditional almanac with its “five limbs”
    pāñjrāpoḷ (Gu.) A Jain refuge and hospital for animals
    pativratā A wife vowed to her husband; the ideal of wifely devotion in the epics and law-books
    pīr (Pe.) A Sufi master or saint
    prakṛti Primordial matter; in Āyurveda, a person’s constitution
    pralaya The dissolution of the world at the end of a cosmic period
    pramā A true cognition, the product of a means of knowledge (pramāṇa)
    pramāṇa A valid means of knowledge
    prasthānatraya The “three foundations” of Vedānta: Upaniṣads, Gītā, Brahmasūtra
    pratītyasamutpāda Dependent origination
    prekṣā dhyāna “Meditation of perception”; a system of meditation developed in the Terāpanthī Jain order from the 1970s
    puruṣa Person; in Sāṃkhya, the conscious self
    puruṣārtha An aim of human life: dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa
    puṣṭimārga “Path of grace”; the devotional tradition of Vallabha
    qaum (Ur.) A people or nation
    qawwālī (Ur.) Devotional song performed at Sufi shrines
    rāga A melodic framework of Indian classical music
    rahit (Pu.) The code of conduct of the Khālsā, codified in the Sikh Rahit Maryādā
    rājadharma The duties of a king
    rasa “Taste” or “relish”; aesthetic emotion
    ṛta Cosmic and moral order in the Ṛgveda
    rūḥ (Ar.) Spirit
    śābdabodha Verbal cognition; the understanding produced by a sentence, as analysed by the grammarians, Mīmāṃsakas and Naiyāyikas
    sāgaropama “Ocean-measure”; a Jain unit of immense time
    sahajdhārī (Pu.) A Sikh who has not taken Khālsā initiation and does not keep uncut hair
    sallekhanā The Jain practice of fasting unto death at the end of life
    samanvaya Coherent connection; harmonious reading; in modern usage, synthesis or harmony
    saṃnyāsa Renunciation; the life of the renouncer (saṃnyāsin)
    saṃsāra The round of rebirth
    saṃskāra A latent impression left by action or experience; also a life-cycle rite
    saṃvāda Dialogue; the title of the record of a dialogue between pandits and philosophers held at Pune in 1983
    saṅkalpa A ritual statement of intention
    sant A devotional poet of the late medieval period, especially of the nirguṇa tradition
    śānta The “tranquil” rasa, admitted by Abhinavagupta and others
    sarnā The sacred grove of the Munda, Oraon and other Adivasi communities of Jharkhand; hence Sarna, a name for their religion
    śāstra A treatise; authoritative teaching on a subject
    sattra In Assam, a monastery and centre of devotion and performance of Śaṅkaradeva’s order
    satya Truth
    satyāgraha “Holding firmly to truth”; Gandhi’s method of non-violent resistance
    śramaṇa A renouncer outside the Vedic fold, such as the Buddhists, Jains and Ājīvikas
    śuddhi “Purification”; the Arya Samaj rite for admitting or readmitting converts to Hinduism
    ṣulḥ-i kull (Pe.) “Peace with all”; Akbar’s policy towards religious communities
    svadharma One’s own duty
    Śvetāmbara “White-clad”; the Jain tradition whose monks and nuns wear white
    tāla A rhythmic cycle in Indian music
    taqlīd (Ar.) “Imitation”; adherence to the rulings of an established school of law
    Tat Khālsā (Pu.) “The true Khālsā”; the Singh Sabha reformers who insisted on a distinct Sikh identity
    tattva A principle or constituent of reality
    tiṇai (Ta.) A poetic landscape in classical Tamil poetics
    tīrthaṅkara “Ford-maker”; one of the twenty-four teachers of the Jain path in each half-cycle
    trivarga The triad of aims: dharma, artha and kāma
    ʿulamāʾ (Ar.) Scholars of Islamic law and theology
    utsarpiṇī, avasarpiṇī The ascending and descending half-cycles of Jain time
    vāda Debate; in Nyāya, honest debate aimed at truth
    varṇa One of the four classes of Brahmanical theory
    vipassanā (Pa.) Insight meditation
    Viśiṣṭādvaita “Qualified non-dualism”; the Vedānta of Rāmānuja
    vyāpti “Pervasion”; the invariable concomitance on which an inference rests
    vyavahāra-naya In Jain thought, the conventional standpoint, from which the soul acts and is bound
    waḥdat al-wujūd (Ar.) “Unity of being”; the metaphysics associated with Ibn ʿArabī
    yama, niyama The restraints and observances, first limbs of Patañjali’s yoga
    yuga One of the four ages: Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali
    zīj (Pe.) A set of astronomical tables in the Islamic tradition
