    Editions, Translations and Further Reading¶

    Editions and translations of the primary texts¶

    The quotations from primary texts in this volume follow the standard editions; readers who wish to check them should use the critical editions and translations listed here (full details in the Select Bibliography). Sanskrit verse and sūtra numbers differ between editions, especially for the Vaiśeṣika Sūtra, the Tattvārthasūtra and the Yoga Sūtra.

    Nyāya Sūtra and Bhāṣya:
    translation with the Vārttika by Jha (Jha 1912–1919).
    Vaiśeṣika Sūtra:
    Candrānanda’s recension, edited by Jambuvijaya (Jambuvijaya 1961).
    Sāṃkhyakārikā:
    text and translation in Larson (Larson 1979).
    Yoga Sūtra and Bhāṣya:
    critical edition of the first chapter by Maas (Maas 2006); translation with commentary by Bryant (Bryant 2009).
    Mīmāṃsā Sūtra and Śābarabhāṣya:
    translation by Jha (Jha 1933–1936).
    Brahmasūtrabhāṣya and Śrībhāṣya:
    translations by Thibaut (Thibaut 1890, 1904).
    Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha:
    translation by Cowell and Gough (Cowell and Gough 1882).
    Tattvopaplavasiṃha:
    edition by Sanghavi and Parikh (Sanghavi and Parikh 1940); study and partial translation by Franco (Franco 1987).
    Mūlamadhyamakakārikā:
    translation by Garfield (Garfield 1995).
    Pramāṇasamuccaya, chapter 1:
    translation by Hattori (Hattori 1968); Nyāyabindu: translation by Stcherbatsky (Stcherbatsky 1930–1932).
    Tattvārthasūtra:
    translation by Tatia (Umāsvāti 1994).
    Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā:
    edition and translation by Torella (Torella 1994); Śivadṛṣṭi, chapters 1–3, by Nemec (Nemec 2011).

    Further reading by chapter¶

    General:
    Matilal (Matilal 1986) and Mohanty (Mohanty 2000) on epistemology; Ganeri’s handbook (Ganeri 2017) for recent scholarship on every school; Daya Krishna (Daya Krishna 1991) for a critique of the received picture; Hiriyanna (Hiriyanna 1932) and Chatterjee and Datta (Chatterjee and Datta 1939) for the older standard surveys.
    Introduction:
    Halbfass (Halbfass 1988) and Nicholson (Nicholson 2010) on the history of the “six systems”; Basham (Basham 1951) on the Ājīvikas.
    Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika:
    Potter (Potter 1977) for the texts; Matilal (Matilal 1968) and Ingalls (Ingalls 1951) on Navya-Nyāya; Ganeri (Ganeri 2011) on the early-modern logicians; Halbfass (Halbfass 1992) on the categories.
    Sāṃkhya and Yoga:
    Larson (Larson 1979); Larson and Bhattacharya (Larson and Bhattacharya 1987); Maas (Maas 2013) on the date and unity of the Yogaśāstra.
    Mīmāṃsā and the Grammarians:
    Clooney (Clooney 1990) on Jaimini; Taber (Taber 2005) on Kumārila; Coward and Kunjunni Raja (Coward and Kunjunni Raja 1990) on the Grammarians.
    Vedānta:
    Deutsch (Deutsch 1969) and Ram-Prasad (Ram-Prasad 2002) on Advaita; Lipner (Lipner 1986) and Carman (Carman 1974) on Rāmānuja; Sharma (Sharma 1962) on Madhva; Stoker (Stoker 2016) and Gupta (Gupta 2006) on the late controversy.
    Cārvāka:
    Bhattacharya (Bhattacharya 2011) and Franco (Franco 1987).
    Buddhist philosophy:
    Siderits (Siderits 2007) and Williams (Williams and Tribe 2000); Westerhoff (Westerhoff 2009) on Nāgārjuna; Dunne (Dunne 2004) on Dharmakīrti; Arnold (Arnold 2005) on the Buddhist–Mīmāṃsā debate.
    Jain philosophy:
    Dundas (Dundas 2002), Jaini (Jaini 1979) and Matilal (Matilal 1981).
    Śaiva philosophy:
    Sanderson (Sanderson 1988); Watson (Watson 2006) and Goodall (Goodall 1998) on the Siddhānta; Dhavamony (Dhavamony 1971) on its Tamil form; Ratié (Ratié 2011) and Lawrence (Lawrence 1999) on the Pratyabhijñā.
    Comparison and debate:
    Matilal (Matilal 1998), Phillips (Phillips 2012) and Solomon (Solomon 1976–1978).
