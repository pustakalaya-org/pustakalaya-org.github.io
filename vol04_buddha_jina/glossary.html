    Glossary¶

    Term Meaning
    Abhidhamma (Skt. Abhidharma) “Higher teaching”; systematic analysis of experience into its constituents; the third basket of the canon
    Abhidharmakośa Vasubandhu’s “Treasury of Abhidharma”, the standard Abhidharma manual outside the Theravāda
    Abhiṣeka Consecration; the initiation that admits a disciple to tantric practice
    Āgama “Tradition”; the Jain canonical scriptures; also the early Buddhist discourse collections in Sanskrit and Chinese
    Ahiṃsā Non-harming; for Jains the supreme ethical principle
    Ājīvika Śramaṇa movement whose best-known leader was Makkhali Gosāla, teaching strict determinism
    Anattā (Skt. anātman) Non-self; the absence of a permanent self in the components of a person
    Anekāntavāda Jain doctrine of the many-sidedness of reality
    Anicca (Skt. anitya) Impermanence
    Aṇuvrata “Lesser vow”; the lay form of the five great vows in Jainism
    Anuyoga “Exposition”; one of the four Digambara classes of scripture (narrative, cosmography and calculation, conduct, ontology)
    Apabhraṃśa The literary form of the late Middle Indo-Aryan vernaculars, much used by Jain poets
    Arahant (Skt. arhat) “Worthy one”; one who has attained liberation; in Jainism, an omniscient teacher
    Āryikā A Digambara nun of the highest grade, wearing a single white sari
    Āsrava In Jainism, the influx of karmic matter into the soul
    Bhaṇḍāra Jain manuscript library
    Bhikkhu, bhikkhunī Buddhist monk, nun
    Bodhisattva Being bound for awakening; in the Mahāyāna, one who vows to become a Buddha for the sake of all beings
    Brahmavihāra The four “divine abodes”: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity
    Caityavāsī “Temple-dweller”; a medieval Śvetāmbara monk living permanently in a temple, opposed by the reformers who founded the later gacchas
    Cāturmāsa The four-month rainy-season halt of Jain mendicants
    Cetanā Intention; for Buddhists, what constitutes karma
    Dāna Giving, generosity
    Devadravya The fund of a Jain temple, which may be spent only on temples and images
    Dhamma (Skt. dharma) The teaching; truth; also a constituent of experience; in Jain ontology dharma is the medium of motion
    Digambara “Sky-clad”; the Jain tradition whose fully initiated monks go naked
    Dīkṣā Initiation; in Jainism, the lifelong renunciation by which one becomes a monk or nun
    Dravya Substance; Jainism recognises six
    Dukkha (Skt. duḥkha) Suffering, unsatisfactoriness
    Gaccha A Śvetāmbara monastic lineage, such as the Tapā or Kharatara Gaccha
    Gaṇadhara One of the chief disciples of a Tīrthaṅkara; Mahāvīra had eleven, led by Indrabhūti Gautama
    Garudhamma The eight “weighty rules” subordinating Buddhist nuns to monks
    Gocarī “Grazing”; the Śvetāmbara alms round, taking a little food from each of several households
    Guṇasthāna One of the fourteen Jain stages of spiritual development
    Hetucakra Dignāga’s “wheel of reasons”, a classification of reasons in inference
    Jātaka Story of a previous life of the Buddha
    Jhāna (Skt. dhyāna) Meditative absorption
    Jina “Victor”; a Tīrthaṅkara; the source of the word “Jain”
    Jīva, ajīva Soul, non-soul (Jain ontology)
    Kālacakra “Wheel of time”; (1) a late Buddhist tantra (11th c.); (2) in Jainism, the cycle of cosmic time, made up of an ascending (utsarpiṇī) and a descending (avasarpiṇī) half
    Karuṇā Compassion
    Kaṭhina The ceremony after the rains retreat at which lay people give the Saṅgha cloth to be made into a robe in a single day
    Kevala-jñāna Omniscience, attained when the “destructive” karmas are destroyed; retained by the soul in liberation
    Madhyamaka “Middle Way” school of Nāgārjuna
    Mahāsiddha “Great adept”; one of the tantric masters of eastern India, traditionally eighty-four
    Mahāvrata One of the five great vows of Jain mendicants
    Mahāyāna “Great Vehicle”; Buddhist movement centred on the bodhisattva path
    Maṇḍala Circle; a sacred diagram used in tantric ritual and meditation
    Mettā (Skt. maitrī) Loving-kindness, friendliness
    Mokṣa Liberation from rebirth
    Muhpattī The mouth-cloth of Śvetāmbara mendicants
    Nayavāda Jain theory of standpoints
    Nibbāna (Skt. nirvāṇa) The ending of craving, suffering and rebirth
    Nirjarā In Jainism, the shedding of accumulated karma
    Niryukti The earliest layer of Prakrit verse commentary on the Śvetāmbara canon
    Pabbajjā (Skt. pravrajyā) “Going forth”; renunciation of the household life; the ordination of a novice
    Pañcakalyāṇaka The five auspicious events of a Tīrthaṅkara’s life; the consecration rite of an image that re-enacts them
    Pāramī, pāramitā Perfection; virtues perfected by a bodhisattva
    Parinirvāṇa The final passing of a Buddha
    Paritta Protective chanting of selected suttas by monks
    Pātimokkha (Skt. prātimokṣa) Code of monastic rules recited fortnightly
    Paṭiccasamuppāda (Skt. pratītyasamutpāda) Dependent origination
    Pavāraṇā The “invitation” at the end of the rains retreat, at which each monk asks to be told of his faults
    Piñchī The peacock-feather whisk of Digambara mendicants
    Piṭaka “Basket”; division of the Buddhist canon
    Prabandha “Composition”; a Jain genre of historical anecdote about kings, ministers, poets and teachers, chiefly from medieval Gujarat
    Prajñā (Pāli paññā) Wisdom
    Pramāṇa Means of valid knowledge
    Pratikramaṇa Jain rite of confession and repentance
    Pudgala Matter (Jain ontology)
    Puñña (Skt. puṇya) Merit; wholesome action that brings happiness and good rebirth
    Rajoharaṇa The woollen whisk of Śvetāmbara mendicants, used to brush away small creatures
    Śalākāpuruṣa One of the sixty-three “illustrious persons” of Jain universal history
    Sallekhanā Jain vow of fasting to death when death is near
    Sāmaṇera A Buddhist novice, keeping ten precepts
    Samiti, gupti The five “carefulnesses” and three “controls” that protect the Jain mendicant’s vows
    Saṃsāra The cycle of birth and death
    Saṅgha The Buddhist monastic community; in Jainism the fourfold community
    Santhārā Another name for sallekhanā
    Saptabhaṅgī The Jain seven-fold predication
    Sarvāstivāda Early Buddhist school holding that dharmas exist in past, present and future
    Śāsanadevatā A guardian deity attending a Tīrthaṅkara, who unlike the Jina may grant worldly help
    Sautrāntika Buddhist school that denied the existence of past and future dharmas
    Śramaṇa Wandering renunciant striving for liberation
    Stūpa Reliquary mound
    Śūnyatā Emptiness; the absence of intrinsic nature
    Sutta (Skt. sūtra) Discourse of the Buddha; in Sanskrit also an aphoristic text
    Śvetāmbara “White-clad”; the Jain tradition whose mendicants wear white
    Syādvāda Jain doctrine of conditional predication
    Taṇhā (Skt. tṛṣṇā) Craving, thirst
    Tārā “Saviour”; the most widely worshipped Buddhist goddess, a protector from the “eight great perils”
    Tathāgata “Thus-gone”; epithet of the Buddha
    Tathāgatagarbha Buddha-nature, the potential for awakening in all beings
    Tattva Category of reality; Jainism recognises seven (or nine)
    Theravāda “Teaching of the Elders”; the Buddhist school of Sri Lanka and mainland Southeast Asia
    Tīrthaṅkara “Ford-maker”; one of the twenty-four Jain teachers of each half-cycle
    Tipiṭaka (Skt. Tripiṭaka) The “three baskets” of the Buddhist canon
    Triratna The “three jewels”: for Buddhists, Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha; for Jains, right faith, knowledge and conduct
    Upāsaka, upāsikā Buddhist lay follower (male, female)
    Upasampadā Higher ordination as a Buddhist monk or nun, conferred by a formal act of the Saṅgha
    Upāśraya A hall provided by the laity in which Śvetāmbara mendicants stay
    Upāya Skill in means
    Uposatha The fortnightly observance day on which the monastic code is recited
    Utsarpiṇī, avasarpiṇī The ascending and descending halves of the Jain cycle of cosmic time, each of six ages (ara)
    Vajrayāna “Diamond Vehicle”; tantric Buddhism
    Vassa The three-month rains retreat of Buddhist monks and nuns
    Vihāra Monastery
    Vinaya Monastic discipline; the first basket of the canon
    Vipassanā (Skt. vipaśyanā) Insight meditation
    Yati A settled Śvetāmbara cleric of the early modern period, owning property and serving lay families
    Yogācāra Mahāyāna school of Asaṅga and Vasubandhu, analysing consciousness
