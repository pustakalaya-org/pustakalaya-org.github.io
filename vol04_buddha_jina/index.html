    Preface¶

    This is Volume 4 of the series Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā: Buddha–Jina Prabhā: Buddhist and Jain Traditions. It introduces the two great Śramaṇa traditions of India as knowledge systems — their teachers and scriptures, their philosophy and logic, their meditative and ethical disciplines, their cosmology and mathematics, their monasteries and libraries, and their art and literature. It is written for undergraduates and teachers in courses on Indian philosophy, religion and Indian Knowledge Systems, and for general readers who want a reliable single-volume account of both traditions.

    The volume tries to be both respectful and historically careful. Dates are given according to current scholarship, with traditional dates identified as such; thus the Buddha is placed in the fifth century BCE (c. 480–400 BCE on the “short chronology”), as is Mahāvīra. Quotations from primary texts are limited to passages whose wording and reference could be checked, and claims that ancient texts “anticipated” modern science or mathematics have been avoided. Chapter 7 includes an account of the decline of Buddhism in India; Chapter 12 surveys Jain grammar, lexicography, astronomy, historical writing and literature; Chapter 13 describes how monks, nuns and lay people lived these religions in practice; and Chapter 14 discusses their interactions, art and literature, caste and gender, and modern history. A select bibliography of standard works is provided for further reading.

    The book is an introduction, not a substitute for the specialist literature. Readers are encouraged to follow the references to the translations and studies on which it relies.

    Nikhil Kumar Rajput
    Ramanujan College, University of Delhi
    Guru Pūrṇimā, 2026
