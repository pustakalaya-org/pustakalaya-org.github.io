    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate and many are disputed; where scholarly opinion varies widely, a broad range is given. Traditional dates are labelled as such.

    Date Texts, persons and events
    3rd millennium BCE Harappan figurines, including the Mohenjo-daro bronze popularly called the “dancing girl”
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Hymns of the Ṛgveda, recited with pitch accents
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts; the Sāmaveda chant traditions take shape
    2nd–1st c. BCE Reliefs of musicians and dancers at Bharhut and Sanchi; Hāthīgumphā inscription of Khāravela
    c. 200 BCE–200 CE Main body of the Nāṭyaśāstra ascribed to Bharata
    early centuries CE Dattila, Dattilam; Tamil Caṅkam poems on bards and dancers
    before Kālidāsa Bhāsa (Trivandrum plays, attribution debated)
    c. 3rd–5th c. Śūdraka, Mṛcchakaṭika
    c. 335–375 Reign of Samudragupta, shown playing a vīṇā on his coins
    4th–5th c. Kālidāsa
    c. 5th–6th c. Cilappatikāram (date debated)
    6th–9th c. Tamil Nāyaṉmārs and Āḻvārs; the Tēvāram hymns
    2nd half of 1st millennium Mataṅga, Bṛhaddeśī, first known definition of rāga
    606–647 Reign of Harṣa, author of three plays
    early 8th c. Bhavabhūti
    late 8th c. Dāmodaragupta, Kuṭṭanīmata, describing a temple performance of Harṣa’s Ratnāvalī
    10th c. (traditional) Nāthamuni compiles the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham
    c. 950–1016 Abhinavagupta; Abhinavabhāratī commentary on the Nāṭyaśāstra
    late 10th c. Dhanañjaya, Daśarūpaka
    996 Tabo monastery founded in Spiti
    c. 1010 Completion of the Bṛhadīśvara temple, Thanjavur; Rājarāja I’s inscription on temple women
    10th–12th c. Classic Cōḻa bronzes of Śiva Naṭarāja
    r. c. 1097–1147 Nānyadeva of Mithilā, Bharatabhāṣya
    1131 Someśvara III, Mānasollāsa
    12th c. Jayadeva, Gītagovinda
    12th c. Rāmacandra and Guṇacandra, Nāṭyadarpaṇa; Pārśvadeva, Saṅgītasamayasāra (12th–13th c.)
    1st half of 13th c. Śārṅgadeva, Saṅgītaratnākara, at Devagiri
    date uncertain Abhinayadarpaṇa, ascribed to Nandikeśvara (proposals range from the early centuries CE to the 13th c.)
    1253–1325 Amīr Khusrau
    late 13th c. Jñāneśvar; Nāmdev (traditionally 1270–1350); the Vārkarī tradition of Pandharpur
    1350 Sudhākalaśa, Saṅgītopaniṣatsāroddhāra
    1374–75 Ghunyat al-Munya, earliest known Persian treatise on Indian music
    1408–1503 Annamācārya
    1428 Saṅgītaśiromaṇi
    c. 1430–1446 Kallinātha, Kalānidhi, at Vijayanagara
    r. 1433–1468 Rāṇā Kumbha of Mewar, Saṅgītarāja
    15th c. Kabīr; Aruṇagirinātar, Tiruppugaḻ
    1449–1568 (traditional) Śaṅkaradeva, founder of Assamese Vaiṣṇavism and of the sattra arts
    1469–1539 Guru Nānak, with the rabāb player Bhāī Mardānā
    1479–1531 Vallabhācārya, founder of the Puṣṭimārga; the aṣṭachāp poets (16th c.)
    1484–1564 Purandaradāsa
    1486–1533 Caitanya; congregational kīrtan in Bengal
    r. 1486–1516 Mān Siṃh Tomar of Gwalior, patron of dhrupad
    1550 Rāmāmātya, Svaramelakalānidhi
    late 16th c. Puṇḍarīka Viṭṭhala; the Kheturi festival and the padāvalī kīrtan style (traditional)
    d. 1589 Tānsen, singer at Akbar’s court
    1604 Compilation of the Ādi Granth by Guru Arjan
    1609 Somanātha, Rāgavibodha
    17th c. Tukārām; Ahobala, Saṅgītapārijāta
    mid-17th c. Veṅkaṭamakhin, Caturdaṇḍīprakāśikā; Kṛṣṇanāṭṭam (Mānavēdan) and Rāmanāṭṭam in Kerala
    17th c. (traditional) Azan Fakir and the zikir songs of Assam
    1666 Faqīrullāh, Rāg Darpan
    r. 1669–1698 Anūp Siṃh of Bikaner, patron of Bhāvabhaṭṭa
    1680–81 Tawang monastery founded
    r. 1719–1748 Muhammad Shāh; rise of khayāl (Niyamat Khān “Sadāraṅg”)
    18th c. Emergence of the sitār and the tablā; Bhāgyacandra and the Manipuri Rāsalīlā; Govindācārya’s naming of the 72 melas
    18th c. Kuñcan Nampyār creates tuḷḷal in Kerala
    1741–1839 Rāmnidhi Gupta (Nidhu Babu), Bengali ṭappā
    1762–1827; 1767–1847; 1775–1835 Śyāmā Śāstri; Tyāgarāja; Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar
    1774–1864 Vedanāyakam Śāstri, Tamil Christian kīrtanai
    1784 William Jones, “On the Musical Modes of the Hindus” (published 1792)
    r. 1798–1832 Serfoji II of Thanjavur; the Thanjavur Quartet
    1813 Muḥammad Riẓā, Naghmāt-i Āṣafī
    1813–1846 Svāti Tirunāḷ of Travancore
    1828 Rammohan Roy founds the Brahmo Sabha; Bengali brahmasaṅgīt
    1834 N. A. Willard, A Treatise on the Music of Hindoostan
    1841 Welsh Calvinistic Methodist mission at Sohra; beginning of Khasi hymnody
    r. 1847–1856 Wājid ʿAlī Shāh of Awadh; ṭhumrī and Lucknow Kathak
    1847–1903 Tanjore Krishna Bhagavatar, harikathā
    1861–1941 Rabindranath Tagore; more than two thousand songs (Rabīndrasaṅgīt)
    1871 S. M. Tagore founds the Bengal Music School
    1880 Annasaheb Kirloskar, Saṅgīt Śākuntal: the Marathi musical drama begins
    1890s Beginning of the anti-nautch campaigns
    1899–1976 Kazi Nazrul Islam
    1901 V. D. Paluskar founds the Gandharva Mahavidyalaya
    1902 First commercial recordings of Indian performers in Calcutta; Gauhar Jan
    1909–1912 Discovery and publication of the “Trivandrum plays” by T. Gaṇapati Śāstrī
    1910–1932 V. N. Bhatkhande, Lakṣyasaṅgītam (1910) and Hindustānī Saṅgīt Paddhati
    1911 Jana-gaṇa-mana first sung at the Calcutta Congress; Saṅgīt Mānāpamān
    1916 First All-India Music Conference, Baroda
    1928 Madras Music Academy founded
    1930 Kerala Kalamandalam founded by Vallathol Narayana Menon
    1930s Reconstructed concert dance of Tamil Nadu named Bharatanāṭyam
    1935 Playback, usually dated to Bhāgya Cakra and Dhoop Chhaon; Joymatī, the first Assamese film
    1936 Rukmini Devi Arundale founds Kalakshetra
    1936–1939 Tagore’s dance-dramas Citrāṅgadā, Caṇḍālikā and Śyāmā
    1940–1971 The harmonium kept off the air by All India Radio
    1947 Madras Devadasis (Prevention of Dedication) Act
    1950s Reconstruction of Odissi (Jayantika)
    1952–53 Sangeet Natak Akademi founded
    1952 Binaca Geetmala begins on Radio Ceylon
    1954 Song and Drama Division of the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting
    1955–56 Gīt Rāmāyaṇ broadcast from All India Radio, Pune
    1957 Vividh Bharati, the popular channel of All India Radio
    1959 Tibetan Institute of Performing Arts founded in exile
    1961 Chhayanaut founded in Dhaka
    1963 First mobile (bhrāmyamān) theatre company in Assam
    1964 Kathak Kendra, Delhi
    1976 First Hundred Drums Wangala festival near Tura
    1977 Koothu-p-pattarai founded in Chennai
    1985 Nazrul Institute founded in Dhaka
    1992 Suman Chattopadhyay, Tomāke cāi; the Bengali “songs about life”
    2000 Sattriya recognised as a classical dance by the Sangeet Natak Akademi
    2001 Kūṭiyāṭṭam proclaimed a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity by UNESCO
    2001 Copyright in Tagore’s works lapses at the end of the year
    2004 Kuvempu’s Jaya Bhārata jananiya tanujāte declared the state song of Karnataka
    2008 Rāmlīlā inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List (proclaimed 2005)
