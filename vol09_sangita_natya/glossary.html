    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Ta. = Tamil, Te. = Telugu, Mal. = Malayalam, Hi. = Hindi/Hindustani, Ur. = Urdu/Persian, Ar. = Arabic, As. = Assamese, Mn. = Meitei/Manipuri, Pj. = Punjabi, Mr. = Marathi, Ka. = Kannada, Be. = Bengali, Ks. = Kashmiri, Mz. = Mizo, Or. = Odia, Tib. = Tibetan). Chapter references point to the main discussion.

    Term Meaning
    abhaṅg (Mr.) Marathi devotional song in the “unbroken” metre, sung above all by the Vārkarīs (Ch. 9)
    abhinaya Acting, representation; of four kinds: bodily (āṅgika), verbal (vācika), costume and make-up (āhārya) and involuntary or psychophysical (sāttvika) (Ch. 3)
    aḍavu (Ta.) Basic unit of movement in Bharatanāṭyam, combining stance, footwork and arm position
    ādhunik gān (Be.) “Modern song”: the Bengali popular song written by lyricists and composers for the gramophone and radio from the 1930s (Ch. 11)
    ākhar (Be.) Improvised lines inserted by the singer of padāvalī kīrtan to draw out the meaning of a song
    akṣara (Karnatak) The unit of time, the “beat” of a tāla; in Hindustani music mātrā (Ch. 5)
    ālāp (Hi.), ālāpana Unmetred, improvised exposition of a rāga at the start of a performance
    alārippu (Ta.) Short opening item of pure dance in the Bharatanāṭyam recital (Ch. 15)
    aṅgahāra Sequence of karaṇas; the Nāṭyaśāstra lists thirty-two
    anubhāva “Consequent”: the outward, visible sign of an emotion
    anupallavi (Te., Ta.) Second section of a kṛti, usually rising to the upper register (Ch. 8)
    araimaṇḍi (Ta.) The half-seated basic stance of Bharatanāṭyam, knees bent and turned outwards (Ch. 15)
    āroha, avaroha The ascending and descending note-sequences of a rāga
    asaṃyuta hasta Single-hand gesture (Ch. 14)
    aṣṭachāp (Hi.) The “eight seals”: eight poets of the Puṣṭimārga, among them Sūrdās, whose songs are sung in havelī worship
    āṭṭaprakāram (Mal.) Acting manual of Kūṭiyāṭṭam, prescribing the elaboration of each act (Ch. 19)
    āvartan (Hi.), āvartanam (Karnatak) One complete cycle of a tāla
    bandiś (Hi.) A composition in khayāl or instrumental music
    bāṇi (Ta.) Style or lineage of a teacher or family in Bharatanāṭyam
    bhāgavata (Ka.) Singer-director of Yakṣagāna, who sings the text and controls the performance (Ch. 20)
    bhajan, bhajana Devotional song; a session of congregational devotional singing (Ch. 9)
    bhāṅgā gān (Be.) “Adapted song”: a song of Tagore that keeps the melody and metre of a Hindustani composition with new Bengali words (Ch. 11)
    bhāva Emotional state; the raw material from which rasa arises
    bhāvgīt (Mr.) Lyric song: a setting of a modern Marathi poem for voice and small ensemble, a main genre of radio light music (Ch. 11)
    Bihu (As.) The three agricultural festivals of Assam; the songs and dances of the spring festival (Ch. 22)
    bol (Hi.) Spoken syllables representing drum strokes or dance steps
    borgīt (As.) Devotional songs of Śaṅkaradeva and Mādhavadeva, sung in Sattriya
    cakkar (Hi.) Spin or pirouette in Kathak
    caraṇam (Te., Ta.) Stanza of a kṛti, often carrying the composer’s signature (Ch. 8)
    cārī Movement of a single foot and leg; the Nāṭyaśāstra lists thirty-two, sixteen earthly and sixteen aerial
    cauka (Or.) The square, wide-kneed basic stance of Odissi; with tribhaṅga, one of its two basic positions (Ch. 17)
    ’cham (Tib.) Masked ritual dance performed by monks at the festivals of Tibetan Buddhist monasteries (Ch. 22)
    cheraw (Mz.) Mizo dance in which dancers step between pairs of bamboo poles clapped together by seated men
    Chhau Dance-drama of Jharkhand, West Bengal and Odisha in three regional styles, two of them masked (Ch. 21)
    dāphā (Newari) Devotional singing groups of the Newar towns of Nepal, singing in named rāgas and tālas
    dāsiyāṭṭam, sadir (Ta.) Names of the court and temple dance of Tamil Nadu before it was renamed Bharatanāṭyam in the 1930s
    dbyangs (Tib.) Melodic chant of the Tibetan Buddhist liturgy, written in a graphic notation (dbyangs yig) (Ch. 22)
    deśī “Regional” music and dance, contrasted with mārga
    devadāsī “Servant of the god”: a woman dedicated to a temple deity, often a dancer and singer; a later umbrella term for several groups of temple and court women (Ch. 15)
    dharmī Mode of representation: lokadharmī (realistic) or nāṭyadharmī (stylised, theatrical)
    dhātu A section of a composed song (prabandha): udgrāha, melāpaka, dhruva, ābhoga (Ch. 6)
    dhrupad (Hi.) Austere vocal genre of north Indian court music, prominent from the 15th century
    dhruvā Songs sung during a Sanskrit play, according to the Nāṭyaśāstra
    dotorā (As., Be.) Long-necked plucked lute of Bengal and western Assam, used in Bāul and Goālpārīyā song
    dṛṣṭi Glance; movement of the eyes
    drutam Section of a Karnatak tāla of two beats, a clap and a wave (symbol O) (Ch. 5)
    dung chen (Tib.) Long telescopic copper trumpet of the Tibetan monastic orchestra, played in pairs
    eḍuppu (Ta.) The point in the tāla cycle at which a composition begins, and to which improvisations return (Ch. 5)
    gamaka Ornament: an oscillation or inflection of pitch, essential to Karnatak music
    gaṇasaṅgīt (Be.) “Mass song”: choral political song of the Indian People’s Theatre Association and its successors (Ch. 11)
    gāndharva The sacred, rule-bound music of the Nāṭyaśāstra and Dattilam
    gati, naḍai (Ta.) Subdivision of the beat into three, four, five, seven or nine units (Ch. 5)
    gauracandrikā (Be.) Song about Caitanya that opens a padāvalī kīrtan performance and sets its mood
    gharānā (Hi.) “Household”: a hereditary lineage and style of Hindustani music or Kathak
    gotipua (Or.) Boy dancers of Odisha who danced in female costume outside the temple; one source of modern Odissi (Ch. 17)
    grāma One of the two (theoretically three) ancient parent scales, ṣaḍja and madhyama
    guru–śiṣya paramparā The lineage of teacher and pupil through which the arts are transmitted
    harikathā Narrative performance combining story-telling, exposition and song by a single performer with accompanists
    hasta, mudrā Hand gesture (Ch. 14)
    havelī saṅgīt (Hi.) Temple music of the Puṣṭimārga in dhrupad and dhamār style, ordered by the hours and festivals of worship
    jāgar (Hi.) “Awakening”: a ritual of the central Himalaya in which singing and drumming bring a deity to possess a medium (Ch. 22)
    janya A rāga “born” from a parent mela in Karnatak music
    jāti (1) Ancient melodic type of the Nāṭyaśāstra, ancestor of the rāga; (2) in tāla, the class of a laghu by its number of beats; (3) in dance, a rhythmic passage of syllables
    jati (Ta.) Rhythmic passage of pure dance performed to syllables recited by the dance master (Ch. 13)
    jhālā (Hi.) Fast concluding section of an instrumental ālāp, using the drone strings
    kaḷarippayaṯṯu (Mal.) The martial art of Kerala, whose exercises are part of the training of Kathakaḷi actors (Ch. 18)
    kalāwant (Hi.) Hereditary court singer of dhrupad
    kalpana svara Improvised passages sung to the names of the notes, each returning to the starting point of the composition (Ch. 8)
    karaṇa Unit of dance combining positions of feet, legs, torso and hands; the Nāṭyaśāstra lists 108
    khālī (Hi.) The “empty” section of a Hindustani tāla, marked by a wave of the hand; cf. tālī (Ch. 5)
    khayāl (Hi., from Ar.) “Imagination”: the leading vocal genre of Hindustani music since the 18th century
    kīrtan, kīrtana Devotional song; in Karnatak music a composition; in Sikh worship the singing of scripture
    korvai (Ta.) A composed rhythmic cadence of several phrases, each stated three times, used by drummers and dancers (Ch. 5)
    kṛti The principal composition form of Karnatak music: pallavi, anupallavi, caraṇam
    kutapa The ensemble of musicians in the theatre of the Nāṭyaśāstra, seated between the doors of the tiring-room (Ch. 2)
    Kūṭiyāṭṭam (Mal.) The Sanskrit theatre of the Kerala temples, the only living tradition of Sanskrit performance (Ch. 19)
    kūttampalam (Mal.) Temple theatre of Kerala in which Kūṭiyāṭṭam is performed
    laghu Section of a Karnatak tāla of variable length, a clap followed by finger-counts (symbol I) (Ch. 5)
    lakṣaṇa, lakṣya Theoretical definition and observed practice; the distinction by which theorists explained the gap between the texts and current music (Ch. 6)
    lāsya The graceful, delicate style of dance, associated with Pārvatī
    laya Tempo; the flow of time in music
    layakārī (Hi.) “Rhythm-making”: the transformation of the density of notes or syllables against the pulse (Ch. 5)
    lengkhawm (Mz.) Mizo practice of singing hymns in an indigenous style, slowly and with the drum, at gatherings and wakes (Ch. 22)
    madhyama Fourth note (ma); cf. madhya, the middle register or tempo (Karnatak madhyama kāla, the middle speed)
    mahārī (Or.) Women dedicated to the Jagannātha temple at Puri, who sang and danced in its rituals (Ch. 17)
    majlis (Ar., Ur.) Assembly; especially the Shia mourning assembly of Muḥarram, with soz, marsiya and nauḥa
    maṇḍala Combination of cārīs; also a stance in dance
    mārga “The path”: the ancient, codified art, contrasted with deśī
    mārgam (Ta.) The sequence of items in a Bharatanāṭyam recital
    mātrā (Hi.) The unit of time, the “beat” of a Hindustani tāla (Ch. 5)
    mela, melakarta Parent scale in Karnatak music; the classification has 72
    miḻāvu (Mal.) Large copper pot drum accompanying Kūṭiyāṭṭam
    mirāsī (Hi., Pj.) Hereditary community of Muslim musicians and genealogists in north India
    mōrā (Ta.) A short rhythmic phrase stated three times to arrive on the first beat or the starting point (Ch. 5)
    mṛdaṅgam (Ta.) The double-headed barrel drum of Karnatak music and Bharatanāṭyam (Ch. 10)
    mūrchanā Modal series obtained by starting a grāma on each of its notes in turn (Ch. 4)
    nāda Sound; nāda-brahman, sound as the absolute
    nāmghar (As.) Village prayer hall of Assamese Vaiṣṇavism, the setting of congregational singing
    naṭṭuvaṉār (Ta.) Hereditary dance master who teaches and conducts a Bharatanāṭyam recital
    nāṭya Drama; the total art of the theatre
    nāṭyasaṅgīt (Mr.) The songs of the Marathi musical drama, mostly based on Hindustani compositions (Ch. 11)
    Nazrulgīti (Be.) The songs of Kazi Nazrul Islam (Ch. 11)
    nepathyagṛha The tiring-room behind the stage in the playhouse of the Nāṭyaśāstra (Ch. 2)
    niraval (Ta.) Improvised melodic variation on a single line of the text of a composition, keeping its place in the tāla (Ch. 8)
    nirvahaṇam (Mal.) In Kūṭiyāṭṭam, the retrospective narration in gesture of the events leading up to an act
    nṛtta Pure dance, without representational meaning
    nṛtya Expressive dance, interpreting words and emotions
    ojāpāli (As.) Assamese narrative performance by a leader (ojā) and a chorus with cymbals (pāli) (Ch. 22)
    padam (Ta., Te.) Slow love song interpreted through abhinaya
    pakhāvaj (Hi.) The double-headed barrel drum of dhrupad, havelī saṅgīt and Kathak (Ch. 10)
    pallavi (Te., Ta.) The refrain and first section of a kṛti; also the single composed line of a rāgam–tānam–pallavi (Ch. 8)
    paṇ (Ta.) Melodic mode of early Tamil music and of the Tēvāram hymns
    pena (Mn.) Meitei fiddle with a coconut-shell body and a bow hung with bells, played in the Lai Haraoba
    prabandha Composed song with text and tāla, as described in the medieval treatises
    prasaṅga (Ka.) The sung text of a Yakṣagāna play
    pūrvaraṅga The ritual preliminaries before a Sanskrit play
    qawwālī (Ur.) Devotional song of the Sufi assemblies of South Asia, especially the Chishtī order
    Rabīndrasaṅgīt (Be.) The songs of Rabindranath Tagore, words and music (Ch. 11)
    rāga Melodic framework defined by notes, characteristic phrases, ornaments and mood
    rāgamālā “Garland of rāgas”: series of paintings or verses personifying rāgas
    raṅgapīṭha, raṅgaśīrṣa The front and rear parts of the stage in the playhouse of the Nāṭyaśāstra (Ch. 2)
    rasa Aesthetic experience, the “taste” of an emotion as savoured by the spectator
    rūpaka Drama; the ten major types (daśarūpaka) of Sanskrit theory
    sahṛdaya “Of like heart”: the sensitive, cultivated spectator
    sam (Hi.) First and principal beat of a tāla cycle
    samāʿ (Ar.) “Listening”: Sufi assembly for music and devotion
    saṃvādī (Hi.) See vādī
    saṃyuta hasta Combined (two-hand) gesture
    sañcāribhāva Transitory emotional state; also vyabhicāribhāva; thirty-three in the Nāṭyaśāstra
    saṅgīta Music; in the treatises, song, instrumental music and dance together
    sattra (As.) Vaiṣṇava monastery of Assam, home of Sattriya dance
    sopāna saṅgītam (Mal.) Kerala temple singing beside the steps of the sanctum, chiefly of the Gītagovinda, with the iṭakka drum
    soz (Ur.) Lament sung without instruments to a rāga-based melody in the Shia majlis
    śruti Microtonal interval; the ancient system divides the octave into twenty-two unequal śrutis
    sthāyī, antarā (Hi.) The first and second parts of a Hindustani composition, the second rising to the upper tonic (Ch. 7)
    sthāyibhāva Durable or dominant emotion; eight in the Nāṭyaśāstra
    sūfiyāna kalām (Ks.) The Sufi art music of Kashmir, sung in maqāms with santūr, sehtār and sāz (Ch. 22)
    sugam saṅgīt (Hi.), sugama saṅgīta (Ka.) “Light music”: the modern lyric song as a radio and concert genre (Ch. 11)
    svara Note; the seven are ṣaḍja, ṛṣabha, gāndhāra, madhyama, pañcama, dhaivata, niṣāda (sa ri ga ma pa dha ni)
    svarūp (Hi.) “Own form”: a boy actor of the Rāmlīlā or Rāslīlā regarded, while in costume, as an embodiment of the deity
    tablā (Hi.) The pair of hand drums of Hindustani music, the wooden dāyāṃ and the metal or clay bāyāṃ (Ch. 10)
    tāla Rhythmic cycle; the theory of musical time
    tālī (Hi.) A clap marking a section of a Hindustani tāla; cf. khālī
    tān (Hi.) A rapid melodic run in khayāl and instrumental music (Ch. 7)
    tāṇḍava The vigorous style of dance, associated with Śiva
    tani āvartanam (Ta.) The percussion solo of a Karnatak concert (Ch. 5)
    tatkār (Hi.) Rhythmic footwork of Kathak
    ṭawāʾif (Ur.) Courtesan; professional woman singer and dancer of north India
    Terukkūttu (Ta.) The all-night street theatre of northern Tamil Nadu, associated with the cult of Draupadī (Ch. 20)
    teyyam (Mal.) Ritual performance of northern Kerala in which the performer becomes the deity (Ch. 21)
    thāṭ (Hi.) Parent scale; Bhatkhande classified Hindustani rāgas under ten
    ṭhekā (Hi.) The basic drum pattern that identifies a Hindustani tāla
    ṭhumrī (Hi.) Light-classical song genre of 19th-century Lucknow, interpreted in Kathak
    tihāī (Hi.) Phrase repeated three times to end on the sam
    tillāna (Te., Ta.) A brilliant composition of rhythmic syllables, the closing item of pure dance in the southern recital (Ch. 15)
    tīrmāṉam (Ta.) A cadence, usually stated three times, that closes a passage of pure dance (Ch. 13)
    tribhaṅga “Three bends”: posture bending at neck, waist and knee, characteristic of Odissi
    uparūpaka “Minor” dramatic type; many were forms of dance and song (Ch. 19)
    vādī, saṃvādī (Hi.) The predominant note of a Hindustani rāga and the second, consonant note, usually a fourth or fifth from it (Ch. 4)
    vāggeyakāra Composer of both words and music, as described in the Saṅgītaratnākara
    vārī (Mr.) The Vārkarī pilgrimage on foot to Pandharpur, made with singing
    varṇam (Ta., Te.) Central, longest item of a Bharatanāṭyam recital; also a Karnatak study piece
    vibhāva “Determinant”: the cause or stimulus of an emotion
    vidūṣaka The Brahmin jester of Sanskrit drama, most fully developed in Kūṭiyāṭṭam (Ch. 19)
    vīṇā (1) Ancient name for stringed instruments, including harps; (2) the stick zither (bīn) of north India; (3) the long-necked lute (Sarasvatī vīṇā) of south India
    vṛtti Dramatic style: verbal, grand, graceful and energetic
    Yakṣagāna (Ka.) The night-long dance-drama of coastal and southern Karnataka (Ch. 20)
    zikir (As., from Ar.) Assamese Muslim devotional songs ascribed by tradition to Azan Fakir (Ch. 22)
