    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Hi. = Hindi/Urdu, Pe. = Persian, Ta. = Tamil, Ma. = Malayalam, Gu. = Gujarati, Mar. = Marathi, Pa. = Pali).

    Term Meaning
    ajrakh (Gu.; Sindhi ajrak) Resist- and mordant-printed cotton of Sindh, Kutch and western Rajasthan, in indigo, red and black
    āl (Hi.) Root of Morinda, a red dye
    angarkhā (Hi.) Coat whose front panels overlap and tie on the chest, worn in Rajasthan, Gujarat and the Deccan (from Sanskrit aṅgarakṣaka, “body-protector”)
    ayas In the Ṛgveda, metal (probably copper or bronze); later iron
    bāgh (Panjabi) “Garden”: a phulkārī embroidered over the whole ground
    bakhiyā (Hi.) Shadow-work stitch of chikan embroidery, worked in herringbone on the reverse of fine cloth
    balutedār (Mar.) Hereditary village servant in Maharashtra, paid with a share of the harvest; the leather-worker was one of them
    bāndhaṇī (Gu., Hi.) Tie-dyeing, in which small points of cloth are bound with thread before dyeing
    bhapkā (Hi.) Receiver of the Kannauj perfume still, containing sandalwood oil
    bidrī Cast zinc-alloy ware inlaid with silver and blackened, named after Bidar
    carmakāra Leather-worker
    charbā (Hi.) Pounced tracing used by painters to transfer and preserve compositions
    charkhā (Hi.) Spinning wheel
    chīṇṭ (Hi.) Spotted or variegated cloth; source of the English “chintz”
    citrakāra Painter; also the name of hereditary painter communities
    cīvara (Pa.) Robe of a Buddhist monk, sewn from cut pieces in a pattern likened to rice-fields
    dābū (Hi.) Mud-resist paste used in Rajasthani printing
    dantakāra Ivory-carver
    darzī (Hi.) Tailor (from Persian darz, “seam”)
    deg (Hi.) Copper still used in Kannauj perfume distillation
    Dhokra, Dokra Lost-wax brass casting of central and eastern India, and the communities who practise it
    dhvaja Standard or flag-staff; the Delhi iron pillar was a dhvaja of Viṣṇu
    dhyāna Meditation verse describing the posture, attributes and colour of a deity, memorised by image-makers
    ekāṇḍa “Single-piece”: a vīṇā whose bowl, neck and head are carved from one log
    gāmochā (Assamese) White cotton cloth with red borders and woven motifs, used as towel and scarf and given at Bihu
    gañjīfā (Pe.) Circular playing cards made at Sawantwadi and elsewhere
    harḍa (Hi.) Myrobalan fruit (Terminalia chebula), a source of tannin used in dyeing
    haritāla Orpiment, a yellow arsenic sulphide pigment (poisonous)
    hiṅgula Vermilion (mercury sulphide), a red pigment (poisonous)
    injiri (Kalabari) “Real Madras handkerchief”: checked cotton woven in the Madras region for the Kalabari of the Niger Delta
    iṭaṅkai, valaṅkai (Ta.) “Left-hand” and “right-hand” caste divisions of south India
    jajmānī (Hi.) Customary relationship in which village artisans and service castes served patron households for annual payments in kind
    jāma (Pe.) Long coat with a fitted bodice and full skirt, tied at the side; formal court dress of Mughal and Rajput India
    jāmdānī (Bengali) Figured weaving in which motifs are inserted by hand as discontinuous supplementary wefts into a fine cotton ground
    jāti Hereditary, endogamous occupational community; “caste” in the narrower sense
    jawārī (Hi.) The buzzing resonance produced by the broad, curved bridge of the sitār and tānpurā; also the bridge itself
    jūtī, mojaṛī (Hi., Panjabi) Closed leather slipper with a pointed or upturned toe, often embroidered, of Rajasthan and Punjab
    kāca Glass
    kalā An art or accomplishment; the Kāmasūtra lists sixty-four
    kalamkārī (Pe.) “Pen-work”: painted and printed cottons of Andhra Pradesh
    kalaśa Water-filled pot used as an auspicious symbol and in ritual
    kalī (Hi.) Triangular gore let into the side of a garment to give fullness
    kāṃsya Bell metal, a high-tin bronze
    kānī (Kashmiri) Small bobbin; the twill-tapestry technique of the finest Kashmir shawls
    kaniki (Swahili) Dark indigo-dyed cotton cloth, a staple of the Indian cloth trade to East Africa
    kānthā (Bengali) Quilt or wrap of layered old cloth, embroidered in running stitch
    karmāra Smith
    kārpāsa Cotton
    kāru In the Ṛgveda, a singer or poet; later, an artisan
    kasūti (Kannada) Counted-thread embroidery of the Dharwad region, worked in four stitches
    khādī (Hi.) Hand-spun and hand-woven cloth, especially as promoted by the national movement
    kimkhāb (Pe.) Brocade woven with gold and silver thread
    koftgarī (Pe.) Gold or silver wire inlaid into a cross-hatched iron or steel surface
    korvai (Ta.) Interlocked-weft technique for contrasting borders in Kāñcīpuram saris
    kulāla, kumbhakāra Potter
    kundan (Hi.) Highly refined gold foil; the technique of setting stones with it
    kuppā (Hi.) Large jar of raw hide for storing and carrying oil or ghee
    lākṣā Lac, the resin of the lac insect
    madhūcchiṣṭa-vidhāna “The method of beeswax”: lost-wax casting
    mañjiṣṭhā Indian madder (Rubia cordifolia), a red dye
    mashak (Hi.) Goatskin water-bag carried by the water-carrier (bhishtī)
    masī Ink
    mīnākārī (Pe.) Enamelling on metal
    mot (Mar.) Large leather bucket drawn up from a well by bullocks; charsā or pur in parts of north India
    mugā, erī, pāṭ (Assamese) The golden, castor-fed and mulberry silks of Assam
    naqqāsh (Pe.) Pattern-drawer, designer
    naqsha (Pe.) Design; the pattern harness of a drawloom
    naqshaband (Pe.) Pattern-maker who ties a design into the harness of a drawloom
    nauvārī (Marathi) Nine-yard sari, worn with a portion passed between the legs and tucked at the back
    navaratna The “nine gems” associated in astrology with the nine planets
    nīlī Indigo
    pādukā Wooden sandal; the footwear of ascetics and the object of worship as the feet of gods and teachers
    pañcaloha “Five metals”: an alloy prescribed in ritual texts for images
    paṭolā (Gu.) Double-ikat silk of Patan, Gujarat
    patrorṇā “Leaf-wool”: in the Arthaśāstra, apparently a silk from caterpillars fed on the leaves of trees
    paṭṭacitra Painting on prepared cloth, especially in Odisha
    pelete bite (Kalabari) “Cut-thread cloth”, made by Kalabari women by cutting and pulling threads from imported Indian checks
    peshwāz (Pe.) High-waisted gown worn by women at the Mughal and later courts
    phulkārī (Panjabi) “Flower-work”: darning-stitch embroidery in silk floss
    prāṇapratiṣṭhā Rite of “establishing the breath” in a consecrated image
    pūnī (Gu., Hi.) Sliver of carded cotton, prepared for spinning
    puruṣamedha Symbolic “human sacrifice” of the Yajurveda, whose victim-list names many occupations
    rājakāriya (Sinhala) Service owed to the king in return for land, under which the Kandyan royal craftsmen worked
    rallī (Sindhi) Pieced and appliquéd cotton quilt of Sindh, Rajasthan and Kutch
    rathakāra Chariot-maker
    ratnaparīkṣā The testing of gems; title of several lapidary treatises
    rogan (Pe.) Painting on cloth with a paste of boiled castor oil and pigments
    sāñcipāt (Assamese) Prepared inner bark of the agaru tree, used for manuscripts in Assam
    satra (Assamese) Vaiṣṇava monastery of the tradition of Śaṅkaradeva
    śilpa Skill, art, craft; a work of art
    śilpaśāstra Treatise on architecture, image-making and allied crafts
    śilpin Skilled craftsman
    śītalpāṭi (Bengali) “Cool mat”: mat woven from split stems of the murtā plant, especially in Sylhet
    śreṇī (Pa. seṇi) Guild, an association of people following the same occupation
    sthapati Master builder or sculptor
    strīdhana A woman’s own property, especially gifts and ornaments received at marriage
    sūcī, saucika Needle; tailor (“needle-man”)
    sūtradhāra, sūtragrāhin “Holder of the cord”: surveyor, architect or carpenter
    svadeśī “Of one’s own country”: the movement to boycott foreign goods and use Indian ones
    svāvalambana Self-reliance; in the khādī movement, spinning for one’s own cloth rather than for sale
    takalī (Hi.) Small hand spindle, promoted by the khādī movement for schoolchildren and travellers
    takṣan, takṣaka Carpenter, carver
    tāla Module of proportion in image-making, roughly the length of the face
    tālīm (Kashmiri) Coded pattern-script used by Kashmir kānī shawl and carpet weavers
    tānā, bānā (Hi.) Warp; weft. Together, tānā-bānā, a figure for the interwoven fabric of a society
    tantuvāya Weaver
    tārkashī (Hi.) Brass-wire inlay in wood
    tolu bommalāṭa (Telugu) Shadow theatre of Andhra Pradesh, performed with large painted puppets of translucent leather
    ukku (Kannada, Telugu) Steel; source of the English “wootz”
    ustād, shāgird (Pe.) Master and apprentice
    vardhakin Joiner; one of the four craftsmen of the śilpaśāstra team
    vāstupuruṣamaṇḍala Diagram of the “person of the site” used to lay out buildings
    vāstuśāstra The science of building and of sites
    Viśvakarman “Maker of all”: the divine craftsman; ancestor claimed by the Viśvakarma artisan communities
    zardozī (Pe.) Embroidery with metal thread and sequins
    zarī (Pe.) Gold or silver thread, usually silver-gilt wire wrapped round silk
