    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate, and many are disputed; where opinion varies, a broad range is given. Traditional dates are not used.

    Date Events, sites and works
    c. 6th millennium BCE Mehrgarh: copper bead containing some of the earliest known cotton fibres; early pottery
    c. 4th millennium BCE Wheel-made pottery and copper working in Baluchistan and the Indus region
    c. 2600–1900 BCE Mature Harappan (Indus) cities: shell, bead, faience, stoneware, copper and bronze crafts; wild silk and cotton textiles; lost-wax “dancing girl”
    early 2nd millennium BCE Copper Hoards of the Ganga–Yamuna Doab; earliest iron in the central Ganga plain (debated)
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Ṛgveda: carpenters, chariot-makers, smiths, weaving; Viśvakarman hymns of the late tenth book (RV 10.81–82)
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts: occupational lists of the Yajurveda; Painted Grey Ware; iron in common use
    c. 700–200 BCE Northern Black Polished Ware; cities of the Ganga valley; punch-marked silver coins
    5th c. BCE Herodotus on Indian “tree-wool” (cotton)
    c. 268–232 BCE Reign of Aśoka
    late centuries BCE Crucible steel in south India; glass beads at Arikamedu and other sites; Sanchi gateways (ivory-carvers of Vidiśā)
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE Composition of the Arthaśāstra (superintendents of mines, gold, yarn)
    mid-1st c. CE Periplus of the Erythraean Sea: Indian cottons, muslins, iron and steel in trade
    1st–2nd c. CE Nasik inscription of Uṣavadāta: endowments deposited with weavers’ guilds
    c. 2nd c. CE Manusmṛti
    c. 375–415 CE Candragupta II; the Delhi iron pillar
    436 and 473 CE Mandasor inscription of the silk-weavers’ guild
    465 CE Indor copper-plate: endowment with a guild of oil-men
    c. 5th–6th c. CE The Tamil epic Cilappatikāram: the artisans’ streets of Pukār
    6th c. CE Varāhamihira’s Bṛhatsaṃhitā (houses, images, wood, gems, perfumes)
    7th c. CE Bāṇa’s Harṣacarita: cloths, cane-work and aloe-bark books sent to Harṣa by Bhāskaravarman of Kāmarūpa
    c. 9th–10th c. Earliest zinc distillation at Zawar
    10th–15th c. Gujarati block-printed cottons traded to Egypt (Fustat, Quseir al-Qadim)
    c. 1010 Rājarājeśvara temple, Thanjavur; Cola bronzes
    c. 1131 Mānasollāsa of Someśvara III
    12th–13th c. Hoysaḷa temples with sculptors’ signatures; Aparājitapṛcchā
    13th c. Yaśodhara’s Jayamaṅgalā commentary on the Kāmasūtra (six limbs of painting)
    1228–1826 Ahom kingdom in Assam; mugā and pāṭ silks worn at court
    1260–1279 The Newar craftsman Anige in Tibet and at Khubilai Khan’s court; White Stūpa of the Miaoying temple, Beijing
    1315 Ṭhakkura Pherū’s Ratnaparīkṣā; his Dravyaparīkṣā on coins
    c. 14th c. Large-scale zinc distillation at Zawar
    15th c. Kabīr and Raidās in Banaras
    1429 Bidar becomes the Bahmanī capital; Bidrī ware develops in the following centuries
    1549 Malik-i Maidān cannon cast at Ahmadnagar
    16th c. Śilparatna of Śrīkumāra (Kerala)
    late 16th c. Mughal imperial workshops; Abu’l Fazl’s Āʾīn-i Akbarī; carpet-weaving at Lahore, Agra and Fatehpur Sikri
    17th c. Dutch and English companies export Indian cottons to Europe; chintz fashion in Europe from the 1660s–70s; Tavernier visits the diamond mines
    1686 France prohibits printed cottons (until 1759)
    1700, 1721 English Acts against Asian and printed calicoes
    1719 Spitalfields silk-weavers attack women wearing calico in London
    1727 Foundation of Jaipur
    1730s–1740s Beaulieu and Cœurdoux record the Pondicherry chintz process
    1734 Hastividyārṇava painted for the Ahom court
    1736 English Act confirms that printed linen-and-cotton cloth is lawful
    1757, 1765 Plassey; the East India Company acquires the dīvānī of Bengal
    1760 Oberkampf’s printworks founded at Jouy-en-Josas, France
    1772 William Bolts, Considerations on India Affairs
    1780s–1790s Mysore rockets used against the East India Company
    1799 Ranjit Singh takes Lahore; the Sikh court becomes a major patron of crafts
    1813 End of the Company’s monopoly of trade with India
    1819, 1846 Kashmir under Sikh rule; then under the Dogra rulers of Jammu
    1820s onward Rapid growth of imports of British cotton cloth and yarn
    1851 Great Exhibition, London; Indian crafts displayed
    1850s–1870s Schools of art at Madras, Calcutta, Bombay and Lahore; first Indian cotton mills
    1859 Travancore proclamation allows Nāḍār women to cover the upper body
    1859–60 Indigo revolt in Bengal
    1860s Government Harness and Saddlery Factory, Cawnpore
    1861–65 American Civil War; cotton boom in western India
    1866 J. Forbes Watson, The Textile Manufactures and the Costumes of the People of India, with volumes of cloth samples
    1868–69 Synthetic alizarin
    1870s Collapse of the Kashmir shawl trade
    1880 George Birdwood, The Industrial Arts of India
    1897 Synthetic indigo marketed
    1902 R. C. Dutt, The Economic History of India under Early British Rule
    1903–08 Svadeśī movement in Bengal; partition of Bengal (1905)
    1906 National Council of Education and Bengal Technical Institute; Banga Lakshmi Cotton Mills
    1907 Indian Society of Oriental Art, Calcutta
    1908 Coomaraswamy, Mediaeval Sinhalese Art
    1909 Coomaraswamy, The Indian Craftsman; Gandhi, Hind Swaraj
    1917 Champaran satyagraha against forced indigo cultivation; spinning wheels found at Vijapur for Gandhi’s ashram (1917 or soon after)
    1919 Kala Bhavana founded at Santiniketan
    1921 Visva-Bharati constituted; bonfires of foreign cloth; Gandhi adopts the loincloth at Madurai
    1922 Exhibition of Bauhaus artists in Calcutta
    1924 Indian Lac Research Institute, Namkum
    1925 All India Spinners’ Association
    1936 Ambedkar, Annihilation of Caste; Wiser, The Hindu Jajmani System
    1948 Indian Co-operative Union founded by Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay; Central Leather Research Institute, Madras
    1952 All India Handicrafts Board (Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay)
    mid-1950s Ambar charkhā adopted by the khādī organisations
    1957 Khadi and Village Industries Commission
    1958 Charles and Ray Eames’s India Report
    1961 National Institute of Design, Ahmedabad
    1964 Crafts Council of India
    1972 Antiquities and Art Treasures Act restricts the export of objects over a hundred years old
    1979 Ahmedabad Declaration on Industrial Design for Development
    1984 SEWA Lucknow begins organising chikan embroiderers
    1985 Handlooms (Reservation of Articles for Production) Act
    1986 National Institute of Fashion Technology, New Delhi
    1987, 1996 Supreme Court rulings on tannery pollution at Kanpur and in Tamil Nadu (Vellore)
    1990 International trade in ivory banned under CITES
    1999, 2003 Geographical Indications of Goods Act passed; in force
    2005 Kala Raksha opens a design school for artisans in Kutch
    2013 UNESCO inscribes Jamdani weaving (Bangladesh)
    2014 UNESCO inscribes the brass and copper craft of the Ṭhaṭherās of Jandiala Guru; the stolen Sripuranthan Naṭarāja returned to India from Australia
    2015 Jaipur a UNESCO Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art; first National Handloom Day
    2016 Flogging of Dalit leather-workers at Una, Gujarat, and the protests that followed
    2017 UNESCO inscribes the śītalpāṭi mat-weaving of Sylhet (Bangladesh)
    2019 Kolhapuri chappals registered as a Geographical Indication
    2019–20 Fourth All India Handloom Census
    2021 Flag Code amended to allow national flags not made of khādī
    2023 UNESCO inscribes the rickshaw painting of Dhaka (Bangladesh); PM Vishwakarma scheme for artisans
    2025 Controversy over sandals resembling Kolhapuris in a Milan fashion show
