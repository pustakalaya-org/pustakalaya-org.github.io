    Glossary of Terms¶

    A¶

    Adjoint differentiation
    Simulator-only method that computes all parameter gradients of a circuit by running it backwards, costing about one extra pass.
    Amplitude encoding
    Storing 2^n normalised numbers in the amplitudes of n qubits; compact, but preparing the state usually needs deep circuits.
    Amplitude estimation
    Fault-tolerant algorithm estimating probabilities or averages with quadratically fewer samples than classical Monte Carlo.
    Angle encoding
    Encoding each feature as a rotation angle on a qubit; simple and shallow but uses one qubit per feature.
    Ansatz
    The chosen structure of a parameterised circuit, such as hardware-efficient layers or a problem-inspired design.

    B¶

    Barren plateau
    A training landscape where gradients shrink exponentially with qubit count, making optimisation practically impossible.
    Basis encoding
    Writing a bit-string directly into qubit states |0> and |1>, one bit per qubit.
    BQP
    Bounded-error quantum polynomial time: the class of problems a quantum computer can solve efficiently with small error.

    C¶

    Circuit Born machine (QCBM)
    A generative model that samples bit-strings from the measurement distribution of a trained quantum circuit.
    Classical shadows
    Compact classical descriptions of a quantum state from randomised measurements, used to predict many properties efficiently.
    Classical surrogate
    A classical model that reproduces a trained quantum model's predictions, testing whether the quantum part is needed.
    CUDA-Q
    NVIDIA's open-source programming model for hybrid quantum-classical programs, optimised for GPU simulation.

    D¶

    Data re-uploading
    Encoding the same input several times between trainable layers to increase a quantum model's expressivity.
    Density matrix
    A matrix description of a quantum state that can represent noise and mixtures, needing 4^n entries for n qubits.
    Dequantisation
    Finding a classical algorithm that matches a claimed quantum speed-up, as Ewin Tang did for recommendation systems.

    E¶

    Entanglement
    Correlation between qubits that cannot be described by treating each qubit separately.
    Error mitigation
    Techniques such as ZNE, PEC and readout correction that reduce noise bias in expectation values at a cost in shots.
    Expectation value
    The average outcome of measuring an observable, estimated on hardware from many shots.
    Exponential concentration
    The tendency of quantum kernel values or cost functions to cluster around a fixed value as qubits grow.
    Expressibility
    How well a parameterised circuit's states cover the space of all possible states.

    F¶

    Fault-tolerant quantum computing
    Computing with error-corrected logical qubits so long algorithms can run reliably; not yet available at useful scale.
    Feature map
    A circuit that maps classical data into a quantum state; the quantum analogue of a kernel feature map.
    Fidelity kernel
    A quantum kernel defined as the squared overlap between the states encoding two data points.

    G¶

    Generalisation
    How well a model trained on some data performs on new, unseen data.

    H¶

    Hamiltonian
    The operator describing a quantum system's energy; used as observables, encodings and learning targets.
    Hardware-efficient ansatz
    A circuit built from gates and connections native to a device, minimising depth but prone to barren plateaus.
    HHL algorithm
    Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd quantum algorithm for linear systems; exponential speed-up only under strict assumptions.
    Hilbert space
    The mathematical space of quantum states; its dimension doubles with every added qubit.
    Hybrid quantum-classical model
    A model where a classical computer optimises or post-processes the outputs of quantum circuits.

    I¶

    Inductive bias
    The assumptions a model family builds in, which decide which patterns it learns easily.
    ISA circuit
    A circuit already transpiled to a specific device's native gates and qubit layout, as required by IBM's V2 primitives.

    K¶

    Kernel method
    A learning approach, such as the support vector machine, that compares data points through a similarity function.

    L¶

    Logical qubit
    An error-corrected qubit encoded across many physical qubits.

    M¶

    Measurement
    Reading out qubits, which collapses the state and returns random outcomes with probabilities set by the state.

    N¶

    NISQ
    Noisy intermediate-scale quantum: today's era of tens to hundreds of imperfect qubits without full error correction.
    Noise model
    A description of gate, readout and decoherence errors used to simulate realistic hardware.

    O¶

    Observable
    A measurable quantity represented by a Hermitian operator, such as Pauli-Z on one qubit.

    P¶

    Parameterised quantum circuit (PQC)
    A circuit with adjustable gate angles that are trained like the weights of a neural network.
    Parameter-shift rule
    Exact gradient method evaluating a circuit at shifted parameter values, usually two runs per parameter.
    PennyLane
    Xanadu's open-source framework for differentiable quantum programming, integrated with PyTorch and JAX.
    Primitive (Qiskit)
    Standard Qiskit interface for running circuits: the Estimator returns expectation values, the Sampler returns counts.
    Projected quantum kernel
    A kernel built from reduced (local) properties of encoded states, designed to avoid exponential concentration.

    Q¶

    QAOA
    Quantum approximate optimisation algorithm: alternating problem and mixer layers trained to find good solutions.
    QRAM
    Quantum random access memory: a proposed device for loading classical data in superposition; not yet practical.
    QSVT
    Quantum singular value transformation, a framework unifying many fault-tolerant quantum algorithms.
    Quantum advantage
    A demonstrated benefit over the best classical methods: in speed, data needed, or practical outcome.
    Quantum annealing
    Analog quantum optimisation that slowly evolves a system towards low-energy solutions of an Ising problem.
    Quantum convolutional neural network (QCNN)
    A layered quantum model with convolution and pooling steps, used for phase recognition.
    Quantum GAN (QGAN)
    A generative adversarial network where the generator, discriminator or both are quantum circuits.
    Quantum-inspired algorithm
    A classical algorithm that borrows ideas from quantum computing, such as tensor networks or sampling tricks.
    Quantum kernel
    A similarity measure between data points computed from their encoded quantum states.
    Quantum natural gradient
    An optimiser that rescales gradients by the geometry of quantum states (the Fubini-Study metric).
    Quantum neural network (QNN)
    A parameterised quantum circuit used as a trainable model, often inside a hybrid network.
    Quantum reservoir computing
    Using a fixed quantum system as a feature generator and training only a classical readout layer.
    Qubit
    The basic unit of quantum information, able to be in a superposition of 0 and 1.
    QUBO
    Quadratic unconstrained binary optimisation, the problem format used by quantum annealers and QAOA.

    S¶

    Sample complexity
    The number of training examples or experiments needed to learn a task to a given accuracy.
    Shot
    One run of a circuit followed by measurement; many shots are averaged, with error shrinking as 1/sqrt(shots).
    Simulability
    Whether a quantum computation can be reproduced efficiently on a classical computer.
    SPSA
    Simultaneous perturbation stochastic approximation: a gradient estimate from two evaluations, popular on noisy hardware.
    Superposition
    A quantum state that is a weighted combination of basis states, such as |0> and |1> together.

    T¶

    Tensor network
    A way of representing large states or matrices as connected small tensors, used for simulation and classical ML.
    Transpilation
    Rewriting a circuit into a device's native gates and connectivity, adding SWAPs where needed.

    U¶

    Unitary
    A reversible, norm-preserving matrix describing an ideal quantum gate or circuit.

    V¶

    Variational quantum classifier (VQC)
    A classifier made of a feature map, a trainable ansatz and a measurement, trained in a hybrid loop.
    VQE
    Variational quantum eigensolver: a hybrid algorithm that minimises a Hamiltonian's energy with a parameterised circuit.

    Z¶

    Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE)
    Error mitigation that runs circuits at amplified noise and extrapolates results back to zero noise.
