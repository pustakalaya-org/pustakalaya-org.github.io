    Glossary of Terms¶

    A¶

    Amplitude
    A complex number attached to each possible outcome of a quantum state; its squared magnitude gives the probability of that outcome.
    Amplitude estimation
    A quantum routine that estimates a probability or average with error shrinking as 1/N rather than 1/√N, quadratically fewer samples than classical Monte Carlo.

    B¶

    Barren plateau
    A training landscape in which gradients of a parameterised quantum circuit vanish exponentially with qubit count, making optimisation impractical.
    BB84
    The first quantum key distribution protocol (Bennett and Brassard, 1984), sending single photons in random bases so that eavesdropping causes detectable errors.
    Bell state
    One of four maximally entangled two-qubit states, such as (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, prepared with a Hadamard followed by a CNOT.
    Bloch sphere
    A geometric picture of a single qubit's pure states as points on a unit sphere; gates are rotations of that sphere.
    Born rule
    The rule that the probability of measuring an outcome equals the squared magnitude of its amplitude.
    BQP
    Bounded-error quantum polynomial time: the class of problems a quantum computer can solve efficiently with small error probability.

    C¶

    Circuit depth
    The number of sequential layers of gates in a circuit; deeper circuits take longer and accumulate more noise.
    CNOT gate
    Controlled-NOT: a two-qubit gate that flips the target qubit when the control qubit is 1; the standard entangling gate.
    Cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC)
    A future fault-tolerant quantum computer large enough to break RSA or elliptic-curve cryptography with Shor's algorithm.

    D¶

    Decoder
    Classical software or hardware that reads error-correction syndromes and decides which correction to apply, ideally in real time.
    Decoherence
    Loss of a qubit's quantum behaviour through unwanted interaction with its environment.
    Density matrix
    A matrix description of a quantum state that can represent mixed states and noise, not just pure superpositions.
    Dequantisation
    Finding a classical algorithm that matches a claimed quantum speed-up, showing the quantum advantage was not real under the same assumptions.
    Dilution refrigerator
    A cryostat that uses a helium-3/helium-4 mixture to cool superconducting qubits to about 10-20 millikelvin.
    Dirac (bra-ket) notation
    Physicists' shorthand for quantum states: |ψ⟩ (ket) is a column vector, ⟨ψ| (bra) its conjugate transpose, and ⟨φ|ψ⟩ an inner product.

    E¶

    Entanglement
    Correlation between qubits so strong that their joint state cannot be described by describing each qubit separately.
    Error mitigation
    Techniques such as zero-noise extrapolation and readout correction that reduce the effect of noise on results without full error correction.

    F¶

    Fault tolerance
    The ability to compute reliably with imperfect components by using error-correcting codes and operations that do not spread errors uncontrollably.
    FeMoco
    The iron-molybdenum cofactor of the nitrogenase enzyme; the standard benchmark molecule for quantum chemistry resource estimates.
    Fidelity
    A measure of how close an actual quantum state or gate is to the ideal one; 99.9% two-qubit fidelity means roughly one error per thousand gates.
    FIPS 203 (ML-KEM)
    NIST's 2024 post-quantum key-encapsulation standard, derived from CRYSTALS-Kyber and based on module lattices.
    FIPS 204 (ML-DSA)
    NIST's 2024 general-purpose post-quantum digital signature standard, derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium.
    FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA)
    NIST's 2024 stateless hash-based signature standard, derived from SPHINCS+; conservative but with large signatures.

    G¶

    GHZ state
    A multi-qubit entangled state such as (|000⟩ + |111⟩)/√2, used to benchmark entanglement across many qubits.
    Grover's algorithm
    A search algorithm finding a marked item among N in about √N steps — a quadratic speed-up over classical search.

    H¶

    Hadamard gate
    The single-qubit gate H that turns |0⟩ into an equal superposition (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and back again.
    Hamiltonian
    The operator describing a system's energy; it governs how a quantum state evolves in time and is central to quantum simulation.
    Harvest now, decrypt later
    An attack in which adversaries record encrypted data today to decrypt it once a capable quantum computer exists.
    HHL algorithm
    Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd algorithm for linear systems of equations; exponentially faster only under strict input and output conditions.
    Hybrid key exchange
    Combining a classical and a post-quantum key exchange, such as X25519MLKEM768, so security holds if either one survives.

    I¶

    Interference
    Amplitudes adding or cancelling, like waves; quantum algorithms use it to boost correct answers and suppress wrong ones.

    L¶

    Logical qubit
    An error-protected qubit encoded across many physical qubits by an error-correcting code.

    M¶

    Magic state
    A special resource state that, injected into a circuit, enables non-Clifford gates such as T in fault-tolerant quantum computers.
    Mosca's inequality
    If the time data must stay secret plus the time to migrate exceeds the time until a quantum attack, the data is already at risk.

    N¶

    Neutral-atom qubit
    A qubit stored in an individual atom held by optical tweezers; arrays of hundreds to thousands of atoms are reconfigurable.
    NISQ
    Noisy intermediate-scale quantum: today's era of machines with tens to thousands of noisy qubits and no full error correction (term coined by John Preskill, 2018).
    No-cloning theorem
    The principle that an unknown quantum state cannot be copied perfectly, which shapes error correction and quantum communication.

    O¶

    OpenQASM 3
    An open assembly-style language for describing quantum circuits, including classical control flow and timing.

    P¶

    Phase estimation
    An algorithm that estimates the eigenvalue phase of a unitary operation; a building block of Shor's algorithm and quantum chemistry.
    Photonic qubit
    A qubit encoded in a particle of light, for example in its path, polarisation or arrival time; well suited to networking.
    Physical qubit
    An actual hardware element — a superconducting circuit, ion, atom, photon or spin — used to store quantum information.
    Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
    Classical cryptographic algorithms designed to resist attacks by quantum computers, running on ordinary hardware.

    Q¶

    QAOA
    Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm: a variational circuit alternating problem and mixer layers to find good solutions to optimisation problems.
    Qiskit
    IBM's open-source Python SDK for building, transpiling and running quantum circuits, now in its 2.x generation.
    qLDPC code
    Quantum low-density parity-check code, such as IBM's bivariate bicycle codes, encoding more logical qubits per physical qubit than surface codes.
    Quantum advantage
    A quantum computer solving a useful problem faster, cheaper or more accurately than the best classical method.
    Quantum annealing
    An analogue approach that slowly changes a quantum system's energy landscape so it settles into low-energy solutions of optimisation problems.
    Quantum Fourier transform (QFT)
    The quantum version of the discrete Fourier transform, acting on amplitudes; used in phase estimation and Shor's algorithm.
    Quantum key distribution (QKD)
    Using quantum states of light to create shared secret keys whose eavesdropping can be detected.
    Quantum supremacy
    A quantum device performing some task, even a useless one, beyond the practical reach of classical computers.
    Quantum utility
    IBM's term for running circuits at scales beyond brute-force classical simulation that may be scientifically useful.
    Qubit
    The basic unit of quantum information: a two-level system that can be in a superposition of |0⟩ and |1⟩.
    QUBO
    Quadratic unconstrained binary optimisation: a problem format of 0/1 variables used by quantum annealers and QAOA.

    R¶

    Readout (SPAM) error
    Errors in state preparation and measurement — for example, recording 0 when the qubit was actually 1.

    S¶

    Shor's algorithm
    Peter Shor's 1994 algorithm that factors integers and computes discrete logarithms efficiently, breaking RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography on a large fault-tolerant machine.
    Stabiliser
    A multi-qubit Pauli operator whose measured value reveals errors without disturbing the encoded information; the basis of most quantum codes.
    Superconducting qubit (transmon)
    A qubit made from a superconducting circuit with Josephson junctions, operated with microwaves at millikelvin temperatures.
    Superposition
    A quantum state that is a weighted combination of basis states, with complex amplitudes that can interfere.
    Surface code
    A widely used error-correcting code on a 2-D grid of qubits needing only nearest-neighbour interactions, with a threshold near 1%.

    T¶

    T1 and T2
    Coherence times: T1 measures energy relaxation from |1⟩ to |0⟩; T2 measures how long phase information survives.
    Threshold theorem
    The result that if physical error rates are below a threshold, error correction can make logical error rates arbitrarily small.
    Transpiler
    Software that rewrites a circuit into a device's native gates and qubit connectivity while optimising depth and gate count.
    Trapped-ion qubit
    A qubit stored in an electrically charged atom held by electromagnetic fields and controlled with lasers or microwaves.

    U¶

    Unitary
    A reversible, length-preserving matrix; every ideal quantum gate is a unitary operation.

    V¶

    VQE
    Variational quantum eigensolver: a hybrid loop in which a quantum circuit estimates energy and a classical optimiser tunes its parameters.
