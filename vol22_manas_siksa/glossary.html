    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Pa. = Pali, Ar. = Arabic, Pe. = Persian, Pj. = Panjabi, Tib. = Tibetan). Pali forms are given for Theravāda terms.

    Term Meaning
    ācārya Teacher, especially one who initiates a student and teaches him the Veda
    adhikāra Eligibility; the qualification (pedagogical, but also social) to receive a teaching or perform a rite
    adhyāsa Superimposition; in Advaita, the mutual confusion of self and not-self
    agrahāra Village or land granted to learned Brahmins, free of tax
    ahaṃkāra “I-maker”; the principle of self-appropriation
    akhlāq (Ar., Pe.) Ethics; the Persian literature of character and its cultivation (Ṭūsī, Dawānī)
    ālayavijñāna “Storehouse consciousness” of Yogācāra Buddhism, holding the seeds of experience
    anirvacanīya “Indeterminable”; neither real nor unreal (Advaita theory of error)
    antaḥkaraṇa The “inner instrument”: mind, intellect, I-maker (and, in Vedānta, citta)
    anubhāva “Consequent”; the outward expression of an emotion in rasa theory
    anusaya (Pa.) Latent tendency (seven in the Pali texts)
    ʿaql (Ar.) Reason, intellect; the rational faculty
    āśrama One of four ways or stages of life: student, householder, forest-dweller, renouncer
    avidyā Ignorance; the root affliction in Yoga, Vedānta and Buddhism (Pa. avijjā)
    bhāṇaka (Pa.) Reciter specialising in a collection of the Buddhist canon
    bhaṇḍāra Jain manuscript library
    bhavaṅga (Pa.) Life-continuum; the passive stream of consciousness between cognitive processes
    bīja Seed; in Yogācāra, a latent karmic potential in the storehouse consciousness
    brahmacārin Vedic student living under the discipline of studentship (brahmacarya)
    brahmavihāra “Divine abiding”: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, equanimity
    buddhi Intellect; the faculty of ascertainment
    carita (Pa.) Temperament (six types in the Visuddhimagga)
    cetasika (Pa.) Mental factor (52 in the Theravāda Abhidhamma)
    citta Mind; in Yoga, the whole mental apparatus; in Abhidharma, a moment of consciousness
    dhāraṇā Fixing the mind on a place; the sixth limb of Yoga
    dhikr (Ar.) “Remembrance”; repetition of the names of God, aloud or in the heart
    dhvani Suggestion; the suggested meaning of poetry
    dhyāna Meditation; the seventh limb of Yoga (Pa. jhāna, meditative absorption)
    dvija “Twice-born”; a member of the three upper varṇas who has undergone initiation
    fanāʾ (Ar.) “Passing away” of the self’s attributes in God, followed by baqāʾ, “abiding” in God
    ghanapāṭha “Dense” recitation; the most elaborate of the Vedic recitation patterns in common use
    ghaṭikā Assembly or college of learned Brahmins in early medieval south India
    guṇa Strand or quality of matter: sattva, rajas, tamas
    gurmukh (Pj.) “Facing the Guru”; one who lives by the Guru’s word (opposite of manmukh)
    guru Teacher; literally “weighty one”
    ḥāl (Ar.) Spiritual state, given by grace and transient (contrast maqām)
    haumai (Pj.) “I-me”; self-centredness, the root disorder in Sikh teaching
    hukam (Pj., from Ar.) The divine order or will
    inshāʾ (Pe.) The art of composing letters and official documents
    jaṭāpāṭha “Braided” recitation of the Veda (AB BA AB, …)
    jīva The individual living being or soul
    kaṣāya Passion (anger, pride, deceit, greed) in Jain doctrine
    khaṇḍ (Pj.) “Realm”; one of the five stages of the Japjī (stanzas 34–37)
    khandha (Pa.) Aggregate; one of the five components of a person (Skt. skandha)
    khyātivāda Theory of perceptual error
    kleśa Affliction; in Yoga, ignorance, I-am-ness, attachment, aversion, clinging to life
    kośa Sheath; one of the five “selves” of the Taittirīya Upaniṣad in later interpretation
    kramapāṭha “Step” recitation of the Veda in overlapping pairs of words
    laṭīfa (Ar.) Subtle centre; in Mujaddidī practice, one of ten (pl. laṭāʾif)
    madrasa (Ar.) College of Islamic higher learning
    mahāvākya “Great saying” of the Upaniṣads expressing the identity of self and Brahman
    maktab (Ar.) Elementary school, often attached to a mosque
    man (Pj.) Mind; the whole inner person in the Sikh scripture (Skt. manas)
    manana Reflection; the second stage of Advaita study
    manas Mind; the inner sense that deliberates and coordinates the senses
    manasikāra (Pa.) Attention; one of the seven universal mental factors
    maqām (Ar.) Station of the Sufi path, attained by effort (contrast ḥāl)
    maṭha Monastery, often a centre of teaching
    nafs (Ar.) Self; in Sufi usage the lower self, “commanding”, “blaming” or “at peace”
    nām simran (Pj.) Remembrance of the divine name; the central Sikh discipline
    nididhyāsana Sustained contemplation; the third stage of Advaita study
    padapāṭha Word-by-word form of a Vedic text
    pāṭhaśālā Traditional school, especially for Vedic or Sanskrit study
    prajñāparādha “Offence against wisdom”; acting against one’s knowledge, a cause of disease in Āyurveda
    prakṛti Primal matter (Sāṃkhya); also one’s constitution (Āyurveda)
    prātiśākhya Phonetic manual of a Vedic branch
    pratyabhijñā Recognition; the Kashmir Śaiva doctrine that liberation is recognising one’s own nature
    puruṣa Consciousness, the pure witness (Sāṃkhya, Yoga)
    qalb (Ar.) Heart; the organ of spiritual perception and understanding
    rasa “Taste”; the aesthetic experience of an emotion in drama and poetry
    rūḥ (Ar.) Spirit; what God breathed into Adam
    sādhāraṇīkaraṇa Generalisation; the de-personalising of emotion in aesthetic experience
    sahaj (Pj.) Natural equipoise; the goal of the Sikh path
    sahṛdaya “One with heart”; the sensitive, cultivated reader or spectator
    śākhā Branch or recension of a Veda preserved by a lineage of teachers
    sākṣin Witness; in Advaita, the consciousness that observes all mental states
    samādhi Meditative absorption
    samāvartana “Return”; the ceremony ending Vedic studentship
    saṃskāra Latent impression left by experience; also a life-cycle rite
    santhiyā (Pj.) Training in the correct recitation of the Sikh scripture
    śāstrārtha Learned disputation
    sati (Pa.) Mindfulness; keeping in mind (Skt. smṛti)
    sattvāvajaya “Conquest of the mind”; the psychological therapy of Āyurveda
    sevā (Pj.) Selfless service, a discipline against haumai
    śikṣā Phonetics; also, in modern usage, education
    siyāq (Pe.) Notation and methods of revenue accounting taught to secretaries
    smṛti Memory; also the class of remembered (non-Vedic) authoritative texts
    śravaṇa Hearing; the first stage of Advaita study
    sthāyibhāva Stable emotion underlying a rasa
    svara Pitch accent of Vedic recitation (udātta, anudātta, svarita)
    taṇhā (Pa.) Craving; the origin of suffering (Skt. tṛṣṇā)
    tattva Principle; one of the 25 (Sāṃkhya) or 36 (Kashmir Śaiva) constituents of reality
    ṭol Traditional Sanskrit school in Bengal and Bihar
    turīya “The fourth”; the self beyond waking, dream and deep sleep
    upanayana Initiation into Vedic study
    vāda Debate aimed at truth (Nyāya)
    vāsanā Disposition formed by accumulated impressions
    vibhāva “Determinant”; the cause of an emotion in rasa theory
    vikṛti “Modification”; any of the eight complex Vedic recitation patterns
    vṛtti Fluctuation or mode of the mind
    vyabhicāribhāva Transitory emotional state (thirty-three in Bharata’s list)
    zenana (Pe. zanāna) Women’s quarters of a house
