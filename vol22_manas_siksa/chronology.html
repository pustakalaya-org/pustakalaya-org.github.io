    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate and many are disputed; where scholarly opinion varies, a broad range is given. Traditional dates appear only where they are labelled as such.

    Date Texts, persons and institutions
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core of the Ṛgveda; oral transmission of the Vedic hymns
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts; Brāhmaṇas; oldest Upaniṣads (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya) towards the end of the period
    5th c. BCE The Buddha (d. c. 400 BCE on the short chronology); Mahāvīra, an older contemporary (traditional dates 599–527 BCE)
    5th–4th c. BCE Takṣaśilā a city of renowned teachers (Jātaka tradition)
    c. 4th c. BCE Pāṇini’s grammar
    3rd c. BCE Inscriptions of Aśoka (r. c. 268–232 BCE), the earliest securely dated deciphered writing in India (the Indus script of the 3rd millennium BCE is undeciphered)
    last centuries BCE Dharmasūtras of Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana, Vasiṣṭha
    2nd c. BCE Patañjali’s Mahābhāṣya
    c. 200 BCE–200 CE Main body of the Nāṭyaśāstra
    c. 100 BCE–200 CE Caraka’s redaction of the Carakasaṃhitā (Meulenbeld)
    1st c. BCE–3rd c. CE Gāndhārī birch-bark scrolls, the oldest surviving Indian manuscripts
    1st c. BCE Pali canon written down in Sri Lanka (by tradition)
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE Arthaśāstra in its present form
    c. 2nd c. CE Manusmṛti; Nāgārjuna
    by c. 500 CE Suśrutasaṃhitā in substantially its present form; Dṛḍhabala completes the Carakasaṃhitā (4th–5th c. CE)
    early centuries CE–c. 6th c. Caṅkam anthologies; Tolkāppiyam; Tirukkuṟaḷ (all dates disputed)
    2nd–5th c. CE Umāsvāti, Tattvārthasūtra
    4th–5th c. CE Īśvarakṛṣṇa, Sāṃkhyakārikā; Asaṅga and Vasubandhu
    c. 325–425 CE Pātañjalayogaśāstra (Yogasūtra with bhāṣya)
    5th c. CE Foundation of Nālandā; Bhartṛhari; Buddhaghosa, Visuddhimagga; Jain council at Valabhī; Talagunda inscription (the ghaṭikā of Kāñcī)
    6th c. CE Gauḍapāda, Māṇḍūkyakārikā
    629–645 Xuanzang’s journey (in India c. 630–643); studies at Nālandā
    673–685 Yijing in India
    7th c. Dharmakīrti
    c. 700–750 Śaṅkara (conventional dates 788–820 are traditional)
    late 8th c. Pāla foundations of Vikramaśilā, Somapura and Odantapurī
    c. 850 Ānandavardhana, Dhvanyāloka
    10th c. Utpaladeva; the Mokṣopāya (source of the Yogavāsiṣṭha)
    c. 950–1016 Abhinavagupta
    11th c. Rājendra Cōḻa’s college at Eṇṇāyiram; Atiśa goes to Tibet (1042)
    c. 1072 Death at Lahore of al-Hujwīrī, author of the Kashf al-Maḥjūb, often called the oldest Persian treatise on Sufism
    11th–12th c. Anuruddha, Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha; Rāmānuja
    c. 1193–1205 Campaigns of Bakhtiyār Khaljī in Bihar and Bengal; Nālandā, Vikramaśilā and Odantapurī sacked
    1234–1236 Dharmasvāmin finds Nālandā damaged but still teaching
    7th–9th c. Tamil Śaiva saints of the Tēvāram; Māṇikkavācakar, Tiruvācakam
    13th c. Meykaṇṭār, Civañāṉapōtam; Madhva (c. 1238–1317; traditional 1199–1278); Śārṅgadeva, Saṅgītaratnākara; Minhāj-i Sirāj, Ṭabaqāt-i Nāṣirī
    1307–1322 Niẓām al-Dīn Awliyāʾ’s conversations recorded in the Fawāʾid al-Fuʾād
    14th c. Vidyāraṇya, Pañcadaśī; Fīrūz Shāh’s madrasa and hospital (dār al-shifāʾ) in Delhi
    15th c. Haṭhayogapradīpikā; Maḥmūd Gāwān’s madrasa at Bidar (1472)
    1469–1539 Guru Nānak; the Japjī
    16th c. Caitanya; Rūpa Gosvāmin, Bhaktirasāmṛtasindhu; Akbar’s educational reforms
    c. 1599 Bāqī Billāh brings the Naqshbandī order to Delhi; Aḥmad Sirhindī (1564–1624) and his Maktūbāt
    1604 Guru Arjan compiles the Ādi Granth
    17th c. Dharmarāja Adhvarin, Vedāntaparibhāṣā; Tāranātha’s history of Buddhism
    18th c. Dars-i Niẓāmī at Farangī Maḥall, Lucknow
    1703–1762 Shāh Walī Allāh, Ḥujjat Allāh al-Bāligha; the Madrasa-yi Raḥīmiyya, Delhi
    1784 Asiatic Society founded in Calcutta
    1822–1826 Munro’s survey of indigenous schools in the Madras Presidency
    1835 Macaulay’s Minute on Indian Education
    1835–1838 William Adam’s reports on education in Bengal and Bihar
    1848 Jotirao and Savitribai Phule’s school for girls in Pune
    1849 Bethune’s school for girls, Calcutta
    1854 Wood’s Despatch
    1857 Universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras
    1866 Seminary at Deoband founded
    1876 Rassundari Devi, Āmār Jīban
    1882 Education (Hunter) Commission; Leitner’s report on the Punjab; Phule’s memorial
    1892 Khalsa College, Amritsar
    1898 Tahzīb-i Niswān, Lahore
    1902 Gurukul Kangri founded
    1904 Sikh Kanya Mahavidyalaya, Ferozepur, takes shape as a boarding school
    1906 Girls’ school at Aligarh (Shaikh ʿAbdullāh and Wahīd Jahān Begum)
    1909–1911 Rokeya Sakhawat Hossain’s school for Muslim girls (Bhagalpur, then Calcutta)
    1915–16 Department of experimental psychology, University of Calcutta
    1916 Indian Women’s University (later SNDT), Pune
    1921 Visva-Bharati
    1922 Girindrasekhar Bose founds the Indian Psychoanalytical Society, Calcutta
    1936 Ambedkar, Annihilation of Caste
    1937 Gandhi’s scheme of Basic Education (Nai Talīm)
    1979 Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction programme begun
    1983 Dharampal, The Beautiful Tree
    2003 National Mission for Manuscripts; UNESCO recognition of Vedic chanting
    2009 Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act
    2010 Nalanda University Act
    2020 National Education Policy
