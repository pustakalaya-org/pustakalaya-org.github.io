    Chapter 22: Encrypted Traffic Analysis (ETA)¶

    When everything is encrypted, the inspector must work with what is left: handshake metadata, packet sizes, timing, certificate features, and domain reputation.

    22.1 TLS fingerprints¶

    JA3 / JA3S.

    A hash of selected ClientHello (JA3) and ServerHello (JA3S) fields. Two clients with the same TLS stack and configuration produce the same JA3. Project: https://github.com/salesforce/ja3.

    JA4 / JA4+.

    A FoxIO-led successor, more robust to TLS 1.3 field randomisation, with per-protocol variants (JA4S, JA4H for HTTP, JA4X for X.509, JA4L for latency). Project: https://github.com/FoxIO-LLC/ja4.

    HASSH.

    Equivalent fingerprint for SSH. Project: https://github.com/salesforce/hassh.

    TLS GREASE.

    Random values inserted by clients to ensure servers tolerate unknown values. JA3 is sensitive to GREASE; JA4 is not.

    22.2 Domain reputation & DNS analytics¶

    • Newly Registered Domains (NRDs) feeds.

    • Passive DNS (Farsight DNSDB, Mnemonic).

    • DNS over HTTPS (DoH) / DNS over TLS (DoT) – hides queries from the local resolver but leaves a TLS handshake to the DoH provider, which can itself be fingerprinted.

    22.3 Certificate analytics¶

    A self-signed or recently issued (Let’s Encrypt) cert plus a fresh domain plus a high-entropy SNI = classic C2 setup. ssl-anomaly Zeek package flags this in seconds.

    22.4 Cisco’s ETA approach¶

    Cisco published the Encrypted Traffic Analytics approach using:

    • the Initial Data Packet (IDP) – first 1500 bytes,

    • the Sequence of Packet Lengths and Times (SPLT),

    • byte distribution of unencrypted bytes.

    Joy (Section 7) exposes these features.
