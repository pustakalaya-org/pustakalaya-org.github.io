    About this book¶

    This book is a friendly but technically faithful walk through the family of technologies that look inside, measure, and reason about network traffic: Deep Packet Inspection (DPI), Network Detection and Response (NDR), and their close cousins (IDS, IPS, NTA, NDR, EDR, XDR, SIEM, SOAR).

    Two readers are kept in mind on every page:

    • the layman, who has perhaps wondered how an internet provider knows you are streaming video, or how a corporate firewall blocks a malicious upload — and who wants to understand without drowning in jargon;

    • the undergraduate computer-science student, who needs the precise terminology, the tools, the open-source projects on GitHub, the command lines, and the techniques used in real Security Operations Centres (SOCs).

    Where the topic gets technical, look for the For the Layman and For the Undergraduate side boxes; one summarises in plain language, the other adds the formal detail.

    How to read it.

    You can read it cover to cover, but each chapter is largely self-contained. Parts I–III lay the foundation and main concepts; Parts IV–V cover the open-source toolset; Part VI explores techniques; Part VII is hands-on; Part VIII looks ahead. Appendices collect tools, GitHub links, and a glossary.

    A note on ethics.

    The techniques described here can be used to defend a network and to violate privacy. The line between the two is authorisation. Inspect only traffic on networks you own or are explicitly authorised to monitor. Local laws (GDPR, India’s DPDP Act, the US Wiretap Act, etc.) impose hard limits. Chapter 30 discusses this in more depth.
