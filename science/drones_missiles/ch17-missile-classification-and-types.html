    Chapter 17: Missile Classification and Types¶

    17.1 Introduction to Missile Classification¶

    Missiles are self-propelled, guided munitions that deliver a payload—typically a warhead—to a designated target by means of aerodynamic, ballistic, or combined flight. Their taxonomy spans an extraordinary range of capability: from man-portable infantry weapons that weigh less than 2 kg and engage targets at 500 m, to intercontinental ballistic missiles whose 10,000 km range and 800 kg thermonuclear re-entry vehicles represent the apex of strategic deterrence. Rational classification relies on several overlapping schemes: trajectory type (ballistic vs. aerodynamic-lift sustained); launch platform (surface, air, subsurface); target type (air, surface, sub-surface, satellite); and speed regime (subsonic, supersonic, hypersonic).

    A complete missile system is composed of five fundamental subsystems:

    1. Propulsion — solid-propellant rocket motor (SRM), liquid-propellant rocket engine (LRE), air-breathing engine (turbojet, turbofan, ramjet, scramjet), or combined-cycle.

    2. Airframe and Structures — body, fins, nose cone, control surfaces, thermal-protection system (TPS).

    3. Guidance, Navigation and Control (GNC) — seekers, inertial measurement unit (IMU), datalinks, autopilot, and flight-termination system.

    4. Warhead and Fuzing — energetic payload; proximity (radar, laser, optical), contact, or command detonation.

    5. Power — thermal battery, turbogenerator, silver–zinc battery for electronics and electromechanical actuators.

    The NATO four-letter code (launch platform \(\to\) target type; e.g. SAM = Surface-to-Air Missile, AAM = Air-to-Air Missile) remains the lingua franca for categorisation and is used throughout this chapter.

    17.2 Ballistic Missiles¶

    17.2.1 Trajectory Mechanics and Phases¶

    A ballistic missile follows a free-fall Keplerian arc during the majority of its flight. Three canonical phases define the trajectory:

    Boost Phase¶

    Thrust from one or more propulsion stages accelerates the missile from rest to burnout velocity \(V_{bo}\). Burnout typically occurs at altitudes of 100–400 km depending on range class and trajectory shaping. The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation gives the ideal velocity increment imparted by a single stage:

    \[\Delta V = I_{sp}\,g_0 \ln\!\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right)\tag{17.1}\]

    where \(I_{sp}\) is specific impulse (s), \(g_0 = 9.80665\) m/s\(^2\) is standard gravitational acceleration, \(m_0\) is initial (wet) mass, and \(m_f\) is final (dry) mass after propellant expenditure. For a multi-stage missile the total velocity increment is the sum over all stages \(i = 1, …, N\):

    \[\Delta V_{total} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} I_{sp,i}\,g_0 \ln\!\left(\frac{m_{0,i}}{m_{f,i}}\right)\tag{17.2}\]

    For an ICBM required to achieve \(V_{bo} \approx 7{,}200\) m/s (depressed trajectory) to \(7{,}900\) m/s (minimum-energy trajectory against a 10,000 km target), with realistic staging mass fractions \(\lambda_i = m_{f,i}/m_{0,i} \approx 0.10\)–\(0.15\) per stage and \(I_{sp} \approx 280\)–\(295\) s (composite solid propellant), three propulsion stages are required. Gravity and drag losses during boost reduce the achievable \(\Delta V\) by 150–400 m/s depending on trajectory profile.

    Mid-Course Phase¶

    Post-burnout, the re-entry vehicle (RV) travels on a purely ballistic arc. In the exo-atmospheric environment (above \(\approx\)80 km) aerodynamic forces are negligible; the RV follows an elliptical orbit with the Earth’s centre at one focus. The apogee altitude \(h_{apo}\) for a minimum-energy ICBM trajectory to surface range \(R\) on a spherical Earth (radius \(R_E = 6{,}371\) km) is:

    \[h_{apo} = R_E\!\left[\sec\!\left(\frac{\psi}{2}\right) - 1\right]\tag{17.3}\]

    where \(\psi = R/R_E\) is the Earth-central angle. For \(R = 10{,}000\) km: \(\psi = 1.570\) rad, \(h_{apo} \approx 1{,}200\) km.

    Modern ICBMs carry post-boost vehicles (PBVs) — manoeuvring buses with storable liquid-propellant engines — that release multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles (MIRVs) on precisely computed vectors to separate aimpoints within a lethal footprint spanning hundreds of kilometres. Bus propellant mass fractions of 0.05–0.10 of total launch mass are typical.

    Terminal (Re-entry) Phase¶

    Re-entry commences at approximately 100 km altitude at velocities of 6–7 km/s. The ballistic coefficient governs both deceleration rate and aerodynamic heating:

    \[\beta = \frac{m}{C_D\,A}\tag{17.4}\]

    where \(m\) is RV mass, \(C_D\) is drag coefficient, and \(A\) is reference cross-sectional area. High-\(\beta\) RVs (heavy, compact) penetrate more deeply before appreciable deceleration; a Mk-21 RV has \(\beta \approx 10{,}000\) kg/m\(^2\). The deceleration–altitude equation of motion:

    \[\frac{dV}{dt} = -\frac{\rho(h)\,V^2}{2\beta} + g\sin\gamma\tag{17.5}\]

    where \(\gamma\) is flight-path angle (negative for descent) and \(\rho(h)\) follows an exponential atmosphere model \(\rho = \rho_0 e^{-h/H_s}\) with scale height \(H_s \approx 7{,}400\) m.

    Terminal Mach number at detonation altitude (\(\sim\)500 m) ranges from Mach 5 (high-drag re-entry body) to Mach 25 (high-\(\beta\), low-drag slender RV entering at steep angle). The stagnation-point heat flux on a spherical-nose RV (Sutton–Graves relation):

    \[\dot{q}_s = C_{SG}\sqrt{\frac{\rho_\infty}{R_N}}\,V_\infty^3\tag{17.6}\]

    where \(C_{SG} = 1.83 \times 10^{-4}\) kg\(^{0.5}\)m\(^{-1}\)s\(^{-0.5}\) is the Sutton–Graves constant, \(R_N\) is nose radius (m), and \(\rho_\infty\), \(V_\infty\) are free-stream density (kg/m\(^3\)) and velocity (m/s). Integrated heat load for an ICBM RV reaches \(\mathcal{O}(10^9)\) J/m\(^2\); carbon–carbon composites with ablative phenolic back-fill plus a low-density silicone-based outer layer are required.

    17.2.3 Re-Entry Vehicle Types¶

    Single Re-entry Vehicle (SRV/MRV).

    Early ballistic missiles carried a single RV. Multiple Re-entry Vehicles (MRV) spread warheads in a fixed, non-independently-targetable pattern (e.g. early Polaris A-3).

    MIRV — Multiple Independently Targetable Re-entry Vehicle.

    The PBV releases each RV with a precisely computed velocity vector aimed at a separate aimpoint. Targeting flexibility is maximised: a single Trident II D5 can deliver up to 8\(\times\)Mk-5 RVs to aimpoints spread across a \(>\)1,000 km footprint. MIRV capability demands highly accurate IMU (drift \(<\)0.01 nmi/hr) and digital terrain maps for bus navigation.

    MaRV — Manoeuvring Re-entry Vehicle.

    The RV uses aerodynamic surfaces or small thrusters during terminal descent to manoeuvre, improving accuracy against moving targets and complicating intercept geometry. Manoeuvrability generates sustained lateral accelerations of 5–15\(g\). The DF-21D ASBM incorporates MaRV capability for carrier-strike missions; cross-range manoeuvre capability reportedly \(\pm\)50–100 km.

    HGV — Hypersonic Glide Vehicle.

    Released at burnout from a ballistic boost stage, the HGV glides at Mach 5–20 at altitudes of 30–80 km, providing continuous lateral manoeuvre capability with unpredictable flight path. Lift-to-drag ratio \(L/D \approx 2\)–\(4\) (blunt lifting body) extends range 30–50% vs. a purely ballistic RV. Examples: DF-17 (equipped with DF-ZF HGV), Avangard (reported Mach 20+, \(>\)2,000 km cross-range capability). The glide range addition over a ballistic baseline:

    \[\Delta R_{glide} = \frac{L}{D} \cdot h_{entry}\tag{17.7}\]

    where \(h_{entry}\) is entry altitude. For \(L/D = 3\), \(h_{entry} = 50\) km: \(\Delta R \approx 150\) km per glide segment; multiple skip-glide cycles compound this.

    17.2.5 Submarine-Launched Ballistic Missiles (SLBM)¶

    SLBMs introduce unique engineering constraints absent from silo-based systems. The Trident II D5 (UGM-133A) is ejected from Ohio-class SSBNs cruising at depths of 30–50 m. Cold launch (gas-generator ejection) accelerates the 59,090 kg missile from the 2.11 m-diameter tube at \(V_{exit} \approx 20\) m/s; the first-stage motor ignites approximately 5 m above the water surface. A solid rubber shroud protects the aerosurfaces during water-column transit.

    Key trajectory milestones:

    1. High-pressure gas fills launch tube; missile ejected upward through water column (\(\Delta t \approx 2\) s).

    2. Rubber shroud ablates/burns away at first-stage ignition.

    3. Guidance computer receives GPS update within 30 s of emergence.

    4. Post-boost vehicle deploys MIRVs over \(\approx\)5 min bus-manoeuvre sequence.

    Specifications: length 13.41 m; diameter 2.11 m; launch mass 59,090 kg; stage 1 \(I_{sp}\) = 283 s (HTPB composite); range \(>\)11,300 km; accuracy (CEP) 90 m with GPS-aided stellar-inertial navigation. The SWC-13A stellar-inertial navigation unit achieves positional accuracy of \(<\)30 m after star-fix update during mid-course.

    17.2.6 Anti-Ship Ballistic Missiles (ASBM)¶

    The DF-21D (CSS-5 Mod-4) pioneered the anti-carrier ASBM concept. Key parameters: range \(\approx 1{,}500\) km; warhead \(\approx 600\) kg conventional penetrator; terminal velocity Mach 10–12. The DF-26 intermediate-range ASBM extends the anti-ship engagement envelope to \(\approx 4{,}000\) km, covering the second island chain.

    The terminal guidance challenge is formidable: the RV must detect and home on a manoeuvring carrier while decelerating from \(V \approx 3{,}000\) m/s to impact. The RV carries an active radar seeker (synthetic aperture or ISAR mode) combined with an inertial reference for mid-course. Cross-range manoeuvre capability \(\pm 50\)–\(100\) km requires aerodynamic control surfaces generating lift forces against a deceleration of \(\mathcal{O}(10^4)\) m/s\(^2\).

    TPS heat load estimate using the Fay–Riddell correlation at the stagnation point:

    \[\dot{q}_{FR} = 0.763\,\Pr^{-0.6}\!\left(\frac{\rho_w \mu_w}{\rho_e \mu_e}\right)^{0.1}\!\sqrt{\rho_e \mu_e \frac{du_e}{dx}}\,(h_{aw} - h_w)\tag{17.8}\]

    Peak \(\dot{q}\) at Mach 12 re-entry is \(\approx 5\)–\(20\) MW/m\(^2\) depending on nose radius; C-SiC or AVCOAT ablative TPS is required.

    17.3 Cruise Missiles¶

    17.3.1 Flight Profile and Operating Principles¶

    Cruise missiles sustain aerodynamic lift throughout their flight, relying on air-breathing propulsion. This permits terrain-following flight at \(<\)50 m altitude and sea-skimming at 2–5 m, yielding extremely low radar-detection ranges. The Breguet range equation governs cruise-missile range:

    \[R = \frac{V\,\eta_p}{c_T} \cdot \frac{L}{D} \cdot \ln\!\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right)\tag{17.9}\]

    where \(V\) is cruise speed (m/s), \(\eta_p\) is propulsive efficiency, \(c_T\) is thrust-specific fuel consumption (TSFC, kg N\(^{-1}\)h\(^{-1}\)), \(L/D\) is lift-to-drag ratio, \(W_0\) is take-off gross weight, and \(W_1\) is final weight.

    Worked example (Tomahawk Block IV): \(V = 240\) m/s; \(\eta_p = 0.65\); \(c_T = 0.052\) kg N\(^{-1}\)h\(^{-1}\); \(L/D = 4.8\); \(W_0/W_1 = 1.38\):

    \[R = \frac{240 \times 0.65}{0.052/3600} \times 4.8 \times \ln(1.38) \approx 1{,}590\text{ km}\tag{17.10}\]

    This is consistent with the published \(>\)1,600 km range.

    17.3.2 Propulsion Systems¶

    Small turbofan (subsonic cruise).

    The Williams International F107-WR-402 turbofan powers the BGM-109C/D Tomahawk: thrust 3.1 kN; TSFC 0.052 kg N\(^{-1}\)h\(^{-1}\); bypass ratio 1.0; mass flow 9.0 kg/s; overall pressure ratio 8:1; turbine inlet temperature 1,300 K; length 0.74 m; mass 60 kg.

    Ramjet sustainer (supersonic/hypersonic cruise).

    BrahMos employs a combined-cycle: solid-rocket booster (acceleration to Mach 2) followed by a liquid-fuel ramjet sustainer producing \(\approx\)20 kN at Mach 3, \(I_{sp,ram} \approx 1{,}400\)–\(1{,}600\) s. The specific thrust in a ramjet:

    \[F_{ram}/\dot{m} = (1 + f)(V_e - V_0) + (p_e - p_0)A_e/\dot{m}\tag{17.11}\]

    where \(f\) is fuel-to-air ratio, \(V_e\) is nozzle-exit velocity, \(V_0\) is free-stream velocity, and \(p_e\), \(p_0\) are nozzle-exit and ambient pressures.

    17.3.3 Integrated Navigation Architecture¶

    Modern cruise missiles combine multiple complementary navigation modes via a federated Kalman filter:

    \[\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + \mathbf{K}_k\bigl(\mathbf{z}_k - \mathbf{H}_k\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}\bigr)\tag{17.12}\]

    1. Inertial Navigation System (INS): Ring-laser gyroscope (RLG) triaxial IMU provides autonomous dead-reckoning position; error growth \(\approx 0.8\) nmi/hr (LN-100G).

    2. TERCOM (Terrain Contour Matching): Radar altimeter measures ground-clearance profile; cross-correlated with stored digital terrain elevation data (DTED); fix accuracy 30–100 m; update rate \(\sim\)1 Hz. Ineffective over featureless terrain (desert, ocean).

    3. DSMAC (Digital Scene Matching Area Correlator): Terminal EO/IR image correlated with stored scene reference; CEP 3–10 m; sensitive to seasonal change, weather, and low-contrast scenes.

    4. GPS/GNSS: Anti-jam military M-code GPS; CEP \(<\)5 m in benign environment; vulnerable to jamming and spoofing in contested airspace.

    Combined (Block IV Tomahawk): CEP \(<\)10 m vs. GPS coordinates; CEP \(<\)3 m with DSMAC terminal update.

    17.3.4 Subsonic Cruise Missiles¶

    BGM-109 Tomahawk (TLAM).

    Length 6.25 m (with booster); diameter 0.52 m; wingspan 2.67 m (deployed); launch weight 1,590 kg; warhead 450 kg PBXN-107 blast-frag (TLAM-C) or WDU-36 1,000-lb penetrator (TLAM-D); cruise speed Mach 0.72 (880 km/h); range \(>\)1,600 km; CEP \(<\)10 m. Block V adds two-way data-link for in-flight retargeting and battle-damage imagery transmission.

    AGM-86C/D ALCM.

    Carried internally by B-52H (rotary launcher, 8 rounds; external, 12 rounds); Williams F107 turbofan; range 1,250 km; speed Mach 0.73; warhead W80-1 variable-yield nuclear (5–150 kT) or 900 kg conventional blast/penetrator; terrain-following flight at 50–100 m.

    Storm Shadow / SCALP-EG.

    Anglo-French; length 5.10 m; wingspan 3.0 m; mass 1,300 kg; Microturbo TRI60-30 turbofan; range 560 km; BROACH 450 kg penetrator warhead (initial hard-target penetrator followed by main charge); dual-mode IIR terminal seeker; CEP \(<\)3 m; very-low RCS due to faceted underside and S-duct intake.

    17.3.5 Supersonic Cruise Missiles¶

    BrahMos.

    India–Russia joint development. Range 500 km (Block III extended); cruise Mach 2.8–3.0 at 14 km altitude; terminal sea-skim at Mach 3.5; length 8.4 m (ground-launched); mass 3,000 kg; warhead 200–300 kg general-purpose or penetrator; active radar seeker with sea-state bias; terminal altitude \(<\)5 m in final 15 km. Mach 3 provides warning time of \(\approx\)25 s from radar horizon (22 km) to impact.

    3M22 Zircon (Tsirkon, SS-N-33).

    Russia; Mach 8–9 at 30–40 km cruise altitude; range \(\approx\)1,000 km; propulsion: dual-mode pulse-detonation or scramjet-assisted ramjet; warhead \(\approx\)300 kg; plasma sheath during terminal phase theoretically defeats active radar intercept; ship-launched from Gorshkov-class frigates and Yasen-class SSGNs.

    Kh-22 (AS-4 Kitchen).

    Air-launched anti-ship/land-attack; Mach 4.6; length 11.67 m; mass 5,780 kg; warhead 1,000 kg conventional or nuclear; 22 km cruise altitude followed by steep terminal dive at Mach 4.6; R201-300 turbojet.

    17.3.6 Stealth Cruise Missiles¶

    17.4 Surface-to-Air Missiles (SAM)¶

    17.4.1 Design Principles and Engagement Geometry¶

    A SAM system must detect, track, discriminate, and intercept an airborne threat within a defended area. The probability of kill \(P_k\) for a single interceptor:

    \[P_k = P_{det} \times P_{track} \times P_{launch} \times P_{intercept} \times P_{fuze} \times P_{lethality}\tag{17.13}\]

    Each term is a conditional probability. Modern SAM systems achieve single-shot \(P_k\) of 0.70–0.95 against non-manoeuvring subsonic aircraft. Against tactical ballistic missiles, \(P_k\) degrades to 0.40–0.80 depending on the target’s speed, manoeuvre, and decoy signature.

    The lethal radius \(r_L\) of a proximity-fuzed warhead fragment pattern (simplified uniform fragment distribution):

    \[r_L = \left[\frac{N_f\,m_f\,v_f^2\,(1 - e^{-k\,l})}{4\pi\,\rho_{tgt}}\right]^{1/3}\tag{17.14}\]

    where \(N_f\) is fragment count, \(m_f\) is individual fragment mass, \(v_f\) is fragment velocity, \(k\) is material absorption coefficient, and \(l\) is penetration path length. Typical \(r_L\): 5–15 m for SHORAD warheads; 15–50 m for medium-SAM warheads; 50–150 m for large-area-defence warheads.

    17.5 Air-to-Air Missiles (AAM)¶

    17.5.1 Engagement Kinematics and No-Escape Zone¶

    The AAM engagement problem requires delivering sufficient kinetic energy to the target such that the missile can execute the terminal manoeuvre and achieve a lethal miss distance. Proportional Navigation (PN) is the dominant homing law:

    \[\dot{\gamma}_m = N' \dot{\sigma} V_c\tag{17.15}\]

    where \(\gamma_m\) is missile flight-path angle, \(N' = 3\)–\(5\) is the effective navigation ratio, \(\dot{\sigma}\) is line-of-sight (LOS) rate, and \(V_c\) is closing velocity. The required lateral acceleration command:

    \[a_{cmd} = N'\,V_c\,\dot{\sigma}\tag{17.16}\]

    For \(N' = 4\), \(V_c = 1{,}500\) m/s, \(\dot{\sigma} = 0.05\) rad/s: \(a_{cmd} = 300\) m/s\(^2 \approx 30.6\,g\).

    The No-Escape Zone (NEZ) boundary is the launch range from which a manoeuvring target cannot escape regardless of evasion tactic. Closing rate:

    \[V_c = V_m + V_t\cos(\theta_{asp})\tag{17.17}\]

    where \(\theta_{asp}\) is the target aspect angle. Lead angle for a beam target:

    \[\sin\lambda = \frac{V_t}{V_m}\sin(\theta_{asp})\tag{17.18}\]

    The NEZ radius (simplified, no gravity, constant speeds):

    \[R_{NEZ} \approx \frac{V_m - V_t\,n_{tgt}}{a_{lat,max}}\cdot V_m\tag{17.19}\]

    where \(n_{tgt}\) is target manoeuvre in \(g\) units. Modern BVRAAM missiles expand the NEZ by 3–5\(\times\) relative to older gun-era SRAAM through sustained high Mach cruise.

    17.6 Anti-Tank Guided Missiles (ATGM)¶

    17.6.1 Guidance Generation Taxonomy¶

    First Generation: MCLOS (Manual Command to Line of Sight).

    The operator simultaneously tracks both the missile (via a tracer flare) and the target, issuing corrections with a joystick via wire link. Human reaction time limits accuracy; CEP \(\approx 2\)–\(3\) m under ideal conditions. Examples: 9M14 Malyutka (AT-3), ENTAC, SS.11. Effective range limited to \(\approx 3{,}000\) m by wire guidance and operator fatigue.

    Second Generation: SACLOS (Semi-Automatic Command to Line of Sight).

    Operator tracks the target only; an IR tracker on the launch unit automatically measures the missile’s angular deviation from the LOS and generates corrections. Milan-2T: CEP \(\approx\)300 mm at 2,000 m; operator-skill-independent accuracy. The guidance law:

    \[\mathbf{u}_{guidance} = K_p(\sigma_{target} - \sigma_{missile}) + K_d\,\dot{\sigma}_{error}\tag{17.21}\]

    where \(K_p\), \(K_d\) are proportional and derivative gains; \(\sigma_{target}\), \(\sigma_{missile}\) are angular coordinates.

    Third Generation: Fire-and-Forget.

    Missile homes autonomously after launch; operator need not track the target. Guidance: IIR FPA or millimetre-wave (mmW) radar seeker. Enables shoot-and-scoot tactics; operator survivability dramatically increased.

    17.6.2 HEAT Warhead Physics¶

    The Monroe (shaped-charge) effect creates a hyper-velocity metal jet upon detonation. Theoretical penetration from the Birkhoff–Riabouchinsky model:

    \[P_{pen} = L_j\,\sqrt{\frac{\rho_j}{\rho_{target}}}\tag{17.22}\]

    where \(L_j\) is jet length and \(\rho_j\), \(\rho_{target}\) are jet (copper \(\approx 8{,}900\) kg/m\(^3\)) and target (RHA steel \(\approx 7{,}860\) kg/m\(^3\)) densities. A practical design rule-of-thumb:

    \[P_{pen} \approx (5\text{--}8)\,D_{sc}\sqrt{\frac{\rho_j}{\rho_{RHA}}}\tag{17.23}\]

    where \(D_{sc}\) is shaped-charge diameter. For \(D_{sc} = 110\) mm: \(P_{pen} \approx 550\)–\(880\) mm RHA equivalent. The standoff distance must be 5–8 \(D_{sc}\) to allow full jet formation; this is achieved by a folding nose spike or crushable nose.

    Tandem HEAT.

    Against ERA (Explosive Reactive Armour), a tandem warhead is essential. The precursor (small) charge detonates the ERA cassette, clearing the path for the main charge. ERA displacement accounts for \(\approx 200\)–\(400\) mm equivalent RHA protection. Tandem penetration:

    \[P_{tandem} = P_{main} - \Delta P_{ERA}\tag{17.24}\]

    Javelin tandem: precursor \(\phi = 23\) mm clears ERA; main charge \(\phi = 110\) mm achieves \(P_{main} > 750\) mm RHA; net penetration post-ERA \(\approx 400\)–\(550\) mm.

    17.7 Anti-Ship Missiles (AShM)¶

    17.7.1 Sea-Skimming Flight Dynamics¶

    A sea-skimming missile flying at altitude \(h\) exploits the Earth’s curvature to minimise radar detection range. The radar line-of-sight (LOS) horizon from a ship radar antenna at height \(H_{radar}\):

    \[R_{hor} = \sqrt{2k_e R_E H_{radar}} + \sqrt{2k_e R_E h}\tag{17.25}\]

    where \(k_e \approx 1.333\) is the effective Earth radius factor (4/3 Earth model for standard atmosphere). For \(H_{radar} = 20\) m and \(h = 3\) m: \(R_{hor} \approx 24\) km, providing warning time of:

    \[t_{warn} = \frac{R_{hor}}{V_{missile}}\tag{17.26}\]

    At Mach 0.9 (\(V \approx 308\) m/s): \(t_{warn} \approx 78\) s; at Mach 3 (\(\approx 1{,}020\) m/s): \(t_{warn} \approx 24\) s; at Mach 9 (\(\approx 3{,}060\) m/s): \(t_{warn} \approx 8\) s — less than CIWS engagement time.

    Altitude maintenance uses a closed-loop radar altimeter:

    \[\dot{h}_{cmd} = K_h(h_{ref} - h_{meas}) + K_{dh}\,\dot{h}_{meas}\tag{17.27}\]

    Minimum achievable sea-skim altitude in Sea State 4 (significant wave height \(\approx\)2.5 m): \(h_{min} \approx 3\)–\(5\) m.

    17.7.5 Saturation Attack Tactics and Ship Vulnerability¶

    A saturation raid is designed to overwhelm layered ship defences. The critical rate equation for defence:

    \[R_{max} = \frac{1}{t_{engage}} \quad [\text{targets/second per weapon}]\tag{17.28}\]

    Phalanx Block 1B: \(t_{engage} \approx 2\) s; \(R_{max} = 0.5\) target/s. A 4-missile salvo arriving in a 3 s window from two azimuths saturates a single Phalanx. Coordinated tactics: (1) separate missile arrivals to \(\Delta t < 1\) s; (2) one decoy missile (large RCS) plus one stealth attacker; (3) mix of high sea-skim plus diving attacker to present two elevation layers simultaneously.

    Ship vulnerability zones:

    • Magazine hit: detonation of own ordnance; catastrophic (\(>\)90% probability of loss).

    • Waterline penetration: flooding; loss of buoyancy; ship may survive with damage control.

    • Superstructure hit: fires, radar/comms damage; combat capability degraded.

    • Engineering spaces: loss of propulsion; ship disabled but may survive.

    Countermeasure hierarchy: outer layer (SM-2/6 at 100–150 km), middle layer (ESSM at 50 km), inner layer (RAM/SeaRAM at 9 km), last-ditch (Phalanx at 2 km), passive (chaff, towed decoys, IR flares, jamming).

    17.8 Anti-Satellite (ASAT) Systems¶

    17.8.1 Direct-Ascent ASAT¶

    Direct-ascent systems launch a KKV on a modified ballistic trajectory to the target satellite’s orbital altitude. The PL-19 Nudol (Russia) demonstrated ASAT capability against Cosmos 1408 (November 2021), creating \(>\)1,500 trackable debris fragments (\(>\)10 cm) and estimated \(>\)15,000 fragments \(>\)1 cm at altitudes 450–650 km.

    Intercept closing velocity for a counter-orbit (retrograde) intercept:

    \[V_c = V_{orbit} + V_{KKV,z} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}} + V_{ascent,z}\tag{17.29}\]

    For LEO at 500 km: \(V_{orbit} = 7.61\) km/s; \(V_{KKV,z} \approx 1\) km/s; \(V_c \approx 8.5\)–\(9\) km/s. Kinetic energy at impact (\(m_{KKV} = 60\) kg, \(V_c = 8{,}500\) m/s):

    \[E_k = \frac{1}{2}\,m_{KKV}\,V_c^2 = \frac{1}{2}(60)(8500)^2 = 2.17\text{ GJ}\tag{17.30}\]

    Equivalent to 519 kg TNT — no conventional warhead required; the debris field IS the kill mechanism.

    17.8.2 Kessler Syndrome and Orbital Debris¶

    The self-sustaining cascade criterion compares collision generation rate with debris decay rate:

    \[\frac{dN}{dt} = \alpha N^2 - \beta N\tag{17.31}\]

    where \(\alpha\) is the collision rate coefficient (proportional to orbital flux and mean cross-section) and \(\beta\) is the decay rate constant (atmospheric drag, predominantly above 200 km). For 400 km altitude, \(\beta \gg \alpha N\): benign, self-cleansing. For 700 km altitude, \(\alpha N \approx \beta\): marginal stability. Above 800 km: \(\alpha N > \beta\) — an existing debris population can self-sustain even without new launches.

    17.8.3 Co-Orbital ASAT¶

    A manoeuvring “inspector satellite” approaches a target in the same orbital plane. Rendezvous \(\Delta V\) using the Clohessy–Wiltshire equations for in-plane manoeuvre:

    \[\ddot{x} - 2n\dot{y} - 3n^2 x = f_x;\qquad \ddot{y} + 2n\dot{x} = f_y\tag{17.32}\]

    where \((x, y)\) are radial/in-track relative positions, \(n = \sqrt{\mu/r^3}\) is mean motion, and \((f_x, f_y)\) are specific thrust components. Russian Cosmos 2542/2543 manoeuvred to within \(\approx\)150 km of USA-245 in January 2020, demonstrating proximity operations.

    17.8.4 Directed-Energy ASAT¶

    Ground-based HEL irradiance at satellite altitude \(R\):

    \[I_{sat} = \frac{P_{laser}\,\eta_{atm}\,D_{ap}^2}{(1.22\,\lambda\,R)^2\,\pi}\tag{17.33}\]

    where \(P_{laser}\) is beam power, \(\eta_{atm}\) is atmospheric transmission (\(\sim\)0.5–0.8), \(\lambda\) is wavelength, and \(D_{ap}\) is aperture diameter. Dazzle threshold \(I_{dazzle} \approx 10\) W/cm\(^2\); damage threshold \(I_{damage} \approx 10\)–\(100\) kW/cm\(^2\). For \(P_{laser} = 1\) MW, \(D_{ap} = 1.5\) m, \(\lambda = 1.06~\mu\)m, \(R = 500\) km, \(\eta_{atm} = 0.6\):

    \[I_{sat} = \frac{10^6 \times 0.6 \times 1.5^2}{(1.22 \times 1.06\times10^{-6}\times 5\times10^5)^2 \pi} \approx 3.1\text{ kW/cm}^2\]

    This marginally achieves damage threshold; adaptive optics (AO) correction of atmospheric turbulence increases \(I_{sat}\) by a factor of 2–5.

    17.9 Loitering Munitions¶

    17.9.1 Concept and Design Philosophy¶

    Loitering munitions (LM), also termed kamikaze drones or suicide UAS, combine the persistent area-surveillance capability of a UAS with the precision-lethality of a guided munition. Defining characteristics:

    • Dual-phase mission: loiter (reconnaissance/target-acquisition mode) then strike (munition mode).

    • Operator-in-the-loop (OITL): human authorises final attack — legally necessary under LOAC for autonomous lethal engagement.

    • Wave-off capability: operator can abort attack; missile continues loitering or returns to pre-programmed abort waypoint.

    • Low unit cost ($10,000–$100,000) enabling expendable mass-attrition operations and cost-asymmetric warfare.

    • Tube-launched or canister-launched for minimal logistical footprint; man-portable.

    17.9.2 Propulsion and Endurance¶

    Electric brushless-DC motor with folding propeller dominates small LM (\(<\)10 kg). Endurance:

    \[t_{endurance} = \frac{\eta_{mot}\,\eta_{prop}\,E_{batt}}{P_{req,cruise}}\tag{17.34}\]

    where cruise power:

    \[P_{req} = \frac{1}{2}\rho V_c^3 C_D A_{ref} + \frac{2\,W^2}{\rho V_c A_{wing} \pi e \mathcal{AR}}\tag{17.35}\]

    (parasite + induced power; \(e\) = Oswald efficiency; \(\mathcal{AR}\) = aspect ratio). Worked example (Switchblade 300): \(V_c = 35\) m/s; \(C_D = 0.055\); \(A_{ref} = 0.008\) m\(^2\); \(W = 25\) N; \(A_{wing} = 0.12\) m\(^2\); \(\mathcal{AR} = 4\); \(e = 0.8\); \(\rho = 1.225\) kg/m\(^3\): \(P_{parasite} \approx 18\) W; \(P_{induced} \approx 5\) W; \(P_{req} \approx 23\) W. Battery: \(E_{batt} = 6\) Ah\(\times\)22.2 V\(\times\)0.85 = 113 Wh; \(\eta_{mot}\eta_{prop} = 0.80\); \(t = 113 \times 3600 \times 0.80 / (23 \times 3600) \approx 4.0\) hr. Actual endurance \(\approx\)10 min (accounting for terminal dive \(\approx\)full throttle and a conservative usable battery fraction of 0.12 at low temperature).

    17.9.3 Seeker and Terminal Guidance¶

    EO/IR gimballed camera provides imagery to the remote operator via encrypted RF data-link. Uncooled microbolometer (320\(\times\)256 pixels, 17 \(\mu\)m pitch) for night operation; EO for day. Terminal guidance:

    \[\dot{\gamma}_m = N_{PNG}\,V_c\,\dot{\sigma}\tag{17.36}\]

    Impact precision: CEP \(<\)1 m at ranges \(<\)10 km under calm wind. Cross-wind correction required at final approach:

    \[\delta_{aileron} = K_{cw}\,\frac{V_{wind}}{V_m}\cos(\psi_{wind} - \psi_m)\tag{17.37}\]

    17.9.4 Key Loitering Munition Systems¶

    Switchblade 300.

    AeroVironment (US); tube-launched; diameter 38 mm (canister); folded length 600 mm; missile mass 2.5 kg; warhead 0.3 kg HEAT or blast-frag; range 10 km; endurance 10 min; speed up to 157 km/h terminal; EO+IR seeker; man-in-the-loop authorisation; wave-off capability; backpack-portable system (6 tubes).

    Switchblade 600.

    23 kg total system; anti-armour warhead (estimated Javelin-equivalent HEAT); range 40 km; endurance 40 min; speed 185 km/h terminal; mmW seeker option. Engages IFV, MBT (thin-roof attack).

    Lancet-3 (ZALA Aero / Kalashnikov).

    12 kg; electric pusher propulsion; EO seeker with target-tracking algorithm; HEAT warhead; range 40 km; extensively used in Russo-Ukraine war (2022–2025) against artillery systems, air-defence radars, armoured vehicles. Estimated unit cost \(\approx\)$35,000.

    Harop (IAI).

    135 kg; 3.0 m span; 6+ hr endurance; range 1,000 km; 23 kg warhead; Williams TJ-150 turbojet; dual-mode: anti-radiation (homes on enemy radar emission via passive radar seeker) or electro-optical (operator selects target via downlink video); used by Azerbaijan (Nagorno-Karabakh 2020) to destroy S-300 and Buk batteries.

    17.9.5 Swarm Effectiveness¶

    Coordinated swarm probability of kill against a single defended target:

    \[P_{k,swarm} = 1 - \prod_{i=1}^{N}(1 - P_{k,i})\tag{17.38}\]

    For \(N = 10\) LMs, each \(P_{k,i} = 0.30\): \(P_{k,swarm} = 1 - 0.70^{10} = 0.972\). This mathematical basis drives the shift toward high-volume, low-cost munitions over exquisite low-density precision weapons.

    17.10 Naval Missiles¶

    17.10.1 Submarine-Launched Cruise Missiles (SLCM)¶

    The BGM-109 Tomahawk SLCM can be launched from the 533 mm torpedo tube of any US SSN/SSBN or from the Virginia Payload Module (VPM): 28 additional VLS cells added to Virginia Block V giving total of 40 SLCM. Launch from torpedo tube: capsule ejects; missile emerges from water; booster ignites; wings deploy.

    BrahMos SLCM.

    Sealed underwater launch canister; solid booster ignites above water; Mach 3 cruise; range 500 km; available for Akula and Yasen-class SSNs.

    17.10.2 Anti-Submarine Weapons¶

    RUR-5 ASROC.

    Standard US surface-ship weapon: rockets the Mk 54 LWT (Light Weight Torpedo, 274 kg) to 22 km; torpedo enters water on parachute; active sonar seeker homes on submarine. ASROC effectively bypasses the acoustic-advantage of the submarine by shortening closing time from \(>\)30 min (helicopter delivery) to \(<\)60 s.

    VL-ASROC.

    VLS-launched ASROC; same Mk 54 payload; fully compatible with Mk 41 cells.

    The Mk 41 VLS is the backbone of US and allied surface-combatant armament. Specifications: 8 cells per module; cell cross-section 0.625 m \(\times\) 0.625 m; depth 7.72 m; compatible missiles: SM-2/3/6, Tomahawk, ASROC, ESSM (4:1 quad-pack). A DDG-51 Flight III carries 96 VLS cells; an Ticonderoga-class cruiser 122 cells.

    SM-6 (RIM-174).

    Dual-role AAW + surface strike; range 240 km; Mach 3.5; active radar seeker (AIM-120 seeker head); BMD capability; 64 kg warhead. At Mach 3.5, SM-6 intercepts Mach 2–3 AShM at \(>\)100 km standoff.

    17.11 Ballistic Missile Defence (BMD)¶

    17.11.1 The Kill Chain¶

    The BMD kill chain involves five sequential, time-critical functions:

    \[t_{total} = t_{detect} + t_{track} + t_{discrim} + t_{intercept} + t_{assess}\tag{17.39}\]

    For a 1,000 km SRBM (\(t_{flight} \approx 5\) min): \(t_{decision} < 90\) s available for commit-to-fire. DSP/SBIRS satellites detect ICBM boost-phase plume within 30 s of launch; AN/TPY-2 X-band GBR provides tracking accuracy \(<\)50 m at 5,000 km range (\(\sigma_{track} < 0.01\) mrad). Debris discrimination (separating warhead RV from decoys and bus fragments) remains the most technically challenging function:

    \[P_{discrim} = f\!\left(\frac{\Delta\beta_{decoy}}{\sigma_{\beta}}\right)\tag{17.40}\]

    where \(\Delta\beta_{decoy}\) is the ballistic coefficient difference between the RV and decoy, and \(\sigma_\beta\) is measurement uncertainty.

    17.11.2 Hit-to-Kill KKV Physics¶

    Modern KKVs destroy their targets by direct hypervelocity impact. Energy at impact:

    \[E_{impact} = \frac{1}{2}(m_{KKV} + m_{RV})\,V_c^2\tag{17.41}\]

    For \(m_{KKV} = 70\) kg, \(m_{RV} = 800\) kg, \(V_c = 10\) km/s: \(E_{impact} = \frac{1}{2}(870)(10^4)^2 = 4.35 \times 10^{10}\) J = 10.4 kton TNT equivalent — sufficient to completely disintegrate the RV. The KKV terminal guidance closing-error budget:

    \[\sigma_{miss} = \sqrt{\sigma_{nav}^2 + \sigma_{seeker}^2 + \sigma_{dacs}^2}\tag{17.42}\]

    DACS (Divert and Attitude Control System): cold-gas or hydrazine thrusters; specific impulse \(\approx\)220 s; \(\Delta V_{dacs} \approx 200\) m/s for terminal correction.

    17.11.3 THAAD — Terminal High Altitude Area Defense¶

    Intercept altitude 40–150 km (upper endo/lower exo-atmosphere); missile speed \(>\) Mach 8.2 (\(\approx\)2.8 km/s); kill mechanism: direct KKV with no explosive warhead; seeker: cryogenically cooled long-wave IIR FPA with autonomous guidance; DACS thruster package; 8 missiles per launcher; AN/TPY-2 X-band AESA radar (2.4 m aperture; 1,000+ km tracking range); deployed OCONUS and CONUS; single-shot \(P_k\) vs. Scud-class \(\approx\)0.84 (14/17 intercept tests through 2022).

    17.11.4 SM-3 Block IIA Midcourse Interceptor¶

    Three stages: Mk 72 solid booster (1.557 m diameter); Mk 104 dual-thrust solid sustainer; Mk 142 DACS stage with KKV. Total length 6.55 m; diameter 0.533 m; weight 1,500 kg; intercept altitude \(>\)180 km; closing speed \(>\)10 km/s. The KKV has \(\approx\)7 kg mass; IIR seeker with 128\(\times\)128 FLIR array; 3-axis divert thrusters (\(\pm\)60 m/s \(\Delta V\) budget).

    17.12 Technology Trends and Future Developments¶

    17.12.1 Hypersonic Weapons¶

    The proliferation of HGVs and hypersonic cruise missiles (Ma \(>\) 5) represents the most significant shift in missile technology since the ICBM era. Key integration challenges:

    1. Thermal management: sustained Mach 5+ flight generates stagnation temperatures \(T_s = T_\infty\!\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}Ma^2\right)\) at Mach 10, \(T_s \approx 19{,}000\) K (equilibrium air); actual wall temperature (with ablation/radiation cooling) \(T_{wall} \approx 2{,}000\)–\(2{,}500\) K. C–C composites and UHTCs (ZrB\(_2\), HfC, TaC; \(T_{melt} > 3{,}000\)°C) are required.

    2. Plasma sheath communication blackout: electron number density \(n_e > 10^{18}\) m\(^{-3}\) at Mach 15 creates plasma frequency \(f_p = \frac{1}{2\pi}\!\sqrt{n_e e^2/m_e \varepsilon_0} > 10\) GHz, blocking conventional RF. Trailing-wire antenna, HF windows, or in-band transmission protocols are solutions.

    3. Seeker window: SiC, ALON, spinel, or sapphire domes at Mach 5–10; \(T_{surface} > 1{,}000\)°C; transmissivity degrades in mid-wave IR. Active cooling of seeker dome required above Mach 8.

    4. Manufacturing tolerances: leading-edge radius variation of \(\pm\)0.5 mm causes \(>\)200% variation in peak heat flux at Mach 15; tolerance stack-up becomes a primary design constraint.

    17.12.2 Directed Energy Adjuncts¶

    Cost exchange ratio (CER) drives transition to HEL and HPM:

    \[\text{CER} = \frac{C_{interceptor}}{C_{threat}}\tag{17.43}\]

    Iron Dome vs. Qassam rocket: CER \(\approx 50{,}000/500 = 100:1\). Iron Beam (Rafael, 100 kW HEL): \(\approx \$3.50\) per engagement (electricity cost) vs. \(>\$50{,}000\) per Tamir kinetic interceptor. HEL is most effective against slow, unarmoured threats (UAS, mortar, unguided rockets); kinetic interceptors remain necessary for fast manoeuvring missiles.

    17.12.3 Artificial Intelligence Integration¶

    LRASM employs autonomous multi-target discrimination using computer vision:

    • Object detection (YOLOv8-equivalent on embedded GPU) at 30 Hz, classifying vessel type from IIR imagery.

    • Cooperative target-deconfliction: swarm of 4 LRASM shares target tracks via TDMA datalink; one missile attacks, others reassign or continue surveillance.

    • Cognitive ECCM: seeker adapts frequency-hop pattern in response to detected jamming spectrum; machine-learning classifier distinguishes jamming from clutter.

    17.12.4 Modular Design and Mass Production¶

    The Ukraine conflict (2022–present) demonstrated the operational primacy of production rate over peak capability. Industrial bottlenecks: ammonium perchlorate (solid rocket oxidiser, concentrated supply); TSMC-class ICs for guidance; PBXN/IMX energetic formulations (limited US capacity \(\approx\)30,000 precision munitions/yr vs. demand). Modular design philosophy (common seeker + variable warhead + common motor + variable airframe) reduces non-recurring engineering cost and enables rapid capability insertion.
