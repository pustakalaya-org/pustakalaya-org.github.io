    Chapter 29: Missile Testing and Evaluation¶

    29.1 Test and Evaluation Framework¶

    Missile systems undergo the most rigorously structured test and evaluation (T&E) process in the defence acquisition cycle. A modern missile programme may expend 10–20% of its total development budget on T&E activities, ranging from sub-milligram explosive sensitivity tests in a laboratory to multimillion-dollar live-fire events at instrumented ranges. The intellectual architecture governing all of these activities is codified in governing DoD and NATO directives.

    29.1.1 Developmental and Operational Testing¶

    Developmental Testing (DT) is contractor-led (with government oversight) and answers the question: does the system meet its technical specification? DT begins at the component level and builds through subsystems to the full integrated system. Test plans are derived from the System Requirements Document (SRD) and allocated to individual test events in the System Test Plan (STP).

    Operational Testing (OT) is government-led (typically by an independent operational test agency such as the US Army Operational Test Command, ATEC, or the Director, Operational Test and Evaluation, DOT&E) and answers the question: is the system operationally effective and operationally suitable in realistic military conditions? OT uses production-representative hardware, military operators who were not involved in development, and tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) from actual field doctrine.

    Independent Verification and Validation (IV&V) of software is a parallel activity mandated by DoDI 5000.87 for all software-intensive weapon systems.

    29.1.2 Test and Evaluation Master Plan¶

    The Test and Evaluation Master Plan (TEMP) is the primary T&E management document. Per DoDI 5000.89, it addresses:

    • Test and evaluation strategy (integrated DT/OT)

    • Critical technical parameters (CTPs) and critical operational issues (COIs)

    • Resource requirements: ranges, models and simulation, special test equipment

    • Schedule: milestones aligned with programme decision points

    • Risk areas requiring additional testing

    The TEMP is updated at each programme milestone (Milestone A, B, C) and at each major test phase.

    29.1.3 Building-Block Test Philosophy¶

    The building-block approach minimises risk by fully characterising lower-level assemblies before committing to expensive system-level tests. The hierarchy is:

    1. Material and component: individual piece-parts; mechanical, electrical, and energetic characterisation.

    2. Subsystem: propulsion (static fire), guidance (iron bird, HWIL), warhead (arena test), structure (static load test).

    3. System integration: combined subsystem operation; captive-carry with delivery platform; all-up-round (AUR) static test.

    4. System flight test: free-flight with full guidance; warhead function (live or inert).

    5. Operational evaluation: fielded-unit operators; representative scenarios; force-on-force exercises.

    29.1.4 Test Range Requirements¶

    A missile test range must provide:

    • Safety: Range safety officer (RSO); flight termination system (FTS) authority; impact area clearance; Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) and Notice to Mariners (NOTMAR); airspace reservation (MOA/ATCAA).

    • Tracking: Precision trajectory data from multiple redundant sources (radar, GPS, optical) to characterise vehicle state.

    • Telemetry: Real-time down-link of all instrumentation channels; ground station with recording and display; sufficient margin for horizon-limited ranges.

    • Environmental monitoring: Weather balloons (density, wind profile); surface winds at launch point and target area; visibility; turbulence.

    29.2 Propulsion Testing¶

    29.2.1 Static Fire Test¶

    The definitive acceptance test for a solid-propellant rocket motor is the static fire test, in which the motor is mounted horizontally or vertically on a load cell and fired to measure thrust, pressure, and total impulse as functions of time.

    Key instrumentation:

    • Thrust: High-capacity load cell (capacity \(\ge 1.5 \times F_{max}\)); Kistler piezoelectric or strain-gauge type; \(\pm 0.2\%\) full-scale accuracy.

    • Chamber pressure: Multiple Kistler or PCB piezoelectric transducers at injector, mid-grain, and nozzle stagnation locations; 100 kHz sample rate.

    • High-speed cameras: 1,000–10,000 fps; capture plume, nozzle erosion, igniter function, and TVC deflection.

    • Thermal: Thermocouples at case exterior (\(\ge 20\) channels); radiometer for plume emission.

    The total impulse \(I_t\) is:

    \[I_t = \int_0^{t_b} F(t)\,dt\tag{29.1}\]

    where \(t_b\) is burn time. The specific impulse \(I_{sp}\) is:

    \[I_{sp} = \frac{I_t}{m_p \cdot g_0}\tag{29.2}\]

    where \(m_p\) is the propellant mass consumed and \(g_0 = 9.80665\,\text{m/s}^2\). The characteristic velocity \(c^*\) provides a figure of merit for the propellant independent of nozzle efficiency:

    \[c^* = \frac{p_c \cdot A_t}{\dot{m}}\tag{29.3}\]

    29.2.2 Altitude Simulation¶

    Rocket motors perform differently at altitude (reduced back-pressure allows greater nozzle expansion). An altitude simulation facility uses a sealed test cell with a large diffuser downstream of the motor nozzle to maintain a low-pressure environment. The equivalent altitude is set by the cell pressure \(p_{cell}\), which can simulate conditions up to 15,000 m (\(p \approx 12\,\text{kPa}\)) in a ground facility.

    The performance gain from altitude (\(\Delta I_{sp}\)) versus sea level:

    \[\Delta I_{sp} = \frac{(p_e - p_a) A_e}{\dot{m} g_0}\tag{29.4}\]

    where \(p_a\) is the ambient (back) pressure. At vacuum, \(p_a = 0\), and the full vacuum \(I_{sp}\) is realised.

    29.2.3 Combustion Instability Testing¶

    Combustion instability (CI) is a catastrophic resonance between acoustic modes of the motor cavity and heat-release oscillations from the burning propellant. The acoustic natural frequencies of a cylindrical chamber of length \(L\) and radius \(R\) are:

    \[f_{mn} = \frac{c}{2\pi}\sqrt{\left(\frac{m\pi}{L}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\chi_{mn}}{R}\right)^2}\tag{29.5}\]

    where \(c\) is speed of sound in the gas, \(m\) is the longitudinal mode number, and \(\chi_{mn}\) is the \(n\)th zero of the derivative of the \(m\)th Bessel function. Detection uses flush-mounted Kistler or PCB dynamic pressure transducers; fast Fourier transform (FFT) identifies modal frequencies. A peak pressure oscillation exceeding \(\pm 3\%\) of mean chamber pressure is considered unstable.

    29.2.4 Burn Rate and Pressure Exponent Measurement¶

    The Crawford bomb (closed bomb) measures strand burn rates at elevated pressures. A thin propellant strand (6 mm dia \(\times\) 50 mm long) burns in a pressurised nitrogen environment; the time to burn between embedded nichrome wires gives \(\dot{r}(p)\). Fitting the Vieille equation:

    \[\ln\dot{r} = \ln a + n \ln p\tag{29.6}\]

    yields the pressure exponent \(n\) and coefficient \(a\). Propellants with \(n > 0.6\) are at risk of dynamic instability (pressure runaway if chamber pressure exceeds design operating point). The stability criterion requires \(n < 1\).

    29.3 Aerodynamic Testing¶

    29.3.1 Wind Tunnel Testing¶

    Wind tunnel testing provides the aerodynamic database (ADB) that underpins flight simulation, autopilot design, and performance prediction. Missile wind tunnel tests span a wide Mach number range:

    Table 29.1. Wind tunnel facilities and applicable Mach number ranges
    Facility Type Mach Range Reynolds Number Notes
    Subsonic \(< 0.6\) \(10^6\)–\(10^7\) Continuous flow; NLF studies
    Transonic 0.6–1.3 \(10^7\)–\(10^8\) Slotted-wall test section
    Supersonic 1.5–5.0 \(10^6\)–\(10^7\) Blow-down or continuous
    Hypersonic 5–12 \(10^5\)–\(10^7\) Gun tunnel or arc-heated
    Ballistic range \(>\) 3 \(10^6\)–\(10^8\) Free-flight model; no sting

    The aerodynamic force and moment coefficients are non-dimensionalised by dynamic pressure \(q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2\), reference area \(S_{ref}\) (typically \(\pi d^2/4\)), and reference length \(d_{ref}\) (body diameter or span):

    \[C_N = \frac{N}{q S_{ref}}, \quad C_m = \frac{M_{pitch}}{q S_{ref} d_{ref}}, \quad C_A = \frac{A}{q S_{ref}}\tag{29.7}\]

    A 6-component internal balance measures all three forces and three moments simultaneously with accuracy \(\pm 0.1\%\) of full scale. Tunnel-to-flight corrections account for wall interference, sting interference, and Reynolds number mismatch.

    29.3.2 Pressure-Sensitive Paint¶

    Pressure-sensitive paint (PSP) contains luminescent molecules (porphyrin, Pd-TFPP) embedded in an oxygen-permeable binder. Intensity \(I\) of luminescence is related to local oxygen partial pressure \(p_{O_2}\) (and hence static pressure) by the Stern-Volmer relation:

    \[\frac{I_{ref}}{I} = A + B \cdot \frac{p}{p_{ref}}\tag{29.8}\]

    where \(A\) and \(B\) are calibration constants and the subscript \(ref\) denotes the wind-off reference state. PSP replaces thousands of pressure taps and provides full-field pressure maps at sub-millimetre resolution.

    29.3.3 Schlieren Photography¶

    Schlieren systems visualise compressibility effects (shocks, expansion fans, boundary-layer transition) by exploiting the dependence of the refractive index \(n\) of air on density \(\rho\):

    \[n - 1 = k\rho, \quad k \approx 2.26 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3/\text{kg}\tag{29.9}\]

    A knife edge at the focal point of a collimated beam intercepts deflected rays; regions of high \(|dn/dy|\) appear bright or dark (depending on orientation), revealing shock positions to \(\pm 0.5\,\text{mm}\).

    29.3.4 Store Separation Testing¶

    Captive trajectory simulation (CTS) in a wind tunnel characterises the trajectory of a missile after ejection from an aircraft pylon. A 6-DOF sting mechanism moves the missile model through the pre-measured flowfield of the carrier aircraft (captured at multiple positions) in a closed-loop controller. The equations of motion integrated at each step:

    \[m\dot{\mathbf{V}} = \mathbf{F}_{aero}(x,y,z,\alpha,\beta,M) + m\mathbf{g}\tag{29.10}\]

    CTS results guide ejection-foot force selection to ensure safe separation clearance (typically \(> 150\,\text{mm}\) from the aircraft structure at all simulated conditions).

    29.4 Guidance and Navigation Testing¶

    29.4.1 Seeker Hardware-in-the-Loop Testing¶

    Hardware-in-the-loop (HWIL) simulation places the actual seeker on a dome test fixture while the guidance computer, autopilot, and 6-DOF simulation run in real time. The IR scene projector (or RF signal generator for radar seekers) stimulates the seeker with a realistic target scenario.

    The closed-loop guidance error \(\varepsilon\) drives the missile toward zero miss distance. The proportional navigation guidance law:

    \[\mathbf{a}_c = N' \dot{\lambda} \cdot V_c \hat{\mathbf{n}}\tag{29.11}\]

    where \(N'\) is the effective navigation ratio (typically 3–5), \(\dot{\lambda}\) is the line-of-sight (LOS) rate, \(V_c\) is closing velocity, and \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) is the unit vector perpendicular to the LOS. The seeker must measure \(\dot{\lambda}\) with sufficient bandwidth and noise rejection to achieve the required miss distance. The miss distance \(M\) for a first-order guidance system with time constant \(\tau\) and closing time \(t_f\) is approximately:

    \[M \approx \frac{1}{2} a_t t_f^2 \cdot \frac{(N'\tau/t_f)^2}{N'-1}\tag{29.12}\]

    where \(a_t\) is target acceleration. HWIL testing validates seeker noise equivalent angle (NEA), gimbal rate limit effects, and countermeasure rejection.

    29.4.2 INS Characterisation on Rate Table¶

    The IMU is characterised on a multi-axis rate table (typically 3-axis, accuracy \(\pm 0.001°/\text{s}\)) in a thermal chamber (\(-54°\)C to \(+71°\)C). A 24-state Kalman filter identifies all error parameters:

    \[\mathbf{x} = [\delta\boldsymbol{\phi},\,\delta\mathbf{v},\,\delta\mathbf{p},\,\mathbf{b}_g,\,\mathbf{b}_a,\,\mathbf{s}_g,\,\mathbf{s}_a,\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_g]^T\tag{29.13}\]

    where \(\delta\boldsymbol{\phi}\) are attitude errors, \(\delta\mathbf{v}\) are velocity errors, \(\delta\mathbf{p}\) are position errors, \(\mathbf{b}_g\) and \(\mathbf{b}_a\) are gyro and accelerometer biases, \(\mathbf{s}_g\) and \(\mathbf{s}_a\) are scale factor errors, and \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_g\) are gyro misalignments. The Kalman update:

    \[\hat{\mathbf{x}}^+ = \hat{\mathbf{x}}^- + \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{z} - \mathbf{H}\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-)\tag{29.14}\]

    where \(\mathbf{K}\) is the Kalman gain matrix, \(\mathbf{z}\) is the measurement vector (from the precision table), and \(\mathbf{H}\) is the observation matrix.

    29.4.3 Guidance Monte Carlo Simulation¶

    A 6-DOF Monte Carlo simulation with 1,000 or more independent runs establishes the circular error probable (CEP) against a variety of target and engagement conditions. Input uncertainty parameters are modelled as distributions:

    • IMU bias: \(\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_b^2)\)

    • Wind: \(\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_w^2)\) per axis

    • Target motion: bounded random walk

    • Atmospheric density: \(\mathcal{N}(\bar{\rho}, \sigma_\rho^2)\)

    • Launch condition errors: position, velocity, attitude

    CEP is the radius within which 50% of impact points fall. For a bivariate normal distribution with equal standard deviations \(\sigma\) in both horizontal axes:

    \[CEP \approx 1.1774 \sigma\tag{29.15}\]

    For unequal sigmas (\(\sigma_x \ne \sigma_y\)):

    \[CEP \approx 0.5887(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)\tag{29.16}\]

    29.4.4 EMI/EMC Testing¶

    Electromagnetic interference (EMI) and electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) are tested per MIL-STD-461G. Key emission limits for missile electronics:

    • RE102: Radiated emissions from electronics (30 MHz–18 GHz); must not degrade seeker performance.

    • CS114: Conducted susceptibility on cables; simulates induced currents from platform emitters.

    • RS103: Radiated susceptibility; apply external fields to verify no functional impairment.

    HERO compliance per MIL-STD-464C requires that with the armed missile installed on the platform, the field at any squib lead shall not exceed:

    \[E_{limit} = \sqrt{\frac{2 P_{fire} R_{squib}}{A_{eff}}}\tag{29.17}\]

    where \(P_{fire}\) is the no-fire power (typically \(1\,\text{mW}\)) and \(A_{eff}\) is the effective antenna area of the squib circuit.

    29.5 Warhead and Fuze Testing¶

    29.5.1 Arena Test¶

    The arena test characterises the fragment spatial distribution of a warhead. The warhead is suspended at the centre of a circular array of witness plates (typically 5 mm mild steel) at a radius of 3–15 m. Static detonation is initiated and fragment perforations are counted and measured.

    Fragment velocity \(V_f\) at radius \(R\) from the detonation point decays as:

    \[V_f(R) = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{C_D \rho_a A_f}{2 m_f} R\right)\tag{29.18}\]

    where \(V_0\) is initial fragment velocity (typically 1,500–2,500 m/s for steel natural fragmentation), \(C_D\) is fragment drag coefficient, \(\rho_a\) is air density, \(A_f\) is mean frontal area, and \(m_f\) is mean fragment mass.

    The Gurney velocity \(\sqrt{2E}\) (related to the explosive energy release per unit mass of casing) provides the theoretical initial velocity:

    \[V_0 = \sqrt{2E} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{C/M}{1 + C/(2M)}}\tag{29.19}\]

    where \(C\) is explosive mass and \(M\) is metal case mass (Gurney equation for a cylinder, assuming uniform radial expansion).

    29.5.2 Detonation Velocity Measurement¶

    Detonation velocity (VOD) is measured by:

    • Probe wires: Shorting pins embedded in the explosive column at known intervals; each pin produces a voltage step as the detonation front passes; time intervals yield VOD.

    • VISAR (Velocity Interferometer System for Any Reflector): Laser Doppler technique; measures interface velocity continuously at ns resolution; particularly suited to in-cylinder measurements.

    The Chapman-Jouguet detonation velocity \(D_{CJ}\) for a given explosive is related to the heat of detonation \(Q_D\) and density \(\rho_0\):

    \[D_{CJ} = \sqrt{2(\gamma^2-1) Q_D}\tag{29.20}\]

    where \(\gamma\) is the product-gas isentropic exponent at the CJ state. For RDX at \(\rho_0 = 1.77\,\text{g/cm}^3\), \(D_{CJ} \approx 8,750\,\text{m/s}\).

    29.5.3 Insensitive Munitions Testing¶

    Insensitive Munitions (IM) testing per MIL-DTL-23659 and STANAG 4439 subjects the complete munition to a suite of unplanned stimuli:

    Table 29.2. Insensitive Munitions (IM) test matrix and response definitions
    Test Stimulus Acceptable Response
    Bullet Impact (BI) 12.7 mm AP, 850 m/s Burn (Type V) or No reaction (Type VI)
    Fragment Impact (FI) 1.6 g at 2,530 m/s Burn or No reaction
    Shaped Charge Jet (SCJ) 84 mm HEAT Deflagration (Type IV) or less
    Slow Cook-off (SCO) \(3.3°\text{C/hr}\) to ignition Burn or No reaction
    Fast Cook-off (FCO) Pool fire (750°C flame) Burn or No reaction
    Sympathetic Detonation (SD) Adjacent round detonates No detonation of test item

    Response types per AOP-39: I = Detonation; II = Partial Detonation; III = Explosion; IV = Deflagration; V = Burn; VI = No reaction. The IM requirement is that no test event produces a response worse than Type IV (deflagration).

    29.5.4 Lethal Radius Determination¶

    Lethality against personnel (unprotected) is assessed by deploying target arrays at various ranges \(R\) from the detonation point. The probability of kill \(P_k(R)\) is fitted to the Weibull or logistic model:

    \[P_k(R) = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{R}{R_{50}}\right)^m\right]\tag{29.21}\]

    where \(R_{50}\) is the lethal radius at which \(P_k = 0.5\) and \(m\) is the Weibull shape parameter. The lethal radius \(R_{50}\) is a key figure of merit in warhead effectiveness specifications.

    29.5.5 Fuze Function Testing¶

    The fuze must function reliably under valid conditions and never function under invalid conditions (safe-arm requirements). Testing covers:

    • Arming sequence: All arming conditions (setback, spin, time, etc.) verified to arm in the design sequence.

    • No-fire environment: Fuze must not initiate under MIL-STD-1316 no-fire environments (drop, heat, EM fields).

    • Function tests: Target simulation by sensor stimulation; verify fuze function within the design window.

    • Safe separation: For aircraft-delivered munitions, fuze must not arm until safe separation distance from the aircraft is exceeded.

    29.6 Environmental Testing¶

    29.6.1 MIL-STD-810H Test Suite¶

    Environmental testing per MIL-STD-810H applies the full suite of natural environments that the missile will encounter during manufacture, storage, transportation, and deployment. Key methods:

    Table 29.3. MIL-STD-810H environmental test methods applicable to missiles
    Method Test Representative Conditions
    501.7 High Temperature \(+71°\)C storage; \(+85°\)C operational
    502.7 Low Temperature \(-46°\)C storage; \(-32°\)C operational
    503.7 Temperature Shock \(-54°\)C to \(+71°\)C in \(< 5\,\text{min}\)
    507.6 Humidity \(95\%\) RH at \(40°\)C for 240 h
    509.7 Salt Fog 5% NaCl spray; 48 h; shipboard exposure
    514.8 Vibration Cat. 4 truck (\(0.01\,\text{g}^2/\text{Hz}\), 5–500 Hz)
    516.8 Shock 40 g, 11 ms half-sine; bench handling
    520.4 Temperature-Altitude \(-56°\)C at \(21 \text{ km}\); air launch

    The severity level is tailored to the specific life-cycle environments using measured field data where available (MIL-STD-810H Annex C tailoring).

    29.6.2 HERO Testing¶

    HERO (Hazards of Electromagnetic Radiation to Ordnance) testing verifies that missile ordnance (squibs, EEDs) is not susceptible to RF fields present in the operational environment. Per MIL-STD-464C:

    \[P_{induced} = \frac{E^2 \cdot A_{eff}}{120\pi}\tag{29.22}\]

    The absorbed power \(P_{induced}\) in the squib circuit must remain below \(P_{no-fire} / 20\) (6 dB margin below the no-fire level) at all operational field strengths. Testing uses a calibrated open TEM cell or antenna to expose the armed missile/squib assembly to specified field levels (MIL-STD-464C Table 1). Safe standoff distances from radar emitters are determined by the HERO safe distance:

    \[R_{safe} = \sqrt{\frac{P_t G_t}{4\pi S_{limit}}}\tag{29.23}\]

    where \(P_t G_t\) is transmitter EIRP and \(S_{limit}\) is the power density limit at the squib.

    29.6.3 Transportation Testing¶

    Transportation vibration is tested per MIL-STD-810H Method 514.8 using measured power spectral density (PSD) profiles for:

    • Category 4 (wheeled vehicle, cross-country): PSD 0.01–0.04 \(\text{g}^2/\text{Hz}\) over 5–100 Hz; 1-hour equivalent per axis.

    • Category 7 (helicopter external sling): 0.5–1.0 \(\text{g}^2/\text{Hz}\) at 10–100 Hz; severe for fragile guidance components.

    • Category 20 (jet aircraft cargo): 0.015 \(\text{g}^2/\text{Hz}\) over 15–2,000 Hz.

    The fatigue damage equivalent of a random vibration environment is estimated from the power spectral density using Miner’s rule with an assumed S-N slope \(b\):

    \[D = \int_0^\infty \frac{n(f)}{N(f)} \, df \approx \frac{T}{K} \left[\int_0^\infty G_{xx}(f)^{b/2} \, df\right]\tag{29.24}\]

    29.6.4 Storage Compatibility¶

    Long-term storage (up to 20 years for strategic systems) requires compatibility testing between the propellant, liner, case material, and lubricants. Off-gassing from propellants (primarily in double-base types: NO, NO\(_2\), aldehydes) is measured by head-space gas analysis. Propellant/metal contact interactions (stress-corrosion cracking of steel) are simulated in accelerated immersion tests at elevated temperature.

    29.7 Flight Test Programme¶

    29.7.1 Mission Planning and Range Safety¶

    Each flight test event requires a comprehensive Mission Package (MP) approved by the Range Safety Officer (RSO) at least 30 days before execution. The MP includes:

    • Test objectives and success criteria (quantitative)

    • Vehicle configuration and mass properties

    • Expected trajectory (nominal and \(3\sigma\) dispersed)

    • Flight termination system (FTS) arm/fire logic and criteria

    • Tracking and telemetry coverage map

    • Hazard areas (debris footprint, fragment hazard)

    • Weather go/no-go criteria (wind, visibility, lightning)

    • Coordination with air traffic control (NOTAM numbers)

    The maximum downrange hazard footprint for an unguided or FTS-terminated vehicle is computed from the energy state at termination. For a ballistic fragment:

    \[R_{max} = \frac{V_{term}^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}\tag{29.25}\]

    where \(V_{term}\) is velocity at FTS termination and \(\theta\) is the trajectory angle at that point.

    29.7.2 Flight Test Instrumentation¶

    Flight test instrumentation (FTI) is a temporary system installed only for test purposes, supplementing the missile’s operational instrumentation. A missile FTI system may include:

    • PCM telemetry encoder: 200–500 channels; 10–50 Mbit/s; PCM/FM or SOQPSK-TG modulation per IRIG-106.

    • GPS/INS hybrid: DGPS position to \(\pm 0.3\,\text{m}\) RMS; velocimeter for velocity \(\pm 0.1\,\text{m/s}\).

    • Miniature pressure transducers: 20–50 distributed surface pressure taps.

    • Accelerometers: 3-axis, \(\pm 100 g\) or \(\pm 500 g\) depending on location.

    • Thermal sensors: 20–50 surface thermocouples; radiometer.

    • Antennas: S-band (2.2–2.4 GHz) telemetry; GPS; FTS command receiver.

    The Nyquist sampling theorem requires a sample rate \(f_s \ge 2 f_{max}\) for each channel; structural dynamics channels may need \(f_s > 2,000\,\text{samples/s}\).

    29.7.3 Tracking Systems¶

    Precision tracking provides an independent trajectory record independent of the telemetry system (which may fail). Tracking systems at major ranges:

    • FPS-16 C-band precision tracking radar: Range accuracy \(\pm 1\,\text{m}\); angle accuracy \(\pm 0.1\,\text{mrad}\) (0.006°); track update rate 10–60 Hz.

    • AN/MPS-39 MOTR (Multiple Object Tracking Radar): Simultaneous track of 10 objects; range 200 km; used at Kwajalein, White Sands.

    • Cinetheodolites: Optical (35 mm or digital); 2 stations required for stereo; photogrammetric reduction to 3D trajectory.

    • GPS transponder: High-accuracy GPS receiver and data logger on missile (combined with telemetry); real-time position to GCS.

    The 3D miss vector is measured by the scoring radar (e.g., TSPI radar) positioned near the target:

    \[\text{Miss} = \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2 + (\Delta z)^2}\tag{29.26}\]

    29.7.4 Flight Profile Phases¶

    A complete guided missile flight test programme progresses through structured flight phases, each building on the previous:

    Phase 1 — Captive Carry (CC). The missile remains on the launch platform (aircraft pylon, ship launcher, or ground vehicle). All umbilical interfaces, MIL-STD-1760 digital bus communications, seeker activation and target acquisition (from the platform), and cockpit displays are verified in a realistic flight environment without the risk of a free-flight event.

    Phase 2 — Motor-Off Separation. The missile is released from the platform with the motor not ignited. This event characterises the separation dynamics and verifies that post-separation aerodynamics are stable, that no mechanical interference occurs, and that the FTI telemetry stream is clean immediately post-release.

    Phase 3 — Motor Ignition. Motor is ignited immediately after separation. Thrust vector, ignition delay, and motor ballistics are verified. The trajectory is monitored for anomalous roll rates or pitch excursions.

    Phase 4 — Guided Flight (Sub-terminal). Seeker acquires target; guidance commands are generated; the autopilot is exercised through the full angle-of- attack (\(\alpha\)) envelope. Trajectory is compared to 6-DOF simulation predictions. Key metrics: time to acquisition, guidance bandwidth, trim drag.

    Phase 5 — Terminal Phase and Lethality. For a live-warhead event, the missile is guided to the target (drone, stationary tank hulk, ship target). Warhead function (proper detonation initiation), miss distance, and battle damage are assessed. For inert rounds, impact scoring is done by optical and radar tracking.

    29.7.5 Data Reduction and Anomaly Resolution¶

    Raw telemetry frames are ingested by the range data reduction system and converted to engineering units using calibration coefficients:

    \[x_{EU} = G \cdot \left(\frac{N}{N_{max}} - \text{offset}\right) + B\tag{29.27}\]

    where \(N\) is the raw PCM count, \(N_{max}\) is the full-scale count, \(G\) is the gain calibration, and \(B\) is the bias offset. Time-alignment between tracks, telemetry, and flight termination data is done by a UTC-referenced IRIG-B time code.

    When an anomaly is detected, the resolution process begins immediately:

    1. Anomaly characterisation: Replay of all telemetry channels; correlation of anomaly time with vehicle state.

    2. Root cause hypothesis: Fault tree analysis; comparison with pre-flight 6-DOF predictions; data from previous tests.

    3. Design/process change: Hardware modification, software patch, or procedure change to eliminate root cause.

    4. Verification: Bench test or additional flight test to confirm fix.

    5. FRACAS closure: Formal closure report; update to design documentation.

    29.8 Lethality Testing¶

    29.8.1 Live Warhead Tests Against Representative Targets¶

    Lethality testing answers the system-level question: given a successful guidance intercept, does the warhead kill the target? Live-warhead tests are conducted at instrumented government test ranges. Target configurations include:

    • Armoured vehicle targets: Full-scale or subscale RHA plate stacks; T-72 or M60 hulks; internal pressure gauges for spall assessment.

    • Aircraft targets: QF-16 or BQM-167 drone targets; fuselage panels with representative structural materials.

    • Ship targets: Decommissioned ship hulks at sea ranges; internal instrumentation; fire and damage assessment team after exercise.

    • Buried targets: Reinforced concrete bunkers; penetration depth measured by post-shot excavation.

    29.8.2 Penetration of Hardened Targets¶

    For a kinetic penetrator impacting concrete at velocity \(V_i\), the penetration depth \(P\) is modelled by the modified Young formula:

    \[P = \frac{0.0795 \, S_N \, m_p}{A_p \, \sqrt{f'_c}} \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{V_i^2}{19974}\right)\tag{29.28}\]

    where \(S_N\) is the nose performance factor, \(m_p\) is penetrator mass (kg), \(A_p\) is cross-sectional area (m\(^2\)), \(f'_c\) is concrete compressive strength (MPa), and \(V_i\) is impact velocity (m/s). For RHA steel penetration (Forrestal correlation):

    \[P_{RHA} = \frac{m_p}{k A_p \rho_{target}} \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{k \rho_{target} V_i^2}{2 \sigma_{target}}\right)\tag{29.29}\]

    where \(k\) is the penetration resistance parameter and \(\sigma_{target}\) is the target flow stress.

    29.8.3 Blast Overpressure Assessment¶

    Blast overpressure from a high-explosive warhead is measured by calibrated pressure transducers (PCB 137 series, Kistler 701) at multiple radii in a free-field (hemispherical detonation) or airburst geometry. The Friedlander waveform model:

    \[p(t) = p_{so}\left(1 - \frac{t}{t^+}\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{\alpha t}{t^+}\right)\tag{29.30}\]

    where \(p_{so}\) is peak overpressure, \(t^+\) is positive phase duration, and \(\alpha\) is the waveform parameter (typically 1.0 for TNT equivalence). The scaled distance \(Z\) (Hopkinson-Cranz scaling):

    \[Z = \frac{R}{W^{1/3}}\tag{29.31}\]

    allows results from different charge sizes to be compared on a universal curve. For TNT, \(p_{so}(Z)\) at scaled distances \(0.5 < Z < 40\,\text{m/kg}^{1/3}\) is tabulated in UFC 3-340-02 (Structures to Resist the Effects of Accidental Explosions).

    29.8.4 Fragment Recovery and Mott Distribution¶

    Post-detonation fragment recovery from buried witness plates (or recovery pits) provides the mass distribution. The Mott formula for natural fragmentation:

    \[N(m > m_0) = N_T \exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{m_0}{\mu}}\right)\tag{29.32}\]

    where \(N(m > m_0)\) is the number of fragments with mass greater than \(m_0\), \(N_T\) is the total fragment count, and \(\mu\) is the Mott distribution parameter (depends on casing geometry and material). The mean fragment mass:

    \[\bar{m} = 2\mu \left[1 - \left(1 + \sqrt{\frac{m_{max}}{\mu}}\right) \exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{m_{max}}{\mu}}\right)\right]^{-1}\tag{29.33}\]

    X-ray radiography of witness plates and high-speed photography provide complementary data on fragment spatial distribution and velocity.

    29.9 Reliability and Failure Analysis¶

    29.9.1 Reliability Estimation from Flight Tests¶

    After \(n\) flight tests with \(k\) successes, the probability of success \(P_d\) is estimated by the maximum likelihood estimate:

    \[\hat{P}_d = \frac{k}{n}\tag{29.34}\]

    The exact (Clopper-Pearson) two-sided confidence interval at confidence level \(1 - 2\alpha\):

    \[P_L = F^{-1}_{Beta}\!\left(\alpha;\, k,\, n-k+1\right)\tag{29.35}\]
    \[P_U = F^{-1}_{Beta}\!\left(1-\alpha;\, k+1,\, n-k\right)\tag{29.36}\]

    where \(F^{-1}_{Beta}(p; a, b)\) is the \(p\)-th quantile of the Beta\((a,b)\) distribution. For \(k = 7\) successes out of \(n = 8\) trials at 90% confidence (\(\alpha = 0.05\)):

    \[P_L = F^{-1}_{Beta}(0.05;\, 7,\, 2) \approx 0.59\tag{29.37}\]

    This means we can state with 90% confidence that the true probability of success is at least 59%. Achieving a demonstrated \(P_d \ge 0.90\) with 80% confidence requires a minimum of \(n = 22\) consecutive successes (from the binomial lower bound).

    29.9.2 Reliability Growth Models¶

    The AMSAA/Crow reliability growth model (per MIL-HDBK-189C) fits cumulative failures \(N(T)\) against cumulative test time or cumulative number of trials \(T\):

    \[E[N(T)] = \lambda T^\beta\tag{29.38}\]

    Parameter estimates by maximum likelihood (for time-truncated data):

    \[\hat{\beta} = \frac{n}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \ln(T/t_i)}\tag{29.39}\]
    \[\hat{\lambda} = \frac{n}{T^{\hat{\beta}}}\tag{29.40}\]

    where \(t_i\) are the failure times and \(T\) is the truncation time. The instantaneous failure rate (MTBF at time \(T\)):

    \[MTBF(T) = \frac{T^{1-\hat{\beta}}}{\hat{\lambda}\hat{\beta}}\tag{29.41}\]

    The Duane plot (\(\log\) cumulative MTBF vs. \(\log\) cumulative time) provides a visual check of whether growth is proceeding at the planned slope.

    29.9.3 Failure Mode Effects and Criticality Analysis¶

    FMECA is performed per MIL-STD-1629A on all missile subsystems. Each failure mode is assigned a Risk Priority Number:

    \[RPN = S \times O \times D\tag{29.42}\]

    where \(S\) is severity (1–10), \(O\) is occurrence likelihood (1–10), and \(D\) is detectability (1 = easily detected; 10 = undetectable). RPN \(\ge 100\) triggers a mandatory corrective action. Criticality \(C_r\) (from the MIL-1629A criticality matrix):

    \[C_r = \beta \cdot \alpha_p \cdot \lambda_p \cdot t\tag{29.43}\]

    where \(\beta\) is the conditional probability of mission failure given the failure mode, \(\alpha_p\) is the failure mode ratio, \(\lambda_p\) is the part failure rate, and \(t\) is operating time.

    29.9.4 FRACAS in Test and Evaluation¶

    During the flight test programme, FRACAS captures every anomaly—not just hardware failures but also software discrepancies, test equipment malfunctions, and operator errors. The database feeds reliability growth models (updated after each test event) and informs the programme manager’s assessment of readiness for the next milestone.

    29.10 Test Ranges and Facilities¶

    29.10.1 United States Test Ranges¶

    White Sands Missile Range (WSMR), New Mexico. The largest overland test range in the United States at approximately 8,300 km\(^2\) (plus out-of-the-range areas extending to 67,000 km\(^2\) for special tests). WSMR hosts a complete suite of tracking and telemetry assets (16 tracking radar sites, 10 telemetry stations), an inertial test complex, ballistic targets, and environmental test facilities. Classified programs are accommodated with appropriate facility controls. WSMR has hosted tests of Patriot, Javelin, ATACMS, and many strategic ballistic missile components.

    Point Mugu Naval Air Warfare Center (NAWCWD), California. The Pacific Missile Test Range provides 58,000 km\(^2\) of sea range. It is the primary range for AIM-9X, AIM-120 AMRAAM, HARM, and ship-launched cruise missile testing, offering overwater flight with uninhabited drop zones.

    Eglin Air Force Base / Gulf Test and Training Range (GTTR), Florida. Encompasses approximately 170,000 km\(^2\) of water range over the Gulf of Mexico. Eglin is the home of the Air Force Research Laboratory Munitions Directorate and hosts the primary test sites for all Air Force air-to-ground and air-to-air munitions.

    China Lake Naval Air Weapons Station (NAWS), California. A 4,500 km\(^2\) desert test site with unrestricted airspace to 18 km. Specialises in air-launched weapons; hosts the Naval Air Weapons Center Weapons Division. Extreme thermal environments (surface temperatures to 65°C) are ideal for EO/IR seeker testing.

    Kwajalein Atoll (Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site, RTS). A 1,700 km downrange site in the Pacific, used for intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) re-entry vehicle testing and ballistic missile defence (BMD) intercept testing. The long-range geometry (ICBM range 5,000–8,000 km) cannot be replicated at any continental US range.

    29.10.2 International Test Ranges¶

    Woomera Range Complex (WRC), South Australia. At 127,000 km\(^2\), Woomera is the world’s largest overland range. The restricted airspace volume extends to unlimited altitude, enabling supersonic and hypersonic testing without altitude limits. Australia’s proximity to equatorial launch windows and the restricted Southern Hemisphere ocean provide unique long-range opportunities. WRC has been used for hypersonic glide vehicle research (HiFiRE programme) and extended-range glide-capable munitions.

    Hebrides Range (HMR), United Kingdom. Located on the Scottish island chain with 150,000 km\(^2\) of instrumented sea range, HMR is operated by QinetiQ under contract to the UK MoD. It is used for Brimstone, CAMM (Common Anti-air Modular Missile), and sea-skimming missile tests. Adverse weather conditions (North Atlantic) are a feature as much as a liability, providing realistic at-sea target environments.

    DGA Essais de Missiles, Landes, France. The French defence procurement agency (DGA) operates a sea range off the Landes coast on the Bay of Biscay. Aster, Scalp EG/Storm Shadow, and Exocet testing has been conducted here. The range includes a dedicated seeker evaluation site with IR and RF scene projectors.

    Vidsel Test Range, Sweden. An overland range in northern Sweden; particularly valuable for cold-weather testing below \(-40\)°C. Used by Saab for RBS-15 and BILL missile tests; NATO partners also use it for cold-environment qualification.

    29.10.3 Flight Termination Systems¶

    Every missile flight test over populated or shared airspace is equipped with a Flight Termination System (FTS). The FTS receives a coded command from the RSO and immediately terminates propulsion and/or destroys the airframe to limit the debris footprint.

    FTS components:

    • Command receiver: Dual-redundant; IRIG-106 Chapter 4 compliant; frequency diversity (dual-frequency) to resist jamming.

    • Termination devices: Linear-shaped charge (LSC) that severs the motor case (terminating thrust); or explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) charge that fragments the airframe.

    • Autonomous FTS (AFTS): Onboard position-based logic terminates flight if the vehicle exits the pre-planned safety volume, independent of ground command (required by Eastern and Western Test Range safety manuals for manned-space but increasingly applied to long-range missile tests).

    The safe distance \(R_{FTS}\) from an FTS debris footprint scales with vehicle kinetic energy at activation:

    \[R_{debris} \approx \frac{1}{g} \sqrt{2 E_{kinetic}} \approx \frac{V_{FTS}}{g} \cdot V_{FTS} = \frac{V_{FTS}^2}{g}\tag{29.44}\]

    at a 45° ballistic trajectory assumption. This provides a rapid first estimate; detailed debris dispersion is computed by Monte Carlo simulation of fragmentation patterns.

    29.11 Test Data Management and Reporting¶

    29.11.1 Data Management Infrastructure¶

    Modern missile test programs generate terabytes of data per flight test event. The FTI PCM stream at 50 Mbit/s over a 60-second flight produces 3.75 GB of raw data per channel (before multiplexing). Data management requirements:

    • Real-time display: selected key parameters on wall screens in the control room; limit alarm monitoring; GO/NO-GO assessment within seconds of event.

    • Archival: all raw data archived to RAID-6 storage; replicated offsite; retention for 25+ years (contractual requirement for strategic programs).

    • Data rights: Test data generated at government ranges is US Government property; access controlled by ITAR, DoD 5220.22-M (NISPOM), and contract DD254.

    29.11.2 Test Reports and Configuration Control¶

    Test results are documented in a Test Report (TR) for each event, covering:

    1. Test objectives and pass/fail criteria (restated from ATP)

    2. Configuration at test (hardware serial numbers, software versions, date)

    3. Environmental conditions at time of test

    4. Results: measured values vs. specified limits; statistical summary

    5. Anomalies: description, preliminary root cause, FRACAS number

    6. Disposition: system status; readiness for next test event

    Configuration control is enforced by a Configuration Control Board (CCB) which approves any deviation between successive test configurations. This ensures that performance improvements between tests are attributable to deliberate design changes, not accidental configuration drift.

    29.11.3 Statistical Methods in T&E¶

    Statistical rigour is essential when drawing conclusions from the inevitably small sample sizes of expensive flight tests. Key methods:

    Sequential probability ratio test (SPRT): Allows accept/reject decisions to be made after each test rather than waiting for a full lot. The likelihood ratio after \(n\) tests:

    \[\Lambda_n = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \frac{f(x_i | H_1)}{f(x_i | H_0)}\tag{29.45}\]

    Accept \(H_0\) if \(\Lambda_n \le B\); accept \(H_1\) if \(\Lambda_n \ge A\); continue testing if \(B < \Lambda_n < A\). For Type I error \(\alpha = 0.10\) and Type II error \(\beta = 0.10\): \(A \approx (1-\beta)/\alpha = 9\) and \(B \approx \beta/(1-\alpha) = 0.11\).

    Bayesian inference: Prior knowledge of system reliability (from component tests, simulation, predecessor systems) is combined with flight test evidence to give a posterior distribution on \(P_d\):

    \[p(P_d | k, n) \propto P_d^k (1-P_d)^{n-k} \cdot \pi(P_d)\tag{29.46}\]

    A Beta(\(\alpha_0, \beta_0\)) prior with \(\alpha_0 + \beta_0 = 10\) (10 equivalent prior tests) combined with \(k = 6\) successes in \(n = 7\) flight tests gives a posterior Beta\((6+\alpha_0,\, 1+\beta_0)\), from which any credible interval may be computed analytically.

    Design of experiments (DoE): A full factorial or response surface design of flight test conditions (launch altitude, closing speed, target aspect, countermeasure setting) with fractional factorial reduction enables maximum information extraction from the fewest test events. A \(2^k\) fractional factorial design with \(k = 5\) factors at 2 levels yields 16 runs (quarter fraction) covering all main effects and two-factor interactions simultaneously.
