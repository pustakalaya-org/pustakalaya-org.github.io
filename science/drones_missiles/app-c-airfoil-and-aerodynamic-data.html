    Appendix C: Airfoil and Aerodynamic Data¶

    C.1 Airfoil and Aerodynamic Data¶

    This appendix provides geometric definitions, performance tables, and atmospheric reference data essential for the aerodynamic design of drone airframes, missile fins, and control surfaces. Airfoil data are taken from the UIUC Airfoil Database, NACA Technical Reports 824 (Abbott & Von Doenhoff), NASA TM-4741, and Xfoil/MSES computational results where experimental data are unavailable at the Reynolds numbers of interest.

    C.2 NACA Airfoil Geometry¶

    C.2.1 NACA 4-Digit Series¶

    A NACA 4-digit designation MPXX encodes: \(M\) = maximum camber (tenths of chord), \(P\) = position of maximum camber (tenths of chord), \(XX\) = maximum thickness (percent of chord). Examples: NACA 2412 (\(m=0.02\), \(p=0.4\), \(t=0.12\)), NACA 0012 (symmetric), NACA 4415.

    Mean camber line:

    \[\begin{align*} \frac{y_c}{c} &= \frac{m}{p^2}\left(2p\,\frac{x}{c} - \left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^2\right) \quad 0 \leq x \leq pc \\ \frac{y_c}{c} &= \frac{m}{(1-p)^2}\left[(1-2p) + 2p\,\frac{x}{c} - \left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^2\right] \quad pc \leq x \leq c \end{align*}\]

    Thickness distribution (NACA 4- and 5-digit):

    \[\frac{y_t}{c} = \frac{t}{0.2}\left[0.2969\sqrt{\frac{x}{c}} - 0.1260\frac{x}{c} - 0.3516\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^2 + 0.2843\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^3 - 0.1015\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^4\right]\tag{C.3}\]

    This gives an open trailing edge (\(y_t \neq 0\) at \(x/c = 1\)). For a closed trailing edge, replace the last coefficient \(-0.1015\) with \(-0.1036\).

    Upper and lower surface coordinates:

    \[\begin{align*} X_U &= \frac{x}{c} - \frac{y_t}{c}\sin\theta, & Y_U &= \frac{y_c}{c} + \frac{y_t}{c}\cos\theta \\ X_L &= \frac{x}{c} + \frac{y_t}{c}\sin\theta, & Y_L &= \frac{y_c}{c} - \frac{y_t}{c}\cos\theta \end{align*}\]

    where \(\theta = \arctan(dy_c/dx)\).

    C.2.2 NACA 5-Digit Series¶

    The 5-digit series uses a modified camber line to shift the position of maximum camber forward (improving \(C_{L,\text{max}}\)). Designation LCPXX: \(L = 3C_{L,\text{design}}/2\), \(P\) = position of max camber (\(\times\)20), \(XX\) = thickness. The reflexed variants (digit 2 in second position, e.g. 23112) give near-zero \(C_{m,\text{ac}}\).

    Camber line (\(0 \leq x \leq pc\)):

    \[\frac{y_c}{c} = \frac{k_1}{6}\left[\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^3 - 3p\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^2 + p^2(3-p)\frac{x}{c}\right]\]

    (\(x \geq pc\)): \(\frac{y_c}{c} = \frac{k_1 p^3}{6}\left(1 - \frac{x}{c}\right)\)

    Values of \(k_1\) and \(p\) for standard 5-digit profiles are tabulated in NACA TR-537.

    C.2.3 NACA 6-Series¶

    The 6-series (e.g. NACA 63\(_2\)-215) was designed for minimum drag over a specific \(C_L\) range (the “bucket”). The mean line is defined through a loading parameter \(a\) (fraction of chord over which the design load is uniform). For \(a = 1.0\) (uniform loading):

    \[\frac{y_c}{c}\bigg|_{a=1} = -\frac{C_{L,i}}{2\pi}\left[\left(1-\frac{x}{c}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{c}\right) + \frac{x}{c}\ln\frac{x}{c}\right]\]

    For \(a < 1\) (loading concentrated forward):

    \[\frac{y_c}{c} = \frac{C_{L,i}}{2\pi(1+a)}\left[(x/c - a)\ln|x/c - a| - (x/c)\ln(x/c) + \tfrac{1}{4}(1+2a-2x/c)\ln(1-x/c) + g + h\,x/c\right]\]

    where \(g\) and \(h\) are constants ensuring \(y_c(0) = y_c(1) = 0\). The thickness form of the 6-series is a separate tabulated distribution (NACA TR-824, Appendix II).

    Notes on Reynolds number effects. The UAV design range \(Re = 5\times10^4\)–\(5\times10^5\) is characterised by:

    • Laminar separation bubbles (LSB) that increase effective drag and reduce \(C_{L,\max}\); hysteresis in stall behaviour.

    • \((L/D)_{\max}\) may be 20–40% lower than the \(Re = 3\times10^6\) value.

    • Thinner profiles (\(t/c = 8\)–\(10\)%) and moderate camber (\(m/c = 3\)–\(5\)%) often outperform thick cambered sections at low \(Re\).

    • Tripping the boundary layer with a zigzag strip at \(x/c \approx 0.1\)–\(0.2\) can eliminate LSB hysteresis and stabilise \(C_{L,\max}\).

    C.4 Wing Aerodynamic Data¶

    C.4.1 Aspect Ratio Effect on Lift Curve Slope¶

    Prandtl lifting-line theory (large AR straight wing):

    \[C_{L\alpha} = \frac{a_0}{1 + a_0/(\pi\,AR)}\tag{C.9}\]

    where \(a_0 = 2\pi\) per radian (thin airfoil) or the section value (from experiment).

    Helmbold formula (moderate to low AR, incompressible):

    \[C_{L\alpha} = \frac{2\pi\,AR}{\sqrt{AR^2 + 4} + 2}\tag{C.10}\]

    Valid for \(AR < 4\); more accurate than Prandtl for AR \(\leq 6\).

    Diederich formula (includes sweep and compressibility, \(M < 0.8\)):

    \[C_{L\alpha} = \frac{2\pi AR}{2 + \sqrt{4 + AR^2\beta^2(1 + \tan^2\Lambda_{c/2}/\beta^2)}}\tag{C.11}\]

    where \(\beta = \sqrt{1-M^2}\), \(\Lambda_{c/2}\) = sweep of half-chord line.

    DATCOM method (full subsonic wing):

    \[C_{L\alpha} = \frac{2\pi AR}{2 + \sqrt{4 + AR^2/\eta^2(1+\tan^2\Lambda_{t})} } \quad \cdot \frac{S_{\text{exposed}}}{S_{\text{ref}}} \cdot \frac{F}{0.9}\]

    where \(\eta = a_0/(2\pi)\), \(F\) = body correction factor.

    C.4.4 Delta Wing Aerodynamics¶

    For a delta wing with leading-edge sweep \(\Lambda_{LE}\):

    Attached flow (linearised theory, Polhamus leading-edge suction analogy):

    \[C_L = K_P \sin\alpha\cos^2\alpha + K_V\cos\alpha\sin^2\alpha\tag{C.13}\]

    where \(K_P\) (potential) and \(K_V\) (vortex) are functions of AR and \(\Lambda_{LE}\) (tabulated in NASA TN-D-3767).

    For slender delta (\(AR < 2\)) at moderate \(\alpha\):

    \[C_L \approx \frac{\pi\,AR}{2}\alpha + 2\pi\sin^2\alpha\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}\tag{C.14}\]

    Leading-edge vortex lifts off at \(\alpha > 5°\)–\(10°\) and provides highly non-linear lift augmentation. Vortex breakdown at \(x_{\text{break}} / L_\Delta\) limits usable \(\alpha\).

    C.5 International Standard Atmosphere¶

    C.5.1 ISA Equations¶

    The ISA (ICAO Doc 7488 / ISO 2533:1975) defines:

    \[\begin{align*} \text{Troposphere } (0\text{--}11\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 288.15 - 6.5\,h \quad [\text{K}; h \text{ in km}] \\ \text{Stratosphere I } (11\text{--}20\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 216.65 \quad [\text{isothermal}] \\ \text{Stratosphere II } (20\text{--}32\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 216.65 + (h-20) \\ \text{Mesosphere I } (32\text{--}47\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 228.65 + 2.8(h-32)\\ \text{Mesosphere II } (47\text{--}51\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 270.65 \quad [\text{isothermal}]\\ \text{Mesosphere III } (51\text{--}71\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 270.65 - 2.8(h-51)\\ \text{Thermosphere } (71\text{--}86\,\text{km}):&\quad T = 214.65 - 2.0(h-71) \end{align*}\]

    Pressure (gradient layers): \(P = P_b\left(\frac{T}{T_b}\right)^{g_0 M_a/(R^* L)}\); isothermal layers: \(P = P_b\exp\!\left[\frac{-g_0 M_a}{R^*T}(h-h_b)\right]\).

    Density: \(\rho = P M_a / (R^* T)\) where \(R^* = 8.31432\) J/(mol·K), \(M_a = 0.0289644\) kg/mol.

    Speed of sound: \(a = \sqrt{\gamma R T / M_a} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287.05 \times T}\).

    Dynamic viscosity (Sutherland):

    \[\mu = \mu_{\text{ref}}\left(\frac{T}{T_{\text{ref}}}\right)^{3/2}\frac{T_{\text{ref}} + S}{T + S}\]

    \(\mu_{\text{ref}} = 1.716\times10^{-5}\) Pa\(\cdot\)s; \(T_{\text{ref}} = 273.15\) K; \(S = 110.4\) K.

    C.5.3 Density Altitude¶

    Definition: Density altitude \(h_d\) is the altitude in the ISA at which the actual air density \(\rho\) would exist. It accounts for non-standard temperature and humidity.

    Calculation (ICAO):

    \[h_d = \frac{T_0}{L}\left[1 - \left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_0}\right)^{R^* L / (g_0 M_a - R^* L)}\right] \approx h_{\text{pressure}} + \frac{T - T_{\text{ISA}}(h)}{L}\tag{C.23}\]

    where \(L = 0.0065\) K/m (lapse rate), \(T_0 = 288.15\) K, \(\rho_0 = 1.225\) kg/m\(^3\).

    Worked example: At an airfield at \(h = 1500\) m ISA + 20°C: \(T_{\text{ISA}}(1500) = 288.15 - 6.5\times1.5 = 278.4\) K. Actual \(T = 278.4 + 20 = 298.4\) K. \(\rho = \frac{84559}{287.05 \times 298.4} = 0.987\) kg/m\(^3\). Density altitude \(\approx 1500 + 20/0.0065 \approx 4577\) m. This significantly reduces propeller thrust and lift, affecting takeoff distance.

    C.6.2 Skin Friction Coefficients¶

    Laminar boundary layer (Blasius, flat plate):

    \[C_f = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}}\tag{C.24}\]

    Turbulent boundary layer (Prandtl–Schlichting):

    \[C_f = \frac{0.455}{(\log_{10} Re_L)^{2.58}}\tag{C.25}\]

    Valid for \(Re_L = 10^6\)–\(10^9\).

    Combined (transition at \(Re_x = Re_{x,\text{tr}}\)):

    \[C_f = \frac{0.455}{(\log_{10} Re_L)^{2.58}} - \frac{A}{Re_L}\]

    where \(A = 1700\) for natural transition at \(Re_{x,\text{tr}} \approx 5\times10^5\); \(A = 3300\) for \(Re_{x,\text{tr}} = 3\times10^6\) (clean laminar flow).

    Compressibility correction (adiabatic wall, Van Driest):

    \[C_{f,\text{comp}} = \frac{C_{f,\text{incomp}}}{\left[1 + 0.1305 M^2\right]^{0.62}}\]

    C.6.4 UAV Component Drag Buildup¶

    The total zero-lift drag is the sum of component wetted area contributions:

    \[C_{D0,\text{total}} = \frac{1}{S_{\text{ref}}}\sum_i C_{f,i}\,FF_i\,Q_i\,S_{wet,i} + C_{D,\text{misc}} + C_{D,\text{L\&P}}\tag{C.28}\]

    where \(FF_i\) = form factor, \(Q_i\) = interference factor, \(S_{wet,i}\) = wetted area of component \(i\), \(C_{D,\text{misc}}\) = miscellaneous drag, and \(C_{D,\text{L\&P}}\) = leakage and protuberance drag.

    C.7 Aerodynamic Derivative Reference¶

    C.7.1 Stability Derivatives (Missile/Drone Body)¶

    For a slender body plus fin configuration (cruciform symmetry), the key derivatives:

    \[\begin{align*} C_{N\alpha} &= C_{N\alpha,\text{body}} + C_{N\alpha,\text{fins}}\left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\alpha}\right)_{\text{fins}} \\ C_{m\alpha} &= C_{N\alpha}\left(x_{\text{ref}} - x_{cp}\right)/D \quad \text{(negative = stable)} \end{align*}\]

    where \(d\sigma/d\alpha\) is the upwash correction at the fin station (fin efficiency factor, typically \(\eta = 0.85\)–\(0.95\)).

    Magnus derivative (rotating body at angle of attack): \(C_{Y_{p\alpha}}\) — cross-force due to spin in the presence of \(\alpha\). Significant for spin-stabilised artillery rounds; small for finned missiles.

    Roll damping:

    \[C_{lp} = -\frac{1}{2V_\infty}\frac{\partial(L')}{\partial p} \approx -C_{L\alpha,\text{fin}}\frac{\bar{y}_f^2}{S_{\text{ref}}(b/2)}\]

    Used to compute roll rate convergence for spin-stabilised drones.
