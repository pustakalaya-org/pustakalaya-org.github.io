    Preface¶

    This is Volume 8 of the series Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā: Kāvya Kalpalatā: Literature and Poetics. It is a first map of the literatures of the Indian subcontinent and of the Indian theory of literature (alaṅkāraśāstra), written for students of Indian studies and comparative literature, for teachers preparing a course, and for general readers who want a reliable account in one volume. It assumes no knowledge of the languages concerned.

    The book follows the literatures roughly in historical order: Vedic, epic and Sanskrit court poetry, drama and prose (Chapters 2–4); the Pali, Prakrit and Apabhraṃśa literatures of the same centuries (Chapter 5); the theory of literature that grew up around them (Chapter 6); classical Tamil, fable and maxim, and bhakti and Sufi poetry (Chapters 7–9); the regional literatures (Chapters 10–11); Persian and Urdu (Chapter 12); and the modern period (Chapter 13). Each chapter can be read on its own, and cross-references lead to the place where a poet or work receives its main treatment. The introduction (Chapter 1) explains the ideas of kāvya, the poet and the reader, and sets out the languages and periods.

    Verses are quoted in transliteration with a close translation, and only where the wording and the reference could be checked. Dates are given as the range accepted in current scholarship, with traditional dates labelled as such. Each chapter ends with suggestions for further reading, and a glossary and a chronology close the book. No survey of this size can be complete: readers will want to go on to the editions and translations cited, and quotations used for scholarly purposes should be checked against a critical edition.

    Nikhil Kumar Rajput
    Ramanujan College, University of Delhi
    Guru Pūrṇimā, 2026
