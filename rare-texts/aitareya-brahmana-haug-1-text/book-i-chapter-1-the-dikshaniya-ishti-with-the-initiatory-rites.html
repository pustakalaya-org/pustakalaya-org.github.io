The present followers of the Vedik religion, the so-called Agnihotris, who take upon themselves the performance of all the manifold sacrificial rites enjoined in the Vedas, begin their arduous career for gaining a place in heaven, after the sacred fires have keen established, with the regular monthly performance of the DarŚa and Pâraimaiṣti or the New and Full Moon sacrifices. Then they bring the Châturmâsya-tsti, and after this rite they proceed to bring the Agnigtoma, the first and model of all Soma sacrifices. By the bringing of the New and Full Moon offerings, the Agnihotri is already initiated into the grand rites ; he is already an adept (Dikṣita) in it. Some of the links of the yajna or sacrifice which is regarded as a chain extending from this earth to heaven, by means of which the successful performer reaches the celestial world, the seat of the gods, are already established by these offerings ; with the deities, whose associate the sacrificer wishes to become after his death, the intercourse is opened ; for they have already received food (haviṅ), prepared [6] according to the precepts of sacred cookery, at his hands, and they have been sitting on the sacred seat (burhis) prepared of the sacrificial grass (Darbha). Thence the performance of the Full and New Moon sacrifices is here called one Dikṣ3â, i.e., one initiatory rite. But if the Agnihotri, who is performing a Soma sacrifce, is already initiated (Dikṣita) by means of the rites just mentioned, how does he require at the opening of the Agnuistoma (Soma-sacrifice) the so-called Diksaṇtya IŚti, or " offering for becoming initiated " ? This question was mooted already in ancient times. Thence, says Asvalâyana in his Srauta sâtras (4, 1), that, some are of opinion, the Soma-sacrifice should be performed, in the case of the means required being forthcoming (the sacrifice is very expensive), after the Full and New Moon sacrifices have been brought ; others opine the Soma sacrifice might be performed before the Full and New Moon sacrifices. No doubt, the Agniṣtoma was in ancient times a sacrifice wholly independent of the Darâa Pârnimâ-izis. This clearly follows from the fact, that just such Iṣtis, as constitute the Full and New Moon sacrifices, are placed at the beginning of the Agnigtoma to introduce it. ↩
