    [Although the meaning of this stanza is clear, there is a great deal of difference of opinion about the interpretation of the words used in it. In my opinion, this is a description of the opinions of atheists like Câxvâka and others, who disputed both the Vedanta and SŚamkhya. doctrines relating to the construction of the world; and therefore, the words used in these stanzas refer to a doctrine contrary to both the Sarhkhya and the Vedânta doctrines. The Vedântist looks upon this world as perishable, and looks for the Imperishable Reality —" satyasya sutyam" (Br. 2. 3. 6)— in it; and looks upon that Real Element as the fundamental support or 'pratigtha —"brakmapuccai pratighâ (Tai. 2. 5. of it. But the demoniac persons believe that this world is 'a-satyam', thet is, not conteining ' satya ' (Reality); and that it is, therefore, 'a-pratigtham', that is, not having a pratigtht or support. But, a doubt may be raised that although the Imperceptible Parabrahman of the Vedantists may in this way be not acceptable to demoniac persons, they may be accepting the Perceptible ISvara, Who is worshipped in the Path of Devotion. Therefore, the third word 'aniâvara' (ana + ISvara) has been used in this stanza, in order to make it perfectly clear that such demoniac persons do not even accept the existence of an Iâvara in the world. When once the fundamental foundation of the world has thus been denied, one is also compelled to deny the explenation of the order of creation of the world contained (i) in the Upanisadic words : " atman ukâŚah sabhutah ¹ Gkuâad vayuh¹ vâyor agnih ¹ agner Gpaṅ adbhyah prthivi¹ prthivya og(adhayah ¹ ogadhibhyah annam anntt purugah 1" (Tai. 2. 1); as also (ii) in the Sarhkhya doctrine, which looks upon Prakrti and Puruga as two independent Fundamental Elements, and looks upon ell perceptible objects as being the result of the mutual support (annyonyââraya), that is, of the intermixiture, of the sattva, rugjas, and tamas constituenis. Because, if this chain or order of succession is accepted, then, going backwards from all the objects in the visible world, one will have to admit1 that there is some origin for the world. Therefore, the demoniac people do not admit that the various objects in the world are parasparasaṅbhuta, that is, created one from the other in a particular order. When once such a belief about the creation of the world is fixed in the mind of any person, the human being itself becomes the most important. object; and it necessarily follows, as a natural conclusion, that all the things in the world have been created only for satisfying the Desire (kâma-vâsanâ) of that human being,. and have no other purpose ; and this meaning is conveyed. by the words " kmanyat kimahaitukaṅ" that is " what other object can it have except to satisfy Desire ?'', used at the end of the stanza, and also by the subsequent stanzas.
