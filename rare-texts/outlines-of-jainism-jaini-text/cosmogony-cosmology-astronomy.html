    The system of Jaina astronomy is characterized by the doctrine of two (different) suns, two moons, and two sets of constellations. The doctrine supposes that ( three appearances of a planet, or of sun or moon, are required in order to compass Mount Meru and return ( to the starting-point. Therefore the doctrine allots i two suns to Jambt-dvipa. This means that the second ¹ appearance of a sun, for instance, in the sky at a given i spot is not that of the sun that appeared first; the two ¹ suns appear alternately, so that the third appearance is i the return of the first sun. l The Jaina books and the Purânas of the Hindus both hold that the sun, moon, etc., revolve round Mount

    Meru. The Paurâṇic opinion was that the revolution i took twenty-four hours, and that it was night north of Hh

    Mount Meru, when the sun was making its half-revolution round the south of Mount Meru and vice versa.

    The dJainas, therefore, held that there are four ; ¹ directions, and the sun's orbit should be divided into four quarters, corresponding to the four directions; and it i f should bring day in succession to the countries in the He south, west, north, and east. The sun must take equal time to traverse each quarter. Therefore, when it has l left one quarter, say the eastern, and gone to the I southern, it is night in the east and day in the south. ¹
