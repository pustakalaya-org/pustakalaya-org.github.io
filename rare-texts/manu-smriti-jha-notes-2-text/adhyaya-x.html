    VERSE XXXI

    "Kullâka thinks that the terms vâkya and hina may either refer (a) to two sets of men or (5) to one only ; (a) under the former supposition, the Vâhyas must be understood to be the Pratiloma offering of a shâdra, i. e, dyogavas, Kṣattrs and Chaṇââlas,—and the Hinas the Pratiloma offspring of Kâattrigas and Vaishyas i. e., Sâtas, Mâgadhas and Vaidehas. Each of these two sets produce fifteen lower races by union with women of the four chief castes and of their own (verse 27) ;— (b) But if the two terms vâhya and hina are referred to one set of males only, they must be understood to denote the six Pratilomas, Chandââlas, Kyattrs, Ayogavas, Vaidehas, Mâagadhas and Sâtas; and it must be assumed that the verse refers to unions between these six Pratiloma races alone. Then the lowest among them, the Chaṇâaâla may produce, with females of the five higher Pratiloma tribes, five more degraded races; the Kṅattr with the four above him, four ; the Ayogava with the three above him ; the Vaideha, 'two, and the Maâgadha one. The total of 54-43-21 is thus 15.—Râghavânṇanda agrees with this interpretation.—Nârâyaṇa, on the other hand, refers the terms ovâhya and hina to one set of males, the three Pritilamas springing from the Shudra ; and assumes that the verse refers to unions of these three with females of the four principal castes and of their own"—Bubhler. .
