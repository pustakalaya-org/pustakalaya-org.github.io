    Glossary¶

    Abbreviations: Skt. = Sanskrit; Hi. = Hindi and related languages; Sant. = Santali; Mu. = Mundari and Ho; Go. = Gondi; Kh. = Khasi; Ta. = Tamil; Ka. = Kannada; Mal. = Malayalam; Mar. = Marathi; Od. = Odia; Lad. = Ladakhi. Many terms are used, with variations, in several languages of a region.

    Term Meaning
    Adivasi (ādivāsī) “Original inhabitant”; political self-designation of tribal communities, especially in central and eastern India
    ādivāsī sāhitya (Hi.) “Adivasi literature”; also the critical movement that defines it by worldview as well as by the author’s identity
    āhar–pain (Hi.) Catchment basins (āhar) and diversion channels (pain) of south Bihar
    akhṛā (Mu., Hi.) Village dance ground of Chotanagpur, also a place of gathering and debate; a symbol of the Jharkhand cultural movement
    amchi Practitioner of Sowa-Rigpa, the Tibetan medical system of the Himalaya
    āṭavika (Skt.) Forest chief or forest people, in the Arthaśāstra and inscriptions
    Bāhā (Sant.) Spring flower festival of the Santal, when the sal flowers
    bānā (Go.) Bowed fiddle of the Pardhan bards
    bahurūpī (Hi.) Itinerant performer who entertains by impersonation and disguise
    baigā (Hi.) Village priest-healer in central India; also the name of a community
    bāvaṛī (Hi.) Stepwell
    bewar (Hi.) Shifting cultivation as practised by the Baiga
    bhagat (Hi.) Healer and ritual specialist among Oraon, Bhil and other communities
    Bhārath (Bhili) Oral Mahābhārata of the Dungri Bhils of the Gujarat–Rajasthan border, recorded by Bhagwandas Patel (1984–87)
    bhopā (Hi.) Priest, medium or epic singer of a folk deity in Rajasthan and Gujarat
    bhuinhārī Tenure of the families of the original settlers in Oraon villages
    boṅgā (Sant., Mu.) Deity or spirit
    bulyañ (Apatani) Clan representatives who mediate and arbitrate disputes in Apatani villages
    cakṣudān paṭ “Eye-giving” picture of a dead person painted by jādu paṭuās for Santal patrons
    chāng ghar Stilt house of the Mising of Assam
    chauk (Mar.) Square ritual painting made by Warli women for a wedding
    dāī (Hi.) Traditional birth attendant
    ḍāin (Hi.) “Witch”; term used in witchcraft accusations
    Denotified Tribes Communities once listed under the Criminal Tribes Act (1871), denotified in 1952; many observe 31 August as Vimukta Divas (“Liberation Day”)
    devarakāḍu (Ka.) Sacred grove of Karnataka
    devrāī (Mar.) Sacred grove of Maharashtra
    devvan (Hi.) Forest of a village deity in Himachal Pradesh
    Dhokra Lost-wax brass casting of central and eastern India
    dikū (Mu., Sant.) Outsider who exploits Adivasis (moneylender, trader, landlord, official); a political category of the Jharkhand movement
    dobashi In Nagaland, an official who advises on and adjudicates customary law
    dokhonā Wrapped garment woven and worn by Bodo women
    dorbar shnong (Kh.) Khasi village council
    ēri (Ta.) Irrigation tank
    Gavrī Ritual dance-drama performed by Bhil men in southern Rajasthan
    ghoṭul (Go.) Youth dormitory of the Muria and related Gond groups
    godnā (Hi.) Tattoo
    grāmadevatā (Skt.) Village deity, usually a goddess, worshipped with non-Brahmin priests in much of south India
    gram sabha Village assembly of all adult residents; given wide powers by PESA and the FRA
    guniyā (Hi.) Healer and diviner in central India
    hãṛiyā (Sant., Mu.) Rice beer
    hima (Kh.) Khasi chiefdom headed by a syiem
    Hoṛ (Sant.) “People”; the Santals’ name for themselves
    hul (Sant.) Rebellion; the Santal rising of 1855–56
    Hynniewtrep (Kh.) “Seven huts”; the Khasi–Jaintia people, after their origin narrative
    iḍital (Sora) Ritual wall painting of the Sora
    jajmānī (Hi.) Hereditary relationship in which village artisans and servants worked for landowning families for shares of grain; a term made familiar by W. H. Wiser
    jana (Skt., Hi.) “People”; one half of the title of this volume
    janajāti (Hi.) “Tribe” in modern Hindi and official usage; a Scheduled Tribe is an anusūcit janajāti
    jādu paṭuā Scroll painters who worked for Santal patrons
    Jāher Erā (Sant.) “Lady of the grove”; Santal deity
    jāherthān (Sant.) Sacred grove of a Santal village
    jāti (Skt., Hi.) “Birth”; a group into which one is born, in ordinary usage a caste
    jhum Shifting cultivation in North-East India
    jingkieng jri (Kh.) Living root bridge of the Khasi and Jaintia hills
    johaḍ (Hi.) Earthen embankment holding monsoon runoff, eastern Rajasthan
    kaṭṭumaram (Ta.) Log raft of the Coromandel coast fishers; the source of the English “catamaran”
    kāpālik (Hi.) Style of Paṇḍvānī drawn from memory and oral lore, with improvisation and acting
    Karam Festival of central and eastern India centred on a branch of the karam tree
    kāvu (Mal.) Sacred grove of Kerala
    khovar Wedding wall painting of the Hazaribagh region, made by scratching through layers of earth
    khuṇṭkaṭṭi (Mu.) Rights of the lineages that first cleared village land
    kohbar (Hi.) Nuptial chamber in Mithila, and its wall paintings
    kohli (Hi.) Water master of a kuhl
    Koitur (Go.) Name used by many Gonds for themselves
    Koyā Punem (Go.) Name used for the Gond religion in modern revival movements
    kuḍimarāmattu (Ta.) Annual maintenance of tanks and channels by cultivators
    kuhl (Hi.) Gravity irrigation channel of the western Himalaya
    kumri Shifting cultivation in the Western Ghats
    kuṇḍ (Hi.) Covered rainwater cistern of the Thar desert
    lakhārā (Hi.) Painter of Pithora murals
    law kyntang (Kh.) Sacred forest of the Khasi
    Lingo (Go.) Gond culture hero, who freed the gods and founded the clan system
    mahuā (Hi.) The tree Madhuca longifolia, its flowers and the spirit distilled from them
    mãjhi (Sant.) Santal village headman
    māndar (Hi.) Double-headed drum of Jharkhand
    mānkī (Mu.) Head of a federation of Munda or Ho villages
    Maraṅ Buru (Sant.) “Great mountain”; principal Santal deity
    mautām (Mizo) Gregarious flowering of bamboo, followed by rat plagues and famine
    morung Men’s house or youth dormitory of Naga villages
    muṇḍā (Mu.) Village headman among the Munda and Ho
    nāeke (Sant.) Santal village priest
    nakṣatra (Skt.) One of 27 lunar mansions; in folk usage, a rain period
    Nuākhāī (Od.) Festival of eating the new rice in western Odisha
    ojhā (Hi.) Diviner and healer in eastern India
    Ol Chiki Script devised for Santali by Raghunath Murmu
    oraṇ (Hi.) Sacred grove or deity’s land in Rajasthan
    ovī (Mar.) Short verse sung by women of Maharashtra at the grindstone and at other work
    paṟai (Ta.) Frame drum of Tamil Nadu, long associated with Dalit drummers and now a symbol of Dalit pride
    pāhan (Mu.) Village priest among the Munda and Oraon
    Paṇḍvānī Chhattisgarhi performance of the Mahābhārata, centred on Bhīma; sung earlier by Pardhan and Devār singers from oral lore
    paṛhā (Mu.) Federation of villages among the Munda and Oraon
    Pardhan Hereditary bards and genealogists of the Gond
    parganā Group of villages under a regional head
    pathalgaḍī “Stone-planting”; the setting up of memorial or boundary stones; a movement of 2017–18 in Jharkhand
    pen (Go.) Deity or ancestral power
    PESA Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996
    phaḍ (Hi., Mar.) (1) Painted scroll of the Pābūjī epic; (2) crop block in Maharashtra’s weir irrigation
    Pithora Ritual mural of the Rathwa and Bhilala
    podu Shifting cultivation in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha
    pramāṇa (Skt.) Means of valid knowledge
    puan Woven cloth of the Mizo
    putu menden Council of elders of an Ao Naga village
    PVTG Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group
    rānu (Sant.) Starter cake for rice beer
    ri raid, ri kynti (Kh.) Community land and privately owned land in Khasi customary law
    sadān (Hi.) Long-settled non-Adivasi inhabitants of Chotanagpur
    Sāri Dharam (Sant.) “True religion”; a name for the Santal religion
    Sarhul Spring festival of the Oraon, Munda and others, when the sal flowers
    Sarna (sarnā) Sacred grove; the name of an indigenous religion of Jharkhand and neighbouring states
    sasan diri (Mu.) Memorial stones over the bones of the dead in a Munda or Ho lineage burial ground
    Scheduled Area Area notified under the Fifth Schedule of the Constitution
    Scheduled Tribe Community notified under Article 342 of the Constitution
    Seng Khasi Movement for the indigenous Khasi religion, founded 1899
    Sohrāe (Sant.) Harvest festival in which cattle are honoured
    sohrāī painting Harvest-time wall painting of the Hazaribagh region
    suraṅga (Ka., Mal.) Horizontal tunnel tapping groundwater
    ṭāṇḍā (Hi.) Banjara caravan camp and, later, settlement, led by a headman (nāyak)
    ṭāṅkā (Hi.) Underground rainwater cistern
    tārpā (Mar.) Gourd-and-bamboo wind instrument of the Warli
    ṭhengā pālī (Od.) “Stick turn”; rotation of forest-patrol duty among households
    tlawmngaihna (Mizo) Mizo ethic of putting others first and helping without seeking reward
    Tribal Panchsheel Nehru’s five principles of tribal policy (1959)
    ulgulān (Mu.) “Great tumult”; Birsa Munda’s rising of 1899–1900
    vanvāsī (Hi.) “Forest dweller”; term preferred by Hindu nationalist organisations
    vedamatī (Hi.) Text-based style of Paṇḍvānī, following a written Hindi Mahābhārata
    yura, zing (Lad.) Irrigation channel and small storage pond in Ladakh
    zabo Integrated forest, pond and terrace system of the Chakhesang Naga
    zawlbuk (Mizo) House in which the young unmarried men of a Mizo village slept and served the village
