    Preface¶

    This is Volume 23 of the series Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā: Jana–Jāti: Tribal and Folk Knowledge. Most of it is about the knowledge held by India’s Adivasi communities: knowledge of forests, fields and sacred groves, of water, food and the sky, of healing, of myth, epic and song, of painting and craft, and of village self-government. One chapter turns to the folk knowledge of village communities that are not Adivasi: cultivators, herders, fishers, artisans and Dalit specialists. The closing chapters describe the law, politics and history, from colonial forest law to the Forest Rights Act, on which the survival of this knowledge depends.

    Janajāti is the Hindi and official word for “tribe”; the title divides it into jana, “people”, and jāti, “birth-group”, the usual word for caste (Section 1.2). The Sanskrit series title and dedication belong to the series as a whole; this volume does not treat Sanskrit categories as the measure of Adivasi knowledge.

    The volume relies on sources that can be identified and checked: sayings are quoted only where their source is known, population figures follow the Census of India, and claims about folk remedies carry safety notes where health is at stake. Adivasi writers, scholars and leaders are named and their published work is described and cited, but their songs and poems are not reproduced; readers should go to the editions listed.

    Two approaches have been avoided: the romantic picture of Adivasis as “noble savages” in timeless harmony with nature, and the absorbing view that treats their religions and knowledge as rustic forms of Hinduism or as raw material awaiting validation by others. The work of Adivasi scholars, writers and artists, and of ethnographers and historians from Verrier Elwin to Virginius Xaxa and Nandini Sundar, is collected in the select bibliography. Maps, diagrams, a glossary and a chronology are included. This volume is an introduction; readers should turn to the works cited, and above all to Adivasi writing and art.

    Nikhil Kumar Rajput, Ramanujan College, University of Delhi
    Guru Pūrṇimā, 2026
