    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate and many are disputed; where scholarly opinion varies, a range is given. Traditional dates are noted as such where they are mentioned in the text.

    Date Texts, persons and events
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core of the Ṛgveda; ṛta, Varuṇa and Mitra; the Vedic rājan, sabhā and samiti
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic Saṃhitās, Brāhmaṇas, early Upaniṣads; royal consecration rites
    5th c. BCE The Buddha (c. 480–400 BCE, short chronology) and Mahāvīra; gaṇa-saṅgha polities of the Gangetic plain
    c. 321–297 BCE Candragupta Maurya (traditionally advised by Cāṇakya)
    c. 300 BCE Megasthenes at the Mauryan court
    c. 268–232 BCE Aśoka; Rock and Pillar Edicts
    c. 3rd–1st c. BCE Dharmasūtras of Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana and Vasiṣṭha
    c. 400 BCE–400 CE Composition of the Mahābhārata (including the Śānti Parvan) and Rāmāyaṇa
    c. 1st c. BCE Khāravela of Kaliṅga (Hāthīgumphā inscription)
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE The Arthaśāstra: Kauṭilya recension c. 50–125 CE; Śāstric redaction c. 175–300 CE (Olivelle)
    early 2nd c. CE Nasik inscription of Uṣavadāta recording guild endowments
    c. 2nd c. CE Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)
    4th c. CE Samudragupta; Allahabad pillar inscription
    c. 4th–5th c. CE Yājñavalkyasmṛti
    c. 5th c. CE Tirukkuṟaḷ
    c. 5th–6th c. CE Nāradasmṛti
    mid-5th–mid-6th c. Damodarpur copper plates: sales of land by the district office of Koṭivarṣa
    c. 500–700 CE Kāmandaka, Nītisāra
    c. 6th–7th c. CE Bṛhaspati and Kātyāyana (lost law books, known from quotations)
    c. 606–647 Harṣa of Kanauj; visit of Xuanzang
    7th c. Bāṇa, Kādambarī; Daṇḍin, Daśakumāracarita
    9th c. Jinasena, Ādipurāṇa; Viśvarūpa’s commentary on Yājñavalkya
    9th–10th c. Medhātithi, commentary on Manu
    919, 921 Uttaramerūr inscriptions of Parāntaka I
    10th c. Somadeva Sūri, Nītivākyāmṛta
    c. 10th–13th c. Caulukya and Vāghelā Gujarat; most of the model documents of the Lekhapaddhati
    c. 1100–1120 Vijñāneśvara, Mitākṣarā
    12th c. Jīmūtavāhana, Dāyabhāga; Lakṣmīdhara, Kṛtyakalpataru; Aparārka
    1131 Someśvara III, Mānasollāsa
    1143–1172 Kumārapāla of Gujarat; Hemacandra
    1148–49 Kalhaṇa, Rājataraṅgiṇī
    1206 Foundation of the Delhi Sultanate
    13th c. Ahoms enter the Brahmaputra valley; earliest mention of dhammasattha in a Pagan inscription; Kākatīya nāyakas
    13th c. Hemādri, Caturvargacintāmaṇi
    1235 Naṣīr al-Dīn Ṭūsī, Akhlāq-i Nāṣirī
    1296–1316 ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn Khaljī; market regulations at Delhi
    1325–1351 Muḥammad b. Tughluq; Ibn Baṭṭūṭa a qāḍī at Delhi
    14th c. Baranī, Fatāwā-yi Jahāndārī; Caṇḍeśvara, Vivādaratnākara and Rājanītiratnākara; Mādhava’s commentary on Parāśara
    c. 1336 Foundation of Vijayanagara
    1351–1388 Fīrūz Shāh Tughluq; Futūḥāt-i Fīrūzshāhī; Fatāwā-yi Tātārkhāniyya
    1368 Bukka I settles a dispute between Jains and Śrīvaiṣṇavas (Śravaṇabeḷagoḷa)
    1425 Virinchipuram inscription: Brahmins of the Paḍaivīḍu kingdom agree to marriage without payment for the bride
    early 16th c. Dalapati, Nṛsiṃhaprasāda, at the Niẓām Shāhī court; Kṛṣṇadevarāya (r. 1509–1529), Āmuktamālyada
    1510 Portuguese conquest of Goa
    1526 Foundation of the Mughal empire
    1556–1605 Akbar; abolition of jizya (1564); ʿIbādat-khāna (from 1575); maḥżar (1579); dahsāla (1580)
    1560–1812 Inquisition at Goa
    1605–1627 Jahāngīr; the chain of justice and twelve ordinances
    1612 Kamalākara Bhaṭṭa, Nirṇayasindhu (Banaras)
    1660s Muhaṇot Naiṇsī, survey of the districts of Marwar
    17th c. Nīlakaṇṭha, Vyavahāramayūkha; Mitra Miśra, Vīramitrodaya
    1660s–1670s Compilation of the Fatāwā-i ʿĀlamgīrī
    1674 Coronation of Śivājī, performed by Gāgā Bhaṭṭa of Banaras
    1699 Khālsā instituted by Guru Gobind Singh (traditional date)
    1708 Death of Guru Gobind Singh; authority passes to the Guru Granth and the Guru Panth
    1679 Reimposition of jizya by Aurangzeb
    1703–1762 Shāh Walī Allāh of Delhi, Ḥujjat Allāh al-bāligha
    c. 1716 Rāmacandrapant Amātya, Ājñāpatra (Marathi treatise on government)
    1748 Sikh bands combined in the Dal Khālsā by resolution (gurmatā), traditional date
    1750s Manugye law book compiled in Burma
    1765 East India Company receives the dīwānī of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa
    1772 Warren Hastings’s Judicial Plan
    1773–1776 Vivādārṇavasetu compiled; Halhed, A Code of Gentoo Laws (1776)
    1786 First Shīʿī congregational Friday prayer at Lucknow, led by Dildār ʿAlī
    1791–1794 Hamilton, The Hedaya (1791); Jones, Institutes of Hindu Law (1794)
    1797–1798 Colebrooke, Digest of Hindu Law (from the Vivādabhaṅgārṇava)
    1799, 1801 Ranjit Singh takes Lahore; assumes the title of Mahārājā
    1801–1839 Ranjit Singh, Mahārājā at Lahore
    1810 Colebrooke, Two Treatises on the Hindu Law of Inheritance
    1829 Bengal Sati Regulation
    1850 Caste Disabilities Removal Act
    1856 Hindu Widows’ Remarriage Act
    1860 Indian Penal Code (in force 1862)
    1864 Abolition of the posts of court paṇḍits and maulavīs
    1871 Criminal Tribes Act
    1872 Indian Evidence Act; Indian Contract Act; Punjab Laws Act
    1873 Jotirao Phule, Gulamgiri
    1880, 1881 Goa decree codifying Hindu usages; French decree permitting Indians to renounce their personal law
    1882, 1887 Tarabai Shinde, Strī-Puruṣ Tulanā; Pandita Ramabai, The High-Caste Hindu Woman
    1891 Age of Consent Act
    1894 Privy Council rejects the family waqf (Abul Fata)
    1902, 1904 Shahu reserves half of Kolhapur’s posts for the backward classes; Baroda law against child marriage
    1905–1915 Rediscovery and publication of the Arthaśāstra by R. Shamasastry
    1913 Mussalman Wakf Validating Act
    1927 Mahad satyagraha; burning of the Manusmṛti (25 December)
    1929 Child Marriage Restraint Act
    1930–1962 P. V. Kane, History of Dharmaśāstra
    1936 B. R. Ambedkar, Annihilation of Caste
    1937 Hindu Women’s Right to Property Act; Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act
    1939 Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act
    1948 Constituent Assembly debate on the uniform civil code (23 November)
    1950 Constitution of India in force (26 January)
    1952 Criminal Tribes Laws (Repeal) Act; the notified communities “denotified”
    1955–1956 Hindu Marriage, Succession, Minority and Guardianship, and Adoptions and Maintenance Acts
    1956 Ambedkar’s conversion to Buddhism at Nagpur
    1960–1965 R. P. Kangle, critical edition and translation of the Arthaśāstra
    1972 Dalit Panthers founded in Bombay
    1985 Shah Bano judgment
    1989 Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act
    1992–1993 Seventy-third Amendment: constitutional status for pañcāyats
    2005 Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act: daughters become coparceners
    2005, 2013 Olivelle, critical edition and translation of the Manusmṛti (2005); translation of the Arthaśāstra (2013)
    2018 Law Commission consultation paper on reform of family law
    2024 Uniform Civil Code of Uttarakhand (in force January 2025)
