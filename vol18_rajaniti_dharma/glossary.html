    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Pa. = Pali, Pk. = Prakrit, Ar. = Arabic, As. = Assamese, Fr. = French, Ka. = Kannada, Te. = Telugu, Pe. = Persian, Ta. = Tamil, Mal. = Malayalam, Pj. = Panjabi). Chapter references are to the chapters in which the term is chiefly discussed.

    Term Meaning
    abhiṣeka Consecration, especially of a king, by sprinkling with water (ch. 5)
    ācāra Conduct; custom; in Dharmaśāstra, the division dealing with rites, daily duties and the rules of varṇa and āśrama (ch. 9, 15)
    ādhi Pledge given as security for a loan (ch. 13)
    adhikaraṇa Office; in Gupta Bengal, the district office (ch. 17)
    adhyakṣa Superintendent of a department of state in the Arthaśāstra (ch. 6)
    agrahāra, brahmadeya Village or land granted to Brahmins, usually free of taxes (ch. 5, 6, 16)
    akṣapaṭala Records office of the state (ch. 6, 17)
    amaram (Ka., Te.) Territory held by a nāyaka for military service (ch. 17)
    amātya Minister; the second constituent of the state (ch. 4)
    anuloma, pratiloma Union of a man with a woman of lower (anuloma) or higher (pratiloma) varṇa (ch. 10, 15)
    ānvīkṣikī Critical inquiry; one of the four sciences of the Arthaśāstra (ch. 3)
    āpaddharma Dharma in times of distress; the rules that apply in emergencies (ch. 5)
    artha Material well-being, wealth and power; one of the aims of life (ch. 3)
    arthaśāstra The science of polity and statecraft; also the treatise ascribed to Kauṭilya (ch. 3)
    aśauca Ritual impurity following birth or death (ch. 15)
    āsedha Restraint of a defendant, in the king’s name, until he answers a claim (ch. 12)
    āśrama One of the four orders or stages of life: student, householder, forest-dweller, renouncer (ch. 15)
    ātatāyin Armed assailant, who may be killed in self-defence (ch. 14)
    aṭavika Forest-dweller; forest people (ch. 6)
    bahiṣkāra Outcasting; exclusion from a caste (ch. 14, 16)
    bandagān (Pe.) Military slaves of the early Delhi sultans (ch. 18)
    bhāga The king’s share of the harvest; the land tax (ch. 6)
    bhukti Possession; as a means of proof, especially of title to land (ch. 12)
    buraṇjī (As.) Chronicle of the Ahom kings of Assam (ch. 17)
    cakravartin (Pa. cakkavattin) “Wheel-turner”; universal monarch (ch. 8)
    caritra Custom; one of the four bases of a lawsuit in the Arthaśāstra (ch. 12)
    caturaṅga The four arms of the army: elephants, chariots, cavalry, infantry (ch. 4, 7)
    dāna Gift; giving, especially to Brahmins and religious institutions; the law of gift (ch. 2, 13)
    daṇḍa Rod; coercive power, punishment, the army (ch. 5, 14)
    daṇḍanīti “Policy of the rod”; the science of government (ch. 3, 14)
    dāsa Slave (ch. 6, 13)
    dattaka Adopted son, “given” by his natural parents (ch. 13, 15)
    dāyabhāga Partition of inheritance; also Jīmūtavāhana’s treatise on it (ch. 11)
    dhamma (Pa., Pk.) The Buddha’s teaching; Aśoka’s moral policy (ch. 8)
    dhammasattha (Pa.) Law book of Burma and mainland Southeast Asia (ch. 9)
    dharma The order that upholds the world and society; duty, right conduct, law (ch. 2, 9)
    dharmaśāstra The scholarly literature on dharma (ch. 9)
    dharmasūtra Prose treatise on dharma; the earliest genre of Dharmaśāstra (ch. 9)
    dharmayuddha Righteous war fought according to rules (ch. 7)
    dhimmī (Ar.) A protected non-Muslim subject of a Muslim state (ch. 18)
    divya Ordeal; “divine” proof (ch. 12)
    dīwānī (Pe.) Right to collect revenue and administer civil justice; a civil court (ch. 19)
    diya (Ar.) Compensation (“blood-money”) for homicide or injury (ch. 18)
    durga Fort; one of the constituents of the state (ch. 4)
    dūta Envoy (ch. 7)
    dvija “Twice-born”; a man of the three higher varṇas who has undergone initiation (ch. 10, 15)
    farmān (Pe.) Order issued under the seal of a Mughal emperor (ch. 18)
    fatwā (Ar.) Legal opinion issued by a muftī (ch. 18)
    fiqh (Ar.) Islamic jurisprudence (ch. 18)
    gaṇa, saṅgha A corporate body; also a polity governed by an assembly of a ruling clan (ch. 8, 13)
    gotra Patrilineal clan; marriage within it was forbidden (ch. 15)
    gurmatā (Pj.) “The Guru’s counsel”; a resolution of the assembled Khālsā, binding on all Sikhs (ch. 18)
    ḥadd (Ar., pl. ḥudūd) Offence with a penalty held to be fixed by revelation (ch. 18)
    ijtihād (Ar.) Independent legal reasoning, practised by a mujtahid (ch. 18)
    jāgīr (Pe.) Assignment of land revenue in place of salary in the Mughal state (ch. 18)
    janapada The populated countryside; one of the constituents of the state (ch. 4)
    jāti Birth; an endogamous hereditary group; “caste” (ch. 1, 10, 16)
    jayapatra “Document of victory”; written decree given to the successful party (ch. 12)
    jizya (Ar.) Poll-tax levied on non-Muslim subjects (ch. 18)
    kalivarjya Practices forbidden in the Kali age (ch. 9)
    kaṇṭakaśodhana “Removal of thorns”; suppression of crime (ch. 12, 14)
    kāraṇavan (Mal.) Senior male manager of a matrilineal household (ch. 16)
    kāyastha Scribe; later, hereditary communities of scribes (ch. 17)
    khālsā (Pj.) The order of initiated Sikhs instituted by Guru Gobind Singh (ch. 18, 19)
    khulʿ (Ar.) Divorce obtained by a wife by agreement, usually by returning the mahr (ch. 18)
    kośa Treasury; one of the constituents of the state (ch. 4, 6)
    kotwāl (Pe.) Chief of police of a town (ch. 18)
    kuṭavōlai (Ta.) “Pot-ticket”; selection by lot in Cōḷa village assemblies (ch. 16)
    kūṭayuddha Concealed war; war by stratagem (ch. 7)
    madad-i maʿāsh (Pe.) Revenue grant to scholars, holy men and shrines (ch. 18)
    madhhab (Ar.) School of Islamic jurisprudence (ch. 18)
    madhyama The “middle” king in the circle of kings (ch. 7)
    mahāpātaka Grave sin (ch. 14)
    mahr (Ar.) Marriage gift owed by the husband to the wife in Islamic law (ch. 18)
    maḥżar (Ar., Pe.) Attestation by witnesses; Akbar’s declaration of 1579 (ch. 18)
    maṇḍala Circle; the circle of kings around a would-be conqueror (ch. 7)
    manṣab (Pe.) Rank in the Mughal nobility (ch. 18)
    mantra Counsel, deliberation (in polity); sacred formula (in ritual) (ch. 4)
    marumakkattāyam (Mal.) Matrilineal inheritance in Kerala (ch. 16)
    mātsyanyāya “Law of the fish”; anarchy in which the strong devour the weak (ch. 5)
    misl (Pj.) One of the Sikh confederacies of the eighteenth century, each led by a sardār (ch. 18)
    mitra Ally; one of the constituents of the state (ch. 4, 7)
    muftī (Ar.) Jurisconsult who issues legal opinions (ch. 18)
    muḥtasib (Ar.) Officer supervising markets and public morals (ch. 18)
    mūvēntar (Ta.) The three crowned kings (Cēra, Cōḷa, Pāṇṭiya) of the Caṅkam poems (ch. 5)
    nāṭu (Ta.) Locality; the basic territorial unit of the Cōḷa state (ch. 5)
    nāyaka Military chief holding territory for service (ch. 17)
    nibandha Digest; a topical compilation of quotations from the smṛtis (ch. 9)
    nikṣepa Deposit (ch. 13)
    nirṇayapatra Written decision of an assembly of paṇḍits (ch. 18)
    nītiśāstra The science of policy; works on polity (ch. 3)
    nīvīdharma Perpetual endowment; only its income is used (ch. 17)
    niyoga Levirate: appointment of a kinsman to beget a son on a widow or the wife of an incapable man (ch. 10, 15)
    paik (As.) Ahom subject registered for labour and military service (ch. 17)
    paṇa Silver coin and unit of account in the Arthaśāstra (ch. 6)
    pañcāyat Council (in principle of five) of a village or caste (ch. 16)
    paṇḍita Learned Brahmin; court expert in Dharmaśāstra under the East India Company (ch. 9, 18, 19)
    pariṣad Assembly of learned Brahmins deciding questions of dharma (ch. 9, 14)
    prāḍvivāka Judge (ch. 12)
    prajā Subjects; offspring (ch. 1, 5)

    prāyaścitta Penance; expiation (ch. 14)
    purohita Royal chaplain (ch. 5)
    puruṣārtha Aims of human life: dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa (ch. 3)

    qāḍī (Ar.) Judge in an Islamic court (ch. 18)

    rājadharma The dharma of the king (ch. 5)
    rājaśāsana Royal edict (ch. 12)
    rājasūya Vedic royal consecration (ch. 2)
    renonçant (Fr.) In French India, one who renounced his personal law (ch. 19)
    ṛṇa Debt; also the three debts with which a man is born (ch. 2, 13)
