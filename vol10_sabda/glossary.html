    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Pa. = Pali, Pk. = Prakrit, Ta. = Tamil, Ka. = Kannada, Te. = Telugu, Pe. = Persian, Ar. = Arabic).

    Term Meaning
    abhidhā Denotation: the primary meaning of a word, learned by convention
    abhihitānvaya Kumārila’s theory that words first express their own meanings, which are then connected into the sentence-meaning
    abugida Modern term for a script in which consonant signs carry an inherent vowel and other vowels are marked by attached signs
    adhikāra A heading rule whose content carries over into a following section of the grammar
    aintilakkaṇam (Ta.) The five divisions of Tamil grammar: letters, words, subject-matter, prosody and figures of speech
    akam (Ta.) “Interior”: classical Tamil love poetry
    ākāṅkṣā Syntactic expectancy between the words of a sentence
    ākhyāta Verb (in Yāska’s four word classes)
    ākṛti “Form”: the universal, as a possible meaning of a word
    anekārtha, nānārtha A dictionary of homonyms (words with several meanings)
    anubandha, it A marker sound attached to a grammatical element and deleted in the final form
    anudātta The low pitch accent
    anuṣṭubh Vedic metre of four eight-syllable lines; ancestor of the śloka
    anusvāra Nasalisation written ṃ
    anuvṛtti Recurrence: the carrying over of words from one rule into following rules
    anvitābhidhāna Prabhākara’s theory that words express their meanings as already connected with one another
    Apabhraṃśa “Falling away”: the last stage of Middle Indo-Aryan, a literary language from c. 6th century
    apavāda A special rule that overrides a general rule (utsarga)
    apoha “Exclusion”: the Buddhist theory that words convey meaning by excluding what they do not apply to
    arisamāsa (Ka.) “Enemy compound”: a compound joining Sanskrit and Kannada words, treated as a fault
    āryā A moraic metre, inherited from Prakrit gāthā poetry
    asiddha “Not effected”: the convention by which later rules of the tripādī are invisible to earlier ones
    bahuvrīhi Possessive (exocentric) compound
    Brāhmī The ancestral script of most South and Southeast Asian scripts, first attested in Aśoka’s inscriptions
    chandas Metre; the science of metre
    deśī Words of regional, non-Sanskrit origin in the Prakrits
    deśya In Telugu and Kannada grammar, a native word not derived from Sanskrit
    Devanāgarī The script, developed from Nāgarī, now most widely used for Sanskrit, Hindi, Marathi, Nepali and other languages
    dhātu Verbal root
    Dhātupāṭha The list of verbal roots of the Pāṇinian system
    dhvani (1) Audible sound, as opposed to the sphoṭa; (2) suggestion, in poetics
    dvandva Copulative compound
    eḻuttu (Ta.) “Letter”: sound and written sign
    gaṇa (1) A class of roots or a list of words in the grammar; (2) a group of three syllables in prosody
    Gāndhārī The Prakrit of the north-west, written in Kharoṣṭhī
    ghanapāṭha The most elaborate mode of Vedic recitation, combining words in pairs and triplets forwards and backwards
    grāmya (Te.) “Rustic”: colloquial words excluded from literary Telugu
    Grantha “Book” script, used in the Tamil country to write Sanskrit; it influenced the Tamil and Malayalam scripts
    guṇa In grammar, the vowels a, e, o (the middle grade of vowel strengthening)
    guru A heavy syllable (two morae)
    iṭai (Ta.) Particle, “intermediate” word
    jāti Universal; also a moraic metre
    kāraka A participant in an action as defined by Pāṇini (agent, object, instrument, recipient, source, locus)
    Kharoṣṭhī Right-to-left script of the north-west, derived from Aramaic
    kośa Dictionary, lexicon
    kramapāṭha “Step” recitation of a Vedic text, in which each word is joined with the next
    kṛt Primary suffix, added to a root
    laghu (1) A light syllable (one mora); (2) brevity, economy (lāghava)
    lakāra Pāṇini’s abstract symbol for a tense or mood
    lakṣaṇā Indication: a secondary meaning used when the primary one does not fit
    lipi Writing, script
    lughat (Ar., Pe.) Lexicography; a dictionary
    madhyamā “Middle” speech: inner speech already in sequence
    mahāprāṇa, alpaprāṇa Aspirated and unaspirated consonants
    maṇipravāḷam “Gems and coral”: the mixed Malayalam–Sanskrit literary style of Kerala
    mātrā (1) Mora, unit of syllable length; (2) vowel sign attached to a consonant
    mleccha Non-Sanskrit speaker; “barbarian”
    Nāgarī The north Indian script of the late 1st millennium from which Devanāgarī developed
    naḥw (Ar.) Syntax, in the Arabic grammatical tradition
    nāman Noun
    nastaʿlīq (Pe.) The hanging style of Perso-Arabic script used for Urdu and Kashmiri
    Nighaṇṭu The Vedic word list on which Yāska’s Nirukta comments
    nipāta Particle
    nirukta “Explanation”: the Vedāṅga concerned with the meaning of words, especially Vedic words; Yāska’s treatise of that name
    nirvacana Explanation of a word by connecting it with a verbal root
    nitya Eternal; also, in grammar, a rule that applies whether or not a competing rule has applied
    padapāṭha Word-by-word recitation of a Vedic text, with sandhi undone
    pakupatam (Ta.) A word that can be analysed into parts, as opposed to pakāpatam, one that cannot (Naṉṉūl)
    Pali (Pa. pāli) “Text”: the language of the Theravāda Buddhist canon
    paribhāṣā Interpretive principle or meta-rule
    paśyantī “Seeing” speech: speech without sequence, Bhartṛhari’s highest level
    peyar (Ta.) Noun
    pluta A prolated vowel of three morae
    Prākṛta Prakrit: a Middle Indo-Aryan language
    prastāra Systematic listing of all metrical patterns of a given length
    pratibhā Intuitive flash in which sentence-meaning is grasped (Bhartṛhari)
    Prātiśākhya Phonetic manual of a Vedic school
    pratyāhāra An abbreviation for a class of sounds formed from the Śivasūtras
    pratyaya Suffix; in prosody, a procedure of enumeration
    prayatna Articulatory effort, manner of articulation
    puṇarcci (Ta.) Combination of sounds; Tamil sandhi
    puṟam (Ta.) “Exterior”: classical Tamil poetry of war, kingship and public life
    Rekhta “Mixed”: an 18th-century name for literary Urdu, especially its poetry
    śābdabodha The cognition produced by a sentence, as analysed by grammarians and logicians
    śakti The denotative power of a word
    samāsa Compound
    sambuddhi Pāṇini’s term for the vocative singular (2.3.49)
    saṃhitāpāṭha Continuous recitation of a Vedic text, with sandhi
    saṃjñā Technical term; a definitional rule
    sandhi Euphonic combination of sounds at junctures
    ṣarf (Ar.) Morphology, in the Arabic grammatical tradition
    śāstrakāvya A poem composed to illustrate a science, above all the rules of grammar, as in the Bhaṭṭikāvya
    Siddhamātṛkā North Indian script of the 6th–10th centuries, ancestor of Nāgarī and of the Siddham script of East Asian Buddhism
    śikṣā Phonetics; a phonetic treatise
    śiṣṭa A “cultivated” speaker whose usage sets the standard (Patañjali)
    Śivasūtras The fourteen sound-lists at the head of Pāṇini’s grammar
    śloka The classical epic metre of four eight-syllable lines
    sphoṭa The unitary word or sentence manifested by sounds
    sthāna Place of articulation
    sūtra A concise rule or aphorism
    sup, tiṅ Pāṇini’s abbreviations for the nominal and the verbal endings
    svara (1) Vowel; (2) pitch accent
    svarita The falling pitch accent
    tadbhava, tatsama A word inherited with sound change from Old Indo-Aryan; a word borrowed unchanged from Sanskrit
    taddhita Secondary suffix, added to a nominal stem
    tatpuruṣa Determinative compound
    tavāfuq (Pe.) Correspondence or agreement between languages (Ārzū)
    tiṇai (Ta.) Poetic situation or landscape in Tamil poetics
    tripādī The last three quarters of the Aṣṭādhyāyī (8.2–8.4)
    udātta The high (raised) pitch accent
    uri (Ta.) Qualifying word
    upasarga Preverb, such as pra or sam
    utti (Ta.) A device of exposition in a treatise; the Tamil counterpart of Sanskrit tantrayukti
    uyartiṇai, aḵṟiṇai (Ta.) The “high class” (humans, gods) and “non-high class” of Tamil nouns
    vaikharī Articulated, audible speech
    varga A section of a synonym dictionary (kośa), grouping words by subject
    vārttika A critical note on a basic text, especially Kātyāyana’s notes on Pāṇini
    vaṭacol (Ta.) “Northern word”: a Sanskrit loanword in Tamil
    Vaṭṭeḻuttu (Ta.) “Rounded letters”: a script of the Tamil country and Kerala descended from Tamil-Brāhmī
    Vedāṅga “Limb of the Veda”: the six auxiliary sciences of phonetics, ritual, grammar, semantic explanation, metre and astronomy
    veṇpā (Ta.) A strict Tamil verse form, used in the Tirukkuṟaḷ
    vibhakti Case ending; case
    vikṛti The complex modes of Vedic recitation derived from the kramapāṭha
    viṉai (Ta.) Verb
    virāma The sign that cancels the inherent vowel of a consonant
    vṛddhi In grammar, the vowels ā, ai, au (the strongest grade)
    vṛtta A fixed syllabic metre
    vyākaraṇa Grammar, “analysis”
    vyañjanā Suggestion, the power by which poetry conveys implicit meaning
    yogyatā Semantic compatibility between the words of a sentence
