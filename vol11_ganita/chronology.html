    Chronology¶

    Dates before about 500 CE are approximate, and many later dates are uncertain. Where scholarly opinion varies, a range is given. Traditional dates are not used.

    Date Authors, works and events
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Ṛgveda (core): decimal number words
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts: names for powers of ten up to \(10^{12}\)
    c. 800–200 BCE Śulbasūtras of Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, Kātyāyana and the Mānava school
    c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE (uncertain) Piṅgala, Chandaḥśāstra
    3rd c. BCE Brāhmī numerals in inscriptions (non-positional)
    last centuries BCE (disputed) Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa
    early centuries CE Jain canonical and commentarial mathematics; Yavanajātaka (date disputed)
    458 Stated date of the Lokavibhāga (word-numerals with zero)
    499 Āryabhaṭa, Āryabhaṭīya
    mid-6th c. Varāhamihira, Pañcasiddhāntikā and Bṛhatsaṃhitā
    628 Brahmagupta, Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta
    629 Bhāskara I, commentary on the Āryabhaṭīya
    662 Severus Sebokht mentions the Indian “nine signs”
    665 Brahmagupta, Khaṇḍakhādyaka
    c. 7th c. (disputed) Bakhshālī manuscript (work dated c. 7th c. by Hayashi; radiocarbon dates 3rd–10th c.)
    683–686 Śaka-dated inscriptions with zero in Cambodia and Sumatra
    718 Gautama Siddha, Jiuzhi li, in China
    735 Arabic Zīj al-Arkand, based on the Khaṇḍakhādyaka
    c. 750 (uncertain) Śrīdhara, Pāṭīgaṇita
    c. 770–773 Indian astronomer at Baghdad; Zīj al-Sindhind
    c. 825 Al-Khwārizmī’s arithmetic of Indian numerals
    c. 850 Mahāvīra, Gaṇitasārasaṅgraha
    864 Pṛthūdakasvāmin’s commentary on Brahmagupta
    876 Gwalior inscriptions with a circle for zero
    904; 932 Vaṭeśvara, Vaṭeśvarasiddhānta; Muñjāla, Laghumānasa
    10th c. Halāyudha’s commentary on Piṅgala (meruprastāra)
    976 Codex Vigilanus: first Hindu–Arabic numerals in Europe
    c. 1030 Al-Bīrūnī, Taḥqīq mā li-l-Hind
    c. 1039 Śrīpati, Gaṇitatilaka and Siddhāntaśekhara
    before 1073 Jayadeva’s cyclic method (quoted by Udayadivākara, 1073)
    11th c. (traditional; later dates proposed) Pāvulūri Mallana, Telugu version of the Gaṇitasārasaṅgraha
    early 12th c. Rājāditya, arithmetic in Kannada
    c. 1150 Hemacandra, Chandonuśāsana; Bhāskara II, Līlāvatī, Bījagaṇita, Siddhāntaśiromaṇi
    1202 Leonardo of Pisa (Fibonacci), Liber Abaci
    c. 1240 Śārṅgadeva, Saṅgītaratnākara
    13th c. Siṃhatilaka Sūri, commentary on Śrīpati’s Gaṇitatilaka
    c. 1318 Ṭhakkura Pherū, Gaṇitasārakaumudī (Apabhraṃśa)
    c. 1340–1425 Mādhava of Saṅgamagrāma
    1356 Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita, Gaṇitakaumudī
    1370 Mahendra Sūri, Yantrarāja (astrolabe)
    1431 Parameśvara, Dṛggaṇita
    1500 Nīlakaṇṭha Somayājī, Tantrasaṅgraha
    1503 Jñānarāja, Siddhāntasundara
    1520 Gaṇeśa Daivajña, Grahalāghava (epoch)
    c. 1530 Jyeṣṭhadeva, Yuktibhāṣā
    1545 Gaṇeśa Daivajña, Buddhivilāsinī
    mid-16th c. Śaṅkara Vāriyar and Nārāyaṇa, Kriyākramakarī
    1587 Fayḍī’s Persian translation of the Līlāvatī
    c. 1600 Kṛṣṇa Daivajña, Bījapallava
    1634–35 ʿAṭāʾ Allāh Rushdī’s Persian translation of the Bījagaṇita
    1646 Munīśvara, Siddhāntasārvabhauma
    1658 Kamalākara, Siddhāntatattvaviveka
    c. 1724–1735 Jai Singh II’s observatories
    1790s Andrew Bell adapts the pupil-teachers of Madras schools (“monitorial system”)
    1817 Colebrooke’s translation of Brahmagupta and Bhāskara II; Calcutta School-Book Society founded
    1819 or 1823 Śaṅkara Varman, Sadratnamālā
    1820s Survey of indigenous schools in the Madras Presidency
    1830s Lancelot Wilkinson teaches astronomy from Bhāskara II at Sehore
    1834 Whish reports the Kerala series
    1835–38 William Adam’s reports on vernacular education in Bengal and Bihar
    1861 Wilkinson’s English translation of Bhāskara II’s Golādhyāya, with the Jyotpatti, published as revised by Bāpūdeva Śāstrī
    1881 Bakhshālī manuscript found (radiocarbon dates of 2017 disputed)
    1887–1920 Srinivasa Ramanujan
    1912 Rangācārya’s edition and translation of the Gaṇitasārasaṅgraha
    1935–38 Datta and Singh, History of Hindu Mathematics
    1965 Publication of Vedic Mathematics (a modern work)
