    Preface¶

    This is Volume 11 of the series Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā: Gaṇita: Mathematics. It traces the history of mathematics in the Indian subcontinent from the geometry of the Vedic altar-builders recorded in the Śulbasūtras, through the place-value numerals and the arithmetic of zero, the astronomical and algebraic treatises of Āryabhaṭa, Brahmagupta and the two Bhāskaras, the combinatorics that grew out of Sanskrit prosody, and the infinite series of the Kerala school, to the exchanges with the Islamic world and Europe and the work of Srinivasa Ramanujan and his successors. Three chapters treat the arithmetic books, the mathematics of trade and building, and mathematics in the regional languages and Persian; a section on the commentators and astronomers of the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries carries the Sanskrit tradition beyond Bhāskara II.

    The mathematics is presented so that it can be checked: rules are restated in modern notation, and worked examples and exercises with answers accompany every chapter. Verses are quoted only where their wording and reference are well established. Modern comparisons are labelled as comparisons, not presented as claims that Indian texts “anticipated” later results; questions that are genuinely open, such as the dating of the Bakhshālī manuscript, whether the Kerala work should be called “calculus”, or the possible transmission of Kerala mathematics to Europe, are presented as debates, with each position attributed to those who hold it. Dates are given as the range accepted in current scholarship, with traditional dates labelled as such.

    The work rests on the scholarship of many others, above all Bibhutibhushan Datta and Avadhesh Narayan Singh, David Pingree, K. S. Shukla, K. V. Sarma, Takao Hayashi, Kim Plofker, Agathe Keller, and K. Ramasubramanian and his colleagues, whose editions and studies are cited in the text and collected in the select bibliography. Brief biographical notes, a glossary of technical terms and a chronology are provided at the end. Readers who wish to use any result or quotation for scholarly purposes should check it against the critical editions cited.

    Nikhil Kumar Rajput
    Ramanujan College, University of Delhi
    Guru Pūrṇimā, 2026
