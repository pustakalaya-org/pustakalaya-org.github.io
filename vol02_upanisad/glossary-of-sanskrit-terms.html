    Glossary of Sanskrit Terms¶

    Term Meaning
    Adhikāra Qualification or fitness of a student to receive a teaching
    Adhyāropa-apavāda Provisional attribution followed by negation; Śaṅkara’s method of teaching
    Adhyāsa Superimposition; in Advaita, the mistaking of the not-Self for the Self and vice versa
    Advaita “Non-duality”; the Vedānta school of Gauḍapāda and Śaṅkara
    Ajāti-vāda Gauḍapāda’s doctrine that nothing has ever truly originated
    Akṣara The imperishable; also “syllable”, especially Oṃ
    Āḻvār (Tamil) One of the twelve Śrīvaiṣṇava poet-saints whose hymns form the Divya Prabandham
    Ānanda Bliss; identified with Brahman in the Taittirīya
    Anātman “Not-self” (Pali anattā); the Buddhist denial of a permanent self, directed against the Upaniṣadic ātman
    Anekāntavāda “Many-sidedness”; the Jain doctrine that reality has many aspects, used against one-sided (ekānta) views such as strict non-duality
    Anirvacanīya “Indeterminable” as either real or unreal; in Advaita, the status of māyā and of the world
    Antaryāmin The “inner controller” (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.7)
    Aparā vidyā Lower knowledge, including the Vedas and their auxiliaries (Muṇḍaka 1.1.5)
    Aparokṣa-jñāna Direct, immediate knowledge
    Apṛthaksiddhi “Inseparability”; in Viśiṣṭādvaita, the relation of souls and matter to Brahman, as of an attribute to its substance or a body to its soul
    Āraṇyaka “Forest text”; Vedic genre between the Brāhmaṇas and the Upaniṣads
    Āśrama Stage or way of life (student, householder, forest-dweller, renouncer)
    Ātman The self; the innermost Self
    Avidyā Ignorance; in Advaita, the root cause of bondage
    Bandhu A hidden “connection” or equivalence between ritual, cosmos and person, the object of Brāhmaṇa and early Upaniṣadic speculation
    Bhakti Devotion to a personal God
    Bhāṣya Commentary
    Bhedābheda “Difference-in-identity”; the view that the self and the world are both different from and identical with Brahman, held by Bhartṛprapañca and Bhāskara
    Brahman The ultimate reality, ground of the world
    Brahmodya A formal contest of riddles or questions on Brahman, held at a sacrifice or a royal court (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3)
    Brahmasūtra Bādarāyaṇa’s aphorisms systematising the Upaniṣads; one of the three foundations of Vedānta
    Brahmavādinī A woman who discourses on Brahman (e.g. Maitreyī)
    Cakra “Wheel”; a centre of the subtle body in later yoga
    Dakṣiṇā The fee or gift given to priests at a sacrifice, and to a teacher
    Devāyana The “path of the gods” after death, leading to no return
    Dvaita “Duality”; the Vedānta school of Madhva
    Ghaṭaka-śruti “Connecting text”; in Rāmānuja’s classification, a passage (such as the inner-controller texts) that reconciles the texts of difference (bheda-śruti) and of non-difference (abheda-śruti)
    Īśvara The Lord; a personal God
    Jāgrat Waking
    Jñāna Knowledge, especially liberating knowledge of the Self
    Jīva The individual, embodied self
    Jīvanmukti Liberation while still living
    Karma Action, especially ritual action; its consequences
    Kośa “Sheath”; one of the five layers of the self in later readings of the Taittirīya
    Kuṇḍalinī The coiled power at the base of the spine in Tantra and Haṭha Yoga
    Lakṣaṇā Secondary or indicative meaning of a word
    Mahāvākya “Great saying”; one of four Upaniṣadic sentences on the Self and Brahman
    Manana Reflection; the second stage of Vedāntic study
    Maṇipravāḷa “Pearls and coral”; the mixed Tamil–Sanskrit prose of the Śrīvaiṣṇava commentaries
    Māyā The power by which the one appears as many; in Advaita, cosmic illusion
    Mithyā “False”; in Advaita, the status of the world as neither real nor unreal, an appearance sublated by the knowledge of Brahman
    Mokṣa Liberation from the cycle of rebirth
    Mukhya Upaniṣad One of the principal (classical) Upaniṣads
    Nāḍī A channel of the subtle body
    Neti neti “Not this, not this”; Yājñavalkya’s negative description of the Self
    Nididhyāsana Sustained contemplation; the third stage of Vedāntic study
    Nirguṇa Without attributes (of Brahman)
    Oṃ The sacred syllable, analysed in the Māṇḍūkya
    Parā vidyā Higher knowledge, by which the imperishable is grasped
    Paramparā Tradition; lineage of transmission
    Pariṇāma Real transformation of a cause into its effect, as milk into curd; in Viśiṣṭādvaita, the world as a real transformation of Brahman’s body
    Pati, paśu, pāśa The Lord, the bound soul and the bonds; the three realities of Śaiva Siddhānta
    Pitṛyāna The “path of the fathers” after death, leading to rebirth
    Prājña “The knowing one”; the self in dreamless sleep, the third quarter of the Māṇḍūkya
    Prajñāna Consciousness, intelligence (Aitareya 3.1.3)
    Prāṇa Breath; vital force
    Prāṇāyāma Breath control
    Prapatti Surrender to God as the means of liberation, in Śrīvaiṣṇava theology
    Prasthāna-traya The “triple foundation” of Vedānta: Upaniṣads, Gītā, Brahmasūtra
    Saguṇa With attributes (of Brahman)
    Śākhā A Vedic school or recension
    Sākṣin The witness; consciousness that observes all states
    Samanvaya “Harmony”; the principle of the Brahmasūtra that all Upaniṣadic passages teach one coherent doctrine of Brahman
    Saṃhitā The collection of mantras forming the core of each Veda
    Samitpāṇi “With fuel in hand”; the posture of a pupil approaching a teacher
    Saṃsāra The cycle of birth, death and rebirth
    Saṃskāra Latent impression left by experience or action
    Sannyāsa Formal renunciation
    Śānti mantra Invocation for peace recited before Upaniṣadic study
    Sat Being, the existent (Chāndogya 6.2)
    Sat-cit-ānanda Being–consciousness–bliss; a later formula for Brahman
    Satkāryavāda The doctrine that the effect pre-exists in its cause (cf. Chāndogya 6.1.4)
    Śravaṇa Hearing the teaching; the first stage of Vedāntic study
    Śreyas / preyas The good and the pleasant (Kaṭha 1.2.1–2)
    Śruti “What is heard”; revealed scripture, the Veda
    Suṣupti Dreamless deep sleep
    Svapna Dream
    Taijasa “The luminous one”; the self in dream, the second quarter of the Māṇḍūkya
    Tāratamya “Gradation”; Madhva’s doctrine of an inherent hierarchy among souls
    Tat tvam asi “That thou art” (Chāndogya 6.8–16)
    Turīya “The fourth”; the Self beyond waking, dream and deep sleep
    Ubhaya-vedānta “The Vedānta of both”; the Śrīvaiṣṇava joining of the Sanskrit Upaniṣads and the Tamil hymns of the Āḻvārs
    Upādhi A limiting adjunct; in Advaita, a condition such as the body or mind through which the one self appears limited and many
    Upāsanā Meditation or worship, often on a symbol of Brahman
    Vaiśvānara “Common to all men”; the self in the waking state, the first quarter of the Māṇḍūkya (Gauḍapāda’s Viśva)
    Vaṃśa A list of teachers in a line of transmission, closing sections of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka
    Vidyā Knowledge; also a specific Upaniṣadic meditation
    Viśiṣṭādvaita “Non-duality of the qualified”; the Vedānta school of Rāmānuja
    Vivarta Apparent transformation; in Advaita, the world as an appearance of Brahman that leaves Brahman unchanged, as a rope appears as a snake
    Viveka Discrimination between the eternal and the non-eternal
