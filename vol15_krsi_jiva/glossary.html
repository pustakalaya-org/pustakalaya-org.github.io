    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Ta. = Tamil, Ka. = Kannada, Te. = Telugu, Mal. = Malayalam, Hi. = Hindi, Pe. = Persian/Arabic, Ma. = Marathi, As. = Assamese). Botanical identifications are the usual ones and are not always certain.

    Term Meaning
    adbhuta “Marvel”; in the tree texts, procedures said to produce extraordinary effects in plants
    aghnyā “Not to be killed”; a Ṛgvedic epithet of the cow
    ahar–pyne (Hi.) Reservoirs (ahar) fed by channels (pyne): the irrigation system of south Bihar
    ahiṃsā Non-injury to living beings
    āhu, sāli, bao, boro (As.) The rice seasons of the Brahmaputra valley: spring-sown summer rice, transplanted winter rice, deep-water rice and dry-season rice
    anicut (from Ta. aṇaikkaṭṭu) A weir across a river diverting water into channels, e.g. the Kallaṇai (Grand Anicut)
    ānūpa Wet, marshy country; contrasted with jāṅgala
    araghaṭṭa Water-wheel carrying pots; ancestor of the geared “Persian wheel”
    araṇya Forest, the wild; contrasted with grāma, the village
    aṣṭavarga “Group of eight” Himalayan tonic plants of uncertain identity
    aśvamedha The royal horse sacrifice
    aśvaśāstra, aśvāyurveda The science and medicine of horses, ascribed to Śālihotra
    aśvattha The pipal, Ficus religiosa; the Bodhi tree
    āṭavika Forest peoples and their chiefs, as seen in the Arthaśāstra
    bārahnājā (Hi.) “Twelve grains”: the mixed sowing of millets, pulses and other crops on the rain-fed terraces of Garhwal and Kumaon
    bhūmi, pṛthivī The earth
    brahmadeya, agrahāra Land or village granted to brahmins
    dakārgala Finding underground water (Bṛhatsaṃhitā ch. 54)
    daśamūla “Ten roots”; a classical group of five tree roots and five herb roots
    dastūr (Pe.) In the Mughal revenue system, the cash rate per bīgha for a crop in an assessment circle
    deśa Country, region; classified as jāṅgala, ānūpa or sādhāraṇa
    devrāī (Ma.), kāvu (Mal.), devarakāḍu (Ka.) Regional names for sacred groves
    dohada “Craving”: the poetic longing of a tree for a woman’s touch; in the tree texts, special feeding to induce flowering
    doṣa The three bodily humours (vāta, pitta, kapha) of Āyurveda, applied also to trees and animals
    droṇa, āḍhaka Traditional measures of volume used for grain and rain
    ēri (Ta.), kere (Ka.), cheruvu (Te.) Irrigation tank behind an earthen bund
    falāḥat (Pe.; Ar. filāḥa) Husbandry, agriculture; the subject of the Arabic and Persian agricultural manuals
    gaṇa A named group of drugs in Suśruta
    garbha “Embryo”; in rain lore, the “conception” of the clouds months before the monsoon
    ghāṇī (Hi.) Wooden oil press turned by a bullock
    girdāwarī (Pe.) The seasonal inspection and recording of crops field by field in the land records of northern India
    go, paśu Cattle; livestock
    go’dhyakṣa Superintendent of cattle in the Arthaśāstra
    grāmya “Of the village”; cultivated or domestic, as opposed to wild (āraṇya)
    guḍa, khaṇḍa, śarkarā Jaggery, coarse granular sugar, crystalline sugar
    gulma, latā, vīrudh Shrub; creeper; spreading or climbing plant
    hastyāyurveda Elephant medicine; the treatise ascribed to Pālakāpya
    iqṭāʿ (Pe.) Assignment of revenue in return for military service (Delhi Sultanate)
    īti Agricultural calamity; traditionally six (excess rain, drought, locusts, rats, parrots, invading kings)
    jajmānī (Hi.) Customary exchange of services for shares of the harvest between village castes
    jāṅgala Dry, open country; source of English “jungle”
    jarīb (Pe.) The surveyor’s measuring chain; under Akbar, lengths of bamboo joined by iron rings
    jhūm Shifting (swidden) cultivation in north-eastern India
    jīva A living being, a soul
    karewa (Kashmiri) Raised plateau of old lake deposits in the Kashmir valley, where saffron is grown
    kedāra, sthala Bunded wet field; dry, rain-fed land
    kharīf, rabī (Pe.) Autumn and spring harvests; Sultanate and Mughal revenue terms, not classical categories
    kṛṣi Agriculture, ploughing
    kṣetra Field
    kudhānya “Lesser grains”; the millets in the medical texts
    kuhl Gravity-fed irrigation channel of the western Himalaya
    kuṇapajala “Carrion water”: fermented liquid manure of animal remains in the tree texts
    lākṣā Lac, the resin of the lac insect
    mada Musth in bull elephants
    mahāmātra, hastipaka Elephant keeper, mahout
    marutam (Ta.) The agricultural landscape of the Tamil tiṇai scheme
    nighaṇṭu Lexicon of drug and plant names
    oṣadhi Herb, plant; in Manu and Caraka, a plant that dies after fruiting
    paik (As.) In the Ahom state, an adult man registered for service in rotation, who received an allotment of rice land
    pañcagavya The five products of the cow (milk, curd, ghee, urine, dung)
    polaj, parautī, chachar, banjar (Pe./Hi.) The Āʾīn-i Akbarī’s classes of land: cultivated every season; briefly fallowed; fallow three or four years; uncultivated five years or more
    pūrta Meritorious public works such as wells, tanks and gardens
    rasa Taste; the six tastes of Āyurvedic pharmacology
    ṛtu Season; six in the Indian year
    śakuna Omen from birds and animals
    śāli, vrīhi, ṣaṣṭika Classes of rice: winter, rainy-season and sixty-day rice
    śamī Prosopis cineraria, the khejri tree
    śamīdhānya, śūkadhānya Pulses (“pod grains”) and cereals (“awned grains”)
    setu Dam, embankment or reservoir
    sītā Furrow; also the ploughed field personified
    sītādhyakṣa Superintendent of crown agriculture in the Arthaśāstra
    sthāvara, jaṅgama Immobile beings (plants) and mobile beings
    tiṇai (Ta.) The landscapes of Tamil poetry
    udbhijja “Sprout-born”; plants
    urvarā, ūṣara Fertile land; saline, barren land
    vanaspati “Lord of the forest”; large tree, or a tree fruiting without visible flowers
    vāpī Stepwell (Gujarati vāv, Hindi bāolī)
    vārttā Livelihood: agriculture, cattle-keeping and trade
    vṛkṣāyurveda The science of the life and care of trees
    zabṭ (Pe.) Mughal revenue assessment by measurement and crop rates
