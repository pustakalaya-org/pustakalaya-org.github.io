    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Pa. = Pali, Ta. = Tamil, Mal. = Malayalam, Ar. = Arabic, Pe. = Persian, Hi. = Hindi/Hindustani, Gu. = Gujarati, Mar. = Marathi, Pj. = Punjabi, Pt. = Portuguese).

    Term Meaning
    adhyakṣa Superintendent; head of a department in the Arthaśāstra’s administration
    āfāqī (Ar./Pe.) “Westerner”; in the Deccan sultanates, an immigrant from Iran, Iraq or Central Asia, as distinct from the Deccan-born nobility
    agrahāra Land, often a whole village, granted to one or more brāhmaṇas
    akṣaya-nīvī Permanent endowment; a capital sum deposited (often with a guild) whose interest funds a gift in perpetuity
    Añjuvaṇṇam Merchant body associated with West Asian Jewish, Christian and Muslim traders on the Malabar coast; named in the Kollam and Cochin copper plates
    antevāsin Apprentice or pupil living with a master
    artha Wealth, material well-being, power; one of the four aims of life
    Arthaśāstra “Science of artha”; the treatise on statecraft attributed to Kauṭilya; a composite text of c. 50 BCE–300 CE
    Ayyāvoḷe Five Hundred (Ta. Ainnūṟṟuvar) “The Five Hundred” of Ayyāvoḷe (Aihole); a medieval merchant association of south India active from Karnataka to Sumatra
    bahī-khātā (Hi.) Traditional merchant’s account book
    banjārā (Hi.) Itinerant community of bullock-train carriers of grain and salt
    bhāga “Share”; the king’s share of the crop
    bīghā (Hi./Pe.) Unit of land area; under Akbar a square of sixty gaz a side, but varying greatly by region
    bolī (Hi./Gu.) Auction of ritual privileges in a Jain temple, the proceeds going to the temple’s funds
    brahmadeya In south India, a village granted to a community of brāhmaṇas, governed by its sabhā
    cartaz (Pt.) Pass issued by the Portuguese to Asian ships in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries
    chauth (Mar.) “A quarter”; the Maratha claim to a quarter of the assessed land revenue of territories outside their direct rule
    dādanī (Pe./Hi.) Advance paid to weavers or producers against future delivery
    dām (Pe.) Mughal copper coin
    dāna Gift; religious giving, especially to brahmins, monks, temples or the poor
    dargāh (Pe.) Shrine at the tomb of a Sufi saint
    dāsa Slave or bondsman
    dastak (Pe.) Pass or permit; in Bengal, the English Company’s pass exempting its goods from customs
    dasvandh (Pj.) The tenth part of income given by Sikhs for the community
    devadāna Land or revenue granted to a temple deity
    devadravya “The god’s wealth”; property given to a Jain image or temple, which may be spent only on the temple
    dharmaśāstra The literature on dharma: law, custom and correct conduct
    dīnāra Gold coin (from Latin denarius), used of Kuṣāṇa and Gupta gold
    gahapati (Pa.) Householder; propertied lay figure of early Buddhist texts
    gaz (Pe.) Yard; Akbar’s standard gaz-i Ilāhī was used in the revenue survey
    Geniza Store of discarded writings in the Ben Ezra synagogue, Fusṭāṭ (Old Cairo), including letters of India traders
    ghulām (Ar./Pe.) Slave, especially a military slave trained as a soldier
    gumāshta (Pe.) Agent; in particular the salaried Indian agent of the East India Company who dealt with weavers
    Habshī (Pe./Hi.) “Abyssinian”; a person of East African origin in India, often of slave origin (see also Siddi)
    hundī (Hi.) Indigenous bill of exchange or credit note
    iqṭāʿ (Ar.) Assignment of revenue from land to an officer in the Delhi Sultanate
    jāgīr (Pe.) Mughal assignment of land revenue in lieu of salary
    jital (Pe.) Small Sultanate coin of billon or copper
    kamal (Ar.) Simple instrument (a board on a knotted string) for measuring star altitudes
    kaṅkāṇi (Ta.) Foreman who recruited, advanced money to and supervised gangs of Tamil labourers for Ceylon and Malaya
    kārkhāna (Pe.) Workshop, especially a royal or noble workshop
    karmakara Hired labourer
    kārṣāpaṇa Standard early Indian coin, especially the silver punch-marked coin (also paṇa)
    kaṭṭumaram (Ta.) “Tied logs”; the log raft of the Coromandel coast, source of catamaran
    khalāsī (from Ar.) Seaman or labourer; at Beypore, the launchers and haulers of ships
    lakṣaṇādhyakṣa Superintendent of the mint in the Arthaśāstra
    laṅgar (Pj./Pe.) Communal kitchen of a Sikh gurdwārā or a Sufi shrine
    lascar (from Pe. lashkar) Asian seaman serving on European ships
    madad-i maʿāsh (Pe.) “Aid for subsistence”; Mughal grant of revenue to scholars, religious men and their families
    mahādāna “Great gift”; one of sixteen grand ritual gifts, such as the tulāpuruṣa
    mahājan (Hi./Gu.) Merchant body or assembly of a town; also a great merchant
    mahājanapada “Great realm”; the territorial states of the mid-first millennium BCE
    mālim (from Ar. muʿallim) Master pilot or navigator
    man (Pe./Hi.), maund Unit of weight of 40 sīr; 37.3 kg in the British Indian standard, but very variable before it
    Maṇigrāmam South Indian merchant association, first recorded in the Kollam plates and at Takuapa on the Thai–Malay peninsula, later also in Tamil Nadu
    manṣab (Ar./Pe.) Rank in the Mughal service, defining pay and obligations
    masula Large, frameless sewn surf boat of the Coromandel coast
    Meluhha Name in Mesopotamian texts for a land generally identified with the Indus region
    munīm (Hi.) Head clerk and accountant of a merchant or banking firm
    nagaram (Ta.) The merchant body of a market town in the Tamil country
    nagarśeṭh (Gu.) Head of the merchant community of a town
    naigama Member of a nigama; in the law books, a body of merchants
    nākhudā (Pe.) Ship’s master or shipowner
    Nānādeśi “Of many countries”; itinerant merchants allied with the Ayyāvoḷe Five Hundred
    nāṭu (Ta.; also nāḍu) Locality or cluster of villages in the Tamil country, with its assembly of peasant landholders (nāṭṭār)
    nau, naukā Boat, ship
    nāvadhyakṣa Superintendent of ships and ports in the Arthaśāstra
    nāvika Sailor, boatman
    nigama Market town; the body of its merchants or townsmen
    paṇḍā (Hi.) Hereditary priest and pilgrims’ guide at a pilgrimage centre
    paṇya Merchandise; goods for sale
    paṇyādhyakṣa Superintendent of commerce in the Arthaśāstra
    paṭṭana Trading town, especially a port town
    paṭvārī (Hi.) Village accountant of north India
    pautavādhyakṣa Superintendent of weights and measures in the Arthaśāstra
    prasāda Food or other offerings consecrated to a deity and returned to worshippers
    pūga Corporate group or association
    puruṣārtha The four aims of human life: dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa
    rati Weight unit based on the seed of Abrus precatorius (guñjā)
    rūpya, rupiya Silver coin; Sher Shāh’s silver coin, ancestor of the rupee
    sabhā Assembly; in Tamil inscriptions, the assembly of the brāhmaṇa landholders of a brahmadeya
    samāhartṛ Chief revenue collector in the Arthaśāstra
    saṃbhūya-samutthāna Partnership; joint undertaking by several persons
    samudra Sea, ocean
    sannidhātṛ Treasurer; officer in charge of stores and treasury in the Arthaśāstra
    sardeshmukhī (Mar.) A further Maratha claim to a tenth of the revenue, as hereditary chief of the deshmukhs
    sarrāf (Ar./Pe.), shroff Money-changer and banker
    sārtha Caravan
    sārthavāha Caravan leader
    seṭṭhi (Pa.), śreṣṭhin Leading merchant or banker; guild head
    shāhbandar (Pe.) Harbour master and chief customs officer of a port
    Siddi (Hi./Mar.) Person of East African descent in western India; the Siddis of Janjira were a dynasty of admirals
    sīr (Pe./Hi.), seer Unit of weight; 80 tolā (933 g) in the British Indian standard
    sītā Produce of crown lands in the Arthaśāstra
    śreṇī Guild; corporate body of craftsmen or traders following one occupation
    śulka Toll, customs duty
    śulkādhyakṣa Superintendent of tolls and customs
    sūtrādhyakṣa Superintendent of yarn and weaving
    Suvarṇabhūmi “Land of gold”; a general name for mainland and island Southeast Asia
    tanka (Pe., from Skt. ṭaṅka) Silver (and gold) coin of the Delhi Sultanate
    tīrtha “Ford”; a place of pilgrimage
    tolā (Hi.) Unit of weight of 12 māshā; fixed at 180 grains (11.66 g) in 1833
    tulāpuruṣa The gift of the donor’s weight in gold or other substances
    ūr (Ta.) Village; the assembly of the landholders of an ordinary village
    vaṇij Merchant
    vāṇijya Trade, commerce
    varāha Gold coin of south India (the “pagoda” of European sources)
    vārttā “Livelihood”; the economic arts of agriculture, animal husbandry and trade
    vyasana Calamity, disaster afflicting the state
    waqf (Ar.) Inalienable endowment for a religious or charitable purpose in Islamic law
    zabṭ (Ar./Pe.) Mughal system of land revenue assessed by measurement
