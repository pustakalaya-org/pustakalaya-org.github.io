    Chapter 8: Kundalini and the Subtle Body¶

    As the supreme Shakti, Kundalini lies coiled at the base of the spine—a sleeping serpent of infinite power, waiting to awaken and ascend to reunion with Shiva at the crown.

    The Tantric understanding of the human being extends far beyond the physical body perceived by the ordinary senses. According to the Tantric teachings, the human organism is a complex, multi-layered structure comprising physical, energetic, mental, and spiritual dimensions—each interpenetrating the others and each playing a vital role in the process of spiritual transformation. This chapter presents the Tantric anatomy of the subtle body in comprehensive detail, culminating in the science of Kundalini awakening.

    8.1 The Three Bodies (Sharira Traya)¶

    Tantric anthropology recognizes three interpenetrating “bodies” or dimensions of the human organism.

    8.1.1 Sthula Sharira (Gross Physical Body)¶

    The Sthula Sharira is the physical body composed of the five gross elements (Pancha Mahabhutas):

    • Perceivable by the ordinary senses

    • Subject to birth, growth, disease, decay, and death

    • Composed of the food sheath (Annamaya Kosha)

    • Sustained by food, water, and breath

    • The outermost vehicle of consciousness

    8.1.2 Sukshma Sharira (Subtle Body)¶

    The Sukshma Sharira is the energetic and mental body that underlies and animates the physical body:

    • Not perceivable by ordinary senses but experienced through practice

    • Contains the Nadis (energy channels), Chakras (energy centers), and Pranas (vital energies)

    • Comprises the Pranamaya Kosha (vital sheath), Manomaya Kosha (mental sheath), and Vijnanamaya Kosha (intellectual sheath)

    • Survives the death of the physical body and carries Samskaras (impressions) from life to life

    • The primary field of Tantric practice and transformation

    8.1.3 Karana Sharira (Causal Body)¶

    The Karana Sharira is the subtlest body, the seed-form from which the other two bodies arise:

    • Corresponds to the Anandamaya Kosha (bliss sheath)

    • Contains the deep-seated impressions (Samskaras) and tendencies (Vasanas) that shape individual destiny

    • Experienced directly only in deep sleep and in advanced meditation

    • The doorway to transcendent consciousness

    8.2 Pancha Kosha (Five Sheaths)¶

    The doctrine of the Pancha Kosha (five sheaths) provides an alternative but complementary map of the human being, originally presented in the Taittiriya Upanishad and fully developed in Tantric practice.

    Table 8.1. The Five Sheaths (Pancha Kosha)
    Kosha Translation Body Function
    Annamaya Kosha Food sheath Sthula Physical structure, nourished by food
    Pranamaya Kosha Vital energy sheath Sukshma Life force, breath, energy circulation
    Manomaya Kosha Mental sheath Sukshma Thoughts, emotions, sensory processing
    Vijnanamaya Kosha Wisdom sheath Sukshma Intellect, discrimination, higher cognition
    Anandamaya Kosha Bliss sheath Karana Deep peace, seed of creation, causal level

    In Tantric practice, the practitioner progressively purifies and refines each sheath, beginning with the gross physical body (through diet, purification, and Hatha Yoga) and proceeding inward through the vital, mental, and intellectual sheaths to the bliss sheath—and ultimately to the Atman (Self) that is beyond all sheaths.

    8.3 Kundalini Shakti¶

    8.3.1 Nature of Kundalini¶

    Kundalini (from kundala, meaning “coiled”) is the primordial cosmic energy that resides within every human being, typically in a dormant or “sleeping” state at the base of the spine. It is described as a luminous serpent of energy coiled three and a half times around the Svayambhu Linga (the self-existent creative principle) at the Muladhara Chakra.

    As Shakti:
    Kundalini is none other than Shakti herself—the supreme creative power of the cosmos—as it exists within the individual.
    As Potential:
    In its dormant state, Kundalini represents the vast untapped spiritual potential that lies within every human being.
    As Bridge:
    Kundalini is the bridge between the individual and the cosmic—when fully awakened, it reunites the individual consciousness (Shakti) with universal consciousness (Shiva) at the Sahasrara Chakra.

    8.3.2 The Dormant State¶

    In most individuals, Kundalini remains dormant, its mouth blocking the entrance to the central channel (Sushumna Nadi). In this state, the individual’s consciousness operates through the ordinary channels of the mind and senses, limited by the five Kanchukas (contractions) described in the Tattva system.

    The dormancy of Kundalini is not a defect but a natural condition—the result of consciousness choosing to experience limitation. The process of Kundalini awakening is therefore not the creation of something new but the activation of what has always been present.

    8.3.3 The Awakening Process¶

    When Kundalini awakens, she uncoils and begins her ascent through the Sushumna Nadi, piercing the chakras one by one. As she passes through each chakra, she activates its latent powers and dissolves the psychic knots (Granthis) that maintain the illusion of separation. Upon reaching the Sahasrara at the crown, Kundalini unites with Shiva, and the practitioner experiences the supreme state of non-dual consciousness.

    8.4 The Nadi System¶

    The Nadis (from the root nad, “to flow”) are the channels or conduits through which Prana (vital energy) flows throughout the subtle body. The Tantric texts describe an elaborate network of these channels permeating the entire body.

    8.4.1 Overview¶

    According to the Hatha Yoga Pradipika and other texts there are 72,000 Nadis (the Shiva Samhita says 350,000), described as radiating from the navel centre, creating an intricate web of energy pathways. Of these, 14 are considered most important, and of these 14, three are paramount.

    8.4.2 The 14 Major Nadis¶

    Table 8.2. The Fourteen Major Nadis
    No. Nadi Location and Function
    1 Sushumna Central channel running from Muladhara to Sahasrara along the spinal axis. The supreme Nadi through which Kundalini ascends. Contains three inner channels: Vajra, Chitrini, and Brahma Nadi.
    2 Ida Left channel, terminating at the left nostril. Lunar (Chandra) energy—cooling, receptive, feminine. Associated with mental and emotional functions (some modern writers compare it, loosely, to the parasympathetic nervous system).
    3 Pingala Right channel, terminating at the right nostril. Solar (Surya) energy—heating, active, masculine. Associated with vital and physical functions (some modern writers compare it, loosely, to the sympathetic nervous system).
    4 Gandhari Runs behind the left eye. Connected to vision and visual perception.
    5 Hastijihva Runs behind the right eye. Connected to vision and visual perception.
    6 Yashasvini Runs between Gandhari and Sarasvati, reaching the left ear. Connected with hearing.
    7 Pusha Runs behind the Pingala, reaching the right ear. Connected with hearing.
    8 Alambusha Runs from the mouth to the anus. Connected with excretion and elimination.
    9 Kuhu Runs in front of the Sushumna, terminating at the genitals. Connected with reproductive functions.
    10 Sarasvati Runs along the tongue. Connected with speech and taste. Associated with the goddess Sarasvati.
    11 Varuni Pervades the entire body. Connected with the water element and fluid regulation.
    12 Vishvodhara Located in the abdominal region between Kuhu and Hastijihva. Connected with digestion and the fire element.
    13 Payasvini Runs between Pusha and Sarasvati, near the right ear. Connected with nutrition and nourishment.
    14 Shankhini Runs between Gandhari and Sarasvati, terminating at the left ear region. Connected with the assimilation of Prana.

    8.4.3 The Three Primary Nadis¶

    The three primary Nadis—Sushumna, Ida, and Pingala—form the central axis of the subtle body and are of paramount importance in Tantric practice.

    Sushumna:
    The “gracious channel,” running through the center of the spine from the Muladhara at the base to the Sahasrara at the crown. Within the Sushumna lie three progressively subtler channels: the Vajra Nadi (fiery), the Chitrini Nadi (lunar), and the Brahma Nadi (transcendent)—the innermost channel through which Kundalini actually travels.
    Ida:
    The “comfort” channel, running on the left side of the spine in a serpentine path, crossing the Sushumna at each chakra, and terminating at the left nostril. Ida carries the cooling, lunar, receptive energy and is associated with the mind (Manas).
    Pingala:
    The “tawny” channel, running on the right side in a mirror-image serpentine path and terminating at the right nostril. Pingala carries the heating, solar, active energy and is associated with vital force (Prana).

    In ordinary states of consciousness, Prana flows predominantly through either Ida or Pingala, alternating over a period of hours (a phenomenon that physiologists describe as the “nasal cycle”, though its period varies from person to person). The goal of many Tantric and yogic practices is to balance the flow through Ida and Pingala so that Prana is redirected into the Sushumna—the prerequisite for Kundalini awakening.

    8.5 The Seven Major Chakras¶

    The Chakras (literally “wheels”) are the major energy centers located along the Sushumna Nadi. While some texts describe more or fewer chakras, the system of seven major chakras is the most widely recognized and practiced.

    8.5.1 Muladhara Chakra — The Root Center¶

    Location:
    Base of the spine, between the anus and genitals (perineum)
    Element:
    Earth (Prithivi)
    Bija Mantra:
    LAM
    Petals:
    4 (crimson/red), carrying the Sanskrit syllables: Vam, Sham, Sham, Sam
    Presiding Deity:
    Brahma (creator) and Dakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    Yellow square (earth element), within which is a downward-pointing red triangle with the Svayambhu Linga encircled by the dormant Kundalini
    Associated Qualities:
    Stability, grounding, survival instinct, the sense of smell, the organ of excretion
    Psychological Correlation:
    Basic security, material needs, connection to the earth, tribal belonging
    When Balanced:
    Sense of security, groundedness, physical vitality, stability
    When Imbalanced:
    Fear, anxiety, insecurity, materialism, lethargy

    8.5.2 Svadhisthana Chakra — The Sacral Center¶

    Location:
    At the root of the genitals, approximately four finger-widths above Muladhara
    Element:
    Water (Apas)
    Bija Mantra:
    VAM
    Petals:
    6 (vermilion/orange), carrying: Bam, Bham, Mam, Yam, Ram, Lam
    Presiding Deity:
    Vishnu and Rakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    White crescent moon (water element) within a lotus
    Associated Qualities:
    Fluidity, creativity, sexuality, the sense of taste, the organ of generation
    Psychological Correlation:
    Pleasure, desire, emotional fluidity, creative expression, sensuality
    When Balanced:
    Creative energy, healthy relationships, emotional intelligence, adaptability
    When Imbalanced:
    Lust, addiction, emotional instability, jealousy, possessiveness

    8.5.3 Manipura Chakra — The Solar Plexus Center¶

    Location:
    At the navel region (some texts say slightly above)
    Element:
    Fire (Agni / Tejas)
    Bija Mantra:
    RAM
    Petals:
    10 (blue-black/golden), carrying: Dam, Dham, Nam, Tam, Tham, Dam, Dham, Nam, Pam, Pham
    Presiding Deity:
    Rudra (the fierce form of Shiva) and Lakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    Red downward-pointing triangle (fire element)
    Associated Qualities:
    Transformation, digestion (physical and psychic), willpower, the sense of sight, the feet (locomotion)
    Psychological Correlation:
    Personal power, self-esteem, ambition, determination, assertiveness
    When Balanced:
    Strong will, good digestion, confidence, leadership ability
    When Imbalanced:
    Anger, domination, aggression, digestive disorders, shame

    8.5.4 Anahata Chakra — The Heart Center¶

    Location:
    At the heart region, center of the chest
    Element:
    Air (Vayu)
    Bija Mantra:
    YAM
    Petals:
    12 (deep red/vermilion), carrying: Kam, Kham, Gam, Gham, Ngam, Cham, Chham, Jam, Jham, Nyam, Tam, Tham
    Presiding Deity:
    Ishvara (the Lord) and Kakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    Two interlocking triangles forming a hexagram (Star of David shape), smoky blue-green in color, representing the union of Shiva (upward triangle) and Shakti (downward triangle)
    Associated Qualities:
    Love, compassion, devotion, the sense of touch, the hands (grasping)
    Psychological Correlation:
    Unconditional love, empathy, forgiveness, compassion, devotion
    Special Feature:
    Contains the Ananda Kanda—a subsidiary lotus of 8 petals below the main 12-petaled lotus, where the Ishta Devata (chosen deity) is visualized
    When Balanced:
    Unconditional love, compassion, emotional balance, harmony
    When Imbalanced:
    Grief, loneliness, codependence, bitterness, inability to give or receive love

    8.5.5 Vishuddha Chakra — The Throat Center¶

    Location:
    At the throat, at the junction of the spinal column and the medulla oblongata
    Element:
    Ether/Space (Akasha)
    Bija Mantra:
    HAM
    Petals:
    16 (smoky purple/lavender), carrying the 16 Sanskrit vowels: Am, Aam, Im, Eem, Um, Oom, Rim, Reem, Lrim, Lreem, Em, Aim, Om, Aum, Am (anusvara), Ah (visarga)
    Presiding Deity:
    Sadashiva (Shiva in his eternal form) and Shakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    White circle (ether element) representing the void/space
    Associated Qualities:
    Communication, expression, purification, the sense of hearing, the mouth (speech)
    Psychological Correlation:
    Self-expression, truth, creativity, communication, listening
    When Balanced:
    Clear communication, creative expression, truthfulness, good listening
    When Imbalanced:
    Inability to express, lies, gossip, throat problems, shyness

    8.5.6 Ajna Chakra — The Third Eye Center¶

    Location:
    Between the eyebrows (the “third eye” or Bhrumadhya)
    Element:
    Mind (Manas) — beyond the five physical elements
    Bija Mantra:
    OM (or AUM)
    Petals:
    2 (luminous white/indigo), carrying: Ham and Ksham—representing Shiva and Shakti, Ida and Pingala, the final duality to be transcended
    Presiding Deity:
    Paramashiva (the transcendent Shiva) and Hakini (Shakti)
    Yantra Form:
    An inverted triangle containing the Itara Linga, within which is the seed syllable OM
    Associated Qualities:
    Intuition, clairvoyance, command (Ajna means “command”), higher perception
    Psychological Correlation:
    Insight, intuition, wisdom, spiritual vision, discernment
    Special Feature:
    The point where Ida, Pingala, and Sushumna converge. Often called the “Guru Chakra” because it is here that the inner Guru is awakened.
    When Balanced:
    Clear intuition, insight, inner vision, wisdom, psychic abilities
    When Imbalanced:
    Confusion, headaches, nightmares, poor concentration, disconnection from intuition

    8.5.7 Sahasrara Chakra — The Crown Center¶

    Location:
    At the crown of the head (some texts say above the head)
    Element:
    Beyond all elements — pure consciousness
    Bija Mantra:
    Silence (or the transcendent OM)
    Petals:
    1,000 (or infinite) — brilliant white/gold, representing the totality of all sounds and all creation
    Presiding Deity:
    Paramashiva — the absolute, in union with Shakti
    Yantra Form:
    The full moon, with a luminous triangle within which rests the supreme Bindu (point of consciousness)
    Associated Qualities:
    Transcendence, unity, liberation, cosmic consciousness
    Psychological Correlation:
    Spiritual enlightenment, connection with the Divine, transcendence of ego
    Special Feature:
    Not technically a “chakra” in the same sense as the others—it is the destination of Kundalini’s ascent, where Shakti reunites with Shiva. Here, all dualities dissolve, and the practitioner experiences Samarasa—the “equal taste” of all reality as pure consciousness.

    8.6 The Three Granthis (Psychic Knots)¶

    The Granthis (literally “knots”) are three critical blockages along the Sushumna that must be pierced for Kundalini to complete her ascent.

    1. Brahma Granthi (at Muladhara):
      The knot of Brahma, associated with attachment to the physical world, material security, and survival instinct. This knot keeps consciousness bound to the physical plane. Piercing it requires overcoming fear, inertia, and excessive attachment to material existence.

    2. Vishnu Granthi (at Anahata):
      The knot of Vishnu, associated with emotional attachment, personal relationships, and the bondage of compassion directed by ego. Piercing it requires transcending personal love to reach universal love, and overcoming the attachment to one’s identity as a “good” or “spiritual” person.

    3. Rudra Granthi (at Ajna):
      The knot of Rudra (Shiva), associated with attachment to psychic powers (Siddhis), spiritual pride, and the subtle attachment to the experience of bliss and vision. This is the most difficult to pierce, as the aspirant must surrender even the attachment to spiritual attainment itself.

    8.7 Methods of Kundalini Awakening¶

    The Tantric texts describe several methods for awakening the dormant Kundalini:

    1. Shaktipat (Descent of Grace):
      The direct transmission of spiritual energy from Guru to disciple. This is considered the most powerful and most reliable method. Shaktipat can occur through touch, gaze, word, thought, or even from a distance.

    2. Mantra Sadhana:
      Sustained and disciplined practice of specific mantras can gradually awaken Kundalini through the vibratory effect of sacred sound on the subtle body.

    3. Pranayama (Breath Control):
      Specific breathing techniques—particularly Bhastrika (bellows breath), Kumbhaka (breath retention), and Nadi Shodhana (alternate nostril breathing)—direct Prana into the Sushumna.

    4. Bandhas and Mudras:
      Energy locks (Mula Bandha, Uddiyana Bandha, Jalandhara Bandha) and seals (Maha Mudra, Khechari Mudra) redirect the flow of Prana.

    5. Meditation and Visualization:
      Concentrated meditation on the chakras, particularly the Muladhara, combined with visualization of Kundalini’s ascent.

    6. Ritual Practice:
      The systematic performance of Tantric ritual, including Nyasa, Puja, and Homa, purifies the subtle body and creates conditions for awakening.

    7. Devotion (Bhakti):
      Intense devotion to the Divine can spontaneously awaken Kundalini through the overwhelming force of love.

    8.8 Signs and Experiences During Awakening¶

    The Tantric texts describe various signs that indicate Kundalini is awakening or has been activated:

    8.8.1 Physical Signs¶

    • Sensations of heat or electricity along the spine

    • Spontaneous body movements (Kriyas)—jerking, swaying, trembling

    • Tingling or vibration in various parts of the body

    • Sensations of energy rising upward through the spine

    • Changes in breathing patterns

    • Unusual physical strength or flexibility

    8.8.2 Psychological Signs¶

    • Intense emotional releases—sudden crying, laughing, or ecstasy

    • Periods of extraordinary clarity and insight

    • Vivid dreams and visions

    • Heightened creativity

    • Experiences of profound inner peace and bliss

    • Temporary intensification of fears or psychological patterns before their dissolution

    8.8.3 Spiritual Signs¶

    • Inner sounds (Nada)—bells, flutes, thundering, the cosmic OM

    • Inner lights—flashes, geometric patterns, the “blue pearl” (Nila Bindu)

    • Experiences of expanded awareness or dissolution of body boundaries

    • Spontaneous states of meditation or absorption (Samadhi)

    • Direct perception of the chakras and Nadis

    • Experiences of non-dual awareness

    8.9 Dangers and Precautions¶

    The Tantric texts are unambiguous: the awakening of Kundalini is a process of tremendous power that, if mishandled, can cause serious physical, psychological, and spiritual harm.

    8.9.1 Potential Dangers¶

    • Physical: Extreme heat or cold, pain along the spine, disruption of bodily functions, nervous system overload; forceful breathing and long retention of breath can also cause fainting and are unsafe in heart disease, high blood pressure, epilepsy, pregnancy and some other conditions

    • Psychological: Overwhelming emotional releases, psychotic-like episodes, severe anxiety or depression, ego inflation

    • Spiritual: Premature opening of psychic perception, attachment to Siddhis (powers), spiritual bypassing of unresolved psychological material

    8.9.2 Essential Precautions¶

    1. Qualified Guidance: Never attempt Kundalini practices without the guidance of a qualified Guru who has personal experience of the process.

    2. Proper Preparation: The body, mind, and nervous system must be thoroughly prepared through preliminary practices.

    3. Gradual Approach: The awakening should be gradual and controlled, not forced or rushed.

    4. Ethical Foundation: A strong ethical foundation is essential; Kundalini energy amplifies whatever is present in the psyche.

    5. Physical Health: The practitioner should be in reasonable physical health and should maintain a pure, moderate diet.

    6. Psychological Stability: Unresolved psychological issues should be addressed before attempting advanced energy practices.

    7. Balanced Practice: Awakening practices should be balanced with grounding practices to prevent the practitioner from becoming destabilized.

    8. Medical Care: Distressing experiences of any kind (panic, insomnia, loss of contact with reality) call for qualified medical or mental-health help, not for more practice.

    Critical Warning: The practices described in this chapter are presented for educational purposes. Actual practice of Kundalini awakening techniques should only be undertaken under the direct supervision of a qualified, experienced teacher. Unsupervised practice—particularly of advanced Pranayama and Bandha techniques—can result in serious physical and psychological harm.
