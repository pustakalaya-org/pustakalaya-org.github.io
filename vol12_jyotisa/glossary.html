    Appendix D: Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Ar. = Arabic, Pers. = Persian, Mal. = Malayalam, Gk. = Greek).

    Term Meaning
    abhivardhita “increased”: in the Jain calendar, a year of thirteen synodic months, or one-twelfth of such a year (\(31\tfrac{121}{124}\) days)
    adhika māsa intercalary lunar month, one containing no saṅkrānti
    agrā Sine of the amplitude: the distance of the Sun’s rising point from the east point, in Sine units
    ahargaṇa “heap of days”: the number of civil days elapsed since an epoch
    akṣajyā, lambajyā Sine of the latitude; Sine of the co-latitude
    akṣakṣetra “latitude figure”: one of the right triangles similar to that of gnomon, equinoctial shadow and hypotenuse
    amānta reckoning in which the lunar month ends at new moon
    antyasaṃskāra “end correction” applied when an infinite series is truncated (Kerala school)
    ārdharātrika “of midnight”: the school following Āryabhaṭa’s midnight-epoch system
    asakṛt “not once”: an iterative procedure repeated until the result is stable
    aṣṭakūṭa the “eight factors” used in matching horoscopes for marriage
    ayanāṃśa the difference between sidereal and tropical longitudes, caused by precession
    bhagaṇa a complete revolution of a planet; the number of revolutions in a cycle
    bhāva a “house” or place of the horoscope
    bhūtasaṅkhyā word-numerals: words standing for digits
    bīja small correction to the parameters of a standard text
    cakra “wheel”: in the Grahalāghava, a cycle of 4016 days used to keep day-counts small
    cara ascensional difference: the excess or deficit of half the day over 15 nāḍikās
    chāyā, chāyākarṇa shadow of the gnomon; the hypotenuse from the tip of the gnomon to the tip of the shadow
    daivajña “knower of fate”: astrologer-astronomer; with gaṇaka and jyotiṣī, a name of the profession
    daśā planetary period in astrology; e.g. viṃśottarī daśā
    deśāntara correction for the observer’s terrestrial longitude
    dhruvaka polar longitude of a junction star
    dṛk, dṛggaṇita “seen”: computation agreeing with observation; Parameśvara’s system; modern ephemeris-based almanacs
    dyujyā “day-radius”: the radius of a body’s diurnal circle, \(R\cos\delta\)
    gaṇaka “calculator”: astronomer or astrologer (Tamil kaṇiyaṉ)
    ghaṭikā, nāḍikā one-sixtieth of a day, 24 minutes; also the water clock measuring it
    gola sphere; the part of a siddhānta dealing with the sphere and cosmography
    golayantra armillary sphere
    graha “seizer”: planet; in astrology the nine grahas include Rāhu and Ketu
    grahaṇa eclipse
    horā (Gk. hōra) half of a zodiacal sign; horoscopic astrology
    jātaka nativity; the branch of astrology dealing with birth horoscopes
    jyā, jīvā “bowstring”: the Sine, half-chord of double the arc
    jyotiḥśāstra, jyotiṣa the science of the heavenly lights: astronomy, astrology and divination
    kakṣyā orbit; the deferent circle
    kalidina the number of days elapsed since the beginning of the Kali age; in Kerala often recorded in kaṭapayādi
    kalpa “day of Brahmā”: 1000 mahāyugas, 4320 million years
    karaṇa (1) handbook of astronomy with a recent epoch; (2) half a tithi
    karṇa hypotenuse; the distance of a planet in the śīghra computation
    kaṭapayādi system in which consonants stand for digits
    kendra (Gk. kentron) anomaly of a planet; cardinal place of a horoscope
    ketu (1) comet (also śikhin, dhūmaketu); (2) the descending node of the Moon (see Rāhu)
    koṣṭhaka “compartment”: a cell of an astronomical table; a table
    kṣaya “loss”: an omitted tithi or month
    kṣepaka, dhruva the mean position of a body at the epoch of a handbook, to which the motion since the epoch is added
    kujyā “earth-Sine”: the ascensional difference expressed as a line in the plane of the diurnal circle
    kuṭṭaka “pulveriser”: method for solving linear indeterminate equations
    lagna ascendant: the point of the ecliptic rising on the eastern horizon
    lambana parallax in longitude
    liptā (Gk. lepton) minute of arc
    mahāyuga cycle of 4,320,000 years comprising the four yugas
    manda “slow”: the correction for the equation of centre
    maṇḍala in Jain astronomy, one of the 184 daily circular paths of the Sun around Meru
    mandocca apogee: the apex of the manda epicycle
    manvantara period of a Manu: 71 mahāyugas
    muhūrta one-thirtieth of a day (48 minutes); an auspicious moment; electional astrology
    nakṣatra lunar asterism; one of 27 divisions of \(13^\circ 20'\)
    nati parallax in latitude
    navāṃśa one-ninth of a zodiacal sign (\(3^\circ 20'\)), the length of a quarter of a nakṣatra; in astrology, the basis of a divisional chart
    pada, pāda quarter of a nakṣatra (\(3^\circ 20'\)); quarter of a verse
    pakṣa (1) fortnight; (2) school of astronomy
    palabhā equinoctial noon shadow of a 12-digit gnomon, giving the latitude
    palakarṇa, akṣakarṇa hypotenuse of the equinoctial shadow
    pañcāṅga “five limbs”: the almanac
    pāta node of an orbit
    prāṇa “breath”: four sidereal seconds, the smallest “real” unit of time
    pūrṇimānta reckoning in which the lunar month ends at full moon
    Rāhu, Ketu mythical demon of eclipses; in astronomy, the ascending and descending lunar nodes
    rāśi zodiacal sign of \(30^\circ\)
    saṃhitā the branch of omens and divination; a compendium (e.g. Bṛhatsaṃhitā)
    saṅkrānti the Sun’s entry into a zodiacal sign
    saptarṣi the Seven Sages: the seven bright stars of the Great Bear; also an era (Laukika era) of Kashmir
    sāraṇī astronomical table
    sāvana the civil day, from sunrise to sunrise; reckoning in civil days
    śaṅku gnomon, conventionally 12 aṅgulas high; also the Sine of the Sun’s altitude
    śānti rite of appeasement performed to avert the effects of a portent
    siddhānta complete treatise of mathematical astronomy
    śīghra “fast”: the correction for the synodic anomaly of a planet
    śīghrocca apex of the śīghra epicycle
    tājika (from Pers./Ar.) Perso-Arabic astrology in Sanskrit
    tantra treatise of astronomy counting from the Kali epoch
    tātkālika gati “instantaneous motion” of a planet
    tithi lunar day: the time in which the Moon gains \(12^\circ\) on the Sun
    tripraśna the “three questions” of direction, place and time
    udāharaṇa worked example; a commentary consisting of worked examples
    udaya, asta rising and setting; in particular the heliacal rising and setting of a planet or star, its first and last visibility near the Sun
    udayāntara correction to time for the obliquity of the ecliptic
    ulkā meteor, fireball; in the omen literature also a class that includes lightning and thunderbolts
    upapatti, vāsanā demonstration or rationale of a rule
    uposatha (Pali) the Buddhist observance day at new and full moon (and on the eighth day of the fortnight for lay followers)
    utpāta portent; an unusual event in the sky, the air or on earth interpreted as an omen
    vakra, mārga retrograde (“crooked”) and direct motion of a planet
    vākya “sentence”: memorised verse tables for the Moon and planets (south India)
    valana “deflection”: angles giving the orientation of an eclipse
    vāra weekday
    vikṣepa latitude of a planet or star
    vināḍikā, pala one-sixtieth of a nāḍikā: 24 seconds
    yantra instrument
    Yantrarāja “king of instruments”: the astrolabe
    yoga (1) element of the almanac based on the sum of solar and lunar longitudes; (2) astrological combination
    yogatārā junction star of a nakṣatra
    yojana a measure of distance of uncertain and variable length
    yuga age or cycle: the five-year cycle of the Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa; the four ages of the Purāṇas
    zīj (Ar., Pers.) astronomical handbook with tables, of the Islamic tradition
