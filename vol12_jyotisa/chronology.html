    Appendix E: Chronology¶

    Dates are approximate unless a text gives its own date or epoch. Traditional dates are labelled as such.

    Date Events, works and authors
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core hymns of the Ṛgveda: 360-day year, Svarbhānu eclipse myth
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts: lists of nakṣatras, ritual calendar
    c. 800–200 BCE Śulbasūtras (Baudhāyana earliest): gnomon method for the east–west line
    2nd half of 1st mill. BCE Probable composition of the Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa (solstice data point to c. 1400–1200 BCE; date disputed)
    late 6th–4th c. BCE North-west India in the Achaemenid empire; possible Mesopotamian influence
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE Arthaśāstra in its present form
    3rd c. CE Chinese translations of the Buddhist Śārdūlakarṇāvadāna, with its nakṣatra lore
    by 5th c. CE Jain Sūryaprajñapti and Candraprajñapti (canon fixed at Valabhī)
    269/270 CE Sphujidhvaja’s Yavanajātaka
    c. 300–500 Early siddhāntas (Saura, Pauliśa, Romaka)
    499 Āryabhaṭa, Āryabhaṭīya (born 476)
    505 Epoch of Varāhamihira’s Pañcasiddhāntikā; Varāhamihira active in the 6th century
    628 Brahmagupta, Brāhma­sphuṭa­siddhānta (born 598)
    629 Bhāskara I’s commentary on the Āryabhaṭīya
    634/635 Aihole inscription of Pulakeśin II, dated 3735 years after the Bhārata war and Śaka 556
    665 Brahmagupta, Khaṇḍakhādyaka
    683 Parahita system in Kerala (traditional date)
    c. 771–773 Indian astronomer at the court of al-Manṣūr; Zīj al-Sindhind
    8th c. Lalla, Śiṣya­dhī­vṛddhida­tantra
    early 9th c. al-Khwārizmī’s Zīj al-Sindhind
    904 Vaṭeśvara, Vaṭeśvarasiddhānta
    8th–11th c. Sūryasiddhānta reaches its present form
    932 Muñjāla, Laghumānasa
    early 11th c. Kālacakratantra, with a handbook-type calendrical astronomy; 1027, first year of the Tibetan sixty-year cycle
    c. 1030 al-Bīrūnī, India
    1039 Śrīpati, Dhīkoṭidakaraṇa
    1042 Rājamṛgāṅka, attributed to King Bhoja
    1092 Brahmadeva, Karaṇaprakāśa
    1099 Śatānanda, Bhāsvatī
    12th c. Malayagiri’s commentaries on the Jain astronomical texts
    1148–49 Kalhaṇa’s Rājataraṅgiṇī, dated in the Saptarṣi (Laukika) era
    1150 Bhāskara II, Siddhāntaśiromaṇi (born 1114)
    1183 Bhāskara II, Karaṇakutūhala
    13th c. Earliest Sanskrit works on tājika (Perso-Arabic astrology)
    c. 1340–1425 Mādhava of Saṅgamagrāma
    c. 1360–1455 Parameśvara of Vaṭaśśeri
    1370 Mahendra Sūri, Yantrarāja, first Sanskrit treatise on the astrolabe
    1431 Parameśvara, Dṛggaṇita
    1478 Makaranda tables, Vārāṇasī
    1496 Keśava of Nandigrāma, Grahakautuka
    1500 Nīlakaṇṭha Somayājī, Tantrasaṅgraha
    1503 Jñānarāja, Siddhāntasundara
    1520 Gaṇeśa Daivajña, Grahalāghava
    c. 1530 Jyeṣṭhadeva, Yuktibhāṣā (Malayalam)
    1587 Fayḍī’s Persian Līlāvatī; Nīlakaṇṭha Daivajña’s Tājikanīlakaṇṭhī
    1600 Rāma Daivajña, Muhūrtacintāmaṇi
    1618 Jahāngīr records the two great comets of November; Kirwitzer observes them at Goa
    1621 Meteorite fall near Jalandhar, recorded by Jahāngīr
    1628 Nityānanda, Siddhāntasindhu
    1646, 1658 Munīśvara, Siddhāntasārvabhauma; Kamalākara, Siddhāntatattvaviveka
    1720s–1730s Jai Singh’s observatories at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Vārāṇasī and Mathurā; Zīj-i Muḥammad Shāhī; Jagannātha’s Samrāṭsiddhānta (1732)
    1786/1792 Madras Observatory
    1787 Bailly, Traité de l’astronomie indienne et orientale
    1790 Davis on the computations of the Sūryasiddhānta; Jones on the Indian zodiac; Playfair, “Remarks on the Astronomy of the Brahmins”
    1796 Laplace, Exposition du système du monde
    late 1790s–1825 Bentley’s papers on the age of the siddhāntas; Historical View of the Hindu Astronomy (1825)
    1817 Colebrooke, translation of the mathematical chapters of Brahmagupta and Bhāskara II
    1819 or 1823 Śaṅkara Varman, Sadratnamālā
    1825 Warren, Kala Sankalita (Madras)
    1834 Whish reports the Kerala series
    1844 Taylor’s Madras catalogue of more than 11,000 stars
    1860 Burgess, translation of the Sūryasiddhānta
    1867 C. Ragoonatha Chary establishes the variability of R Reticuli at Madras; first Indian Fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society (1872)
    1868 Total solar eclipse of 18 August observed in India; spectroscopy of the prominences and the yellow line later ascribed to helium
    c. 1869 Sāmanta Candraśekhara completes the Siddhāntadarpaṇa (published at Calcutta in the late 1890s)
    1872 Pogson finds a comet after the meteor shower of 27 November, in a search for Biela’s comet
    1892 Sudhākara Dvivedī, Gaṇakataraṅgiṇī
    1896 Dīkṣit, history of Indian astronomy; Sewell and Dīkṣit, The Indian Calendar
    1899 Kodaikanal Observatory
    1920 Saha’s ionisation equation
    1955 Report of the Calendar Reform Committee
    1957 National calendar in use (22 March, 1 Caitra 1879 Śaka)
    1970–1994 Pingree, Census of the Exact Sciences in Sanskrit, Series A, vols 1–5
    1975 India’s first satellite, Aryabhata
    2003 National Mission for Manuscripts established
