    Preface¶

    This is the first volume of the series Bhāratīya Jñāna Paramparā. Its subject is the Vedic corpus: the four Saṃhitās of the Ṛgveda, Yajurveda, Sāmaveda and Atharvaveda, with the poetry and myths of the hymns; the Brāhmaṇas and Āraṇyakas; the six Vedāṅgas and the later Upavedas; the solemn and domestic rituals; the oral tradition and the schools that preserved these texts for three thousand years; the world-view and society that they reveal; and their later history and legacy.

    The volume tries to do two things at once. It presents the Vedas as the tradition has understood them—as Śruti, the foundation of Brahmanical learning and practice—and it presents them as historians and philologists read them, as compositions of many generations of poets and ritualists in north-western and northern India between roughly 1500 and 500 BCE. Where these perspectives differ, both are stated and labelled. Dates are given in the mainstream academic form first; traditional dates are identified as such.

    Chapter 1 introduces the corpus, its dating and the history of its study; Chapters 2–7 describe the Saṃhitās and the literature that grew around them; Chapters 8–12 the rituals, the later scheme of applied sciences, the oral tradition and the schools; and Chapters 13–15 the world-view and society of the texts and their later history, including the responses of Buddhist, Jain and Tamil authors. A list of abbreviations, a map of places named in the volume, a glossary and a chronology are provided for reference.

    This edition has been thoroughly revised. Quotations from the Vedic texts have been checked and, where they could not be verified, removed; doubtful or unsourced quotations from modern authors have been omitted; claims that the Vedas anticipated modern science have been replaced by an account of their genuine achievements in phonetics, grammar, geometry and calendrical astronomy; and contested questions—the date of the Ṛgveda, the Sarasvatī, the origins of the Indo-Aryan languages and the evidence of ancient DNA—are presented with the evidence on each side. The volume also treats honestly the origins of varṇa hierarchy and the position of women, which are part of the Vedic legacy and still matter today.

    Sanskrit, Pali and Prakrit words are given in the International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration (IAST), and Tamil words in the transliteration of the Madras Tamil Lexicon (Section 1.14). Translations are the author’s own unless otherwise indicated, and readers are encouraged to consult the critical editions and the scholarly translations listed in the Select Bibliography.

    Nikhil Kumar Rajput
    Ramanujan College, University of Delhi
