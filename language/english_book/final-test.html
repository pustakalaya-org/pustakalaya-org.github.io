    Chapter 60: Final test¶

    Final test — the whole book at once

    Nothing new is taught here. This test is only to show which parts of the book have really sunk in and which should be opened again. The questions are mixed on purpose — one about tenses, the next a phrasal verb, then an article. Real life works the same way: nobody warns you that a grammar chapter is coming up.

    How to take it

    Do it in one sitting, and think for yourself before looking at the answer. Making a mistake is not bad — why you made it is explained in the "Why" part. If your score is above 80%, the book is yours. If it is below 50, that is fine — start again, but this time stop only at the chapters where you made mistakes.

    60.1 What is in the test¶

    The bar below shows how the 30 questions are spread across the five parts of the book. If your mistakes pile up in one colour, go straight back to that part — there is no need to repeat the whole book.

    Six kinds of questions

    Choose (MCQ) · fill in the blank (fill) · spot the mistake (spot) · put in order (reorder) · match pairs (match) · say it aloud (speak). The "say it aloud" questions need a microphone. If you don't have one, listen and repeat, then move on — it doesn't affect your score.

    60.2 Before you start — a three-minute warm-up¶

    These three mistakes have come up most often in the whole book. Look at them once, say the corrected sentences aloud once, and then start the test.

    Three things, one last time

    • Wrong: I am working here since three years.
    • Right: I've been working here for three years.

    • Why: Use for with a length of time, and since with a starting point. And work that is still going on = have been + -ing.

    • Wrong: She is doctor and she is good in surgery.

    • Right: She's a doctor and she's good at surgery.

    • Why: Always use a/an with a profession, and being skilled = good at.

    • Wrong: Tell me where is the lift.

    • Right: Could you tell me where the lift is?
    • Why: When a question sits inside a longer sentence, the order becomes normal again — the verb goes to the end.

    60.3 Final test — the whole book¶

    Exercise 1

    In this sentence, which part of speech is 'quickly'? — She finished the report quickly.

    • Adverb
    • Adjective
    • Verb
    • Preposition

    Hint: It tells how the work was done.

    Answer

    Adverb

    An adverb tells us about a verb — how, when, where. Here 'quickly' tells how 'finished' happened.

    Exercise 2

    Ravi and I finished the work ___ .

    Hint: We did it on our own — the reflexive form.

    Answer

    ourselves

    The reflexive pronoun matches the subject: I → myself, we → ourselves. Note: there is no such word as 'ourself'.

    Exercise 3

    Which sentence is correct?

    • I have been waiting for twenty minutes.
    • I am waiting since twenty minutes.
    • I wait since twenty minutes.
    • I have waited since twenty minutes.
    Answer

    I have been waiting for twenty minutes.

    An action that started earlier and is still going on = have been + -ing. And use 'for' with a length of time, 'since' with a starting point.

    Exercise 4

    Find the wrong word.

    She didn't came to the meeting yesterday.

    Answer

    Wrong word: came.

    Corrected: She didn't come to the meeting yesterday.

    'Did' already shows the past, so the verb stays in its first form — didn't come.

    Exercise 5

    By the time we reached the platform, the train ___ .

    • had left
    • has left
    • left
    • was leaving

    Hint: Two actions in the past — which happened first?

    Answer

    had left

    When two things happened in the past, the earlier one takes 'had'. This is called the past perfect.

    Exercise 6

    She ___ the answer, so just ask her.

    Hint: Verbs of thinking don't go into the continuous.

    Answer

    knows

    Know, understand, believe, like, want — these don't go into the continuous. 'She is knowing' is wrong.

    Exercise 7

    Which sentence is correct?

    • It took an hour to find a university nearby.
    • It took a hour to find an university nearby.
    • It took an hour to find an university nearby.
    • It took a hour to find a university nearby.

    Hint: a/an depends on the sound.

    Answer

    It took an hour to find a university nearby.

    The H in hour is silent, so the sound starts with a vowel — an. University starts with a 'yoo' sound, which is like a consonant — a.

    Exercise 8

    One word is unnecessary — tap it.

    I really enjoy the music in my free time.

    Answer

    Wrong word: the.

    Corrected: I really enjoy music in my free time.

    In a general statement, 'the' is not used. 'The music' would mean one particular music that is playing now.

    Exercise 9

    My sister is very good ___ mathematics.

    Hint: Being skilled.

    Answer

    at

    Skilled in a subject = good at. Good with = good at handling things or people.

    Exercise 10

    One word should be removed — find it.

    Let us discuss about the new policy on Monday.

    Answer

    Wrong word: about.

    Corrected: Let's discuss the new policy on Monday.

    The word 'about' is already inside discuss. Same family: enter the room, reach Delhi, order two teas.

    Exercise 11

    The meeting is ___ 4 pm ___ Monday ___ July.

    • at / on / in
    • in / at / on
    • on / in / at
    • at / in / on
    Answer

    at / on / in

    Exact time = at, day/date = on, month/year = in. Small to big — that is the order.

    Exercise 12

    Each of the three teams ___ its own budget.

    Hint: Is each singular or plural?

    Answer

    has

    Each, every, everyone, nobody are all singular, even when a plural noun follows.

    Exercise 13

    Make the question in the right order.

    Words: jacket / Where / you / did / that / buy

    Answer

    Where did you buy that jacket

    In a question the helping verb (did) comes before the subject (you), and then the verb is in its first form.

    Exercise 14

    The order is wrong — tap the word in the wrong place.

    Could you tell me where is the lift?

    Answer

    Wrong word: is.

    Corrected: Could you tell me where the lift is?

    When a question sits inside a longer sentence, the order becomes normal again — the verb moves to the end.

    Exercise 15

    Which is the active form of 'The report was approved by the committee'?

    • The committee approved the report.
    • The report approved the committee.
    • The committee was approving by the report.
    • The report has approved the committee.
    Answer

    The committee approved the report.

    When changing passive to active, the 'by' part becomes the subject, and the old subject becomes the object.

    Exercise 16

    Rekha said that she ___ busy that evening.

    Hint: In reported speech the tense goes one step back.

    Answer

    was

    When reporting what someone said, the tense goes one step back: 'I am busy' → she said she was busy.

    Exercise 17

    Which sentence is correct for punctuation?

    • It's raining, so take its cover along.
    • Its raining, so take it's cover along.
    • Its raining, so take its cover along.
    • It's raining, so take it's cover along.
    Answer

    It's raining, so take its cover along.

    It's = it is. Its = belonging to it. Here the apostrophe shows a shortened form, not possession.

    Exercise 18

    Where is the stress in 'village'?

    • On the first part — VIL-lage
    • On the second part — vil-LAGE
    • Equally on both
    • Nowhere, all the sounds are equal

    Hint: It has two parts — VIL-lage.

    Answer

    On the first part — VIL-lage

    In most two-part English nouns the stress falls on the first part. This rhythm is what creates fluency.

    Exercise 19

    When speaking, you need time to think. What is best?

    • That's a good question — let me think for a second.
    • Basically, basically, what happens is…
    • Actually, actually, let me tell you…
    • Stay silent for twenty seconds
    Answer

    That's a good question — let me think for a second.

    Repeating filler words (basically, actually) lowers confidence. Buying time with one short, complete sentence sounds natural.

    Exercise 20

    You didn't understand what someone said in a meeting. What is the most natural line?

    • Sorry, I'm not following.
    • I am not understanding.
    • I have a doubt in your talk.
    • Please repeat it again.
    Answer

    Sorry, I'm not following.

    'I'm not following' is neutral — you don't look weak, and you don't blame the speaker. In 'repeat it again', 'again' is unnecessary.

    Exercise 21

    This was lovely, but I should get ___ — it's getting late.

    Hint: A soft way to leave.

    Answer

    going

    'I should get going' = I should leave now. More polite and more natural than 'I need to leave'.

    Exercise 22

    'The new form removed almost all the errors' — which verb fits?

    • eliminated
    • enhanced
    • authorized
    • demonstrated
    Answer

    eliminated

    Eliminate = to remove completely. Enhance means to make something good better, and authorize means to give official permission.

    Exercise 23

    One word is unnecessary — tap it.

    After two night shifts I was very exhausted.

    Answer

    Wrong word: very.

    Corrected: After two night shifts I was exhausted.

    Exhausted is already a very strong word. For more force, say 'utterly exhausted', not 'very'.

    Exercise 24

    Which advanced word sounds best when you describe yourself in an interview?

    • pragmatic
    • enigmatic
    • nefarious
    • onerous
    Answer

    pragmatic

    Pragmatic = practical, focused on results — a word of praise. Enigmatic would mean nobody can figure you out.

    Exercise 25

    The music is too loud. Which sentence is correct?

    • Please turn it down.
    • Please turn down it.
    • Please turn down.
    • Please it turn down.

    Hint: Where does the pronoun go?

    Answer

    Please turn it down.

    Turn down is separable, and the pronoun (it) must go in the middle. 'Turn down it' is never correct.

    Exercise 26

    The order is wrong — tap the wrong word.

    She will look the baby after this evening.

    Answer

    Wrong word: after.

    Corrected: She will look after the baby this evening.

    Look after is inseparable — the object comes after the particle, never in the middle.

    Exercise 27

    Match each idiom to its meaning.

    A B
    on the same page stop work for today
    the ball is in their court unlikely, but worth trying
    call it a day sharing the same understanding
    a long shot now it is their decision
    Answer

    on the same page — sharing the same understanding; the ball is in their court — now it is their decision; call it a day — stop work for today; a long shot — unlikely, but worth trying

    You hear all four in offices every day. Recognising them matters; using them is optional.

    Exercise 28

    Match each Indian English habit to its international form.

    A B
    Myself Ravi move the call up
    I have a doubt graduated in 2019
    prepone the call I'm Ravi
    passed out in 2019 I have a question
    Answer

    Myself Ravi — I'm Ravi; I have a doubt — I have a question; prepone the call — move the call up; passed out in 2019 — graduated in 2019

    All four are perfectly clear in India — you just need a different gear for an international call.

    Exercise 29

    Build one clear email line — without 'kindly' or 'needful'.

    Words: can / we / so / release / invoice / Could / by / the / Friday / approve / you / payment

    Answer

    Could you approve the invoice by Friday so we can release payment

    The formula: what to do + for whom + by when. All three in one line, and the message is perfectly clear.

    Exercise 30

    Say this line aloud. Run 'I have been' together smoothly, and put light stress on 'at'.

    Answer

    Say: I have been working here for three years and I am good at my job

    Three things are right in one line — tense, preposition and word order. In an interview this could be your first answer.

    What your score means

    Above 80% — the book is yours. Now, instead of more practice, you need real conversation. Talk to someone in English for ten minutes every day.
    50 to 80 — your foundation is strong, with a few loose spots. Look at the chart, spot the colour of your mistakes, and go back to only those two or three chapters.
    Below 50 — that's fine, and it is not a bad score at all. It only means the book needs one more read. The second time, things settle in much faster, because your mind recognises them.

    One last thing

    English is not an exam — it is a habit. The real aim of this book was not for you to memorise every rule. The aim was that when you speak English, you are no longer afraid of "I'll make a mistake". Once that fear goes, everything else will come on its own — a little at a time, every day. Those who speak, learn. Those who fear mistakes stay silent. Be one of those who speak.
