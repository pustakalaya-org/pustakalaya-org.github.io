    Chapter 51: Applications & messages¶

    Applications and messages — a ready model for every occasion

    You need leave, the fridge has broken down, you need a certificate from college, you have to send a CV for a job, or you have to tell your boss the train is late — this is the writing you will use most in life. This chapter gives you a complete model for each occasion that you can use directly by changing the names and dates, and a template at the end that works everywhere.

    In one line

    Every application answers three questions in its first lines: what you want, why, and for which dates or details. What you want, why you want it, and when or for what — these three things should be clear right at the start.

    51.1 Leave application — to your manager¶

    These days a leave application at the office is just an email. The manager only has to say "yes" or "no" — so give them everything they need to decide, and nothing extra.

    The four things in a leave email

    Dates (day + date), reason (one line, no details), handover (who will look after the work), and one direct question — "Could you please confirm if these dates are approved?"

    Model: Leave application to manager

    Subject: Leave request: 18–20 November (sister's wedding)

    Dear Ms Iyer,

    I would like to request three days of annual leave, from Wednesday, 18 November to Friday, 20 November, for my sister's wedding in Jaipur.

    I will finish the monthly GST reconciliation before I leave, and Rohan has agreed to handle any urgent vendor queries while I am away. I will be back at my desk on Monday, 23 November.

    Could you please let me know if these dates are approved?

    Thank you,
    Neha Kapoor
    Accounts Executive

    • I would like to request three days of annual leave… Much more polite than "I want leave", yet just as direct. How many days + which leave + from when to when.
    • …and Rohan has agreed to handle… The person covering is already named — this removes the manager's biggest worry.
    • I will be back at my desk on Monday, 23 November. The return date — this short line helps the manager with planning.

    51.2 Leave application — to a school principal¶

    Schools and colleges still use the old letter format — on paper or in an email. Teachers expect to see this format, so it makes sense to follow its rules here.

    Two worlds, two formats

    In a school/college application the old letter format is fine: To, date, Subject, "Respected Sir/Madam", "Thanking you". In office email, drop all of this — there "Dear Ms Iyer" and "Kind regards" are used.

    Model: Leave application to school principal

    To
    The Principal
    Green Valley Public School
    Nagpur

    10 August

    Subject: Application for leave, 6–8 August

    Respected Madam,

    I am Ananya Joshi, a student of Class 9 B. I was unable to attend school from 6 to 8 August because I had a high fever. The doctor advised me to rest at home, and I have attached a copy of the medical certificate.

    I will complete the classwork I missed and submit my pending homework by Friday. Please grant me leave for these three days.

    Thanking you,
    Yours sincerely,
    Ananya Joshi
    Class 9 B, Roll No. 14

    • I was unable to attend school from 6 to 8 August… The leave is already over, so the past tense. If you are asking in advance: "I will not be able to attend…"
    • I will complete the classwork I missed… It answers the teacher's worry in advance — this is the line that makes the application good.
    • Yours sincerely, — not "Yours obediently" "Obediently" is very old. Many schools still teach it, but "Yours sincerely" is right everywhere.

    51.3 Complaint letter — proof, not anger¶

    It is natural to feel angry when you complain. But the person at the company who reads your email will help you faster when they get facts, a reference number and a clear request — not CAPITAL LETTERS.

    The complaint formula

    What happened (with the date and order number) + what you want + by when. Anger, CAPITAL LETTERS and "!!!" do not get results; facts and proof do.

    Model: Complaint email to customer care

    Subject: Complaint: refrigerator not repaired after two visits (Order no. 58213)

    Dear Customer Care Team,

    I am writing to complain about the refrigerator I bought from your Andheri store on 2 September (Order no. 58213; invoice attached).

    Since 20 September, the fridge has not been cooling. Your technician visited on 23 September and again on 30 September, but the problem is still not fixed. As a result, we have had to throw away food twice.

    The product is under warranty. I would like you to either repair it fully or replace it by 15 October. Please confirm the next step by replying to this email.

    Kind regards,
    Vikram Singh
    [Phone number]

    • The order number in the subject Customer care systems run on order numbers — if it is in the subject, the email reaches the right team faster.
    • Since 20 September… visited on 23 and 30 September… A short timeline of dates — nobody can argue with it.
    • I would like you to either repair it fully or replace it by 15 October. Two clear options and one deadline. Firm, but not rude.

    51.4 Request letter — polite, but clear¶

    In requests we often become so polite that the actual point gets lost — "I humbly request you to kindly please…". One please is enough; give clear details everywhere else.

    The right amount of politeness

    "Could you please…" or "I would be grateful if you could…" — that is all. Don't pile "humbly", "kindly" and "please" together. Politeness comes not from words but from giving clear and complete information.

    Model: Request to college office

    Subject: Request for a bonafide certificate — Rohit Kulkarni, TY BCom (Roll No. 2231)

    Dear Sir or Madam,

    I am a third-year BCom student (Division A, Roll No. 2231), and I need a bonafide certificate to apply for an education loan.

    I would be grateful if you could issue the certificate by Wednesday, 21 October, as the bank's deadline is 23 October. I have attached a copy of my college ID card and my fee receipt.

    Please let me know if you need any other document from me.

    Yours faithfully,
    Rohit Kulkarni
    TY BCom, Division A

    • I would be grateful if you could issue the certificate by Wednesday. A polite request + a deadline + the reason for the deadline (the bank's date).
    • Dear Sir or Madam → Yours faithfully The name is not known, hence "faithfully". If the name were known (Dear Mrs Patil), it would be "Yours sincerely".
    • Please let me know if you need any other document. It makes the office's next step easy — it saves back-and-forth.

    51.5 Job application — the cover note¶

    Leaving the email body empty when you send a CV — or writing only "PFA my resume" — is the biggest mistake. A recruiter gives each application only a few seconds; a short, clear cover note sets you apart from the pile.

    The three paragraphs of a cover note

    (1) Which job, and where you saw it. (2) One or two real achievements, with numbers. (3) CV attached + your wish to talk. The whole note should be under 150 words.

    Model: Job application cover note

    Subject: Application for Junior Data Analyst (Ref. JDA-24) — Aditya Rao

    Dear Ms Fernandes,

    I am writing to apply for the Junior Data Analyst role advertised on your careers page.

    I recently completed my BSc in Statistics and spent six months as an intern at a retail company, where I built weekly sales dashboards in Excel and Power BI. These dashboards cut the team's reporting time from two days to half a day.

    My CV is attached. I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can support your analytics team.

    Kind regards,
    Aditya Rao
    [Phone number] · [LinkedIn profile link]

    • The role, reference number and name in the subject HR fills several roles at once — the reference number sends your email to the right folder.
    • …cut the team's reporting time from two days to half a day. Everyone writes "I am hard-working and dedicated". An achievement with numbers is remembered.
    • I would welcome the chance to discuss… A confident ending — more modern than "kindly consider my candidature".

    51.6 Professional messages on WhatsApp and text¶

    Half of office work now happens on WhatsApp. The same rule applies here as in email: the whole point in one message. Sending only "Hi sir" and waiting for a reply wastes the other person's time and yours.

    Four rules for professional messages

    One message, the whole point — who, what, by when. In the first message give your name and context (team, class). After 9 pm, only urgent matters. And ask before sending a voice note — not everyone can listen to one in a meeting.

    Model: WhatsApp to your boss

    Good morning, Mr Khanna. My train is running about 40 minutes late, so I will reach the office by 10:15. The slides for the 11 am meeting are already in your inbox. — Neha

    Model: WhatsApp to a client

    Hello Ms Banerjee, this is Arjun from Brightline Interiors. The cabinets for your kitchen have arrived. Would Tuesday at 11 am suit you for the installation? You can reply here or call me on this number.

    Model: Message to a teacher

    Good evening, Ma'am. This is Ananya Joshi from Class 9 B. I was absent today because of a fever. Could you please tell me which Maths topics were covered, so that I can catch up? Thank you.

    • This is Arjun from Brightline Interiors. The first message from a new number — name + company. Not "Myself Arjun".
    • Would Tuesday at 11 am suit you? Give the client one definite option — "tell me when you are free" slows down the reply.
    • Good evening, Ma'am. "Ma'am / Sir" is perfectly fine for a teacher — that is the norm in school culture.

    51.7 Your own template — fill in the blanks¶

    All the models above are built on one structure. Save it once in your phone's notes — then every new application is just a matter of filling in the blanks.

    Template: application or formal email

    Subject: [What you want]: [dates / reference number]

    Dear [Mr/Ms + surname], — or if you don't know the name: Dear Sir or Madam,

    I am writing to [request / inform you about / complain about / apply for] [the main point, in one line].

    \[Reason or details — 2–3 lines. Dates, numbers, references.\]
    \[What arrangement you have made, or what you have attached.\]

    Could you please [what to do] by [day, date]?

    Kind regards, — or with Dear Sir or Madam: Yours faithfully,
    [Full name]
    [Job title / class / roll number]
    [Phone number]

    Template: professional WhatsApp

    [Good morning / Hello], [Mr/Ms + surname]. This is [your name] from [team / company / class]. [What happened or what you need — one line]. [By when / next step]. Thank you.

    • Before sending, 1: both day and date in the dates? "Wednesday, 18 November" — and check that it really is a Wednesday.
    • Before sending, 2: is the name spelled right? Getting someone's name wrong is the mistake that is noticed fastest.
    • Before sending, 3: is the attachment really attached? Writing "Please find attached" and forgetting the file — this happens every day in every office.
    • Before sending, 4: did you read it aloud once? The ear catches the mistakes the eye misses.

    51.8 The mistakes we really make¶

    These sentences are so common in leave applications and office messages that they start to look right. Most of them are direct translations from Hindi.

    Real mistakes in applications and messages

    • Wrong: I am suffering from fever since two days.
    • Right: I have had a fever for two days.

    • Why: With a duration (two days) use "for", not "since". And for something still going on, use the present perfect: have had.

    • Wrong: Please grant me leave as my health is not well.

    • Right: Please grant me leave as I am unwell.

    • Why: "Health is not well" says the same thing twice. Say it directly: I am unwell / I am not well.

    • Wrong: I want leave for tomorrow.

    • Right: I would like to take leave tomorrow, Friday 20 November.

    • Why: "I want" sounds like a demand. "I would like to" is polite — and writing the date prevents confusion.

    • Wrong: Respected sir, kindly do the needful.

    • Right: Good morning, Mr Khanna. Could you please approve my leave for Friday?

    • Why: On WhatsApp "Respected sir" feels heavy, and "do the needful" does not say what has to be done.

    • Wrong: I am having a doubt in chapter 5.

    • Right: I have a question about Chapter 5.
    • Why: Outside India, "doubt" means disbelief, not a question. And "have" (meaning to possess) is not used with -ing.

    51.9 Practice — Applications¶

    Exercise 1

    A leave email to your manager — which is the best subject line?

    • Leave application
    • Leave request: 18–20 November (sister's wedding)
    • Sir please grant leave
    • Urgent leave needed!!!
    Answer

    Leave request: 18–20 November (sister's wedding)

    Topic + exact dates + a short reason. The manager can decide in the inbox, even without opening the email.

    Exercise 2

    Which greeting is still in use in an application to a school principal?

    • Respected Madam
    • Hey Ma'am
    • Hi Principal
    • Dear Madam ji
    Answer

    Respected Madam

    School and college applications use the old format — 'Respected Sir/Madam'. In an office email, write 'Dear Ms Iyer' instead.

    Exercise 3

    A WhatsApp message to your boss — your train is late. Which is the best message?

    • Hi sir
    • Sir late today
    • Good morning, Mr Khanna. My train is running 40 minutes late, so I will reach by 10:15. The slides for the 11 am meeting are already in your inbox.
    • Sorry sir traffic, coming
    Answer

    Good morning, Mr Khanna. My train is running 40 minutes late, so I will reach by 10:15. The slides for the 11 am meeting are already in your inbox.

    The whole point in one message: what happened, when you will arrive, and how the work is covered. The boss has nothing to ask.

    Exercise 4

    What is the right length for a job cover note?

    • Under 150 words, three short paragraphs
    • Two full pages
    • Just one line: 'PFA my CV'
    • My whole life story from childhood until now
    Answer

    Under 150 words, three short paragraphs

    A recruiter gives each application only a few seconds. Which job, one or two real achievements with numbers, and the CV attached — that is all.

    Exercise 5

    Find the mistake.

    I am suffering from fever since two days.

    Answer

    Wrong word: since.

    Corrected: I have had a fever for two days.

    A duration (two days) takes 'for', not 'since' — 'since' is followed by a starting point (since Monday). And 'I have had a fever' is more natural.

    Exercise 6

    Which word makes this sentence sound odd?

    I cannot come today because my health is not well.

    Answer

    Wrong word: health.

    Corrected: I cannot come today because I am not well.

    'Health is not well' says one thing twice. Say it directly: I am not well / I am unwell.

    Exercise 7

    Find the old phrase that says nothing clearly.

    Kindly do the needful and approve my leave.

    Answer

    Wrong word: needful.

    Corrected: Could you please approve my leave for 18–20 November?

    'Do the needful' leaves it to the reader to work out what to do. Write directly what you want — and for which dates.

    Exercise 8

    I would be ___ if you could issue the certificate by Friday.

    Answer

    grateful / thankful

    'I would be grateful if you could…' is the clearest and most professional way to make a polite request. There is no need for 'humbly request you to kindly please'.

    Exercise 9

    I am writing to ___ for the post of Junior Data Analyst.

    Answer

    apply

    apply for a job / apply for a post. The first line of a cover note tells the reader which job you are writing about.

    Exercise 10

    Match each occasion to its correct first line.

    A B
    Leave email to manager I am writing to apply for the post of Sales Associate.
    Complaint Could you please issue me a bonafide certificate?
    Cover note Good evening, Ma'am. This is Ananya from Class 9 B.
    Request to college office I would like to request leave from 18 to 20 November.
    Message to teacher I am writing to complain about a faulty refrigerator.
    Answer

    Leave email to manager — I would like to request leave from 18 to 20 November.; Complaint — I am writing to complain about a faulty refrigerator.; Cover note — I am writing to apply for the post of Sales Associate.; Request to college office — Could you please issue me a bonafide certificate?; Message to teacher — Good evening, Ma'am. This is Ananya from Class 9 B.

    Each first line states the purpose directly — the reader knows what it is about from the very start.

    Exercise 11

    Build the last line of a leave email in the right order.

    Words: whether / approved? / dates / please / confirm / are / you / Could / these

    Answer

    Could you please confirm whether these dates are approved?

    Could you please + verb (confirm) + whether-clause. End a leave email with a clear question like this.

    Exercise 12

    Say this line — as if you were telling your manager on the phone.

    Answer

    Say: I would like to request two days of leave next week.

    'I would like to request' is much more polite than 'I want leave', and still direct. When speaking, join 'would like' together: wud-laik.

    What to do next

    Whenever you write an application or a professional message this week, open this chapter's template first. After three times with the template, the structure will come to you by itself the fourth time.
