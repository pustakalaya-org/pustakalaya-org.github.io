    Chapter 31: Numbers, dates, time & money¶

    Number, date, time, paise

    Saying numbers seems easy — until an auto driver hears "fifteen" instead of "fifty", or a foreign client hears "twelve lakh" and goes quiet. In this chapter you will learn to say numbers, money, years, dates, times and phone numbers clearly and correctly — and how to turn lakh-crore into million. Press 🔊 on every line and repeat it aloud.

    In one line

    -teen: stress at the end, -ty: stress at the start · hundred and · 10 lakh = 1 million · twenty twenty-six · quarter past / half past / quarter to. One small slip with a number = wrong money, wrong train, wrong day.

    31.1 13 or 30? — the most expensive confusion¶

    thirteen and thirty, fifteen and fifty — they are different in writing, but Indian speakers often say them the same way. The difference is in two things: where the stress is, and whether the ending is long or short.

    -teen Listen -ty Listen
    13 thirTEEN 30 THIRty
    14 fourTEEN 40 FORty
    15 fifTEEN 50 FIFty
    16 sixTEEN 60 SIXty
    18 eighTEEN 80 EIGHty
    • She's fifteen, not fifty! = She is 15 years old, not 50!
    • Platform thirteen, please. = Asking for platform number 13.

    If in doubt, break it up

    With important numbers like money or a platform, confusion is possible, so make it clear: "Fifteen — one five." or "Fifty — five zero." Native speakers do exactly this on the phone.

    31.2 Above 100 — hundred, thousand and "and"¶

    In British and Indian English, and comes after hundred: a hundred and twenty. American English often leaves out the "and". And one firm rule: with a number, hundred / thousand / million never take a plural.

    Number Say Listen
    100 a hundred / one hundred
    120 a hundred and twenty
    305 three hundred and five
    999 nine hundred and ninety-nine
    1,500 one thousand five hundred / fifteen hundred
    4,250 four thousand two hundred and fifty
    70,000 seventy thousand

    ✗

    There were three hundreds people at the wedding.

    ✓

    There were three hundred people at the wedding.

    hundreds of people

    thousands of rupees

    • Hundreds of people were waiting at the station. = A very large crowd (we don't know the exact number) was waiting.

    31.3 Lakh, crore and million — the bridge between two worlds¶

    In India we think in lakh and crore, and in Indian English these are perfectly good words. But a foreign client, an international exam (IELTS) or a global meeting needs thousand, million, billion. The commas go in different places too: Indian 1,25,000 = international 125,000.

    Amount Indian English International
    ₹1,25,000 one lakh twenty-five thousand a hundred and twenty-five thousand
    ₹8,50,000 eight and a half lakh eight hundred and fifty thousand
    ₹25,00,000 twenty-five lakh two and a half million
    ₹3,00,00,000 three crore thirty million
    ₹1,500 crore fifteen hundred crore fifteen billion
    • The bike costs one lakh twenty-five thousand rupees. = The bike costs ₹1,25,000. (Use this when talking within India.)
    • That's about a hundred and twenty-five thousand rupees. = The same amount, said for a foreign friend.
    • Our app has two and a half million users — that's twenty-five lakh. = Our app has 25 lakh users, said both ways.
    • The film made three hundred crore — about thirty-six million dollars. = The film earned 300 crore rupees, which is roughly 36 million US dollars.

    ✗

    My salary is twelve lakhs per annum. (to a US client)

    ✓

    My salary is about 1.2 million rupees a year — roughly fourteen thousand dollars.

    31.4 Decimals, fractions and percentages¶

    After the decimal point, say each digit separately: 3.14 = "three point one four", not "three point fourteen". Say 0 as "zero" (in British English also "nought"); and 0.5 can simply be "point five".

    Written Say Listen
    2.5 two point five
    3.14 three point one four
    0.75 zero point seven five
    ½ a half
    1½ one and a half
    ⅓ · ⅔ a third · two thirds
    ¼ · ¾ a quarter · three quarters
    50% fifty per cent
    7.5% seven point five per cent
    • One and a half kilos of tomatoes, please. = Please give me 1.5 kg of tomatoes (in Hindi: "dedh kilo").
    • Two thirds of the class passed with distinction. = 2 out of every 3 students got a distinction.
    • Prices went up by twelve per cent this year. = Prices rose by 12% this year.

    Small points

    British/Indian spelling is per cent, American is percent — they sound the same. One and a half hours (plural), but half an hour. And "dedh" = one and a half, "dhai" = two and a half.

    31.5 Money and prices¶

    We write ₹250 or Rs 250 — the symbol first; but we say "two hundred and fifty rupees" — rupees after the number. In shops and markets a short form is common too: ₹499 = "four ninety-nine".

    Written Say Listen
    ₹50 fifty rupees
    ₹499 four hundred and ninety-nine rupees / four ninety-nine
    ₹2.50 two rupees fifty (paise)
    ₹1,200 one thousand two hundred / twelve hundred
    $3.99 three ninety-nine / three dollars ninety-nine cents
    £15 fifteen pounds
    • How much is this kurta? — It's eight hundred rupees. = Asking the price, and hearing that it is ₹800.
    • Could you do it for six fifty? = Can you lower the price to ₹650? (bargaining)
    • The bill comes to two thousand three hundred and forty rupees. = The total bill is ₹2,340.
    • Can you split it? That's about six hundred each. = Let's share the bill — about ₹600 per person.

    ✗

    It costs rupees two hundred.

    ✓

    It costs two hundred rupees.

    after

    'k' is informal only

    With friends "fifteen k" (15,000) is fine. In an office email or an interview, say it in full: fifteen thousand. For salary, say "twelve lakh a year" or "twelve lakh per annum", not "12 LPA".

    31.6 Years, dates and ordinal numbers¶

    Years — in pairs of digits¶

    Years are usually said in two parts: 19|98 = nineteen ninety-eight. Only 2000 to 2009 use "two thousand (and) …". After 2010 both ways work, but twenty twenty-six is more common.

    Year Say Listen
    1947 nineteen forty-seven
    1900 nineteen hundred
    2000 the year two thousand
    2005 two thousand and five (US: two thousand five)
    2026 twenty twenty-six
    1990s the nineties

    Ordinal numbers — first, second, third¶

    Dates are always said as ordinals: the fifth, not "the five". Watch out for 11/12/13 (eleventh, twelfth, thirteenth) and for 21/22/23 (twenty-first, twenty-second, twenty-third).

    Number Ordinal Listen
    1st · 2nd · 3rd first · second · third
    5th · 8th · 9th fifth · eighth · ninth
    11th · 12th · 13th eleventh · twelfth · thirteenth
    20th · 21st · 22nd twentieth · twenty-first · twenty-second
    30th · 31st thirtieth · thirty-first

    Dates — British or American?¶

    Style Written Spoken
    British / Indian 5 May 2026 the fifth of May, twenty twenty-six
    American May 5, 2026 May fifth, twenty twenty-six
    India/UK number 05/06/2026 5 June
    US number 05/06/2026 May 6
    • My birthday is on the twenty-first of October. = My birthday is on 21 October.
    • The exam is on March the third. = The exam is on 3 March.
    • I was born in two thousand and five. = I was born in 2005.
    • India became independent on the fifteenth of August, nineteen forty-seven. = India became independent on 15 August 1947.

    ✗

    The meeting is on five May.

    ✓

    The meeting is on the fifth of May. / on May fifth.

    on

    in

    Prepositions — the linking words

    The 05/06 trap

    For a US client, 05/06 = 6 May; for you it is 5 June. In international emails always write the month as a word: 5 June 2026. Problem solved.

    31.7 Telling the time — from o'clock to "7 sharp"¶

    There are two ways: digital (seven fifteen) — easy and always correct; and past / to (quarter past seven) — very common in everyday talk. You need to understand both.

    Time past / to Digital Listen
    7:00 seven o'clock seven
    7:05 five past seven seven oh five
    7:15 quarter past seven seven fifteen
    7:30 half past seven seven thirty
    7:40 twenty to eight seven forty
    7:45 quarter to eight seven forty-five

    past and to

    For the first 30 minutes: minutes + past + the current hour. For the last 30: minutes + to + the next hour. 15 = quarter, 30 = half. If in doubt, say it digitally: seven forty-five is never wrong.

    Hindi Time English
    sawa saat 7:15 quarter past seven
    saadhe saat 7:30 half past seven
    paune aath 7:45 quarter to eight
    dedh baje 1:30 half past one
    dhai baje 2:30 half past two
    • The meeting starts at ten sharp. = The meeting starts at exactly ten — not even one minute late.
    • Let's meet around six. / Let's meet at six-ish. = Let's meet at about six — the exact time doesn't matter.
    • My flight is at eleven forty-five p.m. = My flight is at 11:45 at night.
    • The train departs at nineteen hundred hours. = The train leaves at 19:00 (7 p.m.). The 24-hour clock is used in announcements.
    • What time is it? — It's half past four. = Asking the time, and hearing that it is 4:30.

    ✗

    I'll come at 7 o'clock in the evening p.m.

    ✓

    I'll come at 7 p.m. / at seven in the evening.

    seven o'clock

    British 'half seven'

    In the UK people say "half seven" and mean 7:30 (half past seven). Not 6:30!

    31.8 Phone numbers, measurements and temperature¶

    Phone numbers — one digit at a time, in groups¶

    Say each digit of a phone number separately, in small groups, with a slight pause between groups. Two identical digits = double, three = triple. Say 0 as "oh" or "zero" — both are fine.

    • 98450 22301 → nine eight four five oh, double two three oh one. = Two groups of five digits.
    • My extension is triple four. = My extension is 444.
    • Sorry, was that five oh or five four? = Checking again: was it 50 or 54?

    Measurements, temperature and scores¶

    Written Say Listen
    5'8" five foot eight / five feet eight inches
    70 kg seventy kilos
    12 km twelve kilometres
    2 BHK, 1,100 sq ft a two B-H-K, eleven hundred square feet
    38°C thirty-eight degrees (Celsius)
    −5°C minus five degrees
    250/3 (cricket) two fifty for three
    2–0 (football) two nil / two-zero
    • It was forty-four degrees in Nagpur yesterday! = Yesterday's temperature in Nagpur was 44°C!
    • I'm five foot six and I weigh about sixty kilos. = My height is 5'6" and my weight is about 60 kg.

    ✗

    The office is 12 kms from here. / It's 38 degree.

    ✓

    The office is 12 km (twelve kilometres) from here. / It's 38 degrees.

    31.9 In real life — booking a cab and paying a bill¶

    Ankit is booking a cab to the airport, and later paying a bill at a shop. Listen to every line and repeat it — pay special attention to the numbers.

    Who What they say Listen
    Agent What time is your flight, sir?
    Ankit It's at six forty-five a.m. on the thirteenth.
    Agent Sorry — the thirteenth or the thirtieth?
    Ankit The thirteenth. One three.
    Agent Then the driver will pick you up at four sharp. The fare is eight hundred and fifty rupees.
    Ankit Great. My number is nine eight one oh, double seven, four five two six.
    Cashier That's one thousand two hundred and forty, sir. Card or UPI?

    The daily 3-minute drill

    Whatever number you see on the way — a car plate, a price in a shop, the time on a clock — say it in English in your head. "Seven forty-five… quarter to eight." Within a week, saying numbers will be a job for your tongue, not your brain.

    31.10 Practice — Numbers, dates, time & money¶

    Exercise 1

    When is the meeting?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    The meeting has been moved to the thirteenth of March.

    • 13 March
    • 30 March
    • 3 March
    • 13 May
    Answer

    13 March

    thir-TEEN — the stress is at the end, with a long 'ee'. For 30 it would be THIR-tieth.

    Exercise 2

    How much does the driver want?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    That will be fifty rupees, please.

    • ₹15
    • ₹50
    • ₹500
    • ₹5
    Answer

    ₹50

    FIF-ty: stress at the start, short ending. In fifteen the stress would be on 'teen'.

    Exercise 3

    What time does the flight leave?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    My flight leaves at a quarter to nine.

    • 8:45
    • 9:15
    • 8:15
    • 9:45
    Answer

    8:45

    quarter to nine = fifteen minutes before nine = 8:45 (paune nau).

    Exercise 4

    What is the price of the flat?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    The flat costs one crore twenty lakh rupees.

    • ₹1,20,00,000
    • ₹12,00,000
    • ₹1,20,000
    • ₹1,02,00,000
    Answer

    ₹1,20,00,000

    1 crore + 20 lakh = 1,20,00,000 (= 12 million).

    Exercise 5

    How many users is that in lakh?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Our app now has about one point five million users.

    • 15 lakh
    • 1.5 lakh
    • 150 lakh
    • 1.5 crore
    Answer

    15 lakh

    1 million = 10 lakh, so 1.5 million = 15 lakh.

    Exercise 6

    What is Kavya's number?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Hi, it's Kavya. Please call me back on nine eight four five oh, double two, three oh one.

    • 98450 22301
    • 98450 23011
    • 98405 22301
    • 98450 22310
    Answer

    98450 22301

    oh = 0, double two = 22. In a phone number, every digit is said separately.

    Exercise 7

    When does the train leave?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Train number one two nine five one will depart from platform four at nineteen hundred hours.

    • 7 p.m.
    • 9 p.m.
    • 7 a.m.
    • 9 a.m.
    Answer

    7 p.m.

    Nineteen hundred hours = 19:00 = 7 in the evening. Announcements use the 24-hour clock.

    Exercise 8

    Which platform?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Train number one two nine five one will depart from platform four at nineteen hundred hours.

    • 4
    • 14
    • 19
    • 1
    Answer

    4

    platform four. 12951 is the train number, and 19 is part of the time.

    Exercise 9

    Write what you hear. (Numbers can be digits or words — both are accepted)

    Dictation. Have someone read the sentence aloud, then write it down.

    Answer

    The tickets cost two hundred and fifty rupees each.

    two hundred and fifty = 250. Rupees comes after the number.

    Write what you hear.

    Dictation. Have someone read the sentence aloud, then write it down.

    Answer

    My birthday is on the twenty-first of October.

    Dates are said as ordinals: twenty-first (21st).

    Exercise 11

    Write what you hear.

    Dictation. Have someone read the sentence aloud, then write it down.

    Answer

    I was born in two thousand and five.

    2000–2009: two thousand (and) five.

    Exercise 12

    Write what you hear.

    Dictation. Have someone read the sentence aloud, then write it down.

    Answer

    The shop opens at half past ten.

    half past ten = 10:30 (saadhe das).

    Exercise 13

    How do people usually say the year 2026?

    • twenty twenty-six
    • two zero two six
    • twenty hundred twenty-six
    • two thousand twenty-six hundred
    Answer

    twenty twenty-six

    Years are said in two pairs: 20|26 = twenty twenty-six. ('two thousand and twenty-six' is also correct, but less common.)

    Exercise 14

    A US client asks your budget. ₹1,25,000 in international English is…

    • a hundred and twenty-five thousand rupees
    • twelve lakh fifty thousand rupees
    • one million two hundred and fifty thousand rupees
    • twelve thousand five hundred rupees
    Answer

    a hundred and twenty-five thousand rupees

    1,25,000 = 125,000 = a hundred and twenty-five thousand.

    Exercise 15

    An American email says the deadline is 05/06. What date do they mean?

    • 6 May
    • 5 June
    • 5 May
    • 6 June
    Answer

    6 May

    US style is month/day. That is why you should write the month as a word in international emails.

    Exercise 16

    7:45 = a quarter ___ eight.

    Hint: The next hour is coming.

    Answer

    to

    After 30 minutes: minutes + to + the next hour.

    Exercise 17

    Tap the wrong word.

    There were three hundreds people at the wedding.

    Answer

    Wrong word: hundreds.

    Corrected: There were three hundred people at the wedding.

    With a number, hundred stays singular: three hundred people.

    Exercise 18

    Match the ordinals.

    A B
    1st twelfth
    3rd twenty-first
    12th first
    21st third
    Answer

    1st — first; 3rd — third; 12th — twelfth; 21st — twenty-first

    1, 2, 3 and the numbers ending in them (21, 22, 23) are special; 12 = twelfth.

    Exercise 19

    Say it aloud — make the difference between thir-TEEN and THIR-ty clear.

    Answer

    Say: The train leaves from platform thirteen, not thirty

    thir-TEEN: stress at the end, long 'ee'. THIR-ty: stress at the start, short ending.

    What to do next

    Say your phone number, your birthday and last month's electricity bill aloud in English — three times. Then use these numbers in a real call in the Phone and video calls chapter, and listen in Native speed English to hear how words join together when people speak fast.
