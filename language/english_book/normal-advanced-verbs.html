    Chapter 44: Normal → advanced verbs¶

    Verb upgrades — one word instead of three

    In English, the verb is the engine of the sentence. When the engine is weak — make better, get rid of, give permission — the whole sentence feels slack, even if the grammar is perfect. One precise verb does the work of two or three words, and the sentence at once sounds clear and educated. In this chapter: five upgrades in depth, then a full list of more than twenty, their rules, and the most important question — when not to use them.

    In one line

    Wherever two or three words together describe one action, there is often a single verb that describes the same action more clearly. Fewer words, more impact — this is the first rule of writing English.

    44.1 Five upgrades, one by one¶

    show clearly → demonstrate¶

    Demonstrate does not only mean to show — it means to prove by doing or by giving evidence. That is why it fits in a lab, a class, a product demo and a report. If you are just pointing out a place with your finger, show is the right verb.

    • The trial demonstrated that the cheaper design works just as well. = The trial proved that the cheaper design works equally well.
    • Let me demonstrate the new feature before we discuss pricing. = Before we talk about pricing, let me show the new feature in action.

    When not to

    "Demonstrate me the way to the station" — this is odd. To point out a route, use show. Remember: demonstrate needs evidence or a process behind it.

    make better → enhance¶

    Enhance means to make something that is already good even better. So you do not "enhance" something that is broken — you fix it. Something weak you improve. Enhance carries a feeling of adding polish on top.

    • Better lighting would really enhance these photos. = Better lighting would make these photos look even nicer.
    • We added search to enhance the reading experience. = We added search to make reading better.

    Three different 'betters'

    fix = repair something broken · improve = make something weak better · enhance = make something good even better. You do not say "enhance the bug" — a bug is fixed.

    get rid of → eliminate¶

    Eliminate means to remove completely, leaving not even one. It is a heavy verb — it sounds good with numbers, process, risk and waste. You do not "eliminate" your old clothes; they are simply got rid of.

    • The new form eliminated almost all data-entry errors. = The new form removed almost every data-entry error.
    • We can eliminate two steps and still get the same result. = We can remove two steps and the result will stay the same.

    give permission → authorize¶

    Authorize is not just "saying yes" — it carries authority. The one who authorizes must have official power: a manager, a bank, the government, a system. A mother who allows her child to go out lets him go — she does not authorize it.

    • Only the finance head can authorize a refund above fifty thousand. = Only the finance head can approve a refund of more than fifty thousand.
    • I'm not authorized to share those figures — let me check with my manager. = I do not have the authority to share those numbers — let me ask my manager.

    A very useful line

    "I'm not authorized to do that" works in interviews, support calls and the office — all three. It is a polite and professional way to say no, and it does not make you look at fault.

    take into account → consider¶

    Consider does two jobs: (1) to think about something carefully and (2) to keep something in mind. In both, it is shorter and cleaner than take into account. Note: about never comes after consider.

    • We should consider the delivery cost before choosing a vendor. = We should keep the delivery cost in mind before choosing a vendor.
    • Have you considered doing it the other way round? = Have you thought about doing it the opposite way?

    Two firm rules for consider

    • Wrong: Please consider about my request.
    • Right: Please consider my request.

    • Why: The thing comes straight after consider — no preposition.

    • Wrong: Have you considered to change the design?

    • Right: Have you considered changing the design?
    • Why: After consider, a verb always takes the -ing form, not the "to" form.

    44.2 One weak verb, several right verbs¶

    The biggest mistake is to always replace a weak phrase with one and the same big word. The truth is that every weak phrase opens in several directions — which one you choose depends on the meaning.

    44.3 The big upgrade table¶

    It is not only the words here — the third column tells you where each upgrade fits. Without that column, this table is dangerous.

    Slack phrase One verb Where it fits
    show clearly / prove by doing demonstrate Report, presentation, class, product demo
    make better (something already good) enhance Product, design, experience, taste
    get rid of (completely) eliminate Risk, waste, error, step, cost
    give permission (with authority) authorize Office, bank, payment, access
    take into account consider Everywhere — speaking and writing
    make smaller / cut down reduce Cost, time, size — very common
    make bigger / increase expand Business, team, scope
    find out determine Investigation, report, official writing
    put in place implement Office, software, policy
    say sorry formally apologize Email, customer support
    make sure ensure Email, process, instructions
    give out distribute Stock, salary, files
    look into examine / investigate Investigation, audit, problem
    keep up maintain Machine, record, standard
    cut down slowly taper Medicine, spending — a bit technical
    take part in participate Event, survey, training

    44.4 Fifteen more upgrades — make, give, get, have, take¶

    These are the same "light verb + noun" phrases we use every day. Each has a real sentence (press 🔊) and the last column gives the register — because some of these words sound heavy or odd in speech.

    make ___ phrases¶

    Normal → Advanced Example Meaning When to use it
    make worse → aggravate Skipping physio will only aggravate the injury. To make something already bad even worse. Formal — injury, illness, quarrel, problem. In casual speech "make it worse" is better.
    make possible → enable The new app enables farmers to check prices instantly. To make something possible for someone. Neutral, very common in office and tech. Pattern: enable + someone + to + verb.
    make certain → ensure Please ensure that all files are backed up by Friday. To make certain that something happens. Email and instructions. In speech "make sure" is the natural one.
    make clear → elucidate The professor elucidated the theory with two simple diagrams. To open up a difficult idea and explain it clearly. Very bookish — research papers, lectures. In daily life say "explain" or "clarify".
    make use of → utilize The plant now utilizes waste heat to warm the offices. To put something to work. Technical/report. In 90% of cases plain "use" is better — "utilize" sounds heavy for no reason.

    give / get / find phrases¶

    Normal → Advanced Example Meaning When to use it
    give information → provide Could you provide details of the payment? To supply information or something else. Safe in the office and in email. Pattern: provide something, provide someone with something.
    give an example → exemplify This project exemplifies what a small team can do. To be a perfect example of something. Formal writing. In speech "is a great example of" is more natural.
    get involved → participate Over two hundred students participated in the survey. To take part in an activity. Neutral. Always participate in. Among friends "get involved" or "join in" is fine.
    find a solution → resolve The support team resolved the issue within an hour. To settle a problem. Office, support, disputes. Very useful. For a small thing "sort out" also works.

    have / come / reach / take / keep phrases¶

    Normal → Advanced Example Meaning When to use it
    have an effect → influence Reviews strongly influence what people buy online. To have an effect on someone or something. Safe everywhere. Note: no on after influence — "influence people", but "have an influence on people".
    have a need → require This role requires three years of experience. To need something. Rules, job posts, instructions. In conversation use "need" — "I require water" sounds robotic.
    come to a decision → determine The committee will determine the final budget next week. To decide after careful thought. Official. In speech "decide" is more common. The other meaning of determine: to find out.
    reach an agreement → reconcile The mediator helped them reconcile their differences. To end a disagreement and bring two sides together. Reconcile = to settle relationships or differences, or to match accounts. For simply "we agreed" use reach a consensus or agree.
    take control → assume authority The deputy will assume authority while the director is away. To take over command officially. Very formal — notices, reports. In daily life and at the office use take charge: "She took charge of the project."
    keep safe → safeguard Strong passwords help safeguard your data. To protect from harm. Formal and policy language — data, rights, interests. For a child or a bag, "keep safe" is fine.

    Where the simple verb wins

    use > utilize · explain > elucidate · need > require · decide > determine · make sure > ensure · take charge > assume authority — all of this in conversation. Keep the heavy forms for reports, policies and formal emails. If you ask a friend "Can you elucidate?", they will laugh. Big verbs are for writing, not for the tea stall.

    44.5 Three questions — before you upgrade¶

    A bigger word is not always better. Before replacing any weak phrase, ask yourself three questions:

    The upgrade test

    1. Does the meaning stay the same? — "eliminate the poor" means something completely different. Changing a word can change the meaning.
    2. Can the setting carry it? — at a tea stall, "Could you authorize one more cup?" sounds like a joke.
    3. Can I say it comfortably? — if your tongue stumbles while speaking, that word is not yours yet.

    The truth about interviews

    An interviewer likes clear thinking more than big words. One right verb sounds good; three heavy verbs in every line make you look memorised. In one paragraph, one or two upgrades are plenty. Add spice only as much as the dish can take.

    44.6 Mistakes people make with these verbs¶

    Be careful

    • Wrong: He demonstrated me his new phone.
    • Right: He showed me his new phone.

    • Why: Demonstrate means to prove by doing or by evidence. To show a phone, simply use "show".

    • Wrong: Please enhance this broken link.

    • Right: Please fix this broken link.

    • Why: Something broken is fixed. Enhance is only for something that already works.

    • Wrong: My father authorized me to go to the party.

    • Right: My father let me go to the party.

    • Why: Authorize carries official authority. At home say "let", "allowed me to", or "gave me permission".

    • Wrong: We will eliminate the old sofa this weekend.

    • Right: We will get rid of the old sofa this weekend.

    • Why: For everyday objects, "get rid of" is the natural choice. Eliminate is for abstract things like risk, error and cost.

    • Wrong: Kindly consider about my application.

    • Right: Please consider my application.

    • Why: No preposition comes after consider — and "please" is more natural than "kindly".

    • Wrong: We implemented a new chair in the office.

    • Right: We got a new chair for the office.
    • Why: Implement is for processes, systems and policies — not for objects.

    44.7 One paragraph, two versions¶

    Below is a short office update. First the slack version, then the upgraded one. Notice that the second version is also shorter — that is the real benefit.

    Before

    We made the checkout page better and we got rid of two steps. The data shows clearly that people finish faster now. Before we do it on the live site, the manager has to give permission, and we should also take into account the extra server cost.

    After

    We enhanced the checkout page and eliminated two steps. The data demonstrates that people finish faster now. Before we go live, the manager has to authorize it, and we should also consider the extra server cost.

    44.8 Practice — Verb upgrades¶

    Exercise 1

    In a report you want to say that the figures proved the point. Which verb?

    • demonstrate
    • enhance
    • authorize
    • maintain

    Hint: Showing with evidence.

    Answer

    demonstrate

    Demonstrate = to prove by doing or by evidence. This is what fits the language of reports and presentations.

    Exercise 2

    The website already works well, but we want to make it even better.

    • enhance
    • fix
    • eliminate
    • demonstrate
    Answer

    enhance

    Making something good even better = enhance. If it were broken, it would be 'fix'.

    Exercise 3

    Which sentence is wrong?

    • We will eliminate the old sofa this weekend.
    • The new form eliminated most errors.
    • We eliminated two unnecessary steps.
    • This change eliminates the risk of double billing.

    Hint: In one of them, a big word is in the wrong place.

    Answer

    We will eliminate the old sofa this weekend.

    For an everyday object, use 'get rid of'. Eliminate goes with things like risk, error, step and cost.

    Exercise 4

    Only the finance head can ___ a refund above fifty thousand.

    Hint: Giving permission with authority.

    Answer

    authorize / authorise

    Authorize = to approve with official power. For permission at home use 'let' or 'allow'.

    Exercise 5

    Have you ___ changing the layout instead?

    Hint: A one-word form of take into account.

    Answer

    considered

    After consider, a verb takes -ing: considered changing, not 'considered to change'.

    Exercise 6

    One word here is unnecessary — find it.

    Please consider about my application carefully.

    Answer

    Wrong word: about.

    Corrected: Please consider my application carefully.

    No preposition comes after consider. The same mistake happens with 'discuss about'.

    Exercise 7

    The verb has been chosen wrongly — tap it.

    He demonstrated me his new bike yesterday.

    Answer

    Wrong word: demonstrated.

    Corrected: He showed me his new bike yesterday.

    Showing a bike is just 'show'. Demonstrate needs a process or evidence behind it.

    Exercise 8

    Match each slack phrase with its right one-word form.

    A B
    get rid of completely consider
    give permission officially demonstrate
    take into account eliminate
    show by proving authorize
    Answer

    get rid of completely — eliminate; give permission officially — authorize; take into account — consider; show by proving — demonstrate

    Remember the real meaning of all four upgrades — not the word, but the feeling behind it.

    Exercise 9

    Choose the right 'better'.

    A B
    to repair something broken enhance
    to make something weak better optimize
    to polish something good fix
    to make the best use of speed improve
    Answer

    to repair something broken — fix; to make something weak better — improve; to polish something good — enhance; to make the best use of speed — optimize

    The one Hindi idea of 'making better' becomes four different verbs in English. Choose by the condition of the thing.

    Exercise 10

    Build the upgraded line.

    Words: all / eliminated / almost / errors / process / The / new / data / entry

    Answer

    The new process eliminated almost all data entry errors

    This sentence is shorter and cleaner than 'got rid of almost all errors in data entry'.

    Exercise 11

    How many heavy verbs are fine in one paragraph?

    • One or two
    • One in every sentence
    • The more the better
    • At least five
    Answer

    One or two

    Too many heavy verbs make writing look memorised. Clear thinking has more impact than big words.

    Exercise 12

    Two teams had an old disagreement. A mediator brought them together again. Which verb?

    • reconcile
    • aggravate
    • utilize
    • exemplify

    Hint: Settling differences.

    Answer

    reconcile

    Reconcile = to end a disagreement and bring people together (reconcile their differences). Aggravate is the opposite — it makes things worse.

    Exercise 13

    You are talking to a friend: 'We utilize this app for groceries.' What would sound more natural?

    • We use this app for groceries.
    • We elucidate this app for groceries.
    • We safeguard this app for groceries.
    • We require this app for groceries.
    Answer

    We use this app for groceries.

    In daily life 'use' is the right verb. 'Utilize' is a technical-report word — in conversation it sounds heavy for no reason.

    Exercise 14

    Please ___ that the door is locked before you leave.

    Hint: A one-word form of make certain.

    Answer

    ensure / make sure

    Ensure = to make certain. Use ensure in instructions and email, 'make sure' in conversation — both are correct.

    Exercise 15

    One verb is far too heavy for this setting — find it.

    Hey, can you elucidate where the washroom is?

    Answer

    Wrong word: elucidate.

    Corrected: Hey, can you tell me where the washroom is?

    Elucidate is a lecture and research word. To ask for directions use 'tell me' or 'show me'.

    Exercise 16

    Match each normal phrase with its advanced verb.

    A B
    make worse safeguard
    make possible resolve
    keep safe aggravate
    find a solution enable
    Answer

    make worse — aggravate; make possible — enable; keep safe — safeguard; find a solution — resolve

    All four are useful in formal writing. In speech the simple forms are perfectly fine.

    Exercise 17

    Say this line. Stress the second part of 'authorized' — a-THO-rized.

    Answer

    Say: I am not authorized to share those figures

    This line works on support calls and in the office, and it does not point at you as being at fault — only at the rule.

    What to do next

    Open one of your old emails and mark only the verbs. Wherever two or three words together describe one action, try putting in a single verb. This fifteen-minute exercise teaches more than any vocabulary list.
