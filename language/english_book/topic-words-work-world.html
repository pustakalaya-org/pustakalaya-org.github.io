    Chapter 39: Topic words: work & world¶

    Work and the world — office, technology, environment, news

    You hear "stakeholder" and "deliverable" in meetings, "inflation" and "emissions" in the news, and you need the same words in an IELTS essay. This chapter gives you the words for four big topics — but not on their own. With each word you get its family (economy, economic, economical) and its partner (meet a deadline, reduce emissions). These two things are what turn B1 into B2–C1.

    39.1 One word, a whole family, fixed partners¶

    At B2–C1 level you are not only asked what a word means — you are expected to know whether it is a noun or an adjective, and which verb goes with it. When you learn one word, learn its four or five forms along with it.

    Three questions for every new word

    (1) What is its family? (2) Which verb or adjective goes with it? (3) What would be my own sentence? For prefixes and suffixes see One word, ten words, and for partners see Which word goes with which.

    39.2 Office and meetings¶

    Before a meeting there is an agenda, during it a discussion, afterwards minutes and action items, and then everyone's follow-up — until the deadline arrives. It is a cycle.

    Word Meaning Fixed partner
    agenda the list of topics for a meeting set / circulate the agenda
    minutes the written record of a meeting take / share the minutes
    deadline the last date or time meet / miss / extend a deadline
    follow up (v) / follow-up (n) to check or act again later follow up on the order
    stakeholder a person with an interest in the work key stakeholders
    deliverable something you must hand over agree on the deliverables
    priority what comes first a top priority · prioritise
    escalate to take a problem to a higher level escalate an issue
    delegate to give a task to someone else delegate a task to someone
    bandwidth (spoken) time and energy to take on work I don't have the bandwidth.
    • I'll circulate the agenda by Monday evening. = I'll send the agenda to everyone by Monday evening.
    • We're on track to meet the deadline. = We will finish in time. (on track = going as planned)
    • Could we extend the deadline to Friday? = Can we move the deadline later, to Friday?
    • I'll follow up with the vendor and update everyone. = I'll check with the supplier and tell everyone the result.
    • We need sign-off from all the key stakeholders. = All the important people must approve it. (sign-off = final approval)
    • If the client doesn't reply by Wednesday, let's escalate it. = If there's no answer by Wednesday, we'll raise it with senior people.

    follow up or follow-up?

    Verb = two words: I'll follow up tomorrow. Noun / adjective = with a hyphen: a follow-up email, a quick follow-up. The same rule applies to log in / login, set up / setup, back up / backup. An action = two words; the name of a thing = one word.

    ✗

    I will do the follow up and revert back.

    ✓

    I'll follow up and get back to you.

    follow up

    Indian English habits

    ✗

    We must finish before deadline.

    ✓

    We must meet the deadline. / We must finish before the deadline.

    deadline

    meet the deadline

    The lines to say in a meeting (interrupting, agreeing, taking on work) are in Interview and office — here we only cover words.

    39.3 Technology¶

    We say most tech words in English already, so the mistakes are not in the word but in the verb and preposition: log in to the app, download **from, upload to**.

    Word Meaning Partner
    download / upload copy to your device / copy to the internet upload a file to the drive
    log in / log out enter / leave an account log in to your account
    sign up to create a new account sign up for a free trial
    update a newer version install the latest update
    crash to stop working suddenly The app keeps crashing.
    freeze to get stuck and stop responding (hang) My laptop froze.
    back up to save an extra copy back up your photos
    bandwidth how much data the internet can carry low bandwidth
    outage a period when a service is down a network outage
    encrypted turned into a secret code, so it is safe end-to-end encrypted
    • I can't log in to the portal. It says my password has expired. = I'm unable to enter the portal because my password has expired.
    • The app has been crashing since the last update. = The app has kept stopping suddenly since the last update. (With "since" use has been + -ing; without "since": "It keeps crashing.")
    • Please back up your files before you install the update. = Save a copy of your files before updating.
    • The video call keeps breaking up. I think my bandwidth is low. = The call keeps cutting out — probably because my internet is slow.
    • There was a nationwide outage, so payments failed for two hours. = The service was down across the country, and payments didn't work for two hours.

    ✗

    My laptop got hanged.

    ✓

    My laptop froze. / My laptop hung.

    hang

    hung

    froze

    stopped responding

    ✗

    I will login and check.

    ✓

    I'll log in and check.

    log in

    login

    your login details

    the login page

    ✗

    Please send me the soft copy.

    ✓

    Please send me the file / a PDF.

    the file, a digital copy, a printed copy / a printout

    39.4 Environment — from pollution to renewables¶

    Environment words appear in essays, the news and interviews. The word-family rule helps most here: pollute (verb), pollution (noun), polluted (adj), pollutant (a substance that causes pollution).

    Word Meaning Partner
    pollution harmful dirt in air, water or soil air / water / noise pollution
    emissions smoke and gases released into the air cut / reduce emissions
    carbon footprint the carbon your activities release reduce your carbon footprint
    renewable never runs out (like sun or wind) renewable energy
    fossil fuels coal, petrol, gas depend on fossil fuels
    sustainable able to continue for a long time sustainable development
    deforestation cutting down forests tackle deforestation
    biodiversity the variety of living things loss of biodiversity
    climate change long-term change in the weather tackle / address climate change
    • Every winter, air pollution in Delhi reaches dangerous levels. = Each winter the air in Delhi becomes dangerously polluted.
    • The AQI is over 400 today, so schools are closed. = AQI stands for Air Quality Index. "over" means "more than".
    • India wants to get half its electricity from renewable sources. = Half of the power should come from sun, wind and similar sources.
    • Electric buses could cut the city's emissions significantly. = Electric buses could greatly reduce the city's pollution from vehicles.
    • Growth has to be sustainable, not just fast. = Growth must be able to last, not only be quick.

    The sustain family

    sustain (v) → sustainable (adj) → sustainability (n) → sustainably (adv) → unsustainable (the opposite). "The current pace of growth is unsustainable" — a strong line for an essay.

    ✗

    The pollution is very much in Delhi.

    ✓

    Pollution is very high in Delhi. / Delhi's air is badly polluted.

    high / low

    39.5 News and society — economy, elections, policy¶

    Whether you read a newspaper, listen to the news or write an essay, you will meet these words again and again. Learn the verbs of rising and falling with them: rise, fall, grow, drop, remain stable.

    Word Meaning Partner
    economy a country's system of money and trade the economy grew by 7%
    inflation the rate at which prices rise inflation rose / fell to 5%
    unemployment the state of having no job high youth unemployment
    budget the government's plan for income and spending present the budget
    election voting to choose leaders hold / win an election
    turnout how many people voted a record turnout
    policy an official plan or rule announce / introduce a policy
    survey a set of questions asked to many people conduct a survey · the survey found…
    subsidy government help that lowers a price a fuel subsidy
    • Inflation fell to four per cent last month. = The rate of price rise dropped to 4% last month. (fell to = reached that level; fell by = dropped by that amount)
    • The government has announced a new policy on electric vehicles. = The government has made a new EV policy public.
    • Voter turnout was the highest in thirty years. = More people voted than in any election in the last thirty years.
    • A recent survey found that 60 per cent of young people want to work abroad. = Survey found that — a fixed opening for essays and reports.
    • The new tax is controversial; small traders strongly oppose it. = People disagree about the new tax — small traders are strongly against it.

    ✗

    Unemployment is an economical problem.

    ✓

    Unemployment is an economic problem.

    economic

    economical

    ✗

    The survey says 60% people use UPI daily.

    ✓

    The survey found that 60% of people use UPI daily.

    of

    of

    of

    found / showed

    39.6 Headline English — small words, big meaning¶

    Newspaper headlines have their own grammar: articles are missing, the present simple = something that already happened, to + verb = something in the future, and short, punchy words. Once you understand these, the news becomes much easier.

    Headline Full meaning Trick
    RBI holds rates steady The RBI has kept interest rates unchanged. present = it has happened
    Govt to launch EV policy The government is going to launch a policy. to = about to happen
    Floods hit Assam Floods have affected Assam. hit = affected
    Minister quits A minister has resigned. quit = resign
    Prices soar Prices have risen sharply. soar = rise very fast
    Talks fail Negotiations have failed. talks = negotiations
    • RBI holds rates steady as inflation eases. = Prices are rising more slowly, so the RBI did not change interest rates. (ease = become less severe)
    • Govt to boost spending on rural roads. = The government will spend more on village roads. (boost = increase)
    • Heatwave grips north India; schools shut. = Extreme heat has taken hold of north India, and schools are closed. (grip = take a strong hold)

    Small headline words

    bid = an attempt · curb = limit or control · slam = criticise harshly · probe = investigation · row = a public argument · back = support · axe = cancel or cut. To practise understanding the news, see Real-World English.

    39.7 B2–C1 words — one word, three partners¶

    These ten words are useful in meetings, news and essays alike. Learn each one together with its partner and its family; only then will it become part of your own English. (For bigger verbs, also see Verb upgrades and Advanced words.)

    Word (level) Meaning Partner · family
    significant (B2) large enough to matter a significant increase · significantly
    implement (B2) to put a plan into action implement a plan · implementation
    allocate (B2) to set aside (money, time) for a purpose allocate funds · allocation
    mitigate (C1) to make something less harmful mitigate the risk · mitigation
    unprecedented (C1) never having happened before an unprecedented rise
    substantial (C1) large and solid a substantial amount · substantially
    transparent (B2) open and clear, nothing hidden a transparent process · transparency
    accountable (C1) responsible for your actions hold someone accountable · accountability
    viable (C1) able to work in practice a viable option · viability
    scalable (C1) able to be made bigger a scalable solution · scale up
    • We need a backup supplier to mitigate the risk of delays. = A second supplier will reduce the danger of late deliveries.
    • The city has allocated ₹200 crore to clean the river. = The city has set aside ₹200 crore for cleaning the river.
    • Leaders must be held accountable for their promises. = Leaders should be made answerable for what they promised.
    • Solar power is now a viable option for most homes. = Solar power can now realistically work for most homes.
    • The pandemic caused an unprecedented shift to online work. = The pandemic brought a move to online work that had never happened before.

    One paragraph — all four topics together

    • At this week's meeting, the key stakeholders agreed to implement the new travel policy. Most meetings will now be online, which should cut emissions and save a substantial amount of money. A staff survey found that 70 per cent of employees support the change. = Office (stakeholders, implement), environment (cut emissions), news (survey found) — and one C1 word (substantial).

    39.8 Practice — Work and the world¶

    Exercise 1

    We're on track to ___ the deadline.

    • meet
    • do
    • reach
    • make

    Hint: The strongest partner of deadline.

    Answer

    meet

    meet / miss / extend a deadline.

    Exercise 2

    I'll ___ up with the client tomorrow and let you know.

    Hint: To check again later — a two-word verb.

    Answer

    follow

    follow up (verb) — follow-up (noun): a follow-up email.

    Exercise 3

    Tap the wrong word.

    My laptop got hanged during the meeting.

    Answer

    Wrong word: hanged.

    Corrected: My laptop froze during the meeting.

    For computers use hung / froze. 'Hanged' is only for execution.

    Exercise 4

    Which one is correct?

    • I'll log in and check.
    • I'll login and check.
    • I'll do login and check.
    • I'll log-in and check.
    Answer

    I'll log in and check.

    The verb is log in (two words). login is the noun: login details.

    Exercise 5

    Match each word to its meaning.

    A B
    emissions how many people voted
    renewable government help that lowers a price
    turnout smoke and gases released into the air
    subsidy never runs out
    Answer

    emissions — smoke and gases released into the air; renewable — never runs out; turnout — how many people voted; subsidy — government help that lowers a price

    emissions, renewable — environment; turnout, subsidy — words from the news.

    Exercise 6

    One word is wrong.

    Unemployment is a serious economical problem.

    Answer

    Wrong word: economical.

    Corrected: Unemployment is a serious economic problem.

    economic = of the economy; economical = cheap to run.

    Exercise 7

    A recent survey found that 60 per cent ___ young people want to work abroad.

    Hint: The small word after a percentage.

    Answer

    of

    60 per cent of people, half of the class.

    Exercise 8

    Headline: 'Govt to launch EV policy'. What does it mean?

    • The government is going to launch it.
    • The government has launched it.
    • The government launched it last year.
    • The government refused to launch it.
    Answer

    The government is going to launch it.

    In a headline, 'to + verb' = the future.

    Exercise 9

    Electric buses could ___ the city's emissions.

    • cut
    • do
    • make
    • low
    Answer

    cut

    cut / reduce emissions — a fixed partner.

    Exercise 10

    What happened to inflation?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Here is the business news. Inflation fell to four point two per cent in September, the lowest level this year. Economists say lower food prices were the main reason. The central bank is expected to keep interest rates unchanged next week.

    • It fell
    • It rose
    • It stayed the same
    • It doubled
    Answer

    It fell

    'Inflation fell to four point two per cent' — it dropped to 4.2%.

    Exercise 11

    According to economists, what was the main reason?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Here is the business news. Inflation fell to four point two per cent in September, the lowest level this year. Economists say lower food prices were the main reason. The central bank is expected to keep interest rates unchanged next week.

    • Lower food prices
    • Higher interest rates
    • Cheaper petrol
    • A new tax
    Answer

    Lower food prices

    'lower food prices were the main reason' — food became cheaper.

    Exercise 12

    Who will send the minutes?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Okay, let's wrap up. Priya will send the minutes by this evening. Arjun, can you follow up with the client about the budget? And just a reminder, the deadline for the first draft is Thursday.

    • Priya
    • Arjun
    • The client
    • Nobody
    Answer

    Priya

    'Priya will send the minutes by this evening.'

    Exercise 13

    When is the deadline for the first draft?

    Read this aloud or have someone read it to you:

    Okay, let's wrap up. Priya will send the minutes by this evening. Arjun, can you follow up with the client about the budget? And just a reminder, the deadline for the first draft is Thursday.

    • Thursday
    • This evening
    • Tuesday
    • Next week
    Answer

    Thursday

    'the deadline for the first draft is Thursday.'

    Exercise 14

    Listen and write.

    Dictation. Have someone read the sentence aloud, then write it down.

    Answer

    The server crashed last night, so please back up your files.

    crash = to stop working suddenly; back up (verb) is two words.

    Exercise 15

    What does 'mitigate the risk' mean?

    • make the danger less harmful
    • make the danger bigger
    • ignore the danger
    • measure the danger
    Answer

    make the danger less harmful

    mitigate (C1) = to make less harmful; noun: mitigation.

    Exercise 16

    Put the words in order.

    Words: very / Pollution / Delhi / winter / in / high / is / this

    Answer

    Pollution is very high in Delhi this winter

    Pollution is 'high', not 'very much'.

    Exercise 17

    Say this line in a meeting.

    Answer

    Say: Could we extend the deadline to Friday

    extend a deadline — with a polite 'Could we…?'.
