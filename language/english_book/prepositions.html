    Chapter 12: Prepositions¶

    Preposition — the words that show relationships

    A preposition is a small word that shows the relationship between one thing and another — where it is, when it is, how it happens. In Hindi this job is done by "me, par, se, tak, ke paas", and that is where the biggest confusion starts: one Hindi word can turn into three different prepositions in English.

    In one line

    A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship with another word in the sentence. A word that comes before a noun and shows how it is related to something else.

    • The book is on the table. = The book is resting on top of the table.
    • She went to school. = School was her destination.
    • The cat is under the chair. = The cat is below the chair.

    12.1 Prepositions of place — in one picture¶

    The easiest trick is to remember these not as words but as a picture. Look at the picture below carefully once — and you won't be confused again.

    From big to small

    When giving a place, remember the order — in → on → at:
    in the country → in the city → on the street → at the address. The bigger the place, the more likely "in"; the more exact the point, the more likely "at".

    12.2 Prepositions of time — at, on, in¶

    The same three words are used for time, and the rule is the same: from specific to big.

    Preposition When Examples
    at Exact time at 6 o'clock · at noon · at midnight · at night
    on Day / date on Monday · on 15 August · on my birthday · on Diwali
    in Month, year, season, longer periods in July · in 2026 · in winter · in the morning

    All three in one sentence: The meeting is at 4 pm on Monday in July.

    Other time prepositions¶

    Word Meaning Example
    before earlier than before lunch
    after later than after school
    during throughout a period during summer
    since from a starting point since Monday
    for how long (duration) for two hours
    until / till up to a point in time until evening

    since vs for — this is caught very often

    • Wrong: I am working here since three years.
    • Right: I have been working here for three years.
    • Why: for = how long (duration) → for three years. since = starting from when (a point) → since 2023.

    12.3 The eight types of preposition¶

    Besides place and time, prepositions show other relationships too. Each type answers one question:

    Type Question Common words Example
    1. Place Where? in, on, at, under, near, behind, beside, between The pen is in the box.
    2. Time When? at, on, in, before, after, since, for, during I wake up at six.
    3. Direction / Movement Where to? to, into, onto, from, through, across, towards, along She walked into the room.
    4. Manner / Instrument How? With what? by, with, in, on He wrote it with a pen. · We went by bus.
    5. Agent Who did it (in the passive)? by The poem was written by Tagore.
    6. Purpose What for? for, to He went for vegetables.
    7. Possession Whose? Who has it? of, with The colour of the sky is blue. · a girl with long hair
    8. Source Where from? from, of, out of We get milk from cows.

    Eight direction words — which way are we going¶

    Prepositions of direction go with verbs of moving, going and coming (go, walk, run, jump, come). Note — here the thing is not still; it is moving.

    Word Meaning Example
    to as far as the destination (towards it, and arriving) She goes to school.
    into from outside to inside He jumped into the pool.
    onto from below to on top, onto a surface The cat climbed onto the roof.
    from the starting point I came from Patna.
    through passing inside something, from one end to the other The train went through a tunnel.
    across from one side to the other, over the surface They swam across the river.
    towards in the direction of (not necessarily arriving) The dog ran towards me.
    along following the length of something, beside it We walked along the beach.
    • The children ran towards the gate. = They ran in the direction of the gate — we don't know if they got there.
    • There are shops along the road. = The shops stand all the way down the length of the road.

    to vs towards — a small difference

    to = arriving at the destination — He walked to the station. (he got there)
    towards = only the direction — He walked towards the station. (he was heading that way) British English uses "towards", American English often uses "toward" — both are correct.

    across vs through vs along

    across = over a surface, from one side to the other — across the road
    through = passing inside something — through the forest
    along = following the length, not crossing — along the river

    12.4 Important differences — five pairs that always cause confusion¶

    in vs into

    in = position (already inside) — He is in the room.
    into = movement (from outside to inside) — He walked into the room.

    between vs among

    between = two things — The pen is between Ram and Shyam.
    among = more than two — He was sitting among his friends.

    beside vs besides

    beside = next to — Sit beside me.
    besides = in addition to — Besides English, I know Hindi.

    on vs onto

    on = position — The book is on the table.
    onto = movement — The cat jumped onto the table.

    by vs with

    by = the doer (agent) or the means (bus, train, email) — The letter was written by Ravi. · I came by train.
    with = a tool held in the hand — He cut the cake with a knife. Trick: a person or a means of travel → by; a tool in your hand → with. "Written by a pen" is wrong — it is "written with a pen".

    12.5 The mistakes we really make¶

    The most common preposition mistakes

    • Wrong: Let us discuss about the plan.
    • Right: Let us discuss the plan.

    • Why: No preposition comes after "discuss". Same with enter the room, not "enter into".

    • Wrong: He is good in English.

    • Right: He is good at English.

    • Why: Being skilled at something = good at. Remember: good at maths, good at cricket.

    • Wrong: I will reach at Delhi tomorrow.

    • Right: I will reach Delhi tomorrow.

    • Why: "Reach" is followed directly by the place. But "arrive" takes a preposition: arrive in Delhi.

    • Wrong: She is married with a doctor.

    • Right: She is married to a doctor.

    • Why: Marriage is always "married to".

    • Wrong: I am afraid from dogs.

    • Right: I am afraid of dogs.

    • Why: Fear is always "afraid of".

    • Wrong: Please explain me this.

    • Right: Please explain this to me.
    • Why: After "explain" comes the thing first, then "to" + the person.

    12.6 An easy way to remember¶

    Before choosing a preposition, ask yourself one question — the answer tells you which box to take the word from.

    The most common prepositions — learn these first

    of · in · to · for · with · on · at · from · by · about · into · through · over · under · after · before · between · among · during · without · against · around · near · behind · across · along · towards · since · until · like The first ten (of to about) appear on almost every page of English — get those right and you've won half the battle.

    12.7 Practice — Prepositions¶

    Exercise 1

    The class starts ___ 9 am.

    • at
    • on
    • in
    • for

    Hint: Which one is for an exact time?

    Answer

    at

    Use 'at' with an exact time — at 9 am, at noon, at midnight.

    Exercise 2

    My birthday is ___ December.

    • at
    • on
    • in
    • to
    Answer

    in

    Use 'in' with months — in December, in 2026, in winter.

    Exercise 3

    The medical store is ___ the bank and the school.

    • between
    • among
    • near
    • beside

    Hint: There are two things.

    Answer

    between

    In the middle of two things = between. With more than two it would be 'among'.

    Exercise 4

    There is one mistake in this sentence — tap that word.

    Let us discuss about the budget tomorrow.

    Answer

    Wrong word: about.

    Corrected: Let us discuss the budget tomorrow.

    'Discuss' isn't followed by 'about' — the thing comes straight after it.

    Exercise 5

    Find the wrong word.

    She is very good in mathematics.

    Answer

    Wrong word: in.

    Corrected: She is very good at mathematics.

    Being skilled in a subject = good at.

    Exercise 6

    I have been waiting ___ two hours.

    Hint: Is it a duration or a point in time?

    Answer

    for

    How long = for. If it were 'since 3 o'clock', that would be a point in time.

    Exercise 7

    The keys are ___ the table.

    Hint: They are lying on top of the table.

    Answer

    on

    On a surface = on.

    Exercise 8

    The dog ran ___ me, but stopped halfway.

    • towards
    • into
    • onto
    • at

    Hint: You need the direction, not arriving.

    Answer

    towards

    'towards' shows only the direction — heading that way, without necessarily arriving. 'to' would suggest it got there.

    Exercise 9

    We took a long walk ___ the beach. (following its edge)

    Hint: Walking beside something long.

    Answer

    along

    Walking beside something long (a road, river, beach) = along. To cross it, it would be 'across'.

    Exercise 10

    He opened the lock ___ a hairpin.

    • with
    • by
    • from
    • of

    Hint: A tool held in the hand.

    Answer

    with

    Use 'with' for a tool (instrument) — with a key, with a knife. 'by' is for the doer (agent) or a means of travel: by Ravi, by bus.

    Exercise 11

    Which type of preposition answers the question 'Where to?'

    • Direction / Movement
    • Place
    • Time
    • Purpose

    Hint: Look at the grid you learned.

    Answer

    Direction / Movement

    Where? = place, When? = time, Where to? = direction (to, into, through, across, towards, along), How? = manner, Why? = purpose.

    Exercise 12

    Match each preposition to its meaning.

    A B
    into right next to
    among in addition to
    beside moving from outside to inside
    besides in the middle of more than two
    Answer

    into — moving from outside to inside; among — in the middle of more than two; beside — right next to; besides — in addition to

    into = movement, among = between more than two, beside = next to, besides = in addition to.

    Exercise 13

    Put the words in the right order.

    Words: stop / shop / The / is / to / bus / the / next

    Answer

    The shop is next to the bus stop

    Subject + verb + prepositional phrase — that is the straightforward English order.

    Exercise 14

    Now say this line aloud — join 'next to' together.

    Answer

    Say: The shop is next to the bus stop

    When spoken, 'next to' sounds almost like one word — 'nekstu'.

    What to do next

    Prepositions come from listening, not from memorising. From today, whenever you hear English, notice where the small words fall. You'll see the difference in a week.
