    Glossary¶

    Terms are Sanskrit unless marked (Ta. = Tamil, Mal. = Malayalam, Pe. = Persian, Hi. = Hindi/Hindustani, Ma. = Marathi, Pu. = Punjabi, Mn. = Meitei/Manipuri, Ka. = Kannada).

    Term Meaning
    akhāṛā (Hi.) Wrestling ground and gymnasium; also an armed division of an ascetic order
    akṣauhiṇī In the Mahābhārata, the largest army unit (21,870 chariots and elephants, 65,610 horse, 109,350 foot)
    ālīḍha A wide lunging stance, in the Agni Purāṇa with the right leg held rigid; also a posture of iconography
    amukta A weapon held in the hand and not released, e.g. the sword (in the Agni Purāṇa’s classes of fighting)
    āpaddharma Law or duty in time of distress, when ordinary rules may be relaxed
    ari The enemy; in the circle of kings, the neighbouring king
    Arthaśāstra Treatise on statecraft attributed to Kauṭilya; composite, c. 50 BCE–300 CE
    astra A missile weapon; in the epics, a divine weapon released by spell
    aṭavī Forest people; forest troops recruited by kings
    āyudhapūjā Worship of weapons and tools on the ninth day of Navarātri
    bāgh nakh (Hi.) “Tiger claws”, a concealed clawed weapon worn in the palm
    bārgīr (Ma./Pe.) Trooper riding a horse supplied by the state or his commander
    batta (Hi.) Extra allowance paid to Company troops for field service or service outside the Company’s territory
    bhṛta Hired or mercenary troops
    brahmāstra The most powerful divine weapon of the epics
    caturaṅga “Four-limbed”: the army of chariots, elephants, cavalry and infantry; the ancestor of chess
    cit (Hi.) In wrestling, a fall with both shoulders on the ground
    daṇḍa The rod; coercive power, punishment; the army; also the “staff” battle array
    daṇḍanīti The science of government and punishment
    daṅgal (Hi.) A wrestling tournament
    dhāl (Hi.) Round shield
    dhanurveda “Knowledge of the bow”; the science of arms
    dhanus Bow
    dharmavijaya Righteous conquest, in which the defeated ruler is reinstated as tributary
    dharmayuddha Righteous war; war fought according to rules
    durga Fort
    fauj-i-āʾīn (Pe.) “The army of rules”: the regular army of the Sikh kingdom
    firangī (Hi.) Straight sword, often with a European blade in an Indian hilt
    fitna (Pe.) “Sedition”; the winning over of an opponent’s followers as a technique of politics
    gadā Mace
    gaja, hastin Elephant
    ganimī kāvā (Ma.) The Maratha style of mobile, irregular warfare
    gatkā (Pu.) Stick-fencing; the Sikh martial art
    gurukkaḷ (Mal.) Master of a kaḷari
    iqṭā (Pe.) Assignment of revenue to a military commander in the Delhi Sultanate
    jāgīr (Pe.) Assignment of land revenue to a Mughal officer in lieu of salary
    jaidād (Pe.) Revenue assignment for the support of troops, e.g. that of the Shinde brigades in the Doab
    jamādār (Pe./Hi.) Leader of a band of soldiers; later a junior Indian officer
    jauhar (Hi.) Collective self-immolation of women in a besieged fort
    kaḷari (Mal.) Training ground of kaḷarippayaṟṟu
    kaḷarippayaṟṟu (Mal.) The martial art of Kerala
    kampū (Hi.) A brigade of regular troops in Maratha service (usually derived from Portuguese campo, “camp”; the derivation is not certain)
    kaṭār (Hi.) Push-dagger with a transverse grip
    kavaca Armour
    khaḍga Sword
    khaṇḍā (Hi.) Straight broad double-edged sword
    kṣatriya Member of the warrior and ruling class of the varṇa order
    kūṭayuddha Concealed war: surprise, ambush and stratagem
    kushūn (Pe.) A brigade of Tipu Sultan’s army
    kuśtī (Pe./Hi.) Wrestling
    malla Wrestler
    mallakhamb (Ma.) Gymnastics on a vertical wooden pole or rope
    maṇḍala Circle; the circle of kings in foreign policy; a battle array
    manṣab (Pe.) Rank in the Mughal service, expressed in zāt and sawār numbers
    marma Vital point of the body; marmmam (Mal.), varmam (Ta.)
    misl (Pu.) A confederacy of Sikh fighting bands in the eighteenth century
    mitra Ally
    mukta A weapon that is thrown or released; the Agni Purāṇa distinguishes weapons released by a machine such as the bow (yantramukta) from those released by the hand (pāṇimukta)
    naukar, naukarī (Pe./Hi.) Servant; service, especially military employment
    nāyaka Military chief holding territory from the king (Vijayanagara)
    paik Footsoldier; in Assam, a man liable to service under the Ahom state
    palṭan (Hi.) A battalion of regular infantry (usually traced to English “battalion”; “platoon” has also been proposed)
    pañjī In the warfare of the North-East, a sharpened bamboo stake planted in paths and approaches
    paṭā (Ma./Hi.) Gauntlet sword
    patti, padāti Infantry; foot-soldier
    pindārī (Hi.) Irregular horseman living by plunder, attached to or independent of the Maratha armies
    prakāśayuddha Open war
    puṟam (Ta.) “Exterior”: classical Tamil poetry of war and public life
    pūrbiyā (Hi.) “Easterner”: soldier from Awadh, Banaras or Bhojpur
    rākhī (Pu.) “Protection”: the share of produce paid by villages to a Sikh misl
    ratha Chariot
    sāma, dāna, bheda, daṇḍa The four means (upāya): conciliation, gifts, dissension and force
    ṣāḍguṇya The six measures of foreign policy
    saptāṅga The seven constituents of the state
    sarkhel (Ma.) Admiral of the Maratha fleet
    senāpati Commander of the army
    śastra A weapon held in the hand
    silambam (Ta.) The staff-fighting art of Tamil Nadu
    śiledār (Ma.) Trooper who owned his horse and arms and hired them out with his service
    skandhāvāra Military camp
    sūta Charioteer
    talwār (Hi.) Curved sabre with a disc pommel
    thang-ta (Mn.) “Sword and spear”; the armed martial art of the Meitei
    tūṣṇīṃyuddha “Silent war”: covert action and assassination by secret agents
    uṟumi (Mal.) Flexible sword of Kerala
    vajramuṣṭi Combat with a knuckle-weapon; the weapon itself
    vijigīṣu “Would-be conqueror”; the king at the centre of the circle of kings
    vīrakal (Ka.) Hero-stone, memorial to a warrior
    vīrāṅganā Heroic woman; woman warrior
    vyāyām (Hi.) Exercise; physical culture
    vyūha Battle array
    wootz Indian crucible steel (from Kannada and Telugu ukku)
    yantra Machine, including machines of war
