    A Note on Texts, Translations and Abbreviations¶

    Critical Edition and vulgate. Both epics exist in two forms that a reader will meet. The Critical Edition (CE) is the text reconstructed by modern editors from manuscripts of all regions: for the Mahābhārata, the Poona edition of the Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI, 1933–1966), with the Harivaṃśa edited separately (1969–1971); for the Rāmāyaṇa, the Baroda edition (1960–1975). The vulgate is the text printed before the critical editions and still most widely read: for the Mahābhārata, the northern text of Nīlakaṇṭha (late seventeenth century), found in the Calcutta and Bombay editions, the Gita Press edition and Ganguli’s translation; for the Rāmāyaṇa, the southern text printed with the commentaries of Govindarāja and others. How the critical editions were made, and the debate over them, are described in Sections 2.3 and 4.8.

    References. Mahābhārata references give book (parvan), chapter (adhyāya) and verse in the CE, so that 12.67 is chapter 67 of the twelfth book, the Śānti Parva; the eighteen books are numbered as in Table 4.1. Rāmāyaṇa references give book (kāṇḍa), canto (sarga) and verse in the CE. Where a passage exists only in the vulgate, or a vulgate number is given, this is said; chapter numbers in the two texts differ, sometimes widely. The Bhagavad Gītā is cited by chapter and verse, which are the same in all editions. Purāṇas are cited by book, chapter and verse of the usual printed editions, whose numbering varies.

    Abbreviations. BORI, Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute; CE, Critical Edition; c., circa; r., reigned; trad., traditional. Within the text the epics are named in full; “Mbh.” and “Rām.” appear only in tables.

    Transliteration. Sanskrit, Pali and Prakrit are given in the International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration (IAST), with ṃ for the anusvāra; Tamil follows the transliteration of the Tamil Lexicon of the University of Madras (so ḻ, ṟ, ṉ, as in Āḻvār). Modern places are given in their usual modern spelling (Ayodhya, Varanasi) where the modern town is meant, and with diacritics where the place of the text is meant (Ayodhyā). Persian, Hindi and Punjabi names are given in simplified form. Translations of passages are the author’s unless a translation is cited; they aim at the sense and should be checked against the editions below.

    Dates. The mainstream academic date or range is given first, and traditional dates are labelled as traditional (see Table 1.5).

    A short guide to translations. For the Mahābhārata, the only complete English translation of the critical text is Bibek Debroy’s, in ten volumes (Debroy 2010–2014). The Chicago translation begun by J. A. B. van Buitenen covers Books 1–5 (van Buitenen 1973–1978) and was continued by James Fitzgerald with Books 11 and the first part of 12 (Fitzgerald 2004); its introductions are the best scholarly guide to the books they cover. K. M. Ganguli’s prose translation of the vulgate (1883–1896) is complete, freely available and still useful, but it translates a different text and its English is dated (Ganguli 1883–1896). A reader who wants a single volume should begin with John D. Smith’s abridgement of the critical text, which translates the main narrative and summarises the rest (Smith 2009). For the Harivaṃśa there is Simon Brodbeck’s translation of the critical text (Brodbeck 2019). For the Bhagavad Gītā, van Buitenen prints the CE text with a facing translation and Franklin Edgerton gives a deliberately literal version (van Buitenen 1981; Edgerton 1944). For the Rāmāyaṇa, the annotated Princeton translation of the critical text by Robert Goldman and his collaborators is the standard (Goldman 1984–2017); the notes record the major vulgate passages that the CE excludes. For the Purāṇas, Dimmitt and van Buitenen’s reader gives a selection of myths in good translation (Dimmitt and Buitenen 1978); Edwin Bryant has translated the tenth book of the Bhāgavata (Bryant 2003) and Thomas Coburn the Devī Māhātmya (Coburn 1991). Translations of the Tamil, Khmer and other versions are cited where those versions are discussed.
