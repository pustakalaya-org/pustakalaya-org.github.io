    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate, and many are disputed; where scholarly opinion varies, a range is given. Traditional dates are not used.

    Date Event, work or person
    c. 7500–9000 years ago Drilled teeth at Neolithic Mehrgarh (Baluchistan)
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core of the Ṛgveda; hymns to the Aśvins and to the plants
    c. 1200–1000 BCE Main collection of the Atharvaveda, with its healing charms
    c. 480–400 BCE The Buddha (short chronology); medical rules of the Buddhist monastic code take shape in the following centuries
    c. 268–232 BCE Reign of Aśoka; Rock Edict II on medical provision for humans and animals
    c. 100 BCE – 200 CE Caraka’s redaction of the teaching attributed to Agniveśa (Meulenbeld’s range)
    last centuries BCE – c. 500 CE Composition and revision of the Suśruta Saṃhitā; addition of the Uttaratantra
    c. 4th–5th c. CE Dṛḍhabala completes the Caraka Saṃhitā
    c. 5th c. CE Tirukkuṟaḷ, with a chapter on medicine (date disputed)
    late 5th–6th c. CE The Bower Manuscript written in Central Asia
    c. 600 CE Vāgbhaṭa, Aṣṭāṅgasaṃgraha and Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya
    c. 7th c. CE Kāśyapa Saṃhitā in its extant form; Ravigupta, Siddhasāra
    c. 700 CE Mādhavakara, Rogaviniścaya (Mādhavanidāna)
    late 8th–9th c.  Indian medical works translated into Arabic in Abbasid Baghdad
    c. 850 al-Ṭabarī, Firdaws al-ḥikma, with a summary of Indian medicine
    878 Date of the oldest Nepalese manuscript of the Suśruta Saṃhitā
    9th–13th c.  Major commentators: Jejjaṭa, Candranandana, Gayadāsa, Cakrapāṇidatta (11th c.), Ḍalhaṇa (12th c.), Aruṇadatta and Hemādri (13th c.)
    c. 10th–12th c.  Earliest alchemical works: Rasahṛdayatantra, Rasārṇava
    11th c.  Tibetan translation of the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya; Unani physicians arrive with the Ghaznavids
    12th c.  Compilation of the Tibetan Four Tantras (rGyud bzhi)
    c. 13th–14th c.  Rasaratnasamuccaya; Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā, with early descriptions of pulse diagnosis
    15th c.  Northern and Southern schools of Tibetan medicine
    1512 Miyān Bhuwa’s Maʿdan al-Shifāʾ-i Sikandar-Shāhī: Sanskrit medicine in Persian
    16th c.  Bhāvamiśra, Bhāvaprakāśa, with the first Sanskrit account of syphilis
    1563 Garcia da Orta, Colóquios dos simples e drogas da Índia, Goa
    1628, 1640s Nūr al-Dīn Shīrāzī, Alfāẓ al-adwiya and Ṭibb-i Dārā Shikōhī
    1678–1693 Hortus Malabaricus, based on the knowledge of Itty Achuden and others
    c. 17th–18th c.  Yogaratnākara, with the eightfold examination of the patient
    1688–1696 Desi Sangye Gyatso’s Blue Beryl; medical college at Chakpori, Lhasa
    1794 The “Indian” nose operation near Poona reported in the Gentleman’s Magazine
    1817 Cholera epidemic in lower Bengal begins the first cholera pandemic
    1822–1835 Native Medical Institution, Calcutta; closed in 1835
    1835–1836 First printed Suśruta Saṃhitā; Madhusudan Gupta’s dissection (1836)
    1884, 1902 Dabur (Calcutta) and the Arya Vaidya Sala (Kottakkal) founded
    1893–1912 Hoernle’s edition of the Bower Manuscript
    1896–1897 Plague reaches Bombay; Epidemic Diseases Act
    1902–1909 P. C. Ray, A History of Hindu Chemistry
    1906 Hamdard founded in Delhi by Ḥakīm Ḥāfiẓ ʿAbd al-Majīd
    1907 All-India Ayurvedic Congress founded; Hoernle, Osteology
    1913 Gaṇanāth Sen begins publishing the Pratyakṣaśārīra
    1921–1923 Ayurvedic and Unani Tibbia College, Delhi (1921); Usman Committee
    1931, 1949 Rauvolfia: Siddiqui isolates ajmaline; Sen and Bose; Vakil’s study of hypertension
    1938 Kāśyapa Saṃhitā published from a single Nepalese manuscript
    1948 Report of the Chopra Committee on the Indian systems of medicine
    1961 Men-Tsee-Khang refounded at Dharamsala
    1970 Indian Medicine Central Council Act
    1978 Declaration of Alma-Ata
    2001 Traditional Knowledge Digital Library
    2004, 2008 Saper and colleagues report heavy metals in Ayurvedic products
    2010, 2014 Sowa-Rigpa recognised as an Indian system of medicine; Ministry of AYUSH
    2022 WHO Global Centre for Traditional Medicine, Jamnagar
