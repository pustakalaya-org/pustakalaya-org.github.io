    Plants of the Classical Materia Medica¶

    This appendix gives the usual modern identifications of plants frequently named in the classical and medieval medical texts. It is meant to help in reading the texts and the scholarly literature. It is not a guide to the use of any plant. Many identifications are uncertain or disputed, and the same Sanskrit name may refer to different plants in different regions; where a plant is notably toxic, this is indicated. The poisonous plants of the pharmacopoeia are discussed in Chapter 6.

    Sanskrit name Usual identification English name; notes
    agnimantha Premna species one of the ten roots (daśamūla)
    ahiphena Papaver somniferum opium poppy; narcotic, introduced in the medieval period
    āmalakī Phyllanthus emblica emblic myrobalan, Indian gooseberry
    apāmārga Achyranthes aspera prickly chaff-flower
    āragvadha Cassia fistula Indian laburnum; purgative
    arjuna Terminalia arjuna arjun tree
    arka Calotropis species milkweed; latex toxic
    aśoka Saraca asoca ashoka tree
    aśvagandhā Withania somnifera winter cherry
    aśvattha Ficus religiosa sacred fig
    ativiṣā Aconitum heterophyllum Indian atees; a less toxic aconite, still to be treated with caution
    balā Sida cordifolia country mallow; contains ephedrine-like alkaloids
    bhallātaka Semecarpus anacardium marking nut; severely irritant and toxic
    bhaṅgā Cannabis sativa hemp; intoxicant
    bhṛṅgarāja Eclipta prostrata false daisy
    bibhītakī Terminalia bellirica belleric myrobalan
    bilva Aegle marmelos bael; one of the ten roots

    haridrā Curcuma longa turmeric

    jīraka Cuminum cyminum cumin

    kālamegha Andrographis paniculata green chiretta; a later addition
    kamala Nelumbo nucifera lotus
    kāñcanāra Bauhinia variegata mountain ebony
    kaṇṭakārī Solanum virginianum yellow-berried nightshade; one of the ten roots
    kapikacchū Mucuna pruriens cowhage; pods cause intense itching
    karavīra Nerium oleander oleander; highly poisonous
