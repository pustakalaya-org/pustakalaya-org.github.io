    Chronology¶

    The dates below are approximate. Many, especially those of the Sanskrit rasa texts, are disputed; where opinion varies, a broad range is given. Dates for archaeological sites rest on radiocarbon determinations whose calibration and context are often debated.

    Date Event, site or text
    c. 7000–5000 BCE Early farming villages at Mehrgarh (Baluchistan); early pottery; copper beads
    c. 2600–1900 BCE Mature Harappan (Indus) civilisation: copper and bronze, lost-wax casting, faience, stoneware bangles, etched and long carnelian beads
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core of the Ṛgveda; ayas (metal, probably copper/bronze) and hiraṇya (gold)
    2nd millennium BCE Early iron in the central Ganga plain and Vindhyas (Malhar, Raja Nal-ka-tila; dates of c. 1800–1500 BCE claimed and debated) and in the Deccan (Hallur)
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts; “black metal” (kṛṣṇāyas, iron) named
    c. 1200–600 BCE Painted Grey Ware; early glass in the Ganga plain
    c. 700–200 BCE Northern Black Polished Ware
    c. 5th–2nd c. BCE Punch-marked silver coinage
    1st millennium BCE Mining of lead–zinc–silver ores at Zawar and Rajpura–Dariba (Rajasthan)
    c. 3rd c. BCE onwards Crucible steel in south India (Kodumanal and other sites); in Sri Lanka from the 1st millennium CE
    c. 4th c. BCE–1st c. CE Earliest radiocarbon-dated iron-smelting slag in the Khasi Hills, Meghalaya
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE Arthaśāstra in its present form (traditionally attributed to Kauṭilya, c. 300 BCE)
    1st c. CE Periplus of the Erythraean Sea: Indian iron, steel and indigo in Indian Ocean trade; Arikamedu bead-making
    c. 400 CE Iron pillar now at Mehrauli, Delhi, inscribed for a king “Candra” (probably Candragupta II)
    c. 6th c. CE Varāhamihira’s Bṛhatsaṃhitā (gems, vajralepa)
    7th c. CE Xuanzang records legends of Nāgārjuna’s elixir
    c. 9th–10th c. CE Earliest dated zinc-distillation retorts at Zawar
    c. 9th–13th c. CE Chola bronzes
    987 Ibn al-Nadīm’s Fihrist lists Indian works on medicine and poisons available in Arabic
    c. 10th–11th c. CE Rasahṛdayatantra of Govinda; Rasendramaṅgala attributed to Nāgārjuna (date uncertain, perhaps 8th–12th c.)
    c. 1030 CE Al-Bīrūnī describes Indian rasāyana and a Nāgārjuna of Daihak
    1040s Al-Bīrūnī’s book on precious stones and metals (Kitāb al-jamāhir)
    c. 11th–12th c. CE Rasārṇava; Dhar iron pillar
    c. 1130 Mānasollāsa of the Cālukya king Someśvara III, with its account of lost-wax casting (madhūcchiṣṭa-vidhāna)
    12th–13th c. CE Somadeva’s Rasendracūḍāmaṇi
    13th c. CE Konark temple iron beams; Ānandakanda; Rasaprakāśasudhākara of Yaśodhara
    late 13th–14th c. CE Rasaratnasamuccaya attributed to Vāgbhaṭa; Nityanātha’s Rasaratnākara
    early 14th c. CE Ṭhakkura Pherū’s treatises on gems, coins and metals, based on the Delhi mint and treasury
    14th–16th c. CE Large-scale zinc production at Zawar
    16th c. CE Bhāvamiśra’s Bhāvaprakāśa describes phiraṅgaroga (syphilis) and its treatment with mercurials
    c. 1598 Abuʾl Faẓl’s Āʾīn-i Akbarī: the Mughal mint and refining, saltpetre, distillation of spirits and perfumes
    16th–17th c. CE Āyurvedaprakāśa of Mādhava (17th c.); Golconda diamond trade (Tavernier)
    1605–1627 Reign of Jahāngīr, whose memoirs credit the mother of Nūr Jahān with the discovery of rose attar
    17th c. CE Dutch and English East India Companies buy Bihar saltpetre at Patna for export to Europe
    1700, 1721 English Calico Acts restrict Indian printed cottons
    1720s Diamonds found in Brazil; end of India’s near-monopoly
    1738 William Champion’s English patent for zinc distillation
    1807 Francis Buchanan publishes his account of crucible steel-making in Mysore (observed 1800–1801); he also names laterite
    1842 Henry Yule describes iron smelting in the Khasi Hills
    1859–1862 Indigo disturbances (“Blue Mutiny”) in Bengal
    1880 John Taylor and Sons take over the management of the Kolar gold mines
    1881 V. Ball’s Manual of the Geology of India: Economic Geology records old mine workings and slag heaps
    1892, 1901 P. C. Ray begins manufacturing pharmaceuticals in Calcutta; incorporated as Bengal Chemical and Pharmaceutical Works
    1897 BASF markets synthetic indigo
    1902 Kolar Gold Fields supplied with hydro-electric power from Śivanasamudra on the Kaveri
    1902–1909 P. C. Ray, A History of Hindu Chemistry
    1912 Hadfield’s analysis of the Delhi iron pillar
    1924 Indian Chemical Society founded, with P. C. Ray as first president
    1966, 1967 Hindustan Zinc and Hindustan Copper founded as state companies
    1979 F. R. Allchin argues that Gandhāran pottery vessels were parts of stills
    1983 Craddock, Gurjar and Hegde publish the Zawar zinc retorts
    1984 Willies, Craddock, Gurjar and Hegde publish their survey of the ancient lead and zinc mines of Rajasthan
    1996 D. G. White, The Alchemical Body
    1996 Supreme Court orders on polluting industries in the Taj Trapezium Zone around the Taj Mahal
    1999 Srinivasan’s lead isotope and trace-element study of South Indian metal images
    2000–2002 R. Balasubramaniam’s studies of the Delhi pillar’s protective film
    2001 Closure of the Kolar Gold Fields
    2004, 2008 Saper and colleagues report lead, mercury and arsenic in Ayurvedic products (JAMA)
    2015 Analysis attributes the discoloration of the Taj Mahal mainly to deposited carbon particles and dust
    2025 Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology publishes radiometric dates from Sivagalai and other sites, interpreted as showing iron in the 4th millennium BCE (a claim under review)
