    Chronology of Texts, Travellers and Surveys¶

    Dates are approximate and many are disputed. Where traditional dates differ from those accepted in scholarship, the latter are given. Dates of travellers are those of their journeys.

    Date Texts, travellers and events
    c. 2600–1900 BCE Mature Harappan cities (wider span c. 3300–1300 BCE)
    c. 1500–1200 BCE Core of the Ṛgveda; Nadīstuti (10.75) among the later hymns
    c. 1200–500 BCE Later Vedic texts; Atharvaveda Bhūmi Sūkta; Śulbasūtras (later Vedic period)
    5th c. BCE The Buddha (c. 480–400 BCE, short chronology); the sixteen mahājanapadas as remembered in the Pali canon
    327–325 BCE Alexander in the north-west; voyage of Nearchus
    c. 300 BCE Megasthenes at Pāṭaliputra
    c. 268–232 BCE Reign of Aśoka; edicts naming Jambudvīpa and frontier peoples
    c. 400 BCE–400 CE Composition and expansion of the Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa
    c. 50 BCE–300 CE Arthaśāstra in its present form
    mid-1st c. CE Periplus Maris Erythraei
    c. 150 CE Ptolemy’s Geography; Rudradāman’s Junagadh inscription
    1st–5th c. CE Inscriptions of Indian sailors in the Hoq cave, Socotra
    c. 300–1000 CE Main period of compilation of the Purāṇic bhuvanakośas
    c. 335–375 Samudragupta; Hariṣeṇa’s praise-poem on the Prayāga pillar describes his conquests in concentric zones
    c. 4th–5th c. Vasubandhu, Abhidharmakośa (Buddhist cosmology); early Sanskrit inscriptions in Southeast Asia; Kālidāsa
    399–412 Journey of Faxian
    499 Āryabhaṭa, Āryabhaṭīya
    mid-6th c. Varāhamihira, Pañcasiddhāntikā and Bṛhatsaṃhitā
    628 Brahmagupta, Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta
    629–645 Journey of Xuanzang
    634 Aihole inscription of Pulakeśin II, naming “the three Mahārāṣṭrakas”
    671–695 Journey of Yijing (in India 673–685)
    c. 700–750 Śaṅkara (traditional dates 788–820)
    851 Account of China and India attributed to the merchant Sulaymān
    early 10th c. Rājaśekhara, Kāvyamīmāṃsā, with its division of the regions of India
    982–83 Ḥudūd al-ʿĀlam, the oldest surviving geography in Persian
    c. 1001, 1086 Cōḻa land surveys under Rājarāja I and Kulottuṅga I
    c. 1017–1030 Al-Bīrūnī in the Punjab; Taḥqīq mā li-l-Hind c. 1030
    c. 1025 Cōḻa naval expedition against Śrīvijaya
    1025 Al-Bīrūnī, Taḥdīd nihāyāt al-amākin, on finding the coordinates of places
    1025–26 Maḥmūd of Ghazni’s raid on Somanātha
    1148–49 Kalhaṇa, Rājataraṅgiṇī
    1150 Bhāskara II, Siddhāntaśiromaṇi (with the Bhuvanakośa)
    12th c. Lakṣmīdhara, Tīrthavivecanakāṇḍa
    c. 1200 Sack of Nālandā during Bakhtiyār Khaljī’s campaigns
    1225 Zhao Rugua, Zhufan zhi
    1234–36 Dharmasvāmin in Bihar
    c. 1260 Juzjānī, Ṭabaqāt-i Nāṣirī
    c. 1292–93 Marco Polo on the coasts of southern India
    1318 Amīr Khusrau, Nuh Sipihr, in praise of Hindustan
    1333 Jinaprabhasūri completes the Vividhatīrthakalpa
    1333–c. 1346 Ibn Baṭṭūṭa in India, the Maldives, Sri Lanka and Bengal
    1340s Al-ʿUmarī, Masālik al-abṣār, describes the provinces of the Tughluq empire
    1370 Mahendra Sūri, Yantrarāja, on the astrolabe
    1405–1433 Ming fleets of Zheng He call at Calicut
    c. 1420s–1444 Niccolò de’ Conti in India and Southeast Asia
    1431 Parameśvara, Dṛggaṇita
    1442–43 ʿAbd al-Razzāq Samarqandī at Calicut and Vijayanagara
    c. 1469–72 Afanasy Nikitin in the Bahmani Deccan
    c. 1490 Ibn Mājid, Kitāb al-Fawāʾid
    1498 Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut with a pilot taken on at Malindi
    1512–15 Tomé Pires, Suma Oriental; Duarte Barbosa’s book c. 1516
    c. 1520, c. 1535 Domingos Paes and Fernão Nunes at Vijayanagara
    1540–45 Sher Shāh Sūr rebuilds the trunk road from Bengal to the Punjab
    1576–82 Gulbadan Begum’s Hajj
    1580 Akbar’s ten-year (dahsāla) revenue settlement; the empire divided into twelve ṣūbas
    1590s Abū’l-Faẓl, Āʾīn-i Akbarī
    1594 Amīn Aḥmad Rāzī, Haft Iqlīm
    1615–19 Sir Thomas Roe at the court of Jahāngīr
    1630–1668 Tavernier’s six journeys
    1647 Ṣādiq Iṣfahānī, Shāhid-i Ṣādiq
    c. 1658–69 François Bernier in India
    1695–96 Sujān Rāy Bhandārī, Khulāṣat al-Tawārīkh
    1720s–30s Jai Singh II’s observatories at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Vārāṇasī and Mathurā
    1727 Foundation of Jaipur by Sawai Jai Singh II
    1757 The zamīndārī of the Twenty-Four Parganas granted to the East India Company
    1761 ʿAlī Muḥammad Khān, Mirʾāt-i Aḥmadī
    1765–68 Mīrzā Iʿtiṣām al-Dīn’s journey to Britain
    1767 James Rennell appointed Surveyor General of Bengal
    1779–81 Rennell’s Bengal Atlas
    1782–83 Rennell’s Map of Hindoostan and Memoir
    1783–86 Francis Gladwin’s English translation of parts of the Āʾīn-i Akbarī
    1788 Ghulām Ḥusain Salīm, Riyāẓ al-Salāṭīn
    1794 The Travels of Dean Mahomet
    1799–1803 Mīrzā Abū Ṭālib Khān in Europe
    1802 Lambton begins the Great Trigonometrical Survey
    1805 Francis Wilford admits that his Purāṇic “sources” were forged
    1812–13 Mīr ʿIzzatullāh’s journey from Kashmir to Bukhara for William Moorcroft
    1815 Colin Mackenzie first Surveyor General of India
    1830–43 George Everest Surveyor General; the Great Arc completed in the early 1840s
    1837 English and the regional languages replace Persian in the courts and revenue offices
    1856 Height of Peak XV (Mount Everest) announced
    1861 Translations of the Sūryasiddhānta (Bāpū Deva Śāstrī) and Golādhyāya (Wilkinson) published
    1863 Montgomerie begins training the pundit explorers
    1863–64 Hajj of Nawab Sikandar Begum of Bhopal
    1865–75 Journeys of Nain Singh Rawat
    1871 Cunningham, The Ancient Geography of India
    1878 Survey of India formed from the separate surveys
    1878–82 Journeys of Kishen Singh (1878–82) and Sarat Chandra Das (1879, 1881–82)
    1880–84 Kinthup’s Tsangpo mission
    1881 First synchronous all-India census; first edition of the Imperial Gazetteer of India
    1893 The Durand Line agreed with Afghanistan
    1894–1928 Grierson’s Linguistic Survey of India
    1896 Ś. B. Dīkṣita’s history of Indian astronomy (Marathi)
    1899 Nundo Lal Dey, Geographical Dictionary of Ancient and Mediaeval India
    1905, 1911 Partition of Bengal and its reversal; the capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi
    1907–09 Imperial Gazetteer of India, new edition in 26 volumes
    1913–14 Simla Conference; the McMahon Line proposed
    1936 Orissa formed as a province on linguistic lines; Sind separated from Bombay
    1947 Partition; the Radcliffe awards for the Punjab and Bengal; integration of the princely states
    1953 Andhra State formed
    1956 States Reorganisation Act; National Atlas Organisation founded (later NATMO)
    1960, 1966 Bombay State divided into Maharashtra and Gujarat; Punjab reorganised and Haryana formed
    1969 ISRO founded; remote sensing satellites from 1979
    2000, 2014 Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand formed; Telangana separated from Andhra Pradesh
